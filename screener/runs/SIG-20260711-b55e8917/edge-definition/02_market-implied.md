**The backed-out earnings path arithmetic:**

At an EV of ~$75.4 billion and using the sector-comparable range as the "exit":

If the market ultimately prices Carvana at 15x EV/EBITDA (a still-generous premium to dealer groups at 6–8x, reflecting its superior margin trajectory):
- Required NTM EBITDA to support today's EV: $75.4B ÷ 15x = **$5.0 billion**
- Carvana's Q1 2026 annualised adj. EBITDA run-rate: ~$2.7 billion ($672M × 4)
- Gap: $5.0B vs $2.7B annualised = EBITDA must nearly double from the Q1 run-rate

If instead the market exits at 22x EV/EBITDA (the Seeking Alpha/TIKR forward assumption):
- Required NTM EBITDA to sustain today's EV: $75.4B ÷ 22x = **$3.4 billion**
- Gap: $3.4B vs $2.7B annualised = EBITDA must grow ~26% from Q1's run-rate

**What the price implies:** At the current EV of $75.4 billion, the stock is pricing in one of two scenarios:

- *Multiple-expansion scenario (priced in):* Carvana is awarded a permanent 22–30x EBITDA premium (justified by the new-car optionality / disruption narrative), and NTM adj. EBITDA grows from the Q1 run-rate of ~$2.7B to roughly $3.4–$3.8 billion over the next 12 months — a 26–40% EBITDA lift from Q1's already-record quarter.
- *No-premium scenario (the bear):* If the market re-rates Carvana toward a 12–15x EBITDA multiple (still above dealer peers) and EBITDA growth disappoints, intrinsic EV would be $32–$40 billion, implying a stock price of approximately $20–$30 — roughly 55–70% below the current level (rough calculation: ($40B EV - $3.1B net debt) ÷ 1.10B shares ≈ $34; ($32B EV - $3.1B net debt) ÷ 1.10B shares ≈ $26).

**What is priced, plainly:** The market is paying for a sustained 40%+ unit growth trajectory AND a durable premium multiple. The current price does not contemplate any scenario where the new-car franchise business fails to scale or where the used-car growth rate reverts to historical norms. It is a high-scenario / high-multiple pricing, not a base-case conservative valuation.

*Interpretation:* The current EV/EBITDA of 29–32x (TTM) is pricing a near-perfect execution outcome: EBITDA must reach $3.4–$5.0 billion in the next 12–24 months at 22–15x exit to justify today's EV, compared with the $2.7B Q1 annualised run-rate. The stock is pricing the new-car franchise as a real business worth paying for — not as an experiment worth a small option premium.

## 3. Implied Scenario Interpretation

The market is currently pricing Carvana as a near-perfect execution story. Block 3 (multiple arithmetic) shows that at today's EV of ~$75.4 billion and a TTM EV/EBITDA of 29–32x, the price implies NTM adj. EBITDA must reach $3.4–$5.0 billion — a 26–85% lift from the Q1 2026 annualised run-rate of $2.7 billion — just to tread water at a still-elevated exit multiple. There is no room in the current price for the new-car franchise to stall, for used-car unit growth to slow toward historical norms, or for the EBITDA margin to miss the upward trajectory baked into analyst models. Block 1 (estimate dispersion) reinforces this: analysts sit 77% bullish at ratings level but the consensus price target of ~$93 is 41% above the July 11 close, meaning even the bulls believe the stock is being held back by uncertainty — not that it is overvalued on their estimates. Block 4 (options) confirms the market is not complacent about the July 29 Q2 print: implied volatility in the 77th percentile and a ~15.5% implied move say the options market is treating the upcoming earnings as a binary event, not a routine quarter. Block 5 (short interest) adds a final layer: bears have recently added to positions (SI rose from 72.2M to 74.8M shares), lining up in front of Q2 results, and this latent short base becomes fuel for a squeeze if the results confirm the new-car volume story. Taken together, the market is pricing a high-confidence, high-multiple continuation of the disruption narrative — but the elevated IV and growing short interest reveal that a non-trivial part of the market is paying specifically to be positioned for the thesis to break on the Q2 print.

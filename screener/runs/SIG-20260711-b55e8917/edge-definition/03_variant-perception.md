# M0.6.3 Variant Perception — SIG-20260711-b55e8917

## 1. The Variant

The market is paying for Carvana to disrupt 16,990 dealerships, but Stellantis has structurally capped the pace at one additional acquisition per rolling 12-month period. At that rate, Carvana can reach 8 new-car stores by mid-2027. The new-car gross profit from 8 stores at peak run-rate is roughly $100 million per year — about 3.7% of Carvana's Q1 2026 annualised adj. EBITDA of $2.7 billion. Yet the stock's EV of $75.4 billion sits $53.8 billion above what 8x EBITDA (the dealer-group median) on $2.7 billion would justify. That gap is what the market is pricing as disruption optionality. The cap-constrained new-car path cannot support it.

## 3. Mechanisms Missing from Consensus

1. **mechanism_1 — the Stellantis acquisition cap as a hard ceiling on new-car optionality:** Stellantis updated its dealer consolidation policy on January 6, 2026 to limit any single entity to one CDJR dealership acquisition per rolling 12-month period. This directly caps Carvana's new-car store count at a pace of 1 additional store per year, regardless of capital availability or execution quality. Northcoast Research's John Healy flagged the policy as "not coincidental," but no major sell-side model has published a terminal new-car store count under the cap constraint or derived what that cap implies for the EV premium. Consensus assumption #4 ("the new-car business is treated as option value") is right in form but wrong in pricing: the market IS paying for that optionality (the $53.8B EV premium), but the option's exercise path is structurally gated by an OEM that controls only 8% of the U.S. new-car market and has already signalled it will slow Carvana's accumulation. A single-OEM cap at 1 store per year cannot support a disruption-of-16,990-dealerships narrative within any horizon that a current investor can reasonably own the stock through. (Attacks consensus assumption #4: "the new-car business is expected to remain a single-digit percentage of total revenue through the forecast horizon and is treated as upside optionality, not a base-case driver.")

2. **mechanism_2 — the crossover demand creation metric is not yet reflected as a used-car GPU tailwind in models:** BTIG has reported that 75% of Carvana's new-car buyers initially searched for used vehicles. If this holds at scale, the new-car franchise is structurally pulling customers who would otherwise have competed in the used-car pool — meaning the new-car expansion is simultaneously removing demand pressure from the used-car segment where Carvana earns $6,900+ GPU, not creating a clean volume addition. No sell-side model found in searches run on 2026-07-12 has modelled the demand substitution effect between new-car franchise buyers (who came in used-car shoppers) and the used-car GPU line. If 75% of new-car buyers are diverted from used-car intent, the incremental new-car GPU of ~$6,200 substitutes for an incremental used-car GPU of ~$6,900 — a $700 per-unit opportunity-cost drag that is not captured in BTIG's "<3% total GPU dilution" figure, which treats new-car as an add-on rather than a partial used-car demand cannibalizer. (Attacks consensus assumption #2: "the retail GPU headwind is temporary friction, not a structural margin ceiling — reconditioning efficiencies and ADESA network scale will restore per-unit economics." The substitution drag is a new, persistent component of GPU pressure that is separate from reconditioning headwinds.)

## 6. Verdict

Verdict: variant weak — the Stellantis acquisition cap mechanism (mechanism_1) is a real and documented structural ceiling on the new-car expansion story the market is pricing, and the departure between priced new-car optionality (~$53.8B EV premium) and cap-constrained optionality value (~$1.6–$6.2B) is large and numeric. However, the variant is weakened on two dimensions: (a) the sell-side coverage gap is partial — BTIG has independently covered new-car unit economics (mechanism_2 is not a coverage gap), and the Stellantis cap is flagged in trade press, so the mechanism is not fully invisible to the market; (b) the variant depends on the $53.8B EV gap above a dealer-group floor multiple being attributable to new-car optionality rather than to a permanent used-car market-share premium — the market may rationally price the used-car franchise at a 15–22x EBITDA multiple on its own merits, which would shrink the "misattributed optionality" to a smaller and less certain number. The departure is directionally correct and numerically large, but the causal linkage between the cap and a specific magnitude of stock downside requires a further assumption about what the market is actually paying for, which reduces the variant from proven to weak.

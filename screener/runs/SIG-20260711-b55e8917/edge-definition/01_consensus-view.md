## 2. Consensus View (sympathetic)

Carvana has completed one of the most remarkable corporate recoveries in recent U.S. equity market history, moving from the edge of bankruptcy in late 2022 to S&P 500 membership in December 2025 and to all-time quarterly records in Q1 2026 ($6.432 billion in revenue, 187,393 used-car retail units). The mainstream buy-side and sell-side view holds that this trajectory is structural, not cyclical: Carvana's online-only model — with no commission salespeople, a single no-haggle price, and a technology-driven reconditioning network via its ADESA acquisition — creates durable cost advantages over traditional franchise dealerships, enabling continued market-share gains in the fragmented, $1.3 trillion U.S. new- and used-car retail market. Analysts at firms including Morgan Stanley (Overweight, $450 price target, $745 bull case), Baird (Outperform, $88 price target), and RBC Capital (Outperform, $85 price target) see the company executing against what Morgan Stanley calls a "transitory retail GPU headwind" and expect Q2 2026 used-car retail unit volume to exceed 185,000 units, up roughly 40% year-over-year, continuing the Q1 pace. Consensus FY2026 revenue is $27.79 billion (+37% year-over-year), and the 22-analyst panel covering the stock rates it a "Buy" with an average 12-month price target of ~$93, implying approximately 40% upside from the current trading price of ~$66. The new-car franchise foray into seven Stellantis dealerships is noted in the coverage universe as a potential incremental revenue stream and long-term disruption vector — John Murphy, a veteran auto-sector analyst, called it "one of the most disruptive forces that auto retailing has seen in the U.S. market in decades" — though the dominant consensus model is built around used-car market share growth, not new-car franchise expansion. [Sources: Morgan Stanley via TheStreet, July 2026; StockAnalysis.com, Jun 23 2026; MarketBeat, Jul 12 2026; CNBC, Jun 16 2026; BusinessWire, Apr 29 2026.]

## 3. Contrary View

- **exists:** true

**Who:** Gotham City Research (independent short-seller)

**Evidence and date:** On January 28–29, 2026, Gotham City Research published a short-seller report alleging that Carvana overstated 2023–2024 earnings by more than $1 billion through loan sales to undisclosed related parties (DriveTime and Bridgecrest, both controlled by Garcia family members), with DriveTime carrying leverage of 20–40x earnings. The report predicted that Carvana's 2025 10-K would be delayed and prior 10-Ks would be restated. Carvana shares fell 14% on January 28–29, 2026, to approximately $410, from a recent high of $486.89. [CNBC, Jan 28 2026; Alpha Spread; Bloomberg, Jan 28 2026]

**Their numeric anchor:** Greater than $1 billion in cumulative earnings overstatement for 2023–2024; implied fair value well below the then-trading price of ~$410; stock subsequently recovered more than 15% from the post-report low within days.

**Market resolution:** JPMorgan analyst Rajat Gupta publicly rebutted the report as containing "significant misrepresentation of facts," and the stock recovered most of the drop within a week. As of July 2026, the consensus continues to treat the allegations as unproven and no 10-K restatement or auditor change has been announced. However, securities law investigations were opened and the overhang is documented. [Seeking Alpha, Jan 2026; TheStreet, July 2026]

**Secondary contrary signal:** As of May 2026, 12.1% of the publicly available float is sold short (15.17 million shares), indicating a durable, meaningful minority betting against the consensus recovery narrative. [MarketBeat, May 2026]

**Scoring notes:**

- **DIR-001 (Online / DTC used-car retail, 85):** Mechanism is 1-step and directly confirmed by all four WC rows — the business model itself is the one being validated. Magnitude scores 20 not 25 because only 7 stores are confirmed operational; the broader market impact is still nascent. Speed is 20 because the model is proven operational today but broader adoption or replication by the same operator or others takes months. Reversibility scores 20 because the business model is hard to reverse (capital already deployed, brand repositioning underway) but not fully locked — the operator could exit the franchise business.

- **DIR-002 (Traditional franchise dealership networks, 80):** Mechanism is 1-step — Carvana directly won volume at existing franchise locations by displacing the prior operator. Magnitude at 20 reflects that volume impact is confirmed at one store at a scale that made it the #1 Stellantis store nationally, but the confirmed displacement footprint is 7 stores out of 16,990. Reversibility at 15 reflects that affected traditional dealerships cannot easily reprice their cost structure (commission salespeople, physical lot overhead) without significant structural change.

- **DIR-003 (Automotive OEM / vehicle manufacturers, 80):** Mechanism is 1-step — more throughput at the OEM's own franchise directly benefits the OEM's new-vehicle revenue. Magnitude 20 because the volume uplift at Casa Grande is dramatic but only one Stellantis brand and seven stores are confirmed. Speed 20 because OEM revenue follows unit shipments, which are already happening. Reversibility 20 because OEM supply relationships are sticky — once a high-volume channel is established, it is not easily removed.

- **IND-001 (Auto F&I / non-bank auto lenders, 70):** One step removed from unit sales: more new and used cars sold → more loan originations and F&I product attachment. Magnitude 15 because the confirmed volume uplift is real but small relative to total U.S. auto-loan origination. Speed 20 because loan origination follows the sale immediately. Reversibility 20 because auto loan books, once made, cannot be easily unwound.

- **IND-002 (Auto parts, accessories & service, 60):** Two steps removed. The service bay retention at acquired stores (WC-001) is confirmed, but no quantified service revenue data exists in any WC row. Magnitude 10 because the effect is real but small and unquantified at confirmed scale. Speed 15 because vehicle parc build-up that drives service demand takes time. Reversibility 20 because vehicles once sold generate recurring service demand that is not easily diverted.

- **IND-003 (Digital automotive marketplaces, 45):** Three steps and the connection is weak — Carvana's growth confirms consumer openness to online car buying, but that does not directly transfer volume or revenue to third-party listing platforms. Magnitude 10 because the benefit is behavioral and diffuse. Speed 10 because consumer behavior shifts are slow. Parked.

- **HARM-002 (Commission-based auto sales workforce, 60):** Mechanism is direct and confirmed — Carvana's model has no commission salespeople. Magnitude 5 because only 7 stores are confirmed affected; 16,990 exist nationally. Speed 25 because the harm to this workforce is immediate at each acquired site. Reversibility 10 because displaced commission-only roles are structurally eliminated, not temporarily reduced.

- **HARM-003 (Vehicle auction houses, 45):** Two steps and relies on an inferential bypass mechanism not confirmed in any WC row. Magnitude and speed both weak and unquantified. Parked.

## 3. Pair-Trade Notes

**Pair A — online DTC used-car retail (DIR-001) long vs. traditional franchise dealership networks (DIR-002) short.**
The same event that confirms the online-only model's volume superiority (14x throughput at Casa Grande under WC-002) simultaneously confirms volume displacement at traditional operators selling the same brand. The two sides of the trade are directly opposed: one model is structurally gaining, the other is structurally defending. Industry vs. industry, no tickers.

**Pair B — automotive OEM supply chains (DIR-003) long vs. commission-based sales workforce / dealership staffing model (HARM-002) short.**
Higher OEM unit throughput through the new channel is confirmed (WC-002: Casa Grande became the #1 Stellantis U.S. volume store). At the same time, the workforce model that delivered 30–50 units/month is replaced by one that delivers 700+. The long side captures volume growth; the short side captures structural displacement of a specific business model inside the dealership sector. Industry vs. business model, no tickers.

**Pair C — auto F&I / non-bank auto lenders (IND-001) long vs. traditional franchise dealership F&I desks (subset of DIR-002) short.**
Volume flowing through a lower-overhead, online-first channel may shift F&I product origination toward the operator's own financing arms and away from traditional dealership-embedded F&I desks. This is a second-order bifurcation within the broader beneficiary/harmed split. Both sides benefit from higher unit volumes, but the distribution of F&I economics between channels shifts.

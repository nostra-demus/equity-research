# Earnings Module — AMZN (Synthesis)

## Abstract

Amazon's earnings are accelerating at the operating level: EBIT margin reached a record 13.1% in Q1 2026 and CFO has grown faster than revenue for three consecutive years, driven by an AWS cloud consumption surge (28% growth, fastest in 15 quarters) and advertising scaling at 22–24% with near-zero incremental cost. The biggest driver is the pace at which AWS revenue outpaces the depreciation charge accumulating from an annualized $170B gross capex program — a race between billing ramp and D&A that cannot be tracked quarterly because Amazon does not disclose quarterly D&A. The consensus bar is stretched on operating income: the Street sits at $23.4B for Q2 2026, at the 86th percentile of management's own $20–24B guidance range, discounting all three named cost headwinds. The biggest risk is that $131.8B in FY2025 capex and $43.2B in Q1 2026 alone begin flowing through the Technology and Infrastructure line faster than new AWS billings offset them — an asymmetric, opaque headwind carrying a Low confidence tag because the D&A schedule is not disclosed. The red-flag agent returned a **Critical concerns** verdict; earnings are accelerating on operating metrics but carry material structural opacity around GAAP EPS quality and D&A timing.

## 1A. Module Disconfirmation

**Strongest bear point:** The D&A from the AI capex wave is the single most dangerous and least visible risk. Gross capex ran at $43.2B in Q1 2026 alone; once spent, that depreciation is locked in and will flow through the Technology and Infrastructure line over 5–30 year asset lives regardless of how AWS revenue performs. T&I costs already rose 120 basis points of revenue in FY2025. If the Q4 2025 / Q1 2026 capex cohorts enter the depreciation schedule before the corresponding customer billing begins (management explicitly disclosed a 6–24 month lag), EBIT could compress materially and invisibly — analysts cannot track it quarterly because Amazon does not break out D&A from T&I in the 10-Q.

**Strongest bull point:** The $364B AWS backlog (a 49% jump in a single quarter), combined with management statements that supply — not demand — is the binding constraint, suggests the revenue ramp will outpace D&A if data center installation and billing proceed on the stated timeline. Fulfillment operating leverage is also real and currently delivering: units growing 15% while fulfillment costs grew 9% and shipping costs grew 12% in Q1 2026.

**Single killer risk specific to earnings quality and the beat/miss setup:** The Q1 2026 normalized EPS beat of +69.5% drove a 12.4% upward revision to FY2026 consensus ($8.69), but that beat was materially amplified by Anthropic mark-to-model gains (~$15B booked in Q1 2026 per the 10-Q subsequent event). If Capital IQ's normalized EPS construct did not fully exclude this gain, the FY2026 EPS consensus is built on an inflated Q1 baseline. Every subsequent quarter would then print below run-rate on normalized EPS even on strong operating results — a systematic miss pattern manufactured by an accounting reclassification.

**Disconfirming evidence already visible:** AWS EBIT margin declined from 37.1% (FY2024) to 35.4% (FY2025) — precisely the D&A-overtaking-revenue dynamic — even though Q1 2026 recovered to 37.7%. Strict FCF is negative LTM (−$2.5B) while EBITDA accelerates, confirming the capex wave is real, not theoretical. The operating income consensus sits at the 86th percentile of management's own guidance range, meaning the Street has already partially ignored the risks management named; these are not tail risks, they are named, quantified line items.

## 5. Earnings Setup Summary

### Revenue Setup

The current revenue trajectory is sustainable at a 13–16% pace but not fully organic at the Q1 2026 reported rate of 17%. Approximately 1.8 percentage points of Q1 2026 revenue growth came from a favorable FX translation ($2.9B tailwind); management guided Q2 2026 FX to a near-neutral 10 basis points headwind, meaning the true organic engine is running at 15% and the reported rate will step down sequentially even if underlying demand holds. The single factor that would flip revenue direction is AWS growth decelerating from 28% toward 20% — either from a capacity installation delay or a macro-driven enterprise IT budget freeze. There is no material M&A, FX-restatement, or accounting reclassification inflating the revenue line (FY2025 acquisitions were explicitly immaterial per 10-K Note 5); the growth is organic. The reported FY2025 revenue of $716.9B and the Q1 2026 LTM of $742.8B both reflect genuine consumption-driven expansion across retail and cloud.

### Margin Setup

Current margins are NOT at a normalized run-rate; they are best described as a post-trough recovery still in progress. The FY2022 EBIT trough of 2.6% was caused by over-investment in fulfillment and a Rivian loss — both non-structural. The recovery to 13.1% in Q1 2026 is real and largely company-driven (AWS mix shift, fulfillment efficiency, advertising leverage). Whether 13%+ is sustainable through the next cycle depends on a single question: can AWS revenue growth (currently 28%) outpace the D&A ramp from the $131.8B FY2025 / $43.2B Q1 2026 capex program? If T&I cost rates continue rising by 100–200 basis points per year as new capacity enters the depreciation schedule, EBIT margins will compress from today's record level even as AWS revenue grows — and management does not disclose quarterly D&A, making this invisible until a quarter prints. The segment with the most structural margin-protection is AWS itself, where Trainium chip adoption is expected to provide "several hundred basis points of operating margin advantage" at scale (CEO, Q1 2026 call) — but this is 2–4 years from realization. The company has no contractual cost pass-through in retail; Amazon absorbs input cost increases and passes them to 3P sellers via FBA surcharges, with a 1–2 quarter lag.

### Quality Check

The largest gap between reported and economic earnings is the systematic inclusion of large equity investment marks in GAAP net income: $15.3B in FY2025 gains (Anthropic reclassification + Rivian) inflated reported EPS from a normalized $4.70 to $7.17 — a $2.47/share or 34% inflation. This pattern recurred in FY2021 (+$14.7B), FY2022 (−$15.9B), and FY2025 (+$15.3B), and a further ~$15B was booked in Q1 2026 per the 10-Q subsequent event. The gap is widening as Amazon's strategic investment portfolio grows, particularly the Anthropic position ($60.6B Level 3). The recurring baseline "Other operating expense" of $700–800M per year (before the FY2025 spike to $4.6B) is labeled as non-recurring every year — it is not. For modeling normalized earnings, this synthesis recommends starting from operating income (EBIT) rather than GAAP net income or normalized EPS, because EBIT is unaffected by investment gains, is the metric management guides to, and is the only earnings line that was directly disclosed with guidance. If using EPS, use normalized EPS ($8.69 FY2026 consensus) but apply a SBC haircut (approximately $1.80/share at current run rate) to arrive at true economic earnings — reducing the effective bar to approximately $7.00 on a fully-loaded basis.

### Consensus Bar

For the bar to be beaten materially on operating income in Q2 2026, AWS would need to sustain at or above 28% revenue growth while all three named cost headwinds (Leo ~$1B, fuel ~$1B, SBC seasonal step-up of unknown magnitude) land at the low end simultaneously — a set of conditions that must be true together. The revenue bar, by contrast, is set just below the guidance midpoint ($195.8B consensus vs $196.5B midpoint) and Amazon has beaten revenue consensus in all four of the most recently reported quarters by 1.0%–3.5%. The consensus is most likely set incorrectly on the high side for operating income: the $23.4B number embeds record AWS momentum continuing and zero adverse cost realization from three items management explicitly warned about. The most anchored piece of the current consensus is the FY2026 revenue estimate ($823.4B, a 14.9% growth assumption), which is defensible given the AWS backlog and advertising trajectory. The operating income consensus ($103.8B for FY2026) is less defensible if the T&I cost rate continues rising. A meaningful fraction of the current Q2 2026 revenue beat expectation is also tied to the Prime Day pull-forward into Q2 — an event-driven lift that compresses the Q3 comparison.

## 5b. Leverage & Capital Structure

**Trigger assessment:**
- Net Debt / Adjusted EBITDA (broad basis, includes lease liabilities): $55,518M ÷ $145,731M = **0.38x** at FY2025 year-end — below the 3.0x threshold for Trigger A.
- YoY change: broad-basis net debt moved from $54,199M (FY2024) to $55,518M (FY2025) = +$1,319M absolute increase, or +2.4% — below the 50% threshold. Net debt / EBITDA: 0.45x (FY2024) → 0.38x (FY2025), a decline of 0.07x — below the 1.0x change threshold for Trigger B.
- At the LTM March 2026 level, broad-basis net debt rose to $92,451M (a +70.6% increase from FY2024 year-end $54,199M), driven by a sharp increase in reported long-term debt ($73.4B at Dec-31-2025 to $127.3B at Mar-31-2026, likely reflecting new debt issuance in Q1 2026). However, on a strict basis (financial debt only, no leases), Amazon held net cash of approximately $12.5B at March 31, 2026. The broad-basis LTM jump reflects the lease liability inclusion methodology, not a true leverage deterioration; the strict basis moved from net cash of $46.1B at FY2025 year-end to net cash of $12.5B at Q1 2026-end, a material reduction in the net cash cushion but not a net-debt position.

Neither Trigger A nor Trigger B fires on the criteria as written (annual basis comparison), though the LTM Q1 2026 broad-basis change warrants watching. Leverage is within normal range and did not change materially during the annual period — no dedicated treatment required.

## 8. Note To The Final Synthesizer

- **Dominant earnings trend:** Operating earnings are genuinely accelerating — EBIT grew from $13.3B (FY2022) to $80.0B (FY2025) to an LTM rate of $85.4B, and Q1 2026 operating margin of 13.1% is the highest in Amazon's history. The driver is not one thing but a convergence: AWS growing at 28%, advertising growing at 22%+, fulfillment costs growing slower than units, and the absence of FY2025 one-time charges ($4.6B) creating a YoY base-effect tailwind in FY2026.

- **Earnings quality: split verdict.** Operating income quality is high — CFO/EBITDA at 95%+ for three years, negative cash conversion cycle, no accrual fraud signals. GAAP net income quality is medium: equity investment swings ($15.3B in FY2025, $14.7B in FY2021, −$15.9B in FY2022) make reported EPS structurally unreliable as an earnings trend indicator. Normalized EPS is the right metric but the Q1 2026 normalized EPS of $2.78 may itself contain Anthropic mark-to-model gains that Capital IQ has not separately stripped — this has not been confirmed and is the most consequential quality uncertainty for the FY2026 consensus.

- **Consensus bar:** Revenue bar is beatable (consensus sits just below the guidance midpoint and Amazon has beaten revenue in all four of the most recently reported quarters). Operating income bar is stretched — the $23.4B consensus for Q2 2026 requires everything going right simultaneously. For FY2026, the EBIT consensus of $103.8B implies a T&I cost rate holding at or below current levels; if T&I continues rising, the FY2026 EBIT consensus could be 5–10% too high.

- **Next-quarter setup and Q3 look-ahead:** Q2 2026 is balanced. Q3 2026 looks structurally cleaner: the SBC step-up is Q2-specific, Leo costs begin partial capitalization in Q4, and Prime Day will have already been recognized in Q2 (removing it as a Q3 variable). The D&A risk does not diminish in Q3 — it compounds.

- **Red-flag agent severity verdict (verbatim): Critical concerns.** 2 critical flags, 14 high flags, 10 medium flags, 26 triggered total. This must be conveyed to the master synthesizer without softening. The two critical flags are: (1) Anthropic Level 3 investment ($60.6B, mark-to-model) — already injected ~$15.3B into FY2025 GAAP EPS and ~$15B into Q1 2026; GAAP and possibly normalized EPS are unreliable measures of operating performance and create correlated risk (the most important AWS customer relationship is simultaneously a $60B equity-like holding that would impair alongside any AWS demand slowdown); (2) D&A from AI capex wave — the primary thesis risk is asymmetrically dangerous to the downside, opaque in the quarterly income statement, and cannot be modeled from disclosed data.

- **Top sensitivity variable:** The D&A / T&I cost rate step-up from the AI capex wave. It is the variable with the largest absolute EBIT impact in the sensitivity table (~$7.1B average swing) and the only sensitivity variable classified with Low confidence because the D&A schedule is not disclosed quarterly. Management explicitly described a 6–24 month lag from capex spending to customer billing — meaning the $43.2B spent in Q1 2026 will produce depreciation before it produces AWS revenue in a number of cases.

- **Partial-data cap:** The only partial-data limitation is the absence of analyst-level revision breadth (broker identity gated in Capital IQ export). This triggers the max-60 cap on consensus setup, but the final score of 38 is below 60, so the cap does not bind. No other caps apply. The Q1 2026 normalized EPS attribution (whether Capital IQ's $2.78 excludes the Anthropic subsequent-event gain) is the biggest missing data point — this is not a data-availability gap but a data-quality verification need that could change the entire EPS consensus picture.

- **Biggest missing data point:** Audited quarterly D&A disclosure (Amazon does not break out D&A from T&I in the 10-Q) — this is the single data point that would most improve the quality of the forward earnings model and allow the D&A/capex timing risk to be tracked. Second: audited RPO table confirming the $364B AWS backlog composition (committed contracts vs indicative pipeline).

- **What would change the earnings verdict:** An operating income print near $20–21B for Q2 2026 (vs $23.4B consensus) alongside a weak Q3 guidance would flip the verdict to "Earnings decelerating." AWS growth accelerating beyond 30% for two consecutive quarters with stable T&I cost rates would upgrade the verdict to a cleaner "Earnings accelerating" with higher conviction. The current verdict is warranted but the confidence level is medium, not high, because the primary risk variable (D&A timing) is opaque in quarterly disclosures.

- **Thesis type note (CLAUDE.md §14):** The master synthesizer should be aware that this is partly a "sector-cycle / capex-cycle" thesis, not purely company-specific. The question of whether Q2–Q4 2026 beats or misses consensus is dominated by D&A timing on a $170B-annualized capex program — a capital expenditure cycle bet — not by product innovation or market share dynamics. CLAUDE.md §24 filter 3 (high debt and survival test) does not directly apply given Amazon's net cash position on a strict basis, but the FCF compression (strict FCF −$2.5B LTM) and the AI capex commitment create a cash-allocation dependency that the master synthesizer should register.

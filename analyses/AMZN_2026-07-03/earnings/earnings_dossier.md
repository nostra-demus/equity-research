**Disconfirming evidence already visible:** AWS EBIT margin declined from 37.1% (FY2024) to 35.4% (FY2025) — precisely the D&A-overtaking-revenue dynamic — even though Q1 2026 recovered to 37.7%. ## 5. Earnings Setup Summary

### Revenue Setup

The current revenue trajectory is sustainable at a 13–16% pace but not fully organic at the Q1 2026 reported rate of 17%. Approximately 1.8 percentage points of Q1 2026 revenue growth came from a favorable FX translation ($2.9B tailwind); management guided Q2 2026 FX to a near-neutral 10 basis points headwind, meaning the true organic engine is running at 15% and the reported rate will step down sequentially even if underlying demand holds. The single factor that would flip revenue direction is AWS growth decelerating from 28% toward 20% — either from a capacity installation delay or a macro-driven enterprise IT budget freeze. There is no material M&A, FX-restatement, or accounting reclassification inflating the revenue line (FY2025 acquisitions were explicitly immaterial per 10-K Note 5); the growth is organic. The reported FY2025 revenue of $716.9B and the Q1 2026 LTM of $742.8B both reflect genuine consumption-driven expansion across retail and cloud.

### Margin Setup

Current margins are NOT at a normalized run-rate; they are best described as a post-trough recovery still in progress. The FY2022 EBIT trough of 2.6% was caused by over-investment in fulfillment and a Rivian loss — both non-structural. The recovery to 13.1% in Q1 2026 is real and largely company-driven (AWS mix shift, fulfillment efficiency, advertising leverage). Whether 13%+ is sustainable through the next cycle depends on a single question: can AWS revenue growth (currently 28%) outpace the D&A ramp from the $131.8B FY2025 / $43.2B Q1 2026 capex program? If T&I cost rates continue rising by 100–200 basis points per year as new capacity enters the depreciation schedule, EBIT margins will compress from today's record level even as AWS revenue grows — and management does not disclose quarterly D&A, making this invisible until a quarter prints. The segment with the most structural margin-protection is AWS itself, where Trainium chip adoption is expected to provide "several hundred basis points of operating margin advantage" at scale (CEO, Q1 2026 call) — but this is 2–4 years from realization. The company has no contractual cost pass-through in retail; Amazon absorbs input cost increases and passes them to 3P sellers via FBA surcharges, with a 1–2 quarter lag.

### Quality Check

The largest gap between reported and economic earnings is the systematic inclusion of large equity investment marks in GAAP net income: $15.3B in FY2025 gains (Anthropic reclassification + Rivian) inflated reported EPS from a normalized $4.70 to $7.17 — a $2.47/share or 34% inflation. This pattern recurred in FY2021 (+$14.7B), FY2022 (−$15.9B), and FY2025 (+$15.3B), and a further ~$15B was booked in Q1 2026 per the 10-Q subsequent event. The gap is widening as Amazon's strategic investment portfolio grows, particularly the Anthropic position ($60.6B Level 3). The recurring baseline "Other operating expense" of $700–800M per year (before the FY2025 spike to $4.6B) is labeled as non-recurring every year — it is not. For modeling normalized earnings, this synthesis recommends starting from operating income (EBIT) rather than GAAP net income or normalized EPS, because EBIT is unaffected by investment gains, is the metric management guides to, and is the only earnings line that was directly disclosed with guidance. If using EPS, use normalized EPS ($8.69 FY2026 consensus) but apply a SBC haircut (approximately $1.80/share at current run rate) to arrive at true economic earnings — reducing the effective bar to approximately $7.00 on a fully-loaded basis.

### Consensus Bar

For the bar to be beaten materially on operating income in Q2 2026, AWS would need to sustain at or above 28% revenue growth while all three named cost headwinds (Leo ~$1B, fuel ~$1B, SBC seasonal step-up of unknown magnitude) land at the low end simultaneously — a set of conditions that must be true together. The revenue bar, by contrast, is set just below the guidance midpoint ($195.8B consensus vs $196.5B midpoint) and Amazon has beaten revenue consensus in all four of the most recently reported quarters by 1.0%–3.5%. The consensus is most likely set incorrectly on the high side for operating income: the $23.4B number embeds record AWS momentum continuing and zero adverse cost realization from three items management explicitly warned about. The most anchored piece of the current consensus is the FY2026 revenue estimate ($823.4B, a 14.9% growth assumption), which is defensible given the AWS backlog and advertising trajectory. The operating income consensus ($103.8B for FY2026) is less defensible if the T&I cost rate continues rising. A meaningful fraction of the current Q2 2026 revenue beat expectation is also tied to the Prime Day pull-forward into Q2 — an event-driven lift that compresses the Q3 comparison.

## 4. Reported vs Adjusted Metrics

Amazon does not formally report non-GAAP adjusted EBITDA or adjusted EBIT. The company does report one non-GAAP measure officially: *Free Cash Flow* (defined in the 10-K, p.28). ## 6. Key Trend Summary

**Revenue direction — Stable, with Q1 2026 re-accelerating.** Revenue growth has held in a narrow 9–12% band for five years (FY2021–FY2025), with FY2025 at +12.4% being the top of that range. Q1 2026 printed +16.6% year-over-year — the fastest quarterly growth in several years — driven by AWS accelerating to 28% and retail segments posting double-digit gains. Whether the acceleration sustains or Q1 2026 is an outlier (partly aided by FX tailwinds of $2.9B) is the central question for the forward period.

**Margins — Expanding sharply, recovering from a 2022 trough.** The FY2022 EBIT margin of 2.6% was a multi-year low caused by over-investment in fulfillment capacity during COVID, a Rivian investment loss, and cost inflation. The recovery since then has been consistent and steep: EBIT margin went from 2.6% (FY2022) to 6.4% (FY2023) to 10.8% (FY2024) to 11.2% (FY2025) to a record 13.1% in Q1 2026. Gross margin has expanded 830 basis points from FY2021 to FY2025, driven by mix shift toward high-margin AWS and advertising. The margin expansion trend is the most important development in the financials over this five-year window.

**Seasonality — Material, Q4-concentrated.** Approximately 30% of annual revenue lands in Q4, creating meaningful quarterly volatility in reported earnings and cash flow. AWS partially offsets this because cloud demand is not holiday-driven, but the retail segments (combined 82% of revenue) carry the seasonal shape. Investors and analysts should treat Q4 as the earnings anchor quarter and Q1 as the seasonal trough.

**Inflection points.** Two clear inflection points over this period: (1) FY2022 — an operating losses-adjacent period (positive EBIT but depressed; negative reported EPS from Rivian) from which Amazon has recovered strongly; and (2) the FY2025–2026 capex surge driven by AI investment in AWS. The AI-driven capex step-up (gross capex rose from $83B in FY2024 to $132B in FY2025 to $43B in a single quarter in Q1 2026) is compressing FCF materially in the near term — strict FCF went from +$32.9B (FY2024) to +$7.7B (FY2025) to a negative LTM position of −$2.5B. This is intentional and management has framed it as investing ahead of confirmed customer demand (AWS backlog of $364B as of Q1 2026), but it is a genuine FCF inflection point that the rest of this module should treat as structural for 2025–2027.

## 7. The Single Biggest Revenue Driver

**AWS consumption volume, including AI-driven workloads, is the single driver that would most move Amazon's revenue if it changed by 10–20%.**

AWS represents $128.7B of FY2025 revenue and is growing at 28% annually. A 20% change in AWS's growth rate (from 28% to 34%, or from 28% to 22%) would shift incremental AWS revenue by approximately $7–8B per year. That is only the direct revenue effect. Because AWS runs at a 35.4% EBIT margin — versus 7% in North America and 2.9% in International — a 10% swing in AWS revenue has nearly four times the profit impact of an equivalent retail swing. AWS is already the dominant driver of consolidated operating income (57% of the total from 18% of revenue), so any acceleration or deceleration in AWS growth rate has a disproportionate effect on group profitability.

**Current direction:** Accelerating. Q1 2026 AWS growth of 28% year-over-year is the fastest in 15 quarters and is being driven by two concurrent forces — the ongoing enterprise migration of on-premises workloads to the cloud, and the new and structurally larger wave of generative AI workloads. The $364B revenue backlog (up 49% from $244B just one quarter earlier) means AWS has committed future revenue across multi-year customer contracts well in excess of two years of current run-rate revenue. The binding constraint is supply (data center capacity), not demand. Management is investing $43.2B in a single quarter to bring more capacity online.

**The risk to this direction** is a capacity timing mismatch: if new data center capacity (which takes 6–24 months from cash outlay to customer billing per management disclosure) comes online slower than expected, or if a global recession causes enterprise IT budget freezes before these contracted workloads scale, AWS growth would slow. This is the most consequential single variable for Amazon's revenue trajectory over the next 12–18 months.

## 4. Margin Walk — Which Margin Level Matters Most?

**EBIT margin (operating margin) is the primary margin metric for Amazon**, and specifically the segment-level EBIT margin for AWS vs the Stores segments. Here is why.

Amazon does not separately disclose segment EBITDA. The consolidated gross margin is distorted by the accounting classification: AWS costs run through "Technology and infrastructure," not "Cost of sales," so gross margin understates AWS's true gross economics and overstates the retail gross margin relative to what a standalone retailer would show. Using gross margin to track Amazon would give a false read on whether the margin expansion is structural (mix shift to AWS) or cyclical (retail efficiency gains).

EBIT margin captures the full cost structure including the critical T&I line that carries AWS costs. It is the level at which segment operating income is disclosed. It is also what management guides to (Q2 2026 guidance is for operating income of "$20–24 billion," not gross profit or EBITDA).

EBITDA margin is relevant for understanding the FCF bridge (EBITDA minus capex approximates the investment intensity), but it is not the primary driver metric because Amazon's D&A is economically meaningful — unlike a REIT where D&A is non-economic, Amazon's data centers and fulfillment infrastructure do depreciate, and the current capex wave will produce real D&A growth over the next 3–5 years.

For the retail segments specifically, gross margin and fulfillment cost rate are useful sub-metrics for tracking network efficiency (e.g., fulfillment cost as % of revenue went from 15.4% to 15.2% YoY — a sign of leverage). But the consolidated primary metric is EBIT margin.

## 8. The Single Biggest Margin Driver

**The pace at which AWS revenue growth outpaces D&A from the AI infrastructure capex wave is the single largest margin driver for the next 12–24 months.**

Here is the logic. Amazon is spending at an annualized rate of approximately $170B in gross capex as of Q1 2026, almost entirely for AWS data centers, chips, and power. This capex converts into D&A (flowing through "Technology and infrastructure") over 5–30 years. The immediate effect is that T&I costs rose 23% in FY2025 while total revenue rose only 12% — a 120 bps drag on consolidated EBIT margin from this line alone. This drag will continue and likely worsen in 2026 as Q4 2025 and Q1 2026 capex enters the depreciation schedule.

The only offset is AWS revenue growth. If AWS accelerates from 28% (Q1 2026 rate) toward a higher rate as more capacity comes online and the $364B backlog converts to billings, the revenue can outrun the D&A. If AWS growth decelerates — whether from a demand slowdown, a hyperscaler market-share shift, or a competitor pricing event — D&A continues to grow while revenue slows, and consolidated EBIT margin compresses materially. A 500 bps deceleration in AWS revenue growth (e.g., 28%→23%) at the current run rate would reduce AWS revenue growth by approximately $3B per quarter at Q1 2026 scale, which at AWS's ~37% EBIT margin represents roughly $1B of EBIT at risk per quarter — or approximately 55 bps of consolidated EBIT margin on an annualized basis.

The current direction of this driver is mixed: AWS growth is accelerating (28% in Q1 2026, up from 24% in Q4 2025, the fastest growth in 15 quarters), which is positive. But capex is also accelerating, management has committed to continued heavy investment through at least 2027, and the D&A tail will be long. The Trainium chip cost savings (described as "several hundred bps of operating margin advantage" at scale) are the structural offset — but they are 2–4 years from full realization. Until then, the AWS revenue growth rate is the single variable that most determines whether consolidated margins expand or compress.

Without a full earnings quality breakdown, calling this fully recurring would be premature — that is the domain of the `06_earnings-quality` agent.

## 7. Bar Assessment

**Bar is high** — consensus is above guidance midpoint on operating income and sits at elevated post-Q1-beat levels; estimates have been raised sharply (EPS FY2026 +12.4% in 90 days) and the absolute miss on operating income would require delivering only toward the bottom of the guidance range.

**Rationale:** Management guided Q2 2026 operating income to $20–24B, a wide $4B range. The Street's EBIT consensus sits at $23.4B, at the 86th percentile of that range. This means the Street is effectively betting against the downside risks management explicitly named (Leo launch costs ~$1B, fuel inflation, SBC step-up). If any of those costs come in at the high end of the range, operating income printing near $20–21B would register as a miss against a $23.4B consensus.

On revenue, the bar is more fairly set: consensus of $195.8B sits just below the guidance midpoint of $196.5B (gap of −$0.7B, or −0.4%), which means a print anywhere within the guidance range would register as a beat against the Street number. The revenue bar is therefore beatable.

The asymmetry between a tight revenue bar and a stretched operating income bar is the key setup heading into Q2 2026 results (scheduled July 31, 2026). Amazon has beaten on revenue consistently but the EPS bar has been moved sharply higher after the Q1 print, and the wide guidance range on operating income gives management more room to disappoint than the consensus implies.

## 6. Seasonality Read

Q2 is historically a mid-weight quarter for Amazon, accounting for approximately 23–24% of annual revenue — the second-lightest quarter after Q1 (21–22%). Critically, Q2 2026 is non-standard: management's guidance explicitly assumes Prime Day occurs in Q2 2026 for the US and most large geographies, shifting an event that was Q3 in recent years into Q2. This compresses the typical Q2-to-Q3 step-up (Prime Day effect) and makes Q2 a harder quarter to miss on revenue, but also a harder quarter to meaningfully beat because the Prime Day lift is already embedded in the $194–199B guidance range. Seasonality is neither a pure tailwind nor headwind for Q2 2026 — the Prime Day pull-forward is the dominant calendar effect, and it cuts both ways: it gives the quarter an event-driven demand catalyst but also raises the expected revenue bar relative to a normalized Q2. EBIT margins in Q2 typically benefit from the Prime Day advertising surge (sellers paying for visibility) but are partially offset by the SBC step-up that management explicitly flagged. ## 7. Historical Pattern

Amazon has beaten consensus revenue estimates in all four of the most recently reported quarters, by margins of 1.0%–3.5%. EPS beats have been even larger — 25–70% in three of the four quarters — suggesting the consensus EPS bar has been systematically set too low relative to what Amazon has been delivering on the operating income and operating margin line. However, the Q1 2026 EPS beat of +69.5% was partly driven by non-recurring items (Anthropic mark-to-market reclassification) that inflated reported EPS well above operating earnings power, so the streak of large EPS beats may overstate the underlying consistency of the operating beat pattern. Revenue beats have been more modestly consistent, in a 1–3.5% range, with no revenue miss in at least four quarters. The synthesizer should weight the revenue beat pattern moderately — it is real, but the magnitude has varied — and should be cautious about over-weighting the large EPS beats given their partly non-recurring nature in recent quarters. ## 8. Setup Verdict

**Setup is balanced.**

The weight of evidence does not cleanly favor a beat or a miss. Revenue is likely to clear the Street number — the consensus of $195.8B sits just below the guidance midpoint and the historical beat pattern on revenue is consistent. The pivotal uncertainty is operating income: consensus at $23.4B assumes all the management-flagged cost headwinds (Leo ~$1B, fuel ~$1B, SBC step-up) land at the low end of their range while AWS continues its Q1 momentum. If even two of those three cost items land at the high end, a miss on operating income against the $23.4B consensus is the more likely outcome even on a clean revenue beat.

**The single most important factor in the verdict is whether AWS revenue growth sustains at or above 28% in Q2 2026.** AWS at 28% growth would provide sufficient incremental high-margin revenue to absorb the cost headwinds and print operating income near $23–24B; AWS decelerating even modestly (to 24–25%) would leave the cost headwinds uncompensated and print near the guidance midpoint of $22B, a ~$1.4B miss against consensus.

**The single biggest risk that could flip the verdict in either direction:** On the upside, an early read suggesting Q2 2026 AWS exited the quarter above 30% growth (supply constraints easing as new capacity comes online) would decisively favor a beat. On the downside, a tariff escalation on Chinese-origin goods between now and the July 31 print — specifically one that causes Chinese sellers to materially reduce advertising spend on the platform — could compound the operating income miss risk by suppressing the one line (advertising) that is both the fastest-growing and the highest-margin.

## 9. Second-Quarter Look-Ahead

Q3 2026 looks structurally better than Q2 on two dimensions. First, some of the one-time cost headwinds embedded in Q2 (Leo pre-launch expense, SBC step-up) should moderate — management stated Leo costs will begin partial capitalization in Q4, and the SBC step-up is an annual Q2 event, not a Q3 recurrence. Second, Q3 is seasonally a revenue ramp quarter, historically capturing ~25% of annual revenue versus Q2's ~23%, and the Prime Day effect will have already been recognized in Q2, making the Q3 revenue comparison cleaner. The risk to this more constructive Q3 view is D&A: each additional quarter of $40–45B capex adds to the depreciation base, and the T&I cost line (which carries AWS D&A) will continue rising as a percentage of revenue unless AWS growth accelerates further to offset it.

## 10. Pre-Mortem

If this setup fails — specifically, if Amazon misses Q2 2026 operating income against consensus — the most likely reason we misjudged it would be an underestimation of the pace at which the AI capex wave's depreciation flows through the T&I line. The analysis correctly identifies D&A from the AWS build as the primary margin risk, but the exact timing of when new assets enter the depreciation schedule is not disclosed and requires inference from the 6–24 month capex-to-billing lag the CEO cited. If depreciation accelerates faster than new AWS revenue begins billing, the T&I line expansion compounds the known one-time cost headwinds and the combined impact pushes operating income toward the bottom of the guidance range — a scenario the consensus has not priced. The secondary miss driver would be a larger-than-expected tariff impact on 3P seller advertising spend, which would be difficult to observe in advance and would have compressed the quarter's highest-margin revenue line.

## 9. Earnings Quality Score

**Score: 75 / 100** (Band: Mostly clean but some working capital or adjustment noise)

**Rationale for 75:**

Positive factors pulling the score up:
- CFO/EBITDA has been above 78% every year since FY2021 and above 95% in three of the last three years — one of the strongest cash conversion profiles among large-cap companies.
- CFO has grown faster than revenue for three consecutive years (FY2023–FY2025), a hallmark of improving earnings quality.
- Deferred revenue and AWS backlog are both growing, providing forward cash-flow visibility.
- No receivable factoring, no inventory fraud signals, no aggressive revenue recognition, no off-balance-sheet hiding.
- The negative cash conversion cycle (now −56.9 days) is a structural quality feature.

Factors preventing a higher score:
- Reported EPS is materially inflated by recurring equity investment swings (FY2021 +$14.7B, FY2022 −$15.9B, FY2025 +$15.3B). The year-to-year unpredictability of "Other income" is a persistent quality concern for EPS, even though operating income is unaffected.
- SBC at $19.5B (FY2025) is a real economic cost excluded from analyst "adjusted" earnings. At 2.7% of revenue and 24.3% of EBIT, it is not trivial.
- The two-direction server useful-life change (5→6yr in Jan 2024, then 6→5yr for a subset in Jan 2025) and the heavy-equipment life extension (10→13yr in Jan 2025) represent repeated accounting estimate changes that moved earnings in both directions. Disclosed, but the pattern is elevated.
- Working capital has been a $13–27B annual CFO drag, driven in large part by rapidly expanding receivables as AWS scales. The DPO surge (18% in FY2025) is worth tracking.
- Near-term strict FCF is negative at the LTM level (−$2.5B), driven by AI capex; this is a deliberate management decision but the current reported FCF does not reflect sustainable cash generation.

The 75 score reflects a company with genuinely strong operating cash generation and minimal accounting distortions at the operating level, penalized for recurring equity investment noise in reported EPS, aggressive accounting estimate changes, and large SBC that is systematically stripped from "adjusted" figures.

## 10. The Single Biggest Quality Concern

The single biggest quality risk is the **systematic inclusion of large, recurring equity investment gains and losses in reported earnings**. Amazon's reported diluted EPS of $7.17 in FY2025 includes $2.47/share of equity investment gains (primarily the Anthropic stake reclassification). In FY2022, a $15.9B investment loss turned positive operating earnings into a reported net loss of $2.7B and reported EPS of −$0.27. In FY2021, a $14.7B gain inflated EPS. These swings are real under GAAP, but they bear no relationship to Amazon's operating performance, they are not disclosed as a separate segment, and they have been large enough in three of the last five years to be the dominant driver of reported net income or net loss. An analyst or investor relying on reported EPS ($7.17 in FY2025) without removing this line would draw systematically misleading conclusions about operating earnings trends. Operating income — EBIT — is unaffected by these swings and remains the clean signal. Normalized EPS ($4.70) removes the noise. The risk to earnings quality from this item is not that Amazon is hiding anything: the FTC settlement, the Anthropic reclassification, and the Rivian marks are all disclosed clearly. The risk is that the prominence of reported EPS in financial media and systematic treatment of equity gains as "income" leads to distorted perceptions of earnings growth, particularly as Amazon's strategic investment portfolio grows in size and complexity. If the Anthropic investment is marked up further (as the AI infrastructure buildout continues), FY2026 reported EPS could again be materially inflated. If it is marked down, the inverse. Operating earnings quality is high; reported headline earnings quality is structurally noisy.

## 4. The Single Highest-Sensitivity Variable

The D&A step-up from the AI infrastructure capex wave is the variable that most mechanically determines whether Amazon's EBIT margin expands or compresses over the next 12–24 months. Amazon is spending at an annualized gross capex rate of approximately $170B as of Q1 2026 — almost entirely for AWS data centers, chips, and power infrastructure. This capital enters the depreciation schedule over 5–30 year useful lives and flows through "Technology and infrastructure" costs, the single largest cost line growing above the rate of revenue (T&I rose 23% in FY2025 vs. 12% revenue growth). The current trend is worsening: T&I as a percentage of revenue rose 120 basis points in FY2025 and the quarterly capex pace is still accelerating. The variable is partly company-controlled (management chooses the capex level) but the D&A consequences are not — once capex is spent, depreciation follows automatically. For this driver to swing to the adverse case, one or more of the following would need to happen: the $131.8B gross capex in FY2025 and $43.2B in Q1 2026 alone begins flowing through depreciation faster than AWS revenue scales to absorb it; new data center capacity comes online (starts depreciating) before customers are billed; or management commits to a further acceleration of the capex program that compounds the D&A load. For it to swing toward the favorable case, AWS revenue growth would need to accelerate to 30–35%+ (outrunning D&A growth) or Trainium chip cost savings would need to materialize faster, reducing the per-unit cost of compute in the T&I line. Management has explicitly guided that Trainium will provide "several hundred basis points of operating margin advantage at scale" — but scale is still 2–4 years away per their own framing.

---

## 5. Interaction Effects

Two pairs of variables in this analysis are correlated and can compound:

**AWS growth rate + D&A step-up.** These two variables are mechanically linked: the same capex program that drives D&A growth is also what enables future AWS revenue growth. If capex translates to capacity that is quickly billed to customers, AWS growth accelerates and the D&A headwind is absorbed (favorable interaction). If capacity comes online but billing lags, D&A rises while revenue growth stays flat — both variables move adversely at the same time, producing a double-count to the downside. This is the central earnings risk for Amazon over 2026–2028 and is not a remote tail scenario; management itself described the 6–24 month billing lag explicitly in the Q1 2026 call.

**Advertising growth + North America retail unit volume.** These are partially co-driven by the same underlying variable: consumer spending health and 3P seller participation on the platform. A tariff-driven reduction in China-seller GMV (the tariff variable) would simultaneously suppress 3P services revenue, advertising revenue from those sellers' sponsored-products spend, and indirectly reduce unit volume metrics. All three variables could move adversely together in a tariff escalation scenario, though this report scores them separately.

---

## 6. Non-Linear Or Asymmetric Risks

**D&A on the AI capex wave is sharply asymmetric.** The downside impact on EBIT from a D&A cost rate overshoot is structurally larger than the upside from a favorable case. The reason: depreciation is locked in once capex is spent — Amazon cannot "un-spend" the $131.8B gross capex in FY2025. The only relief valve is to bill customers faster. If billing ramps on schedule, the margin impact is neutral to positive. If billing lags (demand softens, capacity is over-built, or enterprise budgets freeze), D&A accumulates and EBIT compresses with no matching revenue offset. By contrast, the upside from T&I rate stabilization is capped at the rate Amazon can accelerate billing — which is itself constrained by the 6–24 month implementation lag. The bull case for this variable is smaller in absolute terms than the bear case.

**North America retail has high fixed-cost operating leverage.** The fulfillment network — warehouses, robotics, AMZL delivery routes — is predominantly fixed. At 15% unit growth, every dollar of incremental revenue absorbs a declining share of fixed cost (as seen: fulfillment +9% vs. unit volume +15% in Q1 2026). If unit growth decelerates to 7%, the same fixed cost base absorbs far fewer units and fulfillment cost as a percentage of revenue rises sharply. The EBIT impact of a deceleration in unit volume is therefore disproportionately larger than the revenue decline alone would imply. A 53% reduction in unit growth (from 15% to 7%) does not produce a 53% decline in segment EBIT — the operating deleverage amplifies the EBIT decline, likely producing a 70–80% decline in the incremental EBIT from that volume, though the exact magnitude is an inference given the segment does not separately disclose fixed vs. variable cost.

**Advertising has limited downside protection vs. upside optionality.** Advertising spend by 3P sellers is discretionary and can be cut quickly in a demand or tariff shock. At the same time, advertising revenue's near-100% incremental margin means a 10% deceleration (from 22% to 12%) costs nearly $6B in EBITDA — a larger EBIT impact than a 10% deceleration in any other single revenue line except AWS. Advertisers cut budgets in recessions faster than consumers stop buying, so advertising's sensitivity to a demand shock is greater than retail unit volume's.

---

## 7. Earnings Volatility Score

**Score: 42 / 100 (higher = worse — more sensitive to input changes)**

Amazon's earnings are sensitive to a small number of high-magnitude variables — primarily the interplay between AWS revenue growth and the AI capex-driven D&A ramp — but the business's overall structure provides meaningful insulation. AWS revenue is locked into multi-year contracts ($364B backlog), advertising has strong secular demand, and the fulfillment cost base has a clear operating leverage trajectory. These factors anchor earnings against most realistic input changes. The score of 42 reflects that: (1) the AI capex wave creates a genuine, large-dollar D&A exposure that is now asymmetrically skewed to the downside; (2) advertising is a high-magnitude, semi-cyclical line with limited protection in a demand shock; (3) FX and shipping costs provide smaller but real quarterly swing factors. What keeps the score in the "material sensitivity" band rather than the "high volatility" band is the contracted forward revenue in AWS, the company's demonstrated ability to pass shipping cost increases to 3P sellers via FBA surcharges, and the mix shift toward recurring, less cyclical revenue streams (subscriptions, AWS). A worsening macro combined with a capex timing mismatch would push this score toward 55–60.

## 5. Red-Flag Severity Verdict

**Critical concerns**

Two critical flags are triggered. The first is the Anthropic Level 3 investment ($60.6B mark-to-model), which has already injected ~$15B of non-operating gains into Q1 2026 reported GAAP earnings, making both reported and potentially normalized EPS unreliable as measures of operating performance — and creating a correlated risk where the most important AWS customer relationship is also a large equity-like holding that would impair simultaneously with any AWS demand slowdown. The second is the D&A step-up from the AI capex wave, which is the single largest sensitivity variable for FY2026 EBIT yet cannot be modeled precisely from disclosed quarterly data — it is asymmetrically dangerous to the downside (depreciation is locked in once capex is spent), and the confidence level on any quantification is Low.

The single most dangerous red flag is the Anthropic/EPS-quality trap: the Q1 2026 normalized EPS beat of +69.5% drove a 12.4% upward revision to FY2026 consensus, but the beat was materially amplified by non-recurring Anthropic mark-to-model gains. If the FY2026 consensus of $8.69 is built on an inflated Q1 baseline, every subsequent quarter will fall short of that run-rate even on strong operating results. What would resolve this: confirmation from Capital IQ that the Q1 2026 normalized EPS of $2.78 excludes the Anthropic subsequent-event gain, along with a rebuilt FY2026 consensus excluding investment gains from the Q1 baseline.

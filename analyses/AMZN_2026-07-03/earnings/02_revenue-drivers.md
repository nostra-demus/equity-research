**Reporting standard:** US GAAP. **Currency:** USD (millions unless stated). **Fiscal year end:** December 31. **Listing:** Nasdaq. **Date of analysis:** 2026-07-03. **Most recent quarter:** Q1 2026 (ended March 31, 2026).

## 1. Segment Decomposition Status

Segment decomposition applied — 3 segments from business-model module: North America (59% of FY2025 revenue), International (23%), and AWS (18%). Each segment has a materially different revenue driver profile. The business-model segment-map (analyses/AMZN_2026-07-03/business-model/03_segment-map.md) was read and applied. The external-dependency module (10_external-dependency.md) was also read and applied to the cycle-position assessment.

Amazon does not disclose a cyclicality classification for AWS or Advertising within the segment filing. However, the business-model external-dependency module classifies the overall company as "Partly externally driven" with a score of 32/100, driven primarily by regulatory, tariff, and consumer-cycle exposure in the retail segments. AWS is treated as low-cyclical (multi-year contracts, sticky workloads). This is consistent with the MD&A and transcript evidence.

**Cycle-position read (Cycle-Position Rule):** Amazon is not a commodity, auto, or capital-goods company and does not follow a classical commodity price cycle. However, the AWS cloud-adoption cycle is relevant. Current AWS growth of 28% year-over-year in Q1 2026 — the fastest in 15 quarters — reflects the early-to-mid stage of an AI-driven enterprise cloud adoption cycle, not a peak. Evidence: CEO Andy Jassy stated at the Q1 2026 call that "We've never seen a technology grow as rapidly as AI" and explicitly drew the analogy to the first AWS growth wave, saying "we're in the early stages." This is not a run-rate peak; it is an adoption ramp. The retail segments are mid-cycle on consumer spending, with no peak signal visible in Q1 2026 unit growth of 15% year-over-year. No one-time policy or macro tailwind has been identified in the most recent results that would make the current growth non-run-rate, except Q1 2026 FX which added $2.9B (approximately 1.8 percentage points) to the reported 17% revenue growth; the FX-neutral growth rate was 15%. The FX tailwind is flagged as a reversal risk for subsequent quarters.

**Total Revenue = (NA Retail: Online + Physical + 3P Services + Subscriptions + Advertising) + (International: same lines + FX) + (AWS: Cloud customers × consumption per customer × price per unit)**

## 7. The Single Biggest Revenue Driver

**AWS consumption volume, including AI-driven workloads, is the single driver that would most move Amazon's revenue if it changed by 10–20%.**

AWS represents $128.7B of FY2025 revenue and is growing at 28% annually. A 20% change in AWS's growth rate (from 28% to 34%, or from 28% to 22%) would shift incremental AWS revenue by approximately $7–8B per year. That is only the direct revenue effect. Because AWS runs at a 35.4% EBIT margin — versus 7% in North America and 2.9% in International — a 10% swing in AWS revenue has nearly four times the profit impact of an equivalent retail swing. AWS is already the dominant driver of consolidated operating income (57% of the total from 18% of revenue), so any acceleration or deceleration in AWS growth rate has a disproportionate effect on group profitability.

**Current direction:** Accelerating. Q1 2026 AWS growth of 28% year-over-year is the fastest in 15 quarters and is being driven by two concurrent forces — the ongoing enterprise migration of on-premises workloads to the cloud, and the new and structurally larger wave of generative AI workloads. The $364B revenue backlog (up 49% from $244B just one quarter earlier) means AWS has committed future revenue across multi-year customer contracts well in excess of two years of current run-rate revenue. The binding constraint is supply (data center capacity), not demand. Management is investing $43.2B in a single quarter to bring more capacity online.

**The risk to this direction** is a capacity timing mismatch: if new data center capacity (which takes 6–24 months from cash outlay to customer billing per management disclosure) comes online slower than expected, or if a global recession causes enterprise IT budget freezes before these contracted workloads scale, AWS growth would slow. This is the most consequential single variable for Amazon's revenue trajectory over the next 12–18 months.

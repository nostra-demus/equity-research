## 3. Most Severe New Flag

The most severe new flag is the purchase-commitment overhang. The $924.4bn figure is not added to leases, backstops, guarantees, or equity-funding commitments because those are separately disclosed categories that may overlap; it is nevertheless a distinct supplier and power capacity obligation omitted from the upstream summary. Its $213.4bn due-within-12-months component makes the AI build less adjustable if capacity use or monetisation disappoints. The filing's maximum-price-and-volume basis and partial reduction rights are important qualifiers, and the available evidence does not prove a liquidity failure. [Alphabet Q3 FY2026 Form 10-Q, Item 2—Purchase Commitments and Other Contractual Obligations, pp. 57–58; Note 10—Commitments, pp. 32–33]

## 4. Cross-Cutting Patterns

The new finding extends, rather than adds to, the capital-allocation concern: Alphabet is building AI capacity through reported capex, supplier commitments, future leases, and contingent support obligations while the largest revenue pool remains advertising. The obligations must not be summed: the filing presents separate categories, while the upstream report notes overlap among its contingent-commitment components. The pattern is a less flexible cost base whose returns depend on sustained demand and useful deployment of the capacity, not a demonstrated solvency event. [Alphabet Q3 FY2026 Form 10-Q, Item 2—Capital Expenditures and Leases and Purchase Commitments and Other Contractual Obligations, pp. 55–58; Note 10—Commitments, pp. 32–33]

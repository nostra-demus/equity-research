The shares below use FY2025 revenue of $402.836bn and income from operations of $129.039bn. “Profit share” therefore means each segment's contribution to income from operations (profit after operating costs, before interest and tax), not share of net income. ## 2. Dominant Segment

Google Services is unambiguously dominant: it produced 85.08% of FY2025 revenue and $139.404bn of segment operating income. That is 108.0% of consolidated income from operations because Google Cloud adds $13.910bn while Other Bets and Alphabet-level activities subtract $7.515bn and $16.760bn, respectively; these components rebuild the reported $129.039bn total. [FY2025 Form 10-K, Note 15, pp. 86–87]

The current-quarter check does not change the reporting structure but does show a materially different revenue mix: in Q3 2026, Google Services was $96.302bn (75.49% of $127.577bn total), Google Cloud $30.709bn (24.07%), Other Bets $0.372bn (0.29%), and hedging gains $0.194bn (0.15%). Google Cloud's year-on-year increase was driven by infrastructure and platform services and TPU system sales; a quarter is not treated as an annual run rate. [Q3 2026 Form 10-Q, Note 15, pp. 41–42; Item 2, Google Cloud]

## 3. Segment Disclosure Quality

Disclosure quality is useful for revenue and operating income but incomplete for capital allocation. The FY2025 10-K and Q3 2026 10-Q both report Google Services, Google Cloud and Other Bets, with Alphabet-level activities and revenue hedges outside the three reportable segments. Other Bets is a real disclosure limit: it combines multiple operating segments that Alphabet says are not individually material, so Waymo and the other businesses cannot be assessed separately from this filing. Alphabet also does not provide operating-segment assets or capex, which prevents a filed comparison of capital intensity. [FY2025 Form 10-K, Note 15, pp. 86–87; Q3 2026 Form 10-Q, Note 15, pp. 41–42]

Definitions were stable between the FY2025 annual filing and the Q3 2026 filing, but historical segment-profit comparison has a material break within the last three years: beginning in Q2 2024, general-AI-model development teams that had been in Google Services were reported prospectively in Alphabet-level activities. FY2024 also adopted the segment-disclosure standard that added significant-expense disclosures and updated comparative periods. Revenue mix remains comparable, but Google Services versus Alphabet-level operating-income trends around that change should not be treated as a clean like-for-like series. [FY2024 Form 10-K, Note 15; FY2025 Form 10-K, Note 15, pp. 86–87]

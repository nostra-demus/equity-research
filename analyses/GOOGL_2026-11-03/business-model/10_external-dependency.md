# External Dependency Check — GOOGL

Basis: Alphabet Inc. reports in US GAAP and USD. This review uses the FY2025 Form 10-K and the Q3 2026 Form 10-Q, filed October 29, 2026.

## 1. Dependency Table

|---|---|---|---|
| Commodity prices | Mid | AI capacity depends on constrained power supply and a small number of qualified suppliers of specialised AI chips. The Q3 2026 purchase-commitment disclosure includes $802.1bn for a mixed set of technical-infrastructure and inventory commitments, energy take-or-pay contracts, and content licences; it does not disclose the energy or commodity portion, so it cannot be treated as a commodity-cost sensitivity. | [FY2025 10-K, Item 1A — Manufacturing and Supply Chain Risks]; [Q3 2026 10-Q, Item 2 — Liquidity and Capital Resources: Purchase Commitments] |

Search remedies, advertising-technology conduct remedies, Google Play requirements, and EU Digital Markets Act decisions can also alter how the businesses operate. | [Q3 2026 10-Q, Note 10 — Legal Matters: Antitrust Matters]; [Q3 2026 10-Q, Item 1A — Risks Related to Laws, Regulations, and Policies]; [Q3 2026 10-Q, Item 2 — Accrued Legal and Regulatory] |

| Geopolitics | Mid | Sanctions, tariffs, export controls, armed conflict, and political instability may disrupt sourcing, raise costs, limit demand, or impede service in jurisdictions. | Consumer cycle | High | More than 70% of FY2025 revenue came from online advertising, and Alphabet says advertiser expenditure tends to correlate with overall economic conditions. | Industrial cycle | Mid | Google Cloud sells consumption-based services and subscriptions, and Alphabet says Cloud revenue is affected by customer usage, demand, and supply availability. The company has also made long-term infrastructure, energy-service, and leasing commitments; an industry downturn can leave capacity that is not easily redeployed. Offsetting this, Q3 2026 revenue backlog was $567.7bn, of which $560.8bn related to Google Cloud, although recognition still depends on delivery and customer utilisation. | [Q3 2026 10-Q, Item 2 — Google Cloud]; [FY2025 10-K, Item 1A — New Businesses, Products, Services, and Technologies]; [Q3 2026 10-Q, Note 1 — Revenue Backlog] |

## 1A. Named Policy & Subsidy Register — current status as of 2026-11-03

No named subsidy, tariff, tax incentive, mandate, or import/export programme is used as a driver in this report. The government-policy assessment relies only on Alphabet's general filing disclosure of exposure to tariffs, sanctions, export controls, trade barriers, and market-access rules; the available evidence does not establish programme-specific current terms. This is not a claim that such policies do not affect Alphabet. [FY2025 10-K, Item 1A — International Operations]

## 3. Classification

**Partly externally driven.** Advertising demand and regulatory decisions affect the largest revenue pool and the operating model, while power, chips, and geopolitics affect the AI-infrastructure build. Alphabet has real mitigants — product and pricing choices, geographic breadth, FX hedges, and contractual backlog — but it cannot control aggregate advertiser budgets, court and regulator outcomes, power availability, or trade restrictions.

## 4. External Dependency Risk Score

**53/100 — INVERTED: higher = worse.** This is a material-exposure score, not a business-quality score. High dependence on advertiser spending and regulation is partly offset by low direct rate and freight exposure, disclosed FX hedging, and management levers over products, pricing, and infrastructure deployment.

## 5. The Single Biggest Lever

**Advertiser spending / the consumer cycle.** *Inference, not from filings:* a 20% adverse move in broad advertiser budgets would likely do the most damage because online advertising supplied more than 70% of FY2025 revenue and Alphabet says advertiser expenditure tends to correlate with overall economic conditions; Alphabet does not disclose a 20%-shock revenue or profit sensitivity. [FY2025 10-K, Item 1A — Advertising Revenue Risk]

## 2. Aggregate Quality Score

**62/100 — Strong.** The weighting is pricing power 20%, margin stability 20%, customer stickiness 15%, recurring revenue 5%, competitive intensity 10%, industry rate-of-change 10%, capital intensity 8%, regulatory dependence 6%, and commodity dependence, cyclicality, and disclosure quality 2% each; this gives a judgment-weighted result of 61.6, rounded to 62. The core pricing and margin evidence carry the most weight, while the weak rate-of-change score activates the business-quality cap of 65; 62 also stays below the 65 band-anchor ceiling implied by the second-lowest row score of 45.

## 4. Read

Alphabet is a high-margin advertising platform with a growing Cloud business, but it is no longer a low-capital internet model: capex rose from $91.4bn in FY2025 to $132.0bn in the first nine months of 2026 as it built technical infrastructure. [data/GOOGL/Alphabet_Inc_-_Form_10-K(Feb-05-2026).doc, FY2025, Item 7 — Capital Expenditures and Leases]; [data/GOOGL/Alphabet_Inc_-_Form_10-Q(Oct-29-2026).doc, nine months 2026, Item 2 — Capital Expenditures and Leases] The current 33.9% nine-month operating margin is above the 32.0% FY2025 result, but it is not a settled through-cycle level because the filing identifies depreciation, higher infrastructure spending, competition and product mix as sources of margin pressure. [data/GOOGL/Alphabet_Inc_-_Form_10-Q(Oct-29-2026).doc, nine months 2026, Item 1 — Consolidated Statements of Income]; [data/GOOGL/Alphabet_Inc_-_Form_10-K(Feb-05-2026).doc, FY2025, Item 1A — Operating-Margin Risk]

With the rate-of-change score at 40, this looks like a sector / technology-cycle bet rather than a durable compounder until AI-enabled Search and Cloud can sustain monetization and returns after the data-center spend. The key 24-month watch item is Search monetization—paid-click and cost-per-click growth—measured against capex and depreciation growth, not against revenue alone. [data/GOOGL/Alphabet_Inc_-_Form_10-Q(Oct-29-2026).doc, Q3 2026, Item 2 — Monetization Metrics and Capital Expenditures]

## 2. Classification

**Capital allocation concerns.** The FY25 audit and disclosed controls do not show an accounting or integrity breach, but 2026 capital use changed sharply: no common buyback accompanied $42.9bn of common issuance, $19.1bn of mandatory-convertible preferred issuance, and $85.2bn of debt issuance, while capex exceeded operating cash flow by $4.8bn in the first nine months. The $35.3bn of completed acquisitions and the large, partly overlapping off-balance-sheet commitments mean that returns on this investment cycle are not yet proven. [Q3 2026 10-Q, Consolidated Statements of Cash Flows; Note 8; Note 11]

## 3. Most Material Signal

The $75.7bn of data-centre backstops is the most material signal because it adds future financing exposure beyond reported debt, includes $10.0bn connected to the Crux AI related-party venture, and sits beside $130.5bn of uncommenced leases and $22.8bn of financial guarantees; these figures are not summed because the disclosures overlap in part. A further rise in such commitments without clear, separately reported economics for the assets they support would move this from capital-allocation concerns toward governance red flags, whereas primary evidence that the commitments are funded, ring-fenced, and earning adequate returns would reduce the concern. [Q3 2026 10-Q, Note 5; MD&A, Contingent Commitments and Capital Expenditures]

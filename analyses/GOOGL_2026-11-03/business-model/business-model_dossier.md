Advertising supplied more than 70% of FY2025 revenue, while Google Services dominated with an 85.1% share [FY2025 Form 10-K, Item 1A and Note 15, pp. 86–87]. Its biggest negative is a less flexible AI cost base: maximum-basis technical-infrastructure, inventory, energy, and content commitments were $802.1bn while returns remained unproven [Q3 2026 Form 10-Q, Item 2, pp. 57–58]. - **Most important segment:** Google Services. It produced 85.08% of FY2025 revenue. Operating profit rebuilds as $139.404bn Google Services + $13.910bn Cloud − $7.515bn Other Bets − $16.760bn central costs = $129.039bn consolidated. [FY2025 Form 10-K, Note 15, pp. 86–87]

New-unit value creation is therefore not proven. [FY2025 Form 10-K, Item 7]
- **Position versus named peers:** Alphabet’s 32.0% FY2025 EBIT margin trails Meta’s 41.4% and Microsoft’s 46.8% but exceeds Amazon’s 11.2%. Peer return measures are ROE while Alphabet’s is ROIC, so no like-for-like return ranking is supportable. [Competitive Map — GOOGL, §2; Moat — GOOGL, §3–4]

# Business Identity — GOOGL

## 1. What The Company Actually Does

Alphabet reports under U.S. GAAP in U.S. dollars, and its economic core is Google. [data/GOOGL/Alphabet_Inc_-_Form_10-K(Feb-05-2026).doc, FY2025, cover and Item 8] Google gives people worldwide consumer tools including Search, YouTube, Android, Chrome, Maps, Gmail, Play, Gemini, and devices; it sells advertisers access to relevant user attention and actions across those services. [data/GOOGL/Alphabet_Inc_-_Form_10-K(Feb-05-2026).doc, FY2025, Item 1 — Google Services / How We Make Money] It also sells businesses computing, storage, data and analytics, cybersecurity, AI tools, and Workspace software through Google Cloud. [data/GOOGL/Alphabet_Inc_-_Form_10-K(Feb-05-2026).doc, FY2025, Item 1 — Google Cloud] Search helps users find information and take actions, YouTube connects viewers, creators, and advertisers, and Cloud customers pay to run and improve their digital operations. [data/GOOGL/Alphabet_Inc_-_Form_10-K(Feb-05-2026).doc, FY2025, Item 1 — Business] Alphabet also funds smaller, separate businesses, including Waymo, whose revenue comes mainly from autonomous transportation and internet services. [data/GOOGL/Alphabet_Inc_-_Form_10-K(Feb-05-2026).doc, FY2025, Item 1 — Other Bets] Advertising supplied more than 70% of 2025 revenue, so the company remains advertising-led despite its expansion into cloud and other products. [data/GOOGL/Alphabet_Inc_-_Form_10-K(Feb-05-2026).doc, FY2025, Item 1A — Risk Factors]

## 2. How The Company Makes Money

- **Google Services:** `Revenue = ads shown on Search & other Google properties + YouTube ads + Google Network partner ads + consumer subscriptions + Google Play app and in-app sales + Pixel-device sales.` [data/GOOGL/Alphabet_Inc_-_Form_10-K(Feb-05-2026).doc, FY2025, Item 1 — How We Make Money]
- **Google Cloud:** `Revenue = customer consumption of infrastructure, platform, application, and other cloud services + subscription fees for Cloud and Workspace offerings.` [data/GOOGL/Alphabet_Inc_-_Form_10-K(Feb-05-2026).doc, FY2025, Item 1 — Google Cloud]
- **Other Bets:** `Revenue = paid autonomous transportation services + internet-service sales.` [data/GOOGL/Alphabet_Inc_-_Form_10-K(Feb-05-2026).doc, FY2025, Item 1 — Other Bets]

Ad revenue moves with monetizable user activity, advertiser demand, and the value advertisers assign to a resulting click, lead, sale, or brand exposure; this is inference from the filing's performance- and brand-advertising description, not a separately reported formula. [data/GOOGL/Alphabet_Inc_-_Form_10-K(Feb-05-2026).doc, FY2025, Item 1 — How We Make Money] Cloud revenue changes with customer usage, demand, and available supply capacity. [data/GOOGL/Alphabet_Inc_-_Form_10-K(Feb-05-2026).doc, FY2025, Item 7 — Google Cloud revenue trends] Margins are shaped by traffic and content acquisition costs, data-center depreciation, energy, equipment and network costs, and the mix of Cloud, devices, subscriptions, and Other Bets. [data/GOOGL/Alphabet_Inc_-_Form_10-K(Feb-05-2026).doc, FY2025, Item 1A — Risk Factors; Item 7 — Costs and Expenses]

## 3. Business Type Classification

Advertising-led global consumer-internet platform, paired with consumption- and subscription-based enterprise cloud services and early-stage technology bets. [data/GOOGL/Alphabet_Inc_-_Form_10-K(Feb-05-2026).doc, FY2025, Item 1]

## 3a. Sector Overlay & Required-KPI Checklist

No sector overlay for advertising-led global consumer-internet platform with enterprise cloud services and early-stage technology bets — generic read. [frameworks/SECTOR_OVERLAYS.md, Generic operating company row]

## 4. What Drives Variance

Revenue variance starts with advertiser spending and user activity on Google surfaces: the filing identifies macro-linked ad budgets, changing online-ad formats, ad blocking, and limits on ad personalization as risks to advertising revenue. [data/GOOGL/Alphabet_Inc_-_Form_10-K(Feb-05-2026).doc, FY2025, Item 1A — Risk Factors] Cloud adds a separate usage, customer-demand, and available-capacity driver. [data/GOOGL/Alphabet_Inc_-_Form_10-K(Feb-05-2026).doc, FY2025, Item 7 — Google Cloud revenue trends] Margins can change as traffic/content acquisition and technical-infrastructure costs move, or as revenue mix shifts toward Cloud, devices, subscriptions, or Other Bets. [data/GOOGL/Alphabet_Inc_-_Form_10-K(Feb-05-2026).doc, FY2025, Item 1A — Risk Factors; Item 7 — Costs and Expenses] In Q3 2026, management said AI features were increasing Search usage and query growth and that ads were being expanded in AI Overviews and AI Mode; this shows the intended link between AI activity and the advertising model, not proof of durable monetization. [data/GOOGL/Alphabet Inc., Q3 2026 Earnings Call, Oct. 28, 2026, prepared remarks]

# Unit Economics — GOOGL

## 1. Natural Unit

The natural economic unit for Google Services is a monetized advertising engagement—chiefly a paid Search click; Google Search & other generated $224.532bn, or 65.5% of the segment's $342.721bn FY2025 revenue. [FY2025 Form 10-K, Item 7, Revenues; Note 15, pp. 86–87; CIQ Financials→Segments (Revenues, latest annual column) — $342.721bn, vendor export, agrees]

## 2. Unit Economics Table

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue per unit | Cost-per-click (click-driven revenue divided by paid clicks) rose 4% year on year; Alphabet does not disclose the absolute dollar amount per click. | Q3 2026 vs Q3 2025 | Improving | [Q3 2026 Form 10-Q, Item 2, pp. 44, 49] |
| Gross margin per unit | Not disclosed per paid Search click. Alphabet does not allocate cost of revenues to Google Search or to clicks. | FY2025 / Q3 2026 | Unknown | [FY2025 Form 10-K, Item 7, Cost of Revenues; Q3 2026 Form 10-Q, Item 2, Cost of Revenues] |
| Contribution margin per unit (after variable costs) | Not disclosed per paid Search click. | Cost to acquire / build the unit | Not disclosed per paid click. FY2025 aggregate traffic-acquisition costs (TAC) were $59.926bn, or 20.3% of advertising revenue, but Alphabet does not allocate TAC to Search clicks; it therefore cannot be used as a unit cost. | FY2025 | Unknown | [FY2025 Form 10-K, Item 7, Cost of Revenues] |
| Payback period or unit lifetime | Not disclosed. Alphabet gives no paid-click cohort, advertiser-retention, or lifetime data. | FY2025 / Q3 2026 | Unknown | [FY2025 Form 10-K, Item 7, Monetization Metrics; Q3 2026 Form 10-Q, Item 2, Monetization Metrics] |

## 3. Value Creation Read

**Verdict: Unclear from available disclosure.** The observable revenue side improved: paid clicks rose 6% and cost-per-click rose 7% in FY2025; in Q3 2026, paid clicks rose 8% and cost-per-click rose 4%. [FY2025 Form 10-K, Item 7, Monetization Metrics; Q3 2026 Form 10-Q, Item 2, p. 49] But Alphabet does not disclose absolute paid-click volume, dollar revenue per click, per-click TAC or infrastructure cost, contribution margin, or advertiser lifetime; Google Services also mixes Search with YouTube, Network, subscriptions, apps, and devices. Contribution margin multiplied by unit lifetime therefore cannot be compared with unit-acquisition cost, so the single most valuable missing disclosure is contribution profit per incremental paid Search click, including the click volume and TAC/infrastructure allocation that builds it.

## 4. Sensitivity

With paid-click volume held constant—a sensitivity, not a forecast—a 20% change in cost-per-click would change click-driven revenue by 20%, because cost-per-click is defined as click-driven revenue divided by paid clicks. [Q3 2026 Form 10-Q, Item 2, p. 44] It is the input that most directly changes this read, but the filing does not disclose the absolute cost-per-click, click count, or per-click cost, so the dollar and profit effects cannot be calculated. Recent disclosed changes do not establish which input is more volatile: FY2025 paid clicks and cost-per-click changed +6% and +7%, respectively, while Q3 2026 changed +8% and +4%. [FY2025 Form 10-K, Item 7, Monetization Metrics; Q3 2026 Form 10-Q, Item 2, p. 49]

# Competitive Map — GOOGL

## 1. Dominant Segment

Google Services is the dominant segment: it produced FY2025 revenue of $342.721bn (85.08% of $402.836bn total) and $139.404bn of segment operating income; the frozen CIQ sidecar reports the same revenue build, including the $0.127bn unallocated hedging loss. [data/GOOGL/Alphabet_Inc_-_Form_10-K(Feb-05-2026).doc, FY2025, Note 15, pp. 86–87; CIQ Financials→Segments (Revenues, latest annual column) — vendor export]

## 2. Named Competitors

The profit figures below are reported GAAP. Return on equity (ROE) means annual net income divided by average beginning- and end-period stockholders’ equity; the calculations are shown because none of these companies reports a directly comparable pure-search or pure-advertising ROE.

### Competitor A — Meta Platforms

- **Ticker / listing:** META / NasdaqGS.
- **Where they compete:** Meta’s Family of Apps sells advertising against consumer attention, discovery, and advertiser budgets; Alphabet identifies online advertising platforms and social networks as competitors. This is product-overlap inference, not a filed statement that every Meta product substitutes for Google Search. [Meta FY2025 Form 10-K, Item 1 — Competition](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1326801/000162828026003942/meta-20251231.htm); [data/GOOGL/Alphabet_Inc_-_Form_10-K(Feb-05-2026).doc, FY2025, Item 1 — Competition]
- **Scale:** FY2025 revenue was $200.966bn; the more relevant Family of Apps segment had $198.759bn of revenue. [Meta FY2025 Form 10-K, Financial Statements and Note 15](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1326801/000162828026003942/meta-20251231.htm)
- **Profitability / return on capital:** FY2025 operating margin was 41.4% ($83.276bn operating income / $200.966bn revenue). FY2025 ROE was 30.2% ($60.458bn net income / average equity of $199.940bn, calculated as ($217.243bn + $182.637bn) / 2). [Meta FY2025 Form 10-K, Financial Statements, pp. 88–90](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1326801/000162828026003942/meta-20251231.htm)
- **Source named in:** Capital IQ’s frozen Alphabet comparable-company set names Meta; Alphabet’s filing names online-advertising platforms and social networks as competitive categories. [data/GOOGL/Company Comparable Analysis Alphabet Inc.xls, Business Description, as of 2026-11-03 — vendor export; data/GOOGL/Alphabet_Inc_-_Form_10-K(Feb-05-2026).doc, FY2025, Item 1 — Competition]
- **One-line read:** Meta is the clearest large rival for advertising attention and video/social ad spend, rather than a pure general-search rival.

### Competitor B — Amazon.com

- **Ticker / listing:** AMZN / NasdaqGS.
- **Where they compete:** Amazon sells sponsored, display, and video advertising to sellers, vendors, publishers, authors, and others; it also identifies web search engines, comparison-shopping sites, social networks, and advertising providers among its competitors. Its commerce-search and retail-media overlap with Google Services is therefore real but narrower than general web search. [Amazon FY2025 Form 10-K, Item 1 — Business and Competition](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1018724/000101872426000004/amzn-20251231.htm); [data/GOOGL/Alphabet_Inc_-_Form_10-K(Feb-05-2026).doc, FY2025, Item 1 — Competition]
- **Scale:** FY2025 consolidated net sales were $716.924bn; advertising-services revenue was $68.635bn. [Amazon FY2025 Form 10-K, Item 8, Note 10 — Segment Information](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1018724/000101872426000004/amzn-20251231.htm)
- **Profitability / return on capital:** FY2025 operating margin was 11.2% ($79.975bn operating income / $716.924bn sales). FY2025 ROE was 22.3% ($77.670bn net income / average equity of $348.518bn, calculated as ($411.065bn + $285.970bn) / 2). These are consolidated figures, not retail-media margins. [Amazon FY2025 Form 10-K, Financial Statements, pp. 39, 67–68](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1018724/000101872426000004/amzn-20251231.htm)
- **Source named in:** Capital IQ’s frozen Alphabet comparable-company set names Amazon; its own filing describes advertising services and treats web search engines and advertising providers as competitive categories. [data/GOOGL/Company Comparable Analysis Alphabet Inc.xls, Business Description, as of 2026-11-03 — vendor export; Amazon FY2025 Form 10-K, Item 1 — Competition](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1018724/000101872426000004/amzn-20251231.htm)
- **One-line read:** Amazon is a large vertical-search and retail-media competitor for commercial queries and advertiser budgets, not a like-for-like Google Search business.

### Competitor C — Microsoft

- **Ticker / listing:** MSFT / NasdaqGS.
- **Where they compete:** Microsoft’s Search Advertising business comprises Bing, Copilot, Microsoft News, Edge, and third-party affiliates; Microsoft says that business competes with search engines, websites, social platforms, and portals. Alphabet lists general-purpose search engines as a competitive category. [Microsoft FY2026 Form 10-K, Item 1 — Search Advertising and Competition](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/789019/000119312526323660/msft-20260630.htm); [data/GOOGL/Alphabet_Inc_-_Form_10-K(Feb-05-2026).doc, FY2025, Item 1 — Competition]
- **Scale:** FY2026 consolidated revenue was $331.839bn; separately disclosed Search Advertising revenue was $15.176bn. [Microsoft FY2026 Form 10-K, Item 7 and Note 18](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/789019/000119312526323660/msft-20260630.htm)
- **Profitability / return on capital:** FY2026 operating margin was 46.8% ($155.237bn operating income / $331.839bn revenue). FY2026 ROE was 34.0% ($133.749bn net income / average equity of $392.933bn, calculated as ($442.387bn + $343.479bn) / 2). These are consolidated GAAP results, not the return from Search Advertising. [Microsoft FY2026 Form 10-K, Item 8 — Income Statements and Balance Sheets](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/789019/000119312526323660/msft-20260630.htm)
- **Source named in:** Capital IQ’s frozen Alphabet comparable-company set names Microsoft; Microsoft’s own filing explicitly identifies Bing and Copilot search advertising. [data/GOOGL/Company Comparable Analysis Alphabet Inc.xls, Business Description, as of 2026-11-03 — vendor export; Microsoft FY2026 Form 10-K, Item 1 — Search Advertising](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/789019/000119312526323660/msft-20260630.htm)
- **One-line read:** Microsoft is the clearest named general-search rival, with Bing/Copilot and Edge tied directly to its advertising offering.

## 3. Competitive Position

Position vs peers: not disclosed. Alphabet does not disclose Google Services market share or a global digital-advertising share. As a partial, non-filed proxy, StatCounter estimates Google at 91.05% and Bing at 4.80% of worldwide general web-search referrals in October 2026; that supports current search scale but does not measure the broader Google Services segment or a like-for-like year-on-year trend. [Web: StatCounter Global Stats, worldwide search-engine market share, October 2026, retrieved 2026-11-03 — indicative, unverified](https://gs.statcounter.com/search-engine-market-share/worldwide/)

Alphabet’s Q3 2026 filing says Google Search & other revenue rose by $6.8bn year on year, driven by higher search queries, advertiser spending, and improved ad formats and delivery. That is a useful operating signal, but without an industry or competitor growth denominator it does not establish share gain, holding, or loss. [data/GOOGL/Alphabet_Inc_-_Form_10-Q(Oct-29-2026).doc, Q3 2026, Item 2 — Google Advertising]

## 4. Competitive Shape

General web search is **monopoly-leaning**: the October 2026 StatCounter read places Google at 91.05% and Bing at 4.80%, so the two sum to 95.85% of that measured referral market. The source is an indicative, unverified web estimate, not a filed market-share disclosure. This classification applies only to general web search; Google Services also contains YouTube, the network, subscriptions, Play, and devices, for which the available evidence does not provide a comparable top-N share or HHI. [Web: StatCounter Global Stats, worldwide search-engine market share, October 2026, retrieved 2026-11-03 — indicative, unverified](https://gs.statcounter.com/search-engine-market-share/worldwide/)

## 5. Caveat

Alphabet’s FY2025 filing names competitive categories rather than individual companies. The named three-company set is therefore anchored by the frozen Capital IQ comparable-company export and then validated against each rival’s own filing and product disclosures. Peer financials use different fiscal year-ends and are consolidated businesses: they are not margins or returns for the precise advertising/search overlap. A segmented, source-backed global share series covering general search, social/video ads, and retail media would be needed to make a defensible share-gain or share-loss conclusion for Google Services.

The most severe new flag is the purchase-commitment overhang. ## 4. Cross-Cutting Patterns

The new finding extends, rather than adds to, the capital-allocation concern: Alphabet is building AI capacity through reported capex, supplier commitments, future leases, and contingent support obligations while the largest revenue pool remains advertising. The obligations must not be summed: the filing presents separate categories, while the upstream report notes overlap among its contingent-commitment components. The pattern is a less flexible cost base whose returns depend on sustained demand and useful deployment of the capacity, not a demonstrated solvency event.

# Capital-Allocation Scorecard — GOOGL

**Scope and basis.** Reporting currency is USD and the reporting basis is US GAAP. The completed three-year lookback is FY2023–FY2025 (years ended 31 December); FY2026 data below are a separate nine-month update through 30 September 2026, not a fourth completed year. FCF in this report means cash from operations (CFO) less total capital expenditures (capex).

The incremental-ROIC calculation is an **inference, not a filed ROIC**. It uses FY2024 NOPAT of $93,914m = $112,390m operating income × (1 − $19,697m tax expense / $119,815m income before income taxes), FY2025 NOPAT of $107,382m = $129,039m × (1 − $26,656m / $158,826m), and simplified invested capital of $315,800m in FY2024 = $325,084m equity + $14,182m debt − $23,466m cash, versus $433,100m in FY2025 = $415,265m + $48,543m − $30,708m. It is a coarse balance-sheet proxy, includes acquisition and financing effects, and has no segment-return or cost-of-capital comparison; it therefore does not prove that the FY2026 AI and data-centre build will create value.

In 9M FY2026, common and preferred cash dividends totalled $8.134bn while operating FCF was negative $4.8bn because capex exceeded CFO. That is a reason to monitor dividend coverage during the investment step-up, not proof that dividends were debt-funded: reported CFO of $127.1bn exceeded the reported dividend cash payments. ## 5. Capital-Allocation Read

Capital allocation is **mixed**. FY2023–FY2025 buybacks were per-share positive because shares outstanding fell by 761m, but rising repurchase prices, rising stock-based compensation, and no intrinsic-value test prevent a value-accretive conclusion. FY2026 has shifted to $35.4bn of completed acquisitions, $132.0bn of nine-month capex, debt and preferred financing, common-share issuance, and no reported common-share buyback; the returns on that combined commitment are not proven from the available disclosures. The clearest monitoring signal is whether reported cloud and group returns convert that spending into cash and per-share earnings before the increased share count and funding burden become persistent.

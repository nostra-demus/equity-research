## 3. Disclosure Transparency

Alphabet discloses revenue and operating income for Google Services, Google Cloud, and Other Bets, as well as advertising revenue by type and the paid-click/cost-per-click and Network-impression/cost-per-impression metrics. [FY2025 Form 10-K, Item 7 — Revenues and Monetization Metrics and Note 15 — Segments; Q3 2026 Form 10-Q, Item 2 — Revenues and Monetization Metrics]

The limit is material: the CODM does not evaluate segments using asset information, and the filings do not provide segment CapEx, Cloud product margins, or a TPU-system revenue/margin split. That supports a mixed transparency read: the reader can see segment revenue and operating income, but cannot test the capital return of the AI build by segment. [FY2025 Form 10-K, Note 15 — Segments; Q3 2026 Form 10-Q, Note 15 — Segments and Item 2 — Google Cloud]

Across the supplied FY2024, FY2025, and Q3 2026 reports, the four advertising monetisation metrics retained the same labels and definitions. The three-report sample does not meet the required five-year KPI-stability test, and the filings say the company periodically reviews and refines its methods; no supplied filing identifies a KPI being dropped or redefined after deterioration. [FY2024 Annual Report, Item 7 — Revenues and Monetization Metrics; FY2025 Form 10-K, Item 7 — Revenues and Monetization Metrics; Q3 2026 Form 10-Q, Item 2 — Revenues and Monetization Metrics]

## 4. Tone in Bad Times

The Q3 call began with positive framing despite the vendor-consensus EPS miss, but the CFO then gave the $8.2bn other-income loss, negative $9.1bn quarterly FCF, $924bn maximum purchase commitments, and $152bn of pre-commencement leases and other obligations. The same remarks warned of a larger Q4 FX drag, harder Search comparisons, near-term Cloud-margin pressure from third-party capacity, and higher depreciation and data-centre costs. [Q3 2026 earnings call, prepared remarks, 2026-10-28]

Q&A was generally direct: management acknowledged lower AI Mode monetisation and did not manufacture product-margin figures when asked about TPUs, instead stating that it does not disclose product or infrastructure-component margins. The absence of historical adverse-period transcripts limits the conclusion: no Q&A-evasiveness pattern is proven from the two supplied calls. [Q3 2026 earnings call, Q&A — Search deceleration and TPU margins, 2026-10-28; data/GOOGL/manifest.json, frozen inventory, 2026-11-03]

## 5. Candor Read

**Mixed.** In the only recent consensus miss available, management gave the key numerical drivers, openly discussed lower AI Mode monetisation, and raised FY2026 CapEx from $180bn–$190bn to $195bn–$205bn in Q2 before reiterating that range in Q3. [Q2 2026 earnings call, prepared remarks, 2026-07-22; Q3 2026 earnings call, prepared remarks and Q&A, 2026-10-28]

The biggest candor signal is the contrast between that detail and the limited test set: there are only Q2/Q3 2026 verbatim calls, no prior letter or historical operating-guidance series, and no five-year KPI record in the frozen pool. Disclosure Candor is 60/100 and below the 65-point partial-data cap; promotional framing is present, but commentary contradicting the reported numbers, recurring aggressive add-backs, and delayed-disclosure evidence are not proven from available data.

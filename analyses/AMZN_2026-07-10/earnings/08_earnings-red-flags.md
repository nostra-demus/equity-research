## 5. Red-Flag Severity Verdict

**Critical concerns**

Two critical flags are triggered. The first is the Anthropic Level 3 investment ($60.6B mark-to-model), which has already injected ~$15B of non-operating gains into Q1 2026 reported GAAP earnings, making both reported and potentially normalized EPS unreliable as measures of operating performance — and creating a correlated risk where the most important AWS customer relationship is also a large equity-like holding that would impair simultaneously with any AWS demand slowdown. The second is the D&A step-up from the AI capex wave, which is the single largest sensitivity variable for FY2026 EBIT yet cannot be modeled precisely from disclosed quarterly data — it is asymmetrically dangerous to the downside (depreciation is locked in once capex is spent), and the confidence level on any quantification is Low.

The single most dangerous red flag is the Anthropic/EPS-quality trap: the Q1 2026 normalized EPS beat of +69.5% drove a 12.4% upward revision to FY2026 consensus, but the beat was materially amplified by non-recurring Anthropic mark-to-model gains. If the FY2026 consensus of $8.69 is built on an inflated Q1 baseline, every subsequent quarter will fall short of that run-rate even on strong operating results. What would resolve this: confirmation from Capital IQ that the Q1 2026 normalized EPS of $2.78 excludes the Anthropic subsequent-event gain, along with a rebuilt FY2026 consensus excluding investment gains from the Q1 baseline.

## 7. Pre-Mortem — If The Earnings Setup Fails

If the Q2 2026 earnings setup fails — specifically if Amazon misses operating income consensus and the stock sells off on July 31, 2026 — the most likely reason we would have missed it is that the D&A acceleration from Q4 2025 and Q1 2026 capex cohorts entered the technology-and-infrastructure depreciation schedule faster than the billing cycle ramped to offset it. This is the 6–24 month billing lag that management itself disclosed; the problem is that the D&A from $43.2B of Q1 2026 capex has no observable leading indicator in the quarterly P&L (quarterly D&A is not separately disclosed), so the earnings setup could look fine through June 30 and then print a T&I cost line that is 150–200 bps higher than the Street modeled, collapsing operating income from $23.4B consensus toward the $20–21B guidance floor. The specific red flag that would have been the warning sign: T&I as a percentage of consolidated revenue continuing to rise sequentially (from 15.1% in FY2025 annual) rather than stabilizing, which would have been visible if Amazon disclosed quarterly D&A — which it does not. The Anthropic EPS inflation compounds the failure by making the pre-miss period look like exceptional earnings momentum, causing analysts to push estimates higher, which creates a larger absolute miss when operating reality reasserts.

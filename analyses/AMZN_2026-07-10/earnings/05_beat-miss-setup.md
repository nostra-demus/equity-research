**Reporting standard:** US GAAP. **Currency:** USD. **Analysis date:** 2026-07-03. **Next earnings event:** Q2 2026 results, scheduled approximately July 31, 2026.

## 1. Next Quarter Context

The next print is Q2 2026, scheduled for approximately July 31, 2026. Q2 is a seasonally mid-weight quarter — historically accounting for approximately 23–24% of annual revenue versus Q4's 30%+ — with a notable wrinkle: management explicitly stated Q2 2026 guidance assumes Prime Day falls in Q2 in most major geographies (including the US), which is a shift from the traditional Q3 timing. That assumption makes Q2 2026 tougher than a typical Q2 because any shortfall in Prime Day lift would fall directly on the quarter. The consensus bar for operating income is stretched: the Street sits at $23.4B EBIT, at the 86th percentile of management's own $20–24B guidance range, implying analysts are largely discounting the downside risks management explicitly named. Revenue consensus of $195.8B sits just below the guidance midpoint of $196.5B, making the revenue bar comparatively easier to clear. [Source: Q1 2026 Earnings Call, Apr 29, 2026; Capital IQ Estimates, consensus as of 2026-06-26]

## 6. Seasonality Read

Q2 is historically a mid-weight quarter for Amazon, accounting for approximately 23–24% of annual revenue — the second-lightest quarter after Q1 (21–22%). Critically, Q2 2026 is non-standard: management's guidance explicitly assumes Prime Day occurs in Q2 2026 for the US and most large geographies, shifting an event that was Q3 in recent years into Q2. This compresses the typical Q2-to-Q3 step-up (Prime Day effect) and makes Q2 a harder quarter to miss on revenue, but also a harder quarter to meaningfully beat because the Prime Day lift is already embedded in the $194–199B guidance range. Seasonality is neither a pure tailwind nor headwind for Q2 2026 — the Prime Day pull-forward is the dominant calendar effect, and it cuts both ways: it gives the quarter an event-driven demand catalyst but also raises the expected revenue bar relative to a normalized Q2. EBIT margins in Q2 typically benefit from the Prime Day advertising surge (sellers paying for visibility) but are partially offset by the SBC step-up that management explicitly flagged. [Sources: FY2025 10-K, Item 1 p.4, seasonality disclosure; 01_historical-financials, Section 5; Q1 2026 Earnings Call, CFO prepared remarks]

## 7. Historical Pattern

Amazon has beaten consensus revenue estimates in all four of the most recently reported quarters, by margins of 1.0%–3.5%. EPS beats have been even larger — 25–70% in three of the four quarters — suggesting the consensus EPS bar has been systematically set too low relative to what Amazon has been delivering on the operating income and operating margin line. However, the Q1 2026 EPS beat of +69.5% was partly driven by non-recurring items (Anthropic mark-to-market reclassification) that inflated reported EPS well above operating earnings power, so the streak of large EPS beats may overstate the underlying consistency of the operating beat pattern. Revenue beats have been more modestly consistent, in a 1–3.5% range, with no revenue miss in at least four quarters. The synthesizer should weight the revenue beat pattern moderately — it is real, but the magnitude has varied — and should be cautious about over-weighting the large EPS beats given their partly non-recurring nature in recent quarters. The key post-Q1 shift is that the EPS consensus bar has now been raised sharply (+12.4% for FY2026 EPS in 90 days), so the degree of difficulty for a repeat EPS beat is higher. [Capital IQ Estimates, Surprise tab; 04_guidance-consensus, Sections 6 and 7]

## 8. Setup Verdict

**Setup is balanced.**

The weight of evidence does not cleanly favor a beat or a miss. Revenue is likely to clear the Street number — the consensus of $195.8B sits just below the guidance midpoint and the historical beat pattern on revenue is consistent. The pivotal uncertainty is operating income: consensus at $23.4B assumes all the management-flagged cost headwinds (Leo ~$1B, fuel ~$1B, SBC step-up) land at the low end of their range while AWS continues its Q1 momentum. If even two of those three cost items land at the high end, a miss on operating income against the $23.4B consensus is the more likely outcome even on a clean revenue beat.

**The single most important factor in the verdict is whether AWS revenue growth sustains at or above 28% in Q2 2026.** AWS at 28% growth would provide sufficient incremental high-margin revenue to absorb the cost headwinds and print operating income near $23–24B; AWS decelerating even modestly (to 24–25%) would leave the cost headwinds uncompensated and print near the guidance midpoint of $22B, a ~$1.4B miss against consensus.

**The single biggest risk that could flip the verdict in either direction:** On the upside, an early read suggesting Q2 2026 AWS exited the quarter above 30% growth (supply constraints easing as new capacity comes online) would decisively favor a beat. On the downside, a tariff escalation on Chinese-origin goods between now and the July 31 print — specifically one that causes Chinese sellers to materially reduce advertising spend on the platform — could compound the operating income miss risk by suppressing the one line (advertising) that is both the fastest-growing and the highest-margin.

## 9. Second-Quarter Look-Ahead

Q3 2026 looks structurally better than Q2 on two dimensions. First, some of the one-time cost headwinds embedded in Q2 (Leo pre-launch expense, SBC step-up) should moderate — management stated Leo costs will begin partial capitalization in Q4, and the SBC step-up is an annual Q2 event, not a Q3 recurrence. Second, Q3 is seasonally a revenue ramp quarter, historically capturing ~25% of annual revenue versus Q2's ~23%, and the Prime Day effect will have already been recognized in Q2, making the Q3 revenue comparison cleaner. The risk to this more constructive Q3 view is D&A: each additional quarter of $40–45B capex adds to the depreciation base, and the T&I cost line (which carries AWS D&A) will continue rising as a percentage of revenue unless AWS growth accelerates further to offset it.

---

## 10. Pre-Mortem

If this setup fails — specifically, if Amazon misses Q2 2026 operating income against consensus — the most likely reason we misjudged it would be an underestimation of the pace at which the AI capex wave's depreciation flows through the T&I line. The analysis correctly identifies D&A from the AWS build as the primary margin risk, but the exact timing of when new assets enter the depreciation schedule is not disclosed and requires inference from the 6–24 month capex-to-billing lag the CEO cited. If depreciation accelerates faster than new AWS revenue begins billing, the T&I line expansion compounds the known one-time cost headwinds and the combined impact pushes operating income toward the bottom of the guidance range — a scenario the consensus has not priced. The secondary miss driver would be a larger-than-expected tariff impact on 3P seller advertising spend, which would be difficult to observe in advance and would have compressed the quarter's highest-margin revenue line.

Four-quarter summary: Revenue beat every quarter by 1.0%–3.5%. EPS beat three of four quarters, with Q4 2025 in-line and the other three registering very large beats (25%–69%). Without a full earnings quality breakdown, calling this fully recurring would be premature — that is the domain of the `06_earnings-quality` agent.

## 7. Bar Assessment

**Bar is high** — consensus is above guidance midpoint on operating income and sits at elevated post-Q1-beat levels; estimates have been raised sharply (EPS FY2026 +12.4% in 90 days) and the absolute miss on operating income would require delivering only toward the bottom of the guidance range.

**Rationale:** Management guided Q2 2026 operating income to $20–24B, a wide $4B range. The Street's EBIT consensus sits at $23.4B, at the 86th percentile of that range. This means the Street is effectively betting against the downside risks management explicitly named (Leo launch costs ~$1B, fuel inflation, SBC step-up). If any of those costs come in at the high end of the range, operating income printing near $20–21B would register as a miss against a $23.4B consensus.

On revenue, the bar is more fairly set: consensus of $195.8B sits just below the guidance midpoint of $196.5B (gap of −$0.7B, or −0.4%), which means a print anywhere within the guidance range would register as a beat against the Street number. The revenue bar is therefore beatable.

The asymmetry between a tight revenue bar and a stretched operating income bar is the key setup heading into Q2 2026 results (scheduled July 31, 2026). Amazon has beaten on revenue consistently but the EPS bar has been moved sharply higher after the Q1 print, and the wide guidance range on operating income gives management more room to disappoint than the consensus implies.

## 2. The Nearest Dated Catalyst

**Q2 2026 earnings release: July 31, 2026 — 21 days from today.**

This is the only proven-dated, near-term, high-information catalyst in the calendar. On July 31, Amazon will report Q2 2026 results and issue Q3 2026 operating income guidance. The operating income bar is the defining test: consensus sits at $23.4B, at the 86th percentile of management's own $20–24B guidance range. Prime Day in the US and most large geographies fell in Q2 2026 (a management-flagged pull-forward from Q3 in prior years), which both lifts the revenue bar and makes the quarter harder to miss on revenue — but also means any softness in Prime Day conversion rates would be fully visible in Q2 results rather than deferred to Q3. The revenue bar ($195.8B consensus vs $196.5B guidance midpoint) is comparatively easier to clear. On the bullish side: AWS at 28% YoY growth has the momentum to absorb the cost headwinds (Leo ~$1B, fuel ~$1B, SBC step-up), and a Q2 print at or above the $24B top of guidance range would represent a decisive beat against stretched consensus. On the bearish side: all three management-flagged cost items landing at the high end simultaneously would print operating income near $20–21B — a $2–3B miss against a consensus that is already high — and any Q3 guidance below $22B would compound the damage by signaling that D&A from the record Q1 capex is running ahead of AWS revenue recognition.

## 3. The Single Most Important Catalyst

**The Q2 2026 earnings event (July 31, 2026) and the Q3 guidance it carries.**

No other dated or identifiable catalyst in the next 12 months carries the information density or the market-moving potential of the July 31 print combined with Q3 guidance. Here is why: the market is currently pricing 16.4% NOPAT CAGR for a full decade (per the reverse-DCF module), which is only justifiable if the near-term AWS growth trajectory holds and the D&A step-up from Amazon's ~$170B annualized capex program is absorbed by faster billing to customers. The Q2 results are the first substantive data point on whether that hypothesis is working. If AWS sustains 28%+ growth in Q2 and the Q3 guidance is strong (above $25B), the market receives confirmation that the 6-to-12-month billing lag the CEO described is playing out at the short end of the range — bullish for the 16.4% implied growth rate and supportive of the current multiple (11.9x NTM EV/EBITDA). If operating income misses against the high consensus bar and Q3 guidance is soft, the market simultaneously concludes that the D&A lag is at the 24-month end and that the monetization of the AI capex wave is running behind schedule — producing a double hit on both earnings and multiple.

A buyer can time this catalyst precisely: the date is July 31, 2026, 21 days from today. What they cannot predict is the direction. The setup is genuinely two-sided — the earnings module concludes "balanced setup" rather than a lean in either direction. The single most useful preparation is to watch for any early AWS industry data (Azure or GCP reporting before Amazon, hyperscaler capacity announcements) that would narrow the range on whether Q2 AWS growth is at or above the 28% Q1 run rate.

## 4. Negative / Bearish Catalysts

The following dated or windowed events skew toward the downside:

**1. Q2 2026 operating income miss against high consensus (July 31, 2026 — Proven date).** Consensus at $23.4B is at the 86th percentile of management's guidance range. If the three management-flagged cost items (Leo ~$1B, fuel ~$1B, SBC step-up) all hit the high end, operating income could print $20–21B, a $2–3B miss. This is not the tail scenario — it is explicitly described in management's own guidance framing and is the scenario where every risk that management named materializes simultaneously.

**2. Weak Q3 2026 guidance issued at the July 31 event (Proven date for guidance issuance, vague for the number).** The Q3 2026 operating income guide is the market's window into whether the holiday ramp is intact and whether Leo costs are genuinely transitioning to capitalization in Q4. A Q3 guide below $22B, even following a clean Q2 print, would be read as evidence that D&A from the record capex build is running ahead of AWS billings — a structurally negative data point for the 16.4% NOPAT growth thesis.

**3. Amazon Leo commercial launch delay (Q3 2026 window — Vague).** A delay from Q3 into Q4 2026 or later would extend the period during which Leo is a pure cost center (~$1B per quarter YoY increment). Combined with the D&A from Leo's capital assets now sitting on the balance sheet and depreciating, this produces a double-hit on operating income: the cost remains above the line and the capitalization relief is deferred.

**4. Tariff escalation on Chinese-origin goods (No fixed date — Vague, ongoing).** A material US tariff increase on Chinese-origin goods — specifically one that causes Chinese sellers to reduce their Amazon advertising spend — would suppress the highest-margin revenue line (advertising, near-100% incremental margin) at the same time as it reduces 3P services revenue and potentially raises 1P cost of goods. The earnings sensitivity module estimates ~$1.6B average EBIT impact (bear-only; there is no bull scenario from tariffs — the best outcome is neutral). This is not scheduled but is an active live risk as of the Q1 2026 earnings call caveat.

**5. FTC antitrust structural remedy (Vague, no date).** The most severe tail scenario in the calendar. A structural remedy forcing unbundling of Prime benefits from the marketplace or imposing mandatory third-party access requirements would strike the core flywheel economics. The external-dependency module rates regulation as "the single biggest lever" on external risk. No current timeline is visible.

**6. Rising D&A from AI capex wave — ongoing, not a scheduled event, but the most mechanically important risk to the thesis.** The D&A step-up from the $131.8B FY2025 capex and the $43.2B Q1 2026 capex is not a catalyst that will appear on a calendar; it flows through the Technology and Infrastructure cost line every quarter automatically. The earnings sensitivity module ranks it as the #1 EBIT variable (approximately $7.1B average EBIT swing). It is included here because it is the single variable that will most determine whether the Q2 and Q3 earnings events produce a beat or a miss, and because management explicitly described a 6-to-24-month billing lag between capex deployment and customer billing — every quarter that lag extends is a quarter where D&A accumulates without matching revenue.

## 5. Calendar Read

The calendar has two proven-dated catalysts — both on **July 31, 2026** — and everything else is either a bounded window (Leo Q3 launch, Q3 earnings estimated October) or undated/vague (tariff policy, FTC, buyback reactivation, Globalstar close). The near-term events on July 31 are genuinely two-sided: the revenue bar is beatable but the operating income bar is stretched, and the Q3 guidance issued that day carries as much weight as the Q2 result itself. The single most important timing caveat is that the consensus operating income bar for Q2 ($23.4B) sits at the 86th percentile of management's own guidance range, meaning the market has discounted the explicit downside risks management named — a miss would not be a surprise in the context of the guidance range, but it would be a miss against the consensus that the stock is currently trading around. Buyers have precise timing on the July 31 event; they have much less certainty on every other catalyst in this calendar.

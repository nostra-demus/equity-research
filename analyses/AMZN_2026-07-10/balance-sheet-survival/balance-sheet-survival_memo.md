## What This Module Found

Amazon's cash on hand ($86.8B at December 31, 2025) is larger than all of its financial debt ($68.9B), so on the strict basis (cash minus all financial debt) it is $18.0B net cash [FY2025 10-K, Note 6]. Adding the $36.2B of liquid investments (marketable securities it could sell quickly) lifts the broad-basis net cash figure to $54.2B. Financial-debt leverage (debt as a share of yearly profit before interest, tax, depreciation and amortization — EBITDA) is 0.47x, meaning the debt is under half of one year's EBITDA. Only 2.2% of the debt ($2.75B) matures within 12 months, and only 9.4% ($11.6B) within 24 months — a wall covered more than 12 times over by $140B of liquid assets [Q1 2026 10-Q, Note 5]. EBITDA covers interest 64.1 times, so the balance sheet cannot be broken in any stress scenario tested short of a −98.4% earnings collapse. The single most important driver is the enormous liquidity cushion: $160.2B of committed usable liquidity (cash plus committed backup credit lines) versus $7.75B of financial obligations due in the next 12 months — 20.7x coverage. The most important risk is trajectory, not level: gross financial debt jumped from $68.9B at year-end 2025 to $122.6B at March 31, 2026 (a $53.8B increase in one quarter) to fund the AI and AWS (Amazon's cloud business) capex build, and Moody's shifted its outlook from "positive" to "stable" in February 2026 citing that pace [Moody's action, Feb 2026]. If capex stays at the LTM (last twelve months) $151B rate and free cash flow stays modestly negative, the strict basis could turn to a small net debt by late 2026 — though the broad-basis cushion and coverage ratios would remain very wide.

## What Would Change This Read

**What would strengthen it:** Strict FCF turns positive (capex moderates as AWS utilization confirms demand); strict net cash grows; gross debt / EBITDA falls below 0.3x.

**What would weaken it:** Gross financial debt continues growing at the Q1 2026 pace ($53.8B/quarter), EBITDA stagnates, and the strict basis turns modestly net debt (possible trajectory by late 2026), with the broad-basis cushion eroding toward zero.

**Data that would sharpen this read:** Next quarterly filing (Q2 2026 10-Q, expected late July 2026) to track gross debt and liquidity at June 30, 2026; full Moody's / S&P rating rationale reports (only the headline rating is in the pool today) — the single highest-value supplementary data point.

## Bottom Line

- Verdict is **Fortress balance sheet** — Amazon is net cash today on both a strict ($18.0B) and a broad ($54.2B) definition.
- It could be better than it looks because AWS's $364B backlog (customer orders already signed but not yet delivered) is the demand base for the AI/AWS capex, and management has said on the Q1 2026 call that capex is discretionary — cutting growth capex from $151B toward $100–$110B would flip free cash flow from −$2.5B to roughly +$40–50B a year and reverse the net-debt trajectory immediately.
- It could be worse than it looks if debt keeps rising at Q1 2026's pace, EBITDA stagnates, and Moody's follows its outlook shift with an actual downgrade over 2–3 years — though even in that path the broad net cash cushion and coverage ratios stay very wide.
- **Missing evidence:** the full Moody's and S&P rating rationale reports (only the headline rating is in the pool), which would tell us the exact leverage thresholds the agencies watch.
- **One thing to watch next:** the Q2 2026 10-Q — track gross debt and liquid assets at June 30, 2026 to see whether the strict net cash cushion is narrowing at the pace Q1 implied.

## Plain-English Glossary

- **Net cash / net debt (strict basis):** Cash minus all financial debt. If cash is bigger, the company is "net cash."
- **Net cash (broad basis):** Cash plus liquid investments (short-term marketable securities the company could sell quickly) minus all financial debt.
- **EBITDA:** Earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortization — a rough proxy for annual operating cash generation before capital costs.
- **Leverage (debt / EBITDA):** How many years of EBITDA it would take to pay off the debt.
- **EBITDA / interest coverage:** How many times over EBITDA covers the year's interest bill.
- **Maturity wall:** The share of debt that comes due within a stated window (here, 12 or 24 months).
- **Weighted-average maturity (WAM):** The average length of time until the debt falls due, weighted by amount.
- **Maintenance covenant:** A rule in a loan agreement that requires the borrower to stay inside certain financial limits (e.g. a leverage ceiling). Amazon's bonds have none.
- **Free cash flow (FCF):** Operating cash flow minus capex — the cash the business throws off after paying to keep and grow its capacity.
- **LTM:** Last twelve months — a rolling one-year figure.

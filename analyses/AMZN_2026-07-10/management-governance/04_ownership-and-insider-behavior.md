## 5. Ownership / Insider Read

Bezos owns roughly 8.8% of Amazon, and that stake originates from founding the company — not from grants or option exercises. This is the most meaningful single fact: the largest individual shareholder built what he owns over three decades. All other named insiders (Jassy, Olsavsky, and the rest of the executive team) hold RSU grants with no evidence of open-market purchases, meaning their alignment is real but shallower and grant-dependent, not conviction-driven cash investment.

The net insider signal is selling, but it is entirely pre-scheduled under Rule 10b5-1(c) plans — the structural equivalent of saying "I will sell on a pre-set schedule regardless of what I know." Bezos's selling preceded strong quarterly results, not weak ones, and the CFO's plan was terminated before any sales. There is no adverse timing signal.

The biggest control point for minority holders is the absence of a dual-class structure. Bezos's ~8.8% stake gives him no special voting leverage beyond his economic size; the board is elected annually by majority vote; no poison pill or staggered board exists; and the Lead Independent Director is structurally independent of him. Amazon is one of the rare founder-chaired mega-caps where the founder's influence is purely proportional to his economic stake — a positive structural feature for minority holders.

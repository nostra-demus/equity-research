**Data available:** Five earnings transcripts (Q2 2025–Q1 2026), three annual shareholder letters (FY2023–FY2025), FY2025 10-K, FY2024 10-K, FY2023 10-K, Q1 2026 10-Q, Capital IQ Financials / Estimates exports. ## 2. Non-GAAP / Adjustment Aggressiveness

Amazon does not publish a formal adjusted EBITDA or adjusted EBIT table. Its only official non-GAAP metric is Free Cash Flow (reconciled on FY2025 10-K p.28). Analysis uses `earnings/06_earnings-quality.md` as the primary cross-check.

| Signal | Detail | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Recurring "one-off" addbacks** | Amazon does not publish adjusted operating income tables, so there is no formal addback list. However, management verbally presents "ex-charges" operating income in transcripts when material charges occur — Q3 2025 ($4.3B charges) and Q4 2025 ($2.4B charges). This verbal adjustment is reasonable and disclosed. The concern is that restructuring/severance has appeared in every recent year at a baseline of ~$700M–$800M (FY2023, FY2024) and spiked in FY2025 ($4.6B with non-recurring components). Management treats each year's charge as a one-off; the cumulative pattern is not acknowledged. | Q3 2025 transcript, Oct 30, 2025, pp.7–8; Q4 2025 transcript, Feb 5, 2026, p.9; earnings/06_earnings-quality.md §5 (five-year restructuring pattern) |
| **GAAP-to-adjusted gap (magnitude / trend)** | The most important gap is at the reported EPS level: FY2025 reported diluted EPS of $7.17 vs Capital IQ Normalized EPS of $4.70 — a 34.5% inflation from the $15.3B pre-tax investment gain (Anthropic reclassification + Rivian). This recurs: FY2021 +$14.7B gain, FY2022 −$15.9B loss, FY2025 +$15.3B gain. At the operating income line (the number management focuses on), the GAAP-to-adjusted gap is minor: FY2025 GAAP operating income was $80.0B; management's verbal "ex-charges" figure for Q3 and Q4 combined amounts to ~$86.7B ($80.0B + $4.3B Q3 + $2.4B Q4), a ~8% uplift. | earnings/06_earnings-quality.md §7; FY2025 10-K, Item 7, p.27; Capital IQ Income Statement |
| **Does adjusted framing flatter reality?** | For operating income: the verbal adjustments are defensible — they strip out genuine one-time legal settlements ($2.5B FTC, $1.1B Italy tax) and asset impairments. Calling all severance non-recurring is aggressive but not fraudulent. For EPS: Amazon does not formally issue adjusted EPS guidance. The $7.17 reported EPS overstates operating earnings quality by 34%; this is a genuine concern, but Amazon's management consistently directs attention to operating income and CFO rather than net income or reported EPS, which partially mitigates the risk that the misleading reported EPS number is being actively promoted. The two consecutive server useful-life changes (5→6yr Jan 2024, 6→5yr Jan 2025) are disclosed fully on 10-K Note 1, p.41–42 and p.47, though the back-and-forth is unusual for a $301B+ asset base. | earnings/06_earnings-quality.md §7, §8 (Accounting Trap Checklist: change in useful life, score 35/100 severity); FY2025 10-K, Note 1, p.41–42 |

The single largest distortion (Anthropic mark-to-market) is structural to the business and fully disclosed, though the size of the swing ($15.3B in FY2025, −$15.9B in FY2022) is large enough to materially mislead anyone relying on headline EPS.

## 3. Disclosure Transparency

Amazon discloses three reportable segments (North America, International, AWS) with revenue, operating income, and depreciation broken out per Note 10 of the 10-K (p.67–68 of the FY2025 10-K). Within AWS, management provides an annualized revenue run rate and backlog ($364B at Q1 2026 close, stated on Q1 2026 earnings call and confirmed in the 10-Q) but does not break out AI vs core AWS revenue — a notable gap given the scale of AI claims. The company separately discloses advertising services revenue ($56.0B in FY2025), which has become one of its highest-margin businesses, as a separate revenue line but not as a segment with disclosed operating income. Free cash flow is reconciled in the 10-K. KPI disclosure has been consistent period-over-period: the same segment structure, the same revenue breakdowns, and the same FCF reconciliation appear in each annual filing. There is no reduction in transparency over the period reviewed. The one watch point is the deliberate non-disclosure of capex split between maintenance and growth — management acknowledges this limitation in every transcript but does not rectify it, which is a recurring analyst frustration. Overall segment economics are accessible and consistent; the main gap is the absence of an AI-specific revenue or margin disclosure within AWS, which would be the single most useful new disclosure given the $200B capex bet.

## 4. Tone in Bad Times

Q3 2025 was the worst recent quarter by operating income miss: $17.4B reported vs guidance of $19.0B–$22.0B high end, driven by a $4.3B combined special charge. The tone was candid. Jassy led his prepared remarks by stating the reported number, then immediately explaining the two charges with dollar amounts — not leading with the "adjusted" figure or the AWS acceleration. The Q&A on the Q3 2025 call addressed the FTC settlement directly (one question from Justin Post implicitly sought confidence in capacity and demand, which Jassy answered substantively). No question on the FTC settlement itself appeared in the Q&A transcript, which likely reflects that management had quantified it so clearly in the prepared remarks that analysts had no follow-up ambiguity. Q4 2025 adopted the same pattern: three charges named and quantified in Olsavsky's second paragraph, before any business update. On the Q4 2025 call, analysts pressed on FCF guardrails and ROIC, and Jassy gave a direct answer — referencing the prior AWS build cycle, acknowledging uncertainty, and declining to set a specific FCF floor — rather than deflecting. The tone in both calls was promotional in presenting the business opportunity (AI, grocery, Leo) but candid in presenting the financial results, including the charges.

## 5. Candor Read

Management's communications in bad periods hold up. When results were below guidance (Q3 2025, Q4 2025), the charges were named, sized, and attributed to their cause in the first substantive paragraph of prepared remarks — not buried in footnotes or discovered by analysts in the Q&A. The FCF compression was pre-disclosed and then confirmed without spin. The single biggest candor signal, positive in nature, is the combination of pre-warning on FCF compression (FY2024 letter, Apr 2025) and then delivering exactly the warned outcome without retroactive framing. The one legitimate concern is the treatment of restructuring costs: $700M–$800M annually in "other operating expense" in FY2023–FY2024 that gets called non-recurring each year, and the FY2025 spike to $4.6B that blends genuine one-offs (FTC, Italy) with what has become a recurring baseline of severance and reorganization. The two-direction server useful-life change (5→6yr in 2024, back to 6→5yr in 2025) is disclosed clearly but has never been explained in a way that acknowledges the earnings-management optics. The no-integrity-flag-routed status from `business-model/01_disqualifier-scan` means there is no unresolved adverse signal compounding this read. **Verdict: candid / high-trust.** The evidence for this verdict is specific: charges disclosed immediately and accurately, FCF compression pre-signaled, operating income (not reported EPS) foregrounded, and no history of guidance set-to-miss being obscured. The one area short of ideal is the recurring-restructuring pattern.

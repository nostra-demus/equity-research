## 2. What The Company Does

Amazon is three businesses inside one holding company.

- **AWS (cloud):** the world's largest cloud infrastructure business. 18% of revenue but 57% of consolidated operating income ($45.6B of $80.0B FY2025) at a 35.4% operating margin. This is where the profit lives.
- **North America retail:** the online store and its logistics network. 37% of operating income at a 7.0% margin.
- **International retail:** 6% of operating income at a 2.9% margin (first year profitable in FY2025).

Layered across the two retail segments are two high-margin add-ons — advertising and Prime subscriptions.

**Revenue formula (plain English):** money made = (AWS cloud spend by enterprise customers) + (retail goods sold, both first-party and third-party marketplace) + (advertising sold against those retail impressions) + (Prime subscriptions). AWS is the profit engine; retail is the flywheel that generates the ad impressions and the Prime memberships.

## 3. The Variant Perception (§7)

**What everyone already knows.** AWS is growing at 28% year-on-year. Operating income grew 6.5x in three years (from $12.2B FY2022 to $80.0B FY2025). The balance sheet is a fortress (net cash on both the strict and broad measures — see §7 below). Management has delivered on every multi-year promise it has made.

**What is probably priced in.** AWS keeps growing at 28%+ through at least FY2026–FY2027. The depreciation charge (D&A — the accounting cost of writing down data centres and servers over their useful life) from the AI capex wave lands at the short end of the 6–24 month billing lag (i.e. customer revenue arrives before the depreciation bill). Advertising keeps growing at 22–24%. No structural antitrust break-up. The reverse-DCF (working backwards from the price to what growth the market must be assuming — the "what growth is baked in" question) shows the market is pricing in 16.4% NOPAT (net operating profit after tax — the profit the business makes from operating, after tax, before any financing costs) growth every year for a full decade.

**What the engine thinks may be missed.** The Q2 2026 consensus operating income bar of $23.4B sits at the 86th percentile of management's own $20–24B guidance range — the Street has ignored the cost headwinds management explicitly named. The depreciation step-up from $43.2B of Q1 2026 capex alone is invisible in quarterly disclosures because Amazon does not break D&A out of the "Technology and Infrastructure" cost line, so a sustained squeeze on margins could arrive without warning for two or three quarters. Separately, $60.6B of Anthropic equity-like instruments is Level 3 (mark-to-model — priced from a model, not a real market quote) and has been inflating GAAP EPS by more than $15B a year; it is not confirmed whether the Capital IQ normalized EPS of $2.78/share for Q1 2026 excludes the Q1 Anthropic gain — if it does not, the FY2026 EPS consensus is inflated.

**What evidence would prove the edge is real.** The July 31 Q2 print. If T&I cost-as-a-percent-of-revenue rises by 150 basis points (a basis point is one hundredth of a percentage point, so 150 bps is 1.5%) year-on-year while AWS growth stays at or below 26% AND the Q3 guide comes in below $22B — that confirms the D&A-leading-revenue scenario. If T&I is flat or falling and AWS is 28%+, the bull case strengthens.

**Edge score: 35/100.** The engine is not discovering something genuinely new — it is applying management's own disclosed risks more conservatively than the Street. This is calibration, not a real edge. The 35 score caps confidence at 60 per the edge gate.

## 4. Why It Could Work — Bull Case

If billing starts promptly on installation, each $1 of capex generates revenue before the next quarter's D&A charge lands. Confirmed by: Q2 2026 T&I cost-as-a-percent-of-revenue flat or falling; AWS segment operating margin recovering toward 38%+.

2. **Multiple re-rating toward peers.** AMZN trades at 11.9x NTM EV/EBITDA (next twelve months, enterprise value — market cap plus net debt — divided by earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation). The peer-warranted, quality-adjusted multiple is 13.0x. If FY2026 consensus EBITDA of $222.6B is delivered, the stock re-rates from 11.9x to 13x, giving $258/share (+8% from today) [valuation/03_relative-valuation-peers.md].

3. **AI demand keeps pulling forward.** Cloud AI workload growth is real; the $364B backlog (up from $244B in one quarter) suggests demand is not slowing. Confirmed by: two straight quarters of AWS at or above 28%.

**If forced to argue against Watchlist:** the market is pricing a 10-year story, but the 3-year AWS compounding story on its own may justify today's price. AWS growing 28% into a $364B backlog is short-duration, high-confidence cash generation. If D&A absorption is at the short end and AWS accelerates to 30%+ in Q2, the stock trades to $247 (a 3.6% move) within 12 months. The single piece of evidence that would most move this engine toward Buy: a Q2 2026 AWS print of 30%+ with T&I costs flat.

## 5. Why It Could Fail — Bear Case & The Killer Risk (§8)

1. **D&A step-up overtakes AWS billing.** The #1 risk. $170B+ annualized capex creates a depreciation wave that runs 6–24 months ahead of when customers begin paying. T&I costs already rose 120 basis points of revenue in FY2025. Q1 2026 capex was $43.2B, which locks in future depreciation with no advance warning signal in the quarterly disclosures. Severity 90/100; probability 35/100.

2. **AWS growth decelerates.** From 28% to below 22% for two straight quarters would break the demand narrative and compress the multiple at the same time. Severity 80/100; probability 25/100.

3. **Multiple compression as the AI capex narrative fades.** A broad market re-rating of AI-capex-heavy names could send NTM EV/EBITDA below 10x. Severity 70/100; probability 35/100.

4. **Structural antitrust remedy (FTC).** Forced Prime unbundling or marketplace separation would permanently impair the flywheel and cut $400–$600B off enterprise value. Severity 75/100; probability 15/100.

5. **Anthropic Level 3 impairment.** The $60.6B stake is priced from a model. A material write-down (over $20B) triggers a GAAP EPS shock and a P/E re-rating.

**Correlation note:** the top three risks share one driver — the AI capex monetization lag. They do not move independently. When they all fire together, you get the −38.7% bear case, not the sum of three smaller drawdowns.

**Killer risk (one sentence):** Q2 2026 EBIT prints near the guidance floor ($20–21B) and the Q3 guide comes in below $22B on July 31 — signalling the AI capex depreciation wave is ahead of the revenue curve.

## 14. Plain-English Glossary

Terms defined in order of first appearance in this memo. Meanings only — no new numbers.

- **Margin of safety** — the gap between the price you pay and what the analysis says a share is worth; negative means you would be paying above fair value.
- **D&A (depreciation and amortisation)** — the accounting cost of writing down long-lived assets (data centres, servers) over their useful lives.
- **Capex (capital expenditure)** — cash spent to buy or build long-lived assets like data centres and warehouses.
- **Operating margin** — operating profit divided by revenue; the profit per dollar of sales before financing and tax.
- **EBIT (earnings before interest and tax)** — operating profit; the profit the business makes from operations before financing costs and tax.
- **NOPAT (net operating profit after tax)** — EBIT minus the tax on that EBIT; what the operations earn for all providers of capital.
- **Reverse-DCF** — working backwards from the current price to figure out what growth rate the market must be assuming.
- **Level 3 asset (mark-to-model)** — an asset with no observable market price; its value is derived from a model, not a real quote.
- **GAAP EPS (earnings per share, US accounting standards)** — reported profit per share under US Generally Accepted Accounting Principles.
- **Consensus** — the average of analyst forecasts (here, Capital IQ, 53–57 brokers).
- **Basis point (bp)** — one hundredth of a percentage point; 100 bps = 1%.
- **Net cash (strict)** — cash minus all financial debt; positive means cash exceeds debt.
- **Net cash (broad)** — cash plus liquid investments minus all financial debt.
- **EBITDA (earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation)** — a rough proxy for cash operating profit before financing, tax, and asset write-downs.
- **EBITDA/interest coverage** — how many times EBITDA covers annual interest expense; a survival measure.
- **Maintenance covenant** — a lender requirement that the borrower keep certain financial ratios inside a limit; breaching one triggers renegotiation or default.
- **Liquidity runway** — how many months of obligations the company can meet from cash and committed liquidity if operations stop generating cash.
- **NTM EV/EBITDA (next twelve months enterprise value to EBITDA)** — enterprise value (market cap plus net debt) divided by expected EBITDA over the next year.
- **SOTP (sum-of-the-parts)** — valuing each segment separately at its own peer multiple and adding them up.
- **DCF (discounted cash flow)** — the present value of expected future cash flows.
- **Exit multiple** — the multiple applied to the last forecast year's EBITDA to estimate the value at the end of the forecast horizon.
- **Method dispersion** — how much the different valuation methods disagree with each other; wide dispersion means the fair value estimate is genuinely uncertain.
- **Fair value** — an estimate of what a share is worth, independent of the current market price.
- **Backlog / RPO (remaining performance obligation)** — contracted future revenue not yet delivered; RPO is the audited version disclosed in the notes.
- **Segment operating margin** — operating profit divided by revenue, computed at the reporting segment level (AWS, North America, International).
- **Multiple compression / re-rating** — the market lowering (compression) or raising (re-rating) the multiple it pays per dollar of earnings.
- **ROIC (return on invested capital)** — profit the business earns on each dollar of money put into it; compare against the cost of capital to see if it creates value.
- **Incremental ROIC** — ROIC on the newest dollar invested; measures whether recent capex is earning its cost of capital.
- **10-Q / 10-K** — quarterly / annual audited filings with the US SEC.
- **DEF 14A** — the US proxy statement, filed ahead of the annual shareholder meeting; contains executive pay and governance disclosures.
- **Say-on-pay** — the shareholder vote on executive compensation; a high "against" percentage signals institutional dissatisfaction.
- **SBC (stock-based compensation)** — pay given in shares or share-linked units; dilutes existing owners unless offset by buybacks.
- **Buyback** — the company using cash to buy its own shares, reducing share count.
- **GMV (gross merchandise value)** — total dollar value of goods sold through the marketplace, before Amazon's cut.
- **Guidance floor / midpoint / range** — the lowest / middle / full range that management gave for the coming quarter's results.
- **Percentile (of guidance range)** — where a number sits inside management's own range; the 86th percentile means it is near the top.

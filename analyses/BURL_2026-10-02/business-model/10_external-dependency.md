## 3. Classification

**Partly externally driven.** BURL is a U.S.-listed retailer reporting in USD under U.S. GAAP. [Q2 FY26 Form 10-Q, cover and condensed consolidated financial statements] Household purchasing power, weather, trade policy, and freight can materially move sales or merchandise cost, but the off-price model gives management real levers: it buys a majority of merchandise in season, can shift purchases among suppliers and categories, and no brand represented more than 5% of net purchases in FY23–FY25. The $55.5m tariff refund is a one-off accounting benefit, not evidence of a lasting margin lift, because management plans to return it to customers through lower prices. [FY25 Form 10-K, Business—“Our Off-Price Sourcing and Merchandising Model”; FY25 Form 10-K, Business—supplier concentration; Q2 FY26 earnings call, 2026-08-27, prepared remarks]

## 4. External Dependency Risk Score — 52 / 100 (inverted: higher = worse)

This is a material but not dominant external-risk profile. The score is held below the “mostly externally driven” range by flexible in-season sourcing, supplier/category switching, rate hedges, and a business model that can gain trade-down shoppers. It remains above the low-risk range because the core customer’s discretionary budget, seasonal weather, import-policy changes, and logistics cost are all outside management control and can affect the same quarter.

## 5. The Single Biggest Lever

Consumer discretionary spending: a 20% adverse shock to lower-income household purchasing power would be most damaging because it would reduce store traffic and same-store sales, while leaving seasonal inventory and fixed store/distribution costs less flexible. [Q2 FY26 Form 10-Q, “General Economic Conditions” and “Seasonality of Sales and Weather Conditions”]

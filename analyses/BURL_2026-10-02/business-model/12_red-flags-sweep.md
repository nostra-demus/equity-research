## 2. New Red Flags

| Red Flag | Why It Matters | Evidence | Severity /100 *(higher = worse)* |
|---|---|---|---:|
| Self-insurance and claims-retention exposure | Burlington is primarily self-insured for workers’ compensation, employment-practices, health, product and general-liability risks. Its $111.0m insurance reserve at FY25 year-end was $8.2m higher than a year earlier. A claims increase can lift costs beyond accrued reserves; the filing also lists wage-and-hour, California PAGA and other claims. This is not a disclosed material litigation loss: management says final resolution of the current matters is not expected to have a material adverse effect. | [FY2025 Form 10-K, Critical Accounting Policies and Estimates—Insurance Reserves; Note 14—Commitments and Contingencies] | 36 |
| Store-level asset impairment, with a higher interim run rate | H1 FY26 long-lived-asset impairment was $4.4m versus $2.1m in H1 FY25; the charge relates to unrecoverable assets at underperforming stores and assets held for sale. This is a small charge, not proof that the estate is broadly impaired, but it is direct evidence to track while the company continues a large store and supply-chain build-out. | [Q2 FY2026 Form 10-Q, MD&A—Impairment charges—long-lived assets] | 25 |

## 3. Most Severe New Flag

The most material new item is **self-insurance and claims-retention exposure** (36/100). The $111.0m reserve is a real obligation dependent on claims estimates, and BURL retains risk across employee, health, product and general-liability categories. The filing’s conclusion that existing proceedings are not expected to be materially adverse keeps this below a high-severity litigation overhang, but the synthesizer should not treat all claims risk as transferred to insurers. [FY2025 Form 10-K, Critical Accounting Policies and Estimates—Insurance Reserves; Note 14—Commitments and Contingencies]

## 4. Cross-Cutting Patterns

The two new items are localized operating-cost risks, not evidence of a common accounting, governance, or solvency failure. The higher interim store-asset impairment and retained claims exposure matter more if consumer weakness also prevents new stores and supply-chain spending from earning their expected returns; that broader capital-execution risk is already covered upstream. [Q2 FY2026 Form 10-Q, MD&A—Impairment charges—long-lived assets; FY2025 Form 10-K, Critical Accounting Policies and Estimates—Insurance Reserves]

The 7.5% BURL EBIT margin is a reported-basis calculation: $866.3m pre-interest-and-tax income ($816.1m pre-tax income + $71.0m interest expense − $20.9m interest income) divided by $11.567bn total revenue. It is not management's adjusted EBIT, and the peer comparison is indicative only because BURL is FY25 while peers are later LTM periods. [FY2025 Form 10-K (US GAAP), Consolidated Statements of Income, p.43]

For a standard ROIC cross-check, I use a conservative FY25 NOPAT (after-tax operating profit) of $624.3m: $835.1m operating-profit proxy before other income and financing items × (1 − 25.237% effective tax rate). The rate is $206.0m reported tax expense ÷ $816.1m pre-tax income (the filing rounds it to 25.2%). The proxy excludes the full $31.3m other-income line because it includes a one-time layaway-liability gain, and excludes the $0.1m debt-amendment cost. Lease-inclusive invested capital was $6.580bn at FY25 ($2.082bn debt + $3.923bn operating-lease liabilities + $1.807bn equity − $1.233bn cash) and $5.747bn at FY24, or $6.164bn on average; $624.3m ÷ $6.164bn = **10.1%**. [FY2025 Form 10-K (US GAAP), Consolidated Statements of Income, p.43; Consolidated Balance Sheets, p.45; Note 1—Other Income and Income Taxes, pp.51–52]

This calculated 10.1% is **270bp above** CIQ's FY25 ROIC read of 7.4%. That is material and not reconciled: the admitted CIQ output does not disclose its invested-capital definition, while the filing does not disclose management ROIC. I therefore do not substitute the calculation for the vendor read or use either figure to claim an economic spread. [CIQ Financials—Income Statement + Ratios, multi-year columns, FY25 ended 2026-01-31 — vendor export]

**Return on capital vs cost of capital: Not assessable** — FY25 ROIC is 10.1% on the stated lease-inclusive calculation (CIQ: 7.4%), but a group WACC is not disclosed and cannot be built from the admitted data because it lacks a contemporaneous beta, risk-free rate, and equity-risk-premium input. The filing's incremental borrowing rate is a lease-liability method, not a disclosed group WACC. [FY2025 Form 10-K (US GAAP), Note 1—Leases, p.51; CIQ Financials—Income Statement + Ratios, multi-year columns, FY25 ended 2026-01-31 — vendor export]

The through-cycle vendor series is also not a confirmation of widening returns: CIQ ROIC was 9.5% at 2022-01-29 (reclassified) and 7.4% in FY25, recovering to 7.8% on the later LTM measure. The periods are not a clean YoY sequence because CIQ marks the earliest one as reclassified. [CIQ Financials—Income Statement + Ratios, multi-year columns — vendor export]

## 4. Where The Company Sits

**Relative to peers:** Company sits at the **bottom** of named peers on the available EBIT-margin read: BURL 7.5% in FY25 versus Ross 12.7% and TJX 12.2% in later LTM periods. Gross-margin and capital-efficiency comparisons against named peers are insufficient because the upstream competitive-map reports neither peer gross margin nor peer ROIC. [FY2025 Form 10-K (US GAAP), Consolidated Statements of Income, p.43; Capital IQ Quick Comparable Analysis (BURL), Operating Statistics, LTM ended 2026-08-01, as of 2026-09-28 — vendor export]

**Absolute (the economic moat test):** **Not assessable**. The company has a 10.1% calculated FY25 ROIC (CIQ: 7.4%), but no scope-matched WACC is available; peer superiority would not establish an economic moat in any event. [FY2025 Form 10-K (US GAAP), Consolidated Statements of Income, p.43; Consolidated Balance Sheets, p.45; CIQ Financials—Ratios, FY25 ended 2026-01-31 — vendor export]

## 5. Moat Verdict

**No moat proven.** The strongest structural feature is flexible, in-season off-price sourcing paired with a recognised store banner, but it is not shown to protect profits against Ross and TJX: both are larger and have higher reported EBIT margins on the available periods. BURL would need to show, over the next five years, a reproducible return on capital above a verified group WACC and no continuing gap to the named peers' margins before that structure could be treated as an economic moat. [FY2025 Form 10-K (US GAAP), Item 1—Our Off-Price Sourcing and Merchandising Model; Item 1A—Competition; Capital IQ Quick Comparable Analysis (BURL), Operating Statistics, LTM ended 2026-08-01, as of 2026-09-28 — vendor export]

**Moat trajectory: stable — mixed evidence, so direction is not established.** The retail format is not judged fast-changing enough to trigger the module's disruption cap (business-quality rate-of-change score: 54/100), but an exclusively brick-and-mortar model remains exposed to e-commerce and format change. [Business Quality — BURL, Industry rate-of-change / disruption risk row; FY2025 Form 10-K (US GAAP), Item 1A—Competition]

Gross margin rose 200bp and the three-year filing-based EBIT margin rose 220bp, but the contradicting rows matter: CIQ ROIC fell 210bp from the reclassified 2022 figure to FY25, comparable-store sales slowed from 4% to 2%, and CFO-to-EBITDA cash conversion fell 340bp. EBITDA margin was flat at 11%, while market share and realised pricing are not disclosed. The most defensible read is therefore stable, not widening: better FY25 operating margins have not yet shown a durable, peer-defensible economic advantage. [CIQ Financials—Income Statement + Ratios, multi-year columns — vendor export; FY2025 Form 10-K (US GAAP), Consolidated Statements of Income, pp.43–46; FY2025 Form 10-K (US GAAP), MD&A—Comparable Store Sales, p.29]

The next data point that could change this trajectory read is FY26 lease-inclusive calculated ROIC from the FY26 Form 10-K, expected in March 2027. A second consecutive rise from FY25's 10.1% using the same formula would support a widening read; a decline would support erosion. Until then, the disclosed evidence does not prove a widening moat.

## 2. Classification

**Standard professional management** — the supplied primary disclosures show no material related-person transaction, audit qualification, restatement signal, or serial-acquirer pattern. This is not an owner-operator profile: management and directors held 1.3% on the proxy’s beneficial-ownership basis, and capital allocation is dominated by large store and supply-chain investment while financial and lease obligations remain material.

## 3. Most Material Signal

The $875m fiscal-2026 capital-spend plan is the signal most likely to change this classification. FY2025 capex was already $1,104.1m, including $482.0m for stores and $464.0m for supply-chain projects, while cash operating free cash flow was only $176.3m on the stated CFO-minus-total-cash-capex basis. The programme may be value-creating, but the supplied filings do not yet prove returns on the California purchase, Georgia build-out, or planned Arizona distribution center; failure to convert these investments into higher returns without further leverage would move the classification to **Capital allocation concerns**. [FY2025 Form 10-K, MD&A—Cash Flows and Capital Expenditures, pp.32–33; Q2 FY2026 Form 10-Q, MD&A—Capital Expenditures, p.30]

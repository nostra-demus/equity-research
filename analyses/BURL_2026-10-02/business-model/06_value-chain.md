## 1. Stages Occupied

| Retailer / end-customer-facing brand | It buys branded merchandise, principally in season and opportunistically, then sells it through Burlington-operated stores to individual shoppers seeking a discount. | Mid | Weak | [FY25 10-K, Item 1 — Our Stores; Item 1 — Our Off-Price Sourcing and Merchandising Model] |

The supplier-side score is **Mid**, not Strong: Burlington can shift purchases among suppliers and categories and has no brand above 5% of net purchases, but it has no long-term supply assurances and needs branded merchandise at a large discount to sustain its offer. ## 2. Input Cost Pass-Through

| Axis | What it asks | Verdict | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Contractual pass-through** | Is there an escalator, indexed-pricing, or cost-plus clause that moves price automatically? | **No disclosed automatic mechanism.** The available filings describe retail sales rather than customer contracts and do not disclose an escalator, indexed price, or cost-plus provision. On the buying side, merchandise is generally bought by purchase order and has no long-term purchase contracts. This is not proof that no clause can exist in any arrangement. | [FY25 10-K, Item 1A — Availability, Selection and Purchasing of Attractive Brand Name Merchandise] |
| **Realised recovery** | Of a given input-cost increase, how much did the company actually recover — through price, mix, hedging, re-sourcing, or a cost programme? | **Not computable from the pool.** No filing discloses a gross, pre-mitigation merchandise/freight/import-cost shock that can be matched to the observed margin change. | [Q2 FY26 10-Q, MD&A — Gross Margin; FY25 10-K, Item 1A — Inflation and Tariffs] |

The available evidence shows cost relief and operating mitigation, but not a measured customer pass-through rate. In Q2 FY26, a $55.5 million tariff refund reduced cost of sales. Against $2.998 billion of Q2 sales, that is 185 basis points; reported gross margin rose 250 basis points to 46.2%, leaving 65 basis points after removing the refund on the same Q2 basis. Management separately described the ex-refund gross-margin gain as 60 basis points, made up of a 70-basis-point merchandise-margin gain less a 10-basis-point freight increase; the small difference is rounding. This is not a price-pass-through calculation because the filing does not identify the gross input-cost shock or how much of merchant-margin improvement came from price versus merchandise mix, buying terms, or markdowns. [Q2 FY26 10-Q, MD&A — Results of Operations and Gross Margin; Q2 FY26 earnings call, prepared remarks, 2026-08-27]

Burlington's disclosed mitigation tools are buying closer to need, shifting purchases between suppliers and categories, and using those choices to seek better supplier terms; Q2 product-sourcing costs also fell 20 basis points of sales from supply-chain efficiency initiatives. The Q2 call said Burlington had earlier pivoted away from more tariff-exposed categories, which reduced tariff exposure but put pressure on sales, and that it planned to use the whole $55 million refund to offer lower prices in Q3 and Q4. These are cost absorption, mix, and price investment actions — not evidence that customers accepted a price increase. No commodity hedge, source-country shift, or recovery rate is quantified in the available filings. [FY25 10-K, Item 1 — Our Stores; Q2 FY26 10-Q, MD&A — Gross Margin; Q2 FY26 earnings call, prepared remarks and Q&A, 2026-08-27]

**Supplier / input concentration (quantify).** Burlington discloses that no **brand** accounted for more than 5% of net purchases in FY25, FY24, or FY23. It does not disclose purchases or cost of goods sold from the largest supplier or top three suppliers, so brand concentration cannot be converted into supplier concentration. The filing identifies no quantified single-source input. The practical dependency is the availability of attractive branded merchandise at prices below conventional retail; vendors can change terms or stop selling because Burlington has no long-term purchase contracts. [FY25 10-K, Item 1 — Our Off-Price Sourcing and Merchandising Model; Item 1A — Availability, Selection and Purchasing of Attractive Brand Name Merchandise]

## 3. Customer Pricing Power

Burlington has **weak customer pricing power**. It must maintain a discount to other retailers, while department and specialty stores can use markdowns that bring their prices close to Burlington's; customers therefore have many alternatives. The last reported Q2 comparable-store-sales increase was 2%, driven primarily by a higher basket while transactions were relatively flat, so it does not establish that shoppers accepted a price increase. The sources above provide no quantified customer price increase or price/volume elasticity result. The explicit action disclosed in August was the opposite: management planned to reinvest the $55 million tariff refund into lower prices in Q3 and Q4; the related volume response had not yet occurred at the reporting date. [FY25 10-K, Item 1 — Competition; Q2 FY26 earnings call, Q&A, 2026-08-27]

## 4. Economic Control Verdict

**Mixed.** Burlington has a meaningful procurement advantage because it can buy in season, shift suppliers and categories, and its disclosed brand purchases are dispersed; these facts make a single merchandise brand less able to dictate terms. [FY25 10-K, Item 1 — Our Stores; Item 1 — Our Off-Price Sourcing and Merchandising Model] It does not control the customer price: it needs discounted branded inventory, has no long-term supply assurance, and is reinvesting a one-off tariff refund into lower prices to retain value appeal amid intense retail competition. [FY25 10-K, Item 1A — Availability, Selection and Purchasing of Attractive Brand Name Merchandise; Q2 FY26 earnings call, prepared remarks and Q&A, 2026-08-27]

## 5. The Single Biggest Bargaining Risk

The loss of enough attractive branded merchandise at a discount — because vendors improve their own inventory management, limit retail channels, raise terms, or stop selling — would narrow Burlington's customer price gap and hurt traffic and margin. [FY25 10-K, Item 1A — Availability, Selection and Purchasing of Attractive Brand Name Merchandise]

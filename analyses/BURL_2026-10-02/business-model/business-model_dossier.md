# Business Model Reality Check — BURL (Synthesis)

## Abstract

Burlington Stores is a U.S. off-price retailer selling branded apparel, footwear, home goods and related products through a nearly all-store network. Its sole reportable retail segment produces all filed revenue, while growth comes from new stores, comparable-store demand, buying discipline and markdown control. [FY25 10-K, Item 1; Note 1] Flexible in-season sourcing is the clearest positive: no brand exceeded 5% of net purchases in FY23–FY25. [FY25 10-K, Item 1] The clearest negative is unproved unit returns: FY25 cash from operations of $1.231 billion less $1.055 billion of cash capital spending left $176.3 million. [FY25 10-K, MD&A, pp.32–33] No disqualifier triggered; this is an average business worth deeper work only if valuation is cheap.

# Business Identity — BURL

## 1. What The Company Actually Does

Burlington Stores is a U.S. off-price retailer that buys and sells branded, in-season apparel, footwear, accessories, beauty, home goods, toys, gifts, and coats at low everyday prices. [FY25 10-K, Item 1 — Business Overview, p.1] As of January 31, 2026, it operated 1,212 Burlington stores across 46 states, Washington, D.C., and Puerto Rico. [FY25 10-K, Item 1 — Business Overview, p.1] Its business is store-led: more than 99% of net sales come from Burlington-operated stores. [FY25 10-K, Item 1 — Our Stores, p.1] Its stated core shopper is 25–49 years old, generally has household income of $25,000–$100,000, and shops for herself or himself, family, and home; the customer is paying for branded goods at a lower price and a frequently changing selection. [FY25 10-K, Item 1 — Customer Demographic] The company’s practical service is a changing selection of branded goods at lower everyday prices for those shoppers. [FY25 10-K, Item 1 — Business Overview and Customer Demographic] Burlington reports in U.S. dollars under U.S. GAAP; its fiscal year is the 52- or 53-week period ending on the Saturday nearest January 31. [FY25 10-K, Note 1 — Basis of Presentation; Item 1 — Fiscal Year End]

## 2. How The Company Makes Money

`Net sales = store count × transactions per store × average basket (units × realized selling price and product mix)` *(Inference, not from filings; this is the retail arithmetic behind the company’s reported sales drivers.)*

Volume is driven by new-store openings and comparable-store demand: in Q2 FY26, net sales rose 11.0% to $2.998 billion, with $254.8 million of growth from 149 net-new and non-comparable stores since the prior-year Q2 and $42.0 million from 2% comparable-store sales growth. [Q2 FY26 10-Q, MD&A — Results of Operations] Price and mix affect the average basket; management said Q2 comparable sales were driven mainly by a higher basket while transactions were relatively flat, and its price proposition is merchandise sold at up to 60% below other retailers’ prices. [Q2 FY26 earnings call, Q&A, 2026-08-27; FY25 10-K, Item 1 — Business Overview, p.1] Margin depends on merchandise buying and markdowns, purchase discounts and vendor allowances, freight and import costs, and store/distribution costs; reported Q2 gross margin was 46.2%, up from 43.7%, but the increase included $55.5 million of tariff refunds as well as improved merchandise margin, so the reported change is not solely an operating improvement. [Q2 FY26 10-Q, MD&A — Gross Margin]

## 3. Business Type Classification

U.S. store-led off-price apparel, accessories, and home-goods retailer whose economics depend on store productivity, opportunistic in-season buying, and markdown control. [FY25 10-K, Item 1 — Business Overview; Item 1 — Our Off-Price Sourcing and Merchandising Model]

## 3a. Sector Overlay & Required-KPI Checklist *(fix F-SECTOR-1)*

Matched overlay: **Retail / consumer**. [frameworks/SECTOR_OVERLAYS.md, Retail / consumer row]

- **Same-store sales growth — Present:** Q2 FY26 comparable-store sales grew 2%; the first half grew 4%. [Q2 FY26 10-Q, MD&A — Comparable Store Sales]
- **Sales per square foot — Present, management-reported:** management said chain sales productivity had risen from roughly $200–$220 per square foot in 2019 to roughly $350 per square foot. [Q1 FY26 earnings call, Q&A, 2026-05-28]
- **Gross margin — Present:** Q2 FY26 reported gross margin was 46.2%; the filing identifies both tariff refunds and merchandise margin as drivers. [Q2 FY26 10-Q, MD&A — Gross Margin]
- **Inventory turns — Absent as a numerical chainwide KPI:** the filing says buyers focus on inventory turnover, age, and freshness, but it does not disclose a numerical turnover rate. This is a data gap. [FY25 10-K, Item 1 — Our Off-Price Sourcing and Merchandising Model]
- **Store count and unit economics — Present, but partly management estimates:** Q2 FY26 ended with 1,287 stores; management said the last-12-month openings averaged about 27,000 gross square feet and were estimated to exceed $7 million of annual sales with payback below two years. [Q2 FY26 earnings call, prepared remarks and Q&A, 2026-08-27]
- **Online mix — Present only as a bound:** the filing says store sales exceeded 99% of net sales, but it does not give a separate online-sales amount. [FY25 10-K, Item 1 — Our Stores, p.1]

The retail overlay’s red flags are negative same-store sales while store count expands, inventory build and markdown risk, traffic bought through margin cuts, and lease obligations that outlast weak stores. Its valuation norm is EV/EBITDA and P/E tested against same-store sales and unit economics, with an FCFF DCF cross-check. [frameworks/SECTOR_OVERLAYS.md, Retail / consumer row]

## 4. What Drives Variance

Revenue varies first with store count and comparable-store sales: the latest Q2 FY26 sales increase came mostly from new and non-comparable stores, with a smaller contribution from 2% comparable-store sales growth. [Q2 FY26 10-Q, MD&A — Results of Operations] Comparable sales can move with weather, consumer spending, the timing of merchandise releases and promotions, product mix, competition, and marketing; the annual filing also says the second half is seasonally larger and that cold fall weather helps cold-weather apparel. [Q2 FY26 10-Q, MD&A — Comparable Store Sales; FY25 10-K, Item 1 — Seasonality] Margin then moves with the quality and cost of merchandise buys, markdowns, freight and import costs, distribution efficiency, occupancy, and discrete items such as the Q2 tariff refunds; inventory was $1.541 billion at August 1, 2026, including reserve inventory equal to 43% of total inventory. [Q2 FY26 10-Q, MD&A — Gross Margin and Inventory]

## 3. Value Creation Read

**Unit economics verdict: Unclear.** The $3.03m six-month sales proxy shows that the 149 net-new-store cohort added sales, but it cannot establish whether a store creates value because the denominator is not a full store-year and BURL does not disclose store-level build cost or contribution after variable costs.

# Unit Economics — BURL

## 1. Natural Unit

BURL reports in USD under US GAAP. The natural economic unit for Retail - Apparel is a physical Burlington store. The segment is the company's only reportable segment, and the operating disclosures are organized around store openings and comparable-store sales. [FY2025 Form 10-K (US GAAP), Note 1—Segment Information, pp. 52–53; FY2025 Form 10-K, Business—Store Openings, Closings and Relocations]

## 3. Value Creation Read

**Unit economics verdict: Unclear.** The $3.03m six-month sales proxy shows that the 149 net-new-store cohort added sales, but it cannot establish whether a store creates value because the denominator is not a full store-year and BURL does not disclose store-level build cost or contribution after variable costs. Reported consolidated gross margin increased 130 basis points year on year to 45.1%, but that figure is not a store measure and includes $55.5m of tariff refunds recognized in Q2; $55.5m ÷ $5,850.1m of six-month sales equals roughly 95 basis points, so the reported improvement cannot be treated as a normalised store return. [Q2 FY2026 Form 10-Q (US GAAP), MD&A—Results of Operations, pp. 27–28] The Q2 filing also recorded $3.6m of impairment on assets at underperforming stores, evidence that unit outcomes vary. [Q2 FY2026 Form 10-Q (US GAAP), MD&A—Results of Operations, p. 27] The single highest-value missing disclosure is a store-vintage table with opening capital net of landlord allowances, months open, sales ramp, four-wall contribution after store payroll and occupancy, and cash payback.

## 4. Sensitivity

The most decision-relevant missing input is sales and contribution per new store. Holding the disclosed cohort mix and opening timing constant, a 20% move in the $3.03m six-month sales proxy changes it by about $0.61m per net-new-store equivalent ($3.03m × 20%); without store contribution or build cost, that cannot be translated into a change in payback. Comparable-store sales were +4% in the six months ended 2026-08-01 versus +2% in FY2025, but the measure excludes new stores until the first fiscal month one year after grand-opening activities end, so it does not establish the volatility or economics of the new-store cohort. [Q2 FY2026 Form 10-Q (US GAAP), MD&A—Comparable Store Sales; FY2025 Form 10-K (US GAAP), MD&A—Comparable Store Sales, p. 29]

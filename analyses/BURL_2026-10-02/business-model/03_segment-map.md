## 2. Dominant Segment

Retail - Apparel dominates unambiguously: it is BURL's only reportable segment and therefore accounts for 100% of both filed FY2025 total revenue and the net-income performance measure used by the CEO, the chief operating decision maker (CODM). BURL is effectively a single-segment business, not a collection of separately reported merchandise-category businesses. [FY2025 Form 10-K (US GAAP), Note 1—Segment Information, pp. 52–53]

## 3. Segment Disclosure Quality

The disclosure is clear at the reportable-segment level but limited for analysing product economics. The FY2024 and FY2025 10-Ks both report one segment, and the Q2 FY2026 10-Q (period ended 2026-08-01) continues to do so; no change is visible in those available primary filings. The admitted pool does not include the FY2023 10-K, so a conclusion on the entire three-year window is not proven from available data. There is no separate Other or Corporate segment: corporate, store, marketing and selling costs sit within an "other segment expenses" category inside the sole segment. The company provides segment net income and total assets, but not segment EBIT or category-level revenue/profit, so apparel, home, beauty and other merchandise lines cannot be treated as distinct economic segments. [FY2024 Form 10-K (US GAAP), Note 1—Segment Information, pp. 60–61; FY2025 Form 10-K (US GAAP), Note 1—Segment Information, pp. 52–53; Q2 FY2026 Form 10-Q (US GAAP), Note 1—Segment Reporting]

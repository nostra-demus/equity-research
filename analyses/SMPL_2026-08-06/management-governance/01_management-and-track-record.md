## 3. Stated Strategy & Targets

Under the returning CEO, management has framed FY2026 as the early stage of an explicit, three-part turnaround: (1) "strengthening the economics of the business" through cost discipline and pricing (a high-single-digit price increase across most of the portfolio effective September 2026), (2) "consistency and discipline in strategic choices," and (3) "rebuilding brand investment behind superior consumer insights and marketing execution" [Q3 FY26 earnings call, Jul 9, 2026, prepared remarks (CEO Scalzo)]. Management has also stated an explicit, checkable long-run financial target — its own description of what the P&L looked like when the business was healthy: gross margin "approaching 40%," marketing spend "around 10% of sales," and Adjusted EBITDA margin "approaching 20%" — versus a current structure of gross margin in the "middle 30s" and a compressed marketing ratio [Q2 FY26 earnings call, Apr 9, 2026, prepared remarks (CEO Scalzo); reaffirmed as the "long-term algorithm" in the Q3 FY26 call, Jul 9, 2026]. No date is attached to when these targets should be reached — CEO Scalzo explicitly declined to give a turnaround timeline on the Q3 call ("I caution against thinking about turnaround in terms of a time...The when, it's going to come in stages...My crystal ball is not that good") [Q3 FY26 earnings call, Jul 9, 2026, Q&A].

## 5. Management Read

Six months into his second stint, Scalzo and a mostly-new finance function (a 13-month CFO now also doubling as principal accounting officer) are running a business whose most recent proven leadership team — the one that acquired OWYN and set the last guidance print before its own exit — left behind a $391.9M impairment and a guidance miss of over 5% on its final quarterly call. The two quarters since the change have beaten a lowered bar and management has been candid about the shortfall in blunt terms ("not satisfied," "well below our expectations"), which is a genuine positive, but candor about a miss is not the same as proof the turnaround is working: every headline operating metric is still declining year-over-year, and the résumé-driven case for confidence ("substantial value creation," a "decade-long tenure") is exactly the kind of evidence CLAUDE.md §24 says is not evidence. The single biggest management signal is the OWYN acquisition: a $280.4M, debt-funded, board-approved bet that was 71% written off within two years, followed by the CEO who did the deal collecting a $3.465M–$5.2M severance package under a Change-in-Control multiplier the Board had raised just months earlier [2026 Annual Meeting Proxy Statement, "Potential Payments Upon Termination or Change in Control," p.60; cross-ref business-model/12_red-flags-sweep].

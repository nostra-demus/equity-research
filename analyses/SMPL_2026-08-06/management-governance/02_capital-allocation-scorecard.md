## 5. Capital-Allocation Read

Capital allocation over FY2021–FY2026 YTD has been genuinely mixed, and the two extremes are stark: the FY2024 OWYN acquisition — a $280.2M, debt-funded bet dressed as an accretive growth platform — has already had 71% of its purchase price written off within two years, with the company's own goodwill test citing the market's own price decline as the trigger, and the incremental capital it consumed earned roughly a 0% return before the write-down (§4). Against that, the FY2026 buyback program is the best-evidenced positive action in this scorecard: $213.2M spent at an average $18.29, accelerating to $12.14 in the most recent quarter, corroborated by two insiders buying their own stock at nearly the same price in the same window — genuine, observable price discipline, though partly funded by a fresh $150.0M debt draw rather than pure free cash flow. Best use of capital: the FY2026 buyback surge (net share count actually fell 11.3% in nine months, the first period in the five-plus years reviewed where repurchases decisively outran dilution). Worst use of capital: the OWYN acquisition, both on its own terms (71% impaired) and on an opportunity-cost basis (the $280.2M spent in mid-2024 near $30–35/share could have retired ~8–9% of the share count at a price the market has not seen since). No dividend has ever been paid, which is a deliberate policy, not a distress signal, but it means all of the company's capital-return discipline runs through buyback timing alone — and that timing has been inconsistent (small, dilution-losing purchases at $30+ for years, followed by a large, debt-assisted purchase at $12–19 only after a self-inflicted write-down had already cratered the price). **Verdict: mixed** — value-creative on the recent buyback evidence, value-destructive on the M&A record that dominates the five-year capital total, with the serial-acquirer pattern (RF-CAP-004) capping the overall score regardless of the buyback improvement.

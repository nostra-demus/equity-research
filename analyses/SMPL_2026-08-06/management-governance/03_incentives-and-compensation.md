**Read:** The annual cash bonus — 21%-23% of target NEO pay and paid every year — is **100% size-based** (net sales + absolute Adjusted EBITDA, no ROIC, ROCE, EPS, FCF-per-share, or margin metric anywhere in it). The long-term incentive is mixed: the PSU half (25% of total target CEO pay, i.e. 50% of the 59% LTI weight) is genuinely per-share via relative TSR, but that outcome can be moved up to 25-50 percentage points by an absolute Adjusted-EBITDA CAGR modifier that is not a per-share metric. The RSU half of the LTI (another ~25% of target CEO pay) carries no performance condition at all. The new CEO's headline 2026 equity award — 2,000,000 stock options, dwarfing any prior annual grant — has no performance metric beyond the option's own strike price, a materially weaker design than the relative-TSR PSU structure used for the rest of the team in FY2025.

## 4. Incentive Alignment Read

The annual cash bonus — the pay component executives are graded on every single year — pays 100% on net sales and absolute Adjusted EBITDA, both size metrics with no ROIC, EPS, FCF-per-share, or margin component; the Compensation Committee also used its discretion to cut the FY2025 bonus payout below the formula result to fund pay elsewhere in the organization, which is a governance positive (it shows discretion cutting, not padding, pay) but does not change what the formula itself rewards. The three-year PSU program is a real per-share mechanism — its primary driver is relative total shareholder return (TSR) against a food-and-beverage index — but that TSR outcome can be pushed up or down by as much as 25-50 percentage points by an absolute Adjusted-EBITDA growth-rate modifier, and the other half of the long-term award (RSUs) carries no performance condition at all. The single biggest incentive signal right now is the new CEO's 2,000,000-share, purely time-vesting inducement stock option (web-sourced, unverified pending the primary 8-K exhibit) — a package that abandons the relative-TSR PSU discipline used for the rest of the team in favor of a blunt "any share-price rise pays off" grant with no explicit performance hurdle, at a scale that dwarfs the prior CEO's entire annual long-term-incentive grant.

**Conclusion: mixed.** The bonus is size-based; the PSU sleeve of the LTI is genuinely per-share (TSR) with a size-based (EBITDA CAGR) overlay; the RSU sleeve and the new CEO's inducement option carry no performance conditions at all.

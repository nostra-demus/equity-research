## 3. Disclosure Transparency

Management discloses brand-level KPIs that actually drive the thesis — household penetration percentage, consumption/retail-takeaway growth, and buy rate — separately for Quest, Atkins, and OWYN on both calls, and the same KPI set is reported consistently quarter over quarter rather than swapped out when a brand looks bad (Atkins household penetration and its 22–24% consumption decline were disclosed in full on both calls, not omitted) [Q2/Q3 FY26 transcripts]. Two disclosure gaps partially offset this: the 10-K and 10-Qs do not break out maintenance versus growth capex despite a step-change in total capex (0.4% of revenue FY2024 → 1.4% FY2025 → 2.0% TTM), leaving investors to guess how much of that increase is discretionary [earnings/06 §1]; and the FY26 capital-expenditure guidance figure disagreed between the verbatim Q3 transcript ($25M–$30M) and the same-dated Capital IQ Guidance tab ($20M–$25M) — likely a vendor-parsing discrepancy rather than a company inconsistency, but unresolved in the pool [`earnings/04_guidance-consensus.md` §2].

## 4. Tone in Bad Times

The worst recent period by results was FQ2 FY26 (the quarter of the material guidance miss, the $249.0M impairment, and the CEO/CFO/Controller leadership turnover, landing within weeks of each other). On that call the tone was candid rather than promotional: both executives opened with unhedged admissions of disappointment before any positive framing, and the CEO gave granular, brand-specific root-cause diagnosis (competitive bar pressure, an OWYN product-quality defect, over-extended distribution) rather than generic "macro headwinds" language. Q&A on both calls was answered directly and at length — analysts pressed repeatedly on timing, and Scalzo responded with explicit uncertainty rather than deflection ("my crystal ball is kind of cloudy," "the when, it's going to come in stages... my crystal ball is not that good, frankly") instead of a false-precision turnaround date. No question went unanswered or was redirected without substance in either transcript. The one recurring stylistic pattern worth flagging is that blunt near-term admissions are consistently followed by extended "why I remain confident" passages built on category attractiveness and brand equity — legitimate context, but a repeated rhetorical structure that softens the landing of each admission.

## 5. Candor Read

Management's communication under the current CEO is genuinely candid on the two dimensions that matter most — owning misses and answering questions directly — with blunt, specific, unhedged language used for both the FQ2 guidance miss and the OWYN acquisition failure, and no evasive non-answers detected across two full verbatim Q&A sessions. The strongest evidence for trust is the OWYN admission itself ("our marketplace execution was poor and our brand performance fell well short of our plans") — a specific, self-critical statement rare in CPG earnings calls, made without deflecting to competitive or macro excuses. The strongest evidence against full trust is structural rather than tonal: Adjusted EBITDA, the company's primary "clean" KPI, has excluded a $391.9M impairment pattern recurring across three of the last four quarters, and integration/restructuring costs have been labelled "one-off" while recurring every period for over two years — a genuine non-GAAP discipline problem that sits alongside, not instead of, the candid verbal tone. No unresolved adverse integrity signal was routed from `business-model/01_disqualifier-scan` (§3 of that report found all eight hard disqualifiers clean and flagged nothing for this agent to weigh), so this read is not compounded by an unaddressed integrity concern per CLAUDE.md §24 Filter 1. On balance: **mixed** — candid tone and direct Q&A behavior, undercut by aggressive non-GAAP framing that structurally hides a real, recurring pattern of M&A-driven value destruction from the headline metric investors are pointed to.

`RF-DISC-002 (recurring "one-off" / aggressive non-GAAP add-backs)`

**Read.** The capital-structure and election mechanics are shareholder-friendly on their face (single class, no staggered board, annual elections, a real majority-vote-resignation policy for uncontested elections). But the Delaware charter/bylaw stack layered on top — no written consent, no shareholder right to call a special meeting, board-controlled vacancy-filling, blank-check preferred, for-cause-only removal at a majority-of-outstanding (not majority-present) threshold, and advance-notice bylaws — is a standard but real anti-takeover and entrenchment toolkit that the 10-K itself flags as capable of "impair[ing] a takeover attempt" and "mak[ing] the removal of management more difficult." None of this crosses into a classified board or dual-class stock, but it is not a "clean" shareholder-rights charter either.

## 5. Minority-Shareholder Protection Read

The board is legally independent by the numbers — 10 of 11 directors, all three Nasdaq-mandated committees fully independent, single-class stock, annual (not staggered) elections, and a real majority-vote-resignation policy — and there is no promoter, founder, or government owner to misalign it (RF-OWN-004 does not apply: SMPL is widely held, largest holder Blackrock at 13.9% is a passive index manager, and combined insider ownership is 8.76% [2026 Annual Meeting Proxy Statement, p.66]). Related-party leakage is essentially absent: no disclosed related-party sales, loans, or fees, and a clean anti-hedging/anti-pledging policy. Against that, two specific findings keep this from "strong": first, three of the ten "independent" directors — including the Chairman and Vice Chairman — are current partners at the same outside firm (Centerview Capital Consumer) and jointly inherited the SPAC sponsor's board-nomination rights, a true-independence concentration Nasdaq's technical test does not catch; second, the Delaware charter/bylaw stack (no written consent, no shareholder right to call a special meeting, for-cause-only removal at a majority-of-all-outstanding-shares bar, blank-check preferred) gives the board — not shareholders — control of the escalation path if a future dispute arises, exactly the tools a captured or complacent board could use against an activist or an unwanted suitor. The single biggest entrenchment risk to minority holders is that combination: a board whose Chair/Vice-Chair/one-more-seat cluster around one advisory firm sits behind a charter that shareholders cannot bypass without going through that same board. **Verdict: adequate** — not "strong," because the independence-in-substance and entrenchment findings above are real and specific, not "weak/entrenched," because there is no controlling owner, no material RPT leakage, and shareholder support (97.3% say-on-pay) has been high on the one vote this pool can confirm.

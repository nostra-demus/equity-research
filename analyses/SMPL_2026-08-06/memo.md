## 2. What the Company Does

SMPL sells protein bars, shakes, and low-carb snacks under three brands: **Quest**, **Atkins**, and **OWYN**. It designs and markets the products but owns no factories — every item is made by outside contract manufacturers (an "asset-light" model, meaning it spends very little on plants and equipment: capital spending is only about 1.4–2.0% of sales). It sells wholesale to large North American retailers, with Walmart and Amazon together roughly 49% of sales, and about 98% of sales in North America.

The revenue model in plain terms:

**Revenue ≈ (units sold across Quest, Atkins, OWYN) × (price per unit).** Quest is now the whole story — it is 63.7% of the first nine months of FY2026 revenue and rising. Profit is what's left after paying the contract manufacturers for ingredients and packaging (largely protein and plastic, and none of it hedged against price swings), plus marketing and overhead.

## 3. The Variant Perception

Does the engine see something the market doesn't? Mostly no — and the memo says so plainly.

- **What everyone already knows:** SMPL has had a rough 18 months — four straight quarters of falling revenue, a five-year drop in gross margin of about 800 basis points (8 percentage points), a $391.9 million pile of write-downs across three of the last four quarters, a CEO/CFO/Controller reshuffle inside 12 months, and the OWYN acquisition now 71% written off. The stock trades well below its own five-year valuation history.
- **What is probably priced in:** The reverse-DCF (working backwards from today's price to see what future the market is assuming) implies the market expects free cash flow — the actual cash the business generates after spending to keep running — to fall about 9% a year for six straight years. That is worse than company guidance (which only covers 1–2 years) and worse than even the engine's own gloomiest labeled scenario. Sell-side analysts are nearly as skeptical: only 4 of 11 rate it a Buy.
- **What the engine thinks may be missed:** Six years of decline is arithmetically harder to justify than the visible evidence supports. Quest is still gaining household penetration (up 120 basis points to 20.5%, a multi-year high) even as the overall company shrinks, and the balance sheet is safe — $197.8 million of liquidity against $0 of near-term debt. So this is a "does the turnaround land?" question, not a "does the company survive?" question.
- **What would prove the engine is actually different:** Honestly, not much — because the edge is thin. The 8-analyst average price target is $14.88, within 3% of the engine's own $15.27 base case. The Street already models a similar fair value. **Edge score: 35/100** — this is a well-triangulated restatement of consensus, not a proprietary insight. The genuinely unknown question — will the September-2026 price increase land without driving customers away, and will Quest close its 16.7-point gap to category growth — is unknown to everyone, engine and Street alike, and resolves on dated events.

## 4. Why It Could Work — Bull Case

The drivers that most move the thesis up, each with cited evidence and, where given, the rough earnings or value swing:

1. **Quest is still gaining ground.** Quest is 63.7% of nine-month FY2026 revenue and household penetration rose +120 basis points to 20.5%, a multi-year high, even as the consolidated business shrinks [`business-model/99`]. Confirmation would be retail takeaway closing the 16.7-point gap to category growth in the Oct-23-2026 print.
2. **The stock is below two independent fair-value methods.** Peer comparison points to $13.76 (+21.4%), an intrinsic cash-flow model to $17.53 (+54.7%), and even the sell-side average of $14.88 (+31.3%) sits above today's price [`valuation/99`].
3. **The price increase could land softly.** If the September-2026 high-single-digit price increase meets demand less sensitive than management's own "1 or higher" fear (i.e., customers keep buying), net revenue rises about 2%, worth roughly +$7.7 million of Adjusted EBITDA (earnings before interest, tax, depreciation, and amortization — a rough proxy for operating cash profit) [`valuation/99` §8]. This is the single biggest swing factor in the whole analysis.
4. **The three-beat streak.** Revenue and earnings per share beat consensus three straight quarters, each by more (revenue +0.1%→+7.3%, EPS +8.1%→+19.5%) [`earnings/99`]. A fourth beat showing genuine year-on-year improvement (not just clearing a lowered bar) would be a real signal.
5. **Balance-sheet optionality.** With $197.8 million of liquidity, no near-term debt, and net leverage (net debt divided by yearly cash profit) of only 1.18x, the company could pause buybacks and pay down debt quickly if it chose to [`balance-sheet-survival/99`].

## 5. Why It Could Fail — Bear Case & The Killer Risk

The risks that most move the thesis down:

1. **The price increase backfires.** If demand is as sensitive as management fears ("1 or higher") while unhedged protein and packaging costs keep rising, net revenue falls about 3%, worth roughly −$12.8 million of Adjusted EBITDA [`valuation/99` §8]. This is the same variable as bull driver #3, in reverse.
2. **The margin keeps sliding.** Gross margin — sales left after making the product — has fallen from 40.7% to about 32.5% over five years, with no clear stop yet, and worsened again in the latest nine months [`business-model/99`]. A genuine (non-write-down) ~330 basis point Adjusted EBITDA margin decline is real, not an accounting artifact [`earnings/99`].
3. **The "adjusted" earnings hide recurring damage.** Adjusted EBITDA has excluded $391.9 million of write-downs across three of the last four quarters — 56.3% of GAAP (official, unadjusted) EBITDA in FY2025 (RF-DISC-002, Critical). 4 of 5 accrual-quality checks are triggered (RF-EQ-001) [`earnings/99`].
4. **No proven moat.** Business quality scores 40/100 (weak), moat 30/100 ("no moat proven, eroding trajectory"). Return on invested capital (the profit earned on each dollar of money put into the business), at ~7.0–8.8%, only marginally clears its cost of capital [`business-model/99`].
5. **Debt is rising to fund buybacks.** Net leverage more than doubled in one year (0.54x→1.18x), purely to fund $213.2 million of buybacks against only $92.1 million of free cash flow, while cash conversion fell from 80.1% to 62.9% [`balance-sheet-survival/99`].

**The killer risk, in one sentence:** A third material, debt-funded acquisition before OWYN stabilizes — exactly the §24 Filter 4 serial-acquirer pattern already tripped once (RF-CAP-004, Critical).

## 6. Avoid-Big-Risks Check (§24)

Two of the six survival filters tripped on evidence:

- **Filter 4 — serial acquirer (RF-CAP-004, Critical, unresolved).** The $280.2 million OWYN deal (2024, funded by debt) lost 71% of its value to write-downs within 24 months, with roughly 0% incremental return on the money before the write-down. It was the second material debt-funded deal in six years, after Quest (~$982.1 million, 2019) [`management-governance/99` §5A]. **This is the binding cap on the whole dossier** — it forecloses Buy, Strong Buy, and even Starter Position Only, and holds the rating at Watchlist until the company goes a full cycle without a third deal.
- **Filter 2 — turnaround without proven improvement (RF-MGT-004, High).** Zero years of delivered operating improvement; management itself calls FY2026 "the early stages," and every headline metric is still declining year-on-year [`management-governance/99` §5A]. On its own this filter would cap the rating at Starter Position Only — but Filter 4 is more restrictive and wins.

Survival read (the good news): no other filter tripped. There is no controlling owner with misaligned incentives (SMPL is widely held, single share class). The balance sheet is safe — net leverage 1.18x, no debt due within 36 months (a single $400 million loan due March 2030), and the structure survives a 60% cut to earnings without breaching its one covenant. This is not a survival story; the risk is entirely whether the turnaround lands.

## 10. What Would Change Our Mind

The specific, observable events that would force a downgrade or exit:

- A **third material, debt-funded acquisition** before OWYN stabilizes → confirms the serial-acquirer pattern is structural; moves the rating from Watchlist toward Avoid.
- A **fourth consecutive-quarter write-down** → confirms the "adjusted" earnings framing structurally hides recurring value destruction.
- **Price-increase demand at or above management's "1 or higher" fear** → confirms the market's skepticism was right; pushes the price toward the $8.42 bear.
- A **miss below the FQ4 FY2026 guided floor** ($322M net sales / $52M Adj. EBITDA) on Oct-23-2026 → breaks the beat streak; confirms the underlying decline.
- **Covenant confirmation that the addback definition is materially looser than disclosed** → would mean the 80.3% indicative covenant headroom is illusory.

What would *raise* confidence toward an upgrade: a full year with no third acquisition and no further write-down; the price increase landing soft; Quest closing its 16.7-point takeaway gap; and SEC-confirmed proof (a Form 8-K exhibit) that the new CEO's pay has per-share performance conditions, not just time-vesting options.

## 13. Bottom Line

- **What it does:** sells Quest, Atkins, and OWYN protein and low-carb snacks, made by outside factories, sold mostly through Walmart and Amazon.
- **Why it may go up:** two independent methods put fair value at $13.76–$17.53 versus today's $11.33 — a real ~26% cushion — and the market is pricing in a longer decline (six years) than the evidence supports.

- **What the data supports:** a clean, sufficient set of primary filings across all six modules — the 10-K, two 10-Qs, the 2026 proxy, two transcripts — with a fresh price and full analyst coverage.
- **What's missing:** brand-level profit, the exact covenant definition, SEC-confirmed CEO pay.
- **Buy now or wait:** wait. A Critical, unresolved capital-allocation red flag caps the rating no matter how good the valuation looks.
- **The one thing to watch next:** the Oct-23-2026 print and the first FY2027 guide.

## 14. Plain-English Glossary

- **Watchlist** — the engine's decision to track, not buy: interesting but not clean enough to own today.
- **Basis points (bps)** — hundredths of a percentage point; 100 bps = 1 percentage point. Used for small changes in margins.
- **Asset-light** — a business that owns few factories or machines; here, all products are made by outside manufacturers.
- **Gross margin** — the share of sales left after paying to make the product.
- **EBITDA** — earnings before interest, tax, depreciation, and amortization; a rough proxy for operating cash profit. "Adjusted" EBITDA removes items management calls one-off; "GAAP" EBITDA is the official, unadjusted figure.
- **Free cash flow (FCF)** — the actual cash a business generates after the spending needed to keep running (operating cash flow minus capital spending).
- **Cash conversion** — how much of reported profit actually turns into cash.
- **Reverse-DCF** — working backwards from today's price to find what future growth or decline the market is assuming.
- **Intrinsic DCF (discounted cash flow)** — estimating value by projecting future cash and discounting it back to today.
- **Margin of safety** — the cushion between the price you pay and your estimate of fair value.
- **Net debt / net leverage** — net debt is total debt minus cash (strict basis); net leverage is net debt divided by yearly cash profit. Higher leverage means more risk.
- **Covenant** — a rule in a loan agreement the borrower must keep to; "headroom" is how far the company is from breaching it.
- **Return on invested capital (ROIC)** — the profit earned on each dollar of money put into the business; compare it against the cost of capital (what that money costs to raise).
- **EV / enterprise value** — the value of the whole company including debt; EV/EBITDA prices the business against its cash profit.
- **Household penetration** — the share of households that buy the brand.
- **Elasticity** — how much demand falls when price rises; "1 or higher" means a price increase is roughly fully or more-than offset by lost volume.
- **Say-on-pay** — the shareholder vote on executive pay.
- **Accrual-quality flags** — warning signs that reported profit may be running ahead of the cash behind it.

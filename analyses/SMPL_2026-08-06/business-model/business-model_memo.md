## What This Module Found

Simply Good Foods designs and markets protein and low-carb snacking brands — Quest, Atkins and OWYN — all made by outside contract manufacturers (SMPL owns no factories) and sold wholesale to large North American retailers [`02_business-identity.md`]. Quest is the dominant and only growing brand, at 63.7% of nine-month FY2026 revenue, up from 59.5% in FY2025; Atkins is shrinking (down 24.6% in the quarter) and OWYN is small and still being integrated [`03_segment-map.md`].

The single most important positive is that the asset-light model turns sales into real cash with very little capital: operating cash flow (the cash the business actually generates) was positive in all four of the last fiscal years, including $178.5m in FY2025, on capital spending of only 1.4-2.0% of sales [`07_business-quality.md`, `01_disqualifier-scan.md`].

The single most important negative is a five-year, roughly 800-basis-point (8 percentage point) fall in gross margin (the profit left after making the product) — from 40.7% in FY2021 to 33.3% over the last twelve months — and it worsened by a further ~470bps in the most recent nine months, not stabilized [`07_business-quality.md`, `09_moat.md`]. No moat is proven, and return on capital (the profit earned on each dollar invested) of ~7.0% only marginally clears an estimated ~5.8% cost of capital (what that money costs to raise) and fails a more realistic ~7.8% size-adjusted estimate [`09_moat.md`]. No automatic disqualifier triggered — all eight checks are clean [`01_disqualifier-scan.md`].

## What Would Change This Read

Toward higher conviction:
- Gross margin recovering toward the historical 38-40% band after the September 2026 price increase absorbs its expected volume hit, without further Walmart/Amazon shelf-space cuts.
- A full fiscal year with no further material impairments or leadership turnover.

Toward avoid:
- A third material acquisition before OWYN is stabilized.
- Further deterioration in Quest's own household penetration or retail takeaway — the one brand still growing.
- A widening of the 16.7-point gap between category growth and SMPL's own retail takeaway.

---

## Bottom Line

- **Verdict:** Average business — worth deeper work only if valuation is cheap. Not a quality-compounder case at this stage.
- **Could be better than it looks:** the asset-light model still throws off real cash ($178.5m operating cash flow in FY2025 on capex of 1.4-2.0% of sales), and Quest is still gaining household penetration (+120bps YoY to 20.5%) even as the consolidated business shrinks; the chairman and an independent director both bought stock near FY2026 lows.
- **Could be worse than it looks:** if the September 2026 price increase triggers the volume loss management itself expects while Walmart or Amazon further trim distribution, SMPL has no proven moat to defend share — the Atkins collapse (-24.6% quarterly) could spread to Quest.
- **What's missing:** brand-level (or at least Quest-only) gross margin, EBITDA and invested-capital disclosure — the company blends all three financially dissimilar brands into one reported segment, so nobody can tell whether Quest's own economics are healthy or are being subsidized by mix.
- **One thing to watch next:** the gap between category growth and SMPL's own retail takeaway (16.7 points in FQ3 FY2026) — whether Quest starts losing ground the way Atkins already has.

## Plain-English Glossary

- **Gross margin:** the profit left after the cost of making the product, before other expenses.
- **Basis point (bps):** one hundredth of a percentage point; 800bps = 8 percentage points.
- **Operating cash flow:** the cash the business actually generates from running its operations.
- **Return on capital:** the profit earned on each dollar invested in the business.
- **Cost of capital:** what the money used in the business costs to raise.
- **Net debt/EBITDA:** debt minus cash, divided by yearly operating earnings — how many years of earnings would clear the debt (0.5x here means the balance sheet is lightly indebted).
- **Impairment / write-down:** an accounting charge recognizing that an asset (here the OWYN acquisition) is worth less than paid for it.
- **Price elasticity "at 1 or higher":** a 1% price rise loses roughly 1% or more of volume, so the price increase may not add net revenue.

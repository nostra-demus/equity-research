## What This Module Found

SMPL carries low debt relative to its profits: net debt (total debt minus cash) of $276.1M against $234.6M of adjusted EBITDA (a rough measure of yearly operating cash profit) over the trailing twelve months, or 1.18x [01_capital-structure-and-leverage.md §5,§7]. That is the good news. The story, though, is the direction: that leverage more than doubled in one year, from 0.54x at the FY2025 year-end, because management borrowed an extra $150M (taking the term loan from $250M to $400M) to help fund $213.2M of share buybacks against only $92.1M of free cash flow — the cash left after running the business and spending on equipment — in the same 39-week window [01 §6; 03_liquidity-runway.md §4]. Operations did not deteriorate; management chose to add debt.

There is no near-term refinancing problem. The entire $400.0M of debt is a single floating-rate bullet loan (one lump sum, no yearly paydowns) due March 2030 — 0% of it comes due inside 36 months [02_maturity-wall-and-refinancing.md §1,§2]. Liquidity is not a constraint either: $197.8M of committed liquidity (cash $123.9M plus $73.9M of confirmed revolver availability) sits against $0 of committed obligations in the next 12 months, plus a $119.4M free-cash-flow cushion on top (though that cash flow is down 32.0% year on year) [03 §3].

The single most important risk is behavioral, not structural: management borrowing to fund discretionary buybacks while the adjusted-EBITDA margin sits at a multi-year low (16.9% versus 19–20% in FY2024–FY2025) and cash conversion is falling (cash from operations divided by adjusted EBITDA down from 80.1% to 62.9% over two years) [01 §6; 04 §1].

## What Would Change This Read

**Would strengthen it:** a pause or slowdown in debt-funded buybacks that lets net leverage fall back toward the 0.5–0.8x FY2025 range; a current credit rating confirming BB-/BB or better at today's leverage; confirmation of the covenant-EBITDA add-back definition showing no material gap to reported adjusted EBITDA.

**Would weaken it:** continued buybacks at the current ~$284.3M annualized pace combined with a genuine earnings decline; a large sudden revolver draw (e.g., for a debt-financed acquisition) that switches on the springing covenant; further margin erosion below the current 16.9% trough; a confirmed hedge-free floating-rate exposure meeting a sustained rate-hike cycle.

**Data needed:** the full Credit Agreement exhibit (covenant-EBITDA definition, add-back caps, change-of-control / cross-default language); an updated credit-rating action reflecting the post-Nov-2025 structure [§6].

## Bottom Line

- **Verdict: Solid.** Low debt (1.18x net leverage), no maturity due for 3.6 years, and it survives a 30–60% profit drop on every axis tested.
- **Why it could be better than it looks:** the empty maturity wall and $197.8M of committed liquidity give the company room to act through a downturn; the covenant only breaks at a roughly 80% profit decline with no precedent in its own history.
- **Why it could be worse than it looks:** leverage doubled in one year purely to fund buybacks, the profit margin is at a multi-year low (16.9%), and cash conversion is falling (80.1%→62.9%).
- **What is missing:** the full Credit Agreement exhibit (covenant-EBITDA definition and add-back caps) and a current credit rating — the covenant-headroom score is capped at 60 because of the first gap.
- **The one thing to watch next:** whether management keeps buying back stock with new debt through an earnings decline — the only modeled path to a $37M–$73M 12-month funding gap, and a reversible policy choice, not a structural weakness.

## Plain-English Glossary

- **Net debt (strict basis):** total debt minus cash — here $276.1M.
- **Adjusted EBITDA:** a company-defined rough measure of yearly operating cash profit; used because reported GAAP profit was negative due to a one-off non-cash impairment.
- **Net leverage:** net debt divided by adjusted EBITDA — how many years of profit the debt equals (1.18x).
- **Bullet loan:** a loan repaid in one lump sum at maturity, with no yearly paydowns along the way.
- **Free cash flow (FCF):** cash left after running the business and paying for equipment.
- **Covenant / covenant headroom:** a rule in the loan agreement the company must not break; headroom is the cushion before it would.
- **Springing covenant:** a covenant that only switches on under a set condition — here, if the revolver is drawn beyond $22.5M.
- **Interest coverage:** how many times profit covers the interest bill (11.7x).
- **Cash conversion:** how much of the profit turns into actual operating cash (fell from 80.1% to 62.9%).

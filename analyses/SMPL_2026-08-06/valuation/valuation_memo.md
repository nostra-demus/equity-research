## What This Module Found

The stock is modestly cheap: $11.33 today against a $15.27 base-case fair value, a margin of safety (the cushion between price and fair value) of 25.8% [scenario-and-fair-value, `08`].

The number doing the most work is what the price implies about the future. To justify $11.33, the market is betting free cash flow — the cash the business throws off after spending to keep running — falls about 9% a year for six straight years in a row (FY2026–FY2031), or that the operating margin drops to 11.4% and stays there forever [reverse-dcf, `05`]. That is harsher than the company's own guidance, than the analyst consensus, and even than this module's own worst labelled case. The evidence supports a decline for maybe the next one to two years (FY2026 guidance −6.9% revenue, FY2027 consensus −4.5%), not for four more years on top of that, given Quest is still growing, a price increase lands in September 2026, and easing Atkins comparisons that management itself flags.

The single most important risk is a value trap: the discount is only partly deserved on the fundamentals. Business quality scores 40/100 (Weak) and the moat read is "No moat proven" with an "eroding trajectory" — gross margin down 4 of 5 years, return on capital (ROIC, the profit earned per dollar invested) drifting 7.1%→6.0%, and Quest itself losing category share [business-model `07`, `09`]. The base case also leans heavily on the far future: 66.4% of the DCF's value sits in the terminal value (the estimated worth of all cash flows beyond the forecast window), so $15.27 is largely a bet that today's deterioration stabilizes rather than continues. If Quest decelerates and commodity costs worsen, $8.42 — not $15.27 — becomes the relevant anchor.

## What Would Change This Read

**Would make it cheaper (toward the $8.42 bear):** Quest's decline or Atkins' erosion speeding up; a third debt-funded acquisition before OWYN stabilizes (a §24 serial-acquirer pattern already flagged by management-governance); another round of net-negative FY2027 estimate cuts; commodity costs worsening beyond the FY2026 guide.

**Would make it more expensive (toward base/bull):** the September-2026 price increase landing at or below management's elasticity assumption; Quest re-accelerating toward its historical growth rate; two to three straight quarters of margin stabilization at or above the TTM trough; the quality discount narrowing as peers re-rate.

**Data needed to tell which way it breaks:** FQ4 FY2026 results (price-increase volume response and brand-level Quest/Atkins/OWYN trends); FY2027 guidance (first full post-impairment year); confirmation the CEO transition stops the senior-leadership turnover.

## Bottom Line

- **Verdict:** Modestly undervalued — 25.8% margin of safety to a $15.27 base fair value at $11.33.
- **Why it could be better than it looks:** two independent methods (peers $13.76, DCF $17.53) agree within 27% and both sit well above price; even the module's own structural-reset floor ($13.09) is above today's price; the market is pricing in a six-year decline worse than any evidenced case.
- **Why it could be worse:** business quality is 40/100 with no proven moat and an eroding trajectory, and 66.4% of the base-case value sits in the terminal value — if the deterioration continues, $8.42 (a 25.7% loss) is the real anchor.
- **What's missing:** brand-level (Quest/Atkins/OWYN) profit disclosure — SMPL gives brand revenue but no brand profit, so the "Quest is doing the heavy lifting" read cannot be sized in dollars and a true sum-of-the-parts cannot be built.
- **One thing to watch next:** the September-2026 price increase and FQ4 FY2026 volume response — the first hard evidence of whether the turnaround the base case assumes is actually happening.

## Plain-English Glossary

- **Margin of safety** — the cushion between today's price and estimated fair value; here 25.8%.
- **Free cash flow (FCF)** — the cash a business generates after the spending needed to keep running.
- **Enterprise value (EV)** — the whole-company price: market value of equity plus net debt.
- **Net debt** — total debt minus cash on hand ($324.58M here).
- **EV/EBITDA** — enterprise value divided by earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation; a common multiple for comparing companies.
- **Terminal value** — the estimated worth of all cash flows beyond the explicit forecast window; 66.4% of the DCF here.
- **ROIC (return on capital)** — the profit earned on each dollar invested; drifting 7.1%→6.0%.
- **WACC (cost of capital)** — what the company's money costs to raise; 7.93% here.
- **Reverse-DCF** — working backwards from today's price to see what future growth (or decline) the market is assuming.
- **Moat** — a durable advantage that protects profits from competitors; the module found none proven.

## 2. The Nearest Dated Catalyst

The nearest dated catalyst is the **FQ4 FY2026 earnings release, estimated Oct-23-2026**. Management has already given a specific numeric range for the quarter ($322M–$332M net sales, $52M–$57M Adjusted EBITDA), and Street consensus sits within 0.5% of that midpoint on both lines — a genuinely matched bar, not an easy one to clear [`earnings/04_guidance-consensus.md` §3]. What makes this print two-sided rather than a formality: management chose to ship below what stores were actually selling this quarter to reset customer inventories, so a headline decline (guided at −13% to −10% year-over-year) does not by itself tell you whether demand is stabilizing or eroding. A fourth straight beat, on top of the current three-quarter streak, would support the reset story; a miss — especially one paired with a repeat of FQ3's pattern where the headline beat but gross margin still missed — would confirm the Street's still-falling FY2027 estimates (net revision breadth of −4 to −6 across revenue, EBITDA and EPS over the last three months) [`earnings/04_guidance-consensus.md` §4–§5].

## 3. The Single Most Important Catalyst

The catalyst with the largest potential impact is not a single date — it is the **September-2026 price increase and how much sales volume it costs the company**, a mechanism management itself frames as carrying real risk: "elasticities to be at 1 or higher... there's going to be a volume impact" [Q3 FY26 earnings call, Jul-9-2026]. This is the swing factor the valuation module calls the single biggest driver between its bull case ($21.98/share) and bear case ($8.42/share) [`valuation/07_scenario-and-fair-value.md` §3, §6], and it is the mechanism the entire "gross margin approaching 40%, Adjusted EBITDA margin approaching 20%" long-run target depends on [`management-governance/01_management-and-track-record.md` §3]. A buyer *can* time the moment the price increase takes effect (September 2026, a disclosed month) — but cannot yet time when its true net effect becomes visible in the numbers, because that requires a full quarter under the new prices, which points to the FQ1 FY2027 print (roughly January 2027) rather than any date currently confirmed in this pool. The Oct-23-2026 FQ4 print (§2 above) may offer an early qualitative read through management's FY2027 framing, but the hard number resolves later, on an unconfirmed date.

## 4. Negative / Bearish Catalysts

- **FQ4 FY2026 miss below the guided floor (Oct-23-2026)** — revenue below $319M or Adjusted EBITDA below $52M would be a genuine break from three straight quarters of beats and would confirm, not merely echo, the Street's still-falling FY2027 revision trend [`earnings/05_beat-miss-setup.md` §4].
- **September-2026 price increase landing at or above management's own "1 or higher" elasticity fear** — the company's own stated base case is that volume losses could match or exceed the price gain; a bear-case outcome here (−$12.8M Adjusted EBITDA in the sensitivity model) is not a tail risk, it is close to what management itself warns is plausible [`earnings/07_earnings-sensitivity.md` §2].
- **Continued debt-funded share buybacks through a genuine earnings decline** — undated and thematic, not a scheduled event, but flagged explicitly by the balance-sheet-survival module as the one path to a real funding gap: net leverage already more than doubled in a year (0.54x to 1.18x) funding buybacks, and a 30%–60% Adjusted EBITDA decline combined with continued buybacks at the current ~$284.3M annualized pace would open an estimated $37M–$73M 12-month funding gap [`balance-sheet-survival/99_balance-sheet-survival-synthesis.md` §1A].
- **A third material acquisition before OWYN stabilizes (§24 Filter 4)** — no deal is announced or scheduled, so this is not a dated catalyst, but it is a named standing risk: SMPL is already a serial acquirer under §24's test (Quest 2019, ~$982.1M; OWYN 2024, ~$280.2M, 71% impaired within 24 months), and the capital-allocation module states explicitly that a third deal before OWYN stabilizes would move the classification to "governance red flags" [`business-model/11_capital-allocation-governance.md` §3]. If a deal were announced, it should not be read as bullish news on the strength of "growth platform" language alone — the company's own record on that framing (OWYN) is the worst-scoring line in the entire capital-allocation scorecard.
- **~Jan-2027 AGM, if say-on-pay support drops materially or no Chair-succession plan is disclosed** — see §1 row above; a governance-confidence signal, not a solvency or earnings event, but real for a board where three of ten "independent" directors (including the Chair and Vice Chair) come from the same outside firm [`management-governance/05_board-and-shareholder-rights.md` §1].
- **Tariff and commodity cost inflation (undated, thematic)** — unhedged, and named by the earnings-sensitivity module as the single highest-magnitude variable in the model (±$27.0M Adjusted EBITDA, about 12% of the FY2026 guidance midpoint) [`earnings/07_earnings-sensitivity.md` §4]. No decision date exists for this risk, so per Core Principle 1 it is not listed as a dated catalyst — but buyers should not read its absence from the table above as an absence of risk.

## 5. Calendar Read

This calendar carries exactly one well-evidenced near-term proven date — the Oct-23-2026 FQ4 FY2026 print, which also delivers the first FY2027 guide — plus one dated operational event (the September-2026 price increase) whose real financial effect will not be visible until a later, still-unconfirmed print. Beyond those two, the calendar is either far outside the 12-month window (the Mar-2030 debt maturity), inferred from a pattern rather than filed (the ~Jan-2027 AGM), or genuinely undated and thematic (tariffs, commodities, GLP-1, the buyback-funded-leverage risk, and the standing serial-acquirer risk under §24 Filter 4). The near-term events themselves skew two-sided rather than clearly bullish: management's own framing of both the FQ4 shipment reset and the price increase carries an explicit, self-disclosed downside case, not just an upside one. The single most important timing caveat: the catalyst that would matter most to the thesis — whether the price increase adds net revenue or gets given back to volume loss — cannot actually be timed yet, because the quarter that will prove it out (FQ1 FY2027, roughly January 2027) has no confirmed report date anywhere in this pool.

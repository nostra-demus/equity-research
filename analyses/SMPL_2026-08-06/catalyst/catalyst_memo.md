## What This Module Found

SMPL is not just a thematic story: it has one proven, dated near-term catalyst. That is the FQ4 FY2026 earnings release, estimated Oct-23-2026, where management has already put numbers on the table — $322M–$332M net sales and $52M–$57M Adjusted EBITDA (a rough measure of operating cash profit, before interest, tax, and non-cash charges) [`catalyst/01_catalyst-calendar.md` §1, §2]. That print also carries the first formal FY2027 outlook, which the earnings module calls more important to the setup than the FQ4 numbers themselves.

The single most important catalyst is the high-single-digit list-price increase taking effect September 2026 across most of the portfolio. It is the main lever management is using to fix its margin problem, and the valuation module's single biggest swing factor between its $21.98 bull case and $8.42 bear case [`catalyst/01_catalyst-calendar.md` §3; `valuation/07_scenario-and-fair-value.md` §3, §6]. The catch: its net effect — will customers keep buying at the higher price, or walk away — will not be provable until an FQ1 FY2027 print that has no confirmed date yet.

The most important risk is that same price increase. Management itself warned on the call that price elasticity (how much sales volume drops when price rises) could run "1 or higher" — meaning volume lost could roughly match or exceed the gain from higher prices, cutting about $12.8M off Adjusted EBITDA on the affected base. This is close to management's own base case, not a tail scenario [`earnings/07_earnings-sensitivity.md` §2]. A second risk: an FQ4 miss below the guided floor on Oct-23-2026 would break a three-quarter beat streak.

The calendar is two-sided, not clearly bullish. And one piece of disconfirming evidence is already visible: net leverage (net debt versus Adjusted EBITDA — how many years of profit it would take to repay debt) has more than doubled in a year, from 0.54x to 1.18x, funding continued buybacks even as profit has been under pressure. So the calendar's one capital-return item (the Jan-6-2026 $200M buyback authorization increase) is happening alongside rising leverage, which cuts against reading it as a clean bullish signal [`balance-sheet-survival/99_balance-sheet-survival-synthesis.md` §1A; `catalyst/01_catalyst-calendar.md` §4].

## What Would Change This Read

- **FQ4 print, Oct-23-2026 — better:** A fourth straight beat (revenue >$338M / Adjusted EBITDA >$60M), gross margin beating the guided "strongest of the year," and an FY2027 guide showing Atkins's decline narrowing and the price increase adding net revenue.
- **FQ4 print, Oct-23-2026 — worse:** A miss below the guided floor (<$319M revenue / <$49M Adjusted EBITDA), a repeat of FQ3's pattern (GAAP gross margin missed even as revenue/EBITDA beat), or an FY2027 guide implying continued double-digit Adjusted EBITDA decline.
- **Price increase, Sept-2026 (resolves ~FQ1 FY2027) — better:** Elasticity below management's fear (e.g. 0.7x), net realized revenue up ~2% on the affected base (+$7.7M Adjusted EBITDA).
- **Price increase — worse:** Elasticity at or above "1 or higher," net revenue on the affected base down ~3% (−$12.8M Adjusted EBITDA).
- **~late Jan-2027 AGM (inferred, no filed date):** Say-on-pay holding near the prior 97.3% and a named Chair-succession plan disclosed would reassure; a drop in support or no succession plan would not (Chairman Kilts is age 77, near the board's age-78 mandatory-retirement threshold).
- **Refinancing (Mar-17-2030 bullet, background):** Leverage falling back toward 0.5x–0.8x supports refinancing near the current 200bps margin; continued debt-funded buybacks through a real earnings decline, or a downgrade of the stale S&P BB- rating, would not.

## Bottom Line

- **Verdict:** Dated, evidenced near-term catalysts — a real near-term calendar, but the catalyst that matters most cannot yet be fully timed.
- **Could be better than it looks:** A fourth consecutive beat on Oct-23-2026 with an FY2027 guide framing the price increase as net-positive, against a consensus set within 0.5% of the guided midpoint — a real test passed, not a layup.
- **Could be worse than it looks:** Price-increase elasticity landing at or above management's own "1 or higher" fear (~−$12.8M Adjusted EBITDA), and/or an FQ4 miss below the guided floor that breaks the beat streak.
- **What is missing:** A confirmed report date for the quarter that actually proves out the price increase's net effect (FQ1 FY2027). Nothing in this pool fixes that date. The exact Oct-23-2026 date is vendor-derived (calendar-matched), not yet a company press release.
- **One thing to watch next:** The Oct-23-2026 FQ4 print and its first FY2027 guide — but do not read a beat as proof the turnaround has worked. It is the fourth quarter of evidence, not the 2–3 years §24 requires.

## Plain-English Glossary

- **Adjusted EBITDA** — a rough measure of operating cash profit, before interest, tax, and non-cash charges; management's own guided range is $52M–$57M for FQ4.
- **Elasticity** — how much sales volume falls when a price rises; management fears "1 or higher," meaning volume lost could match or exceed the price gain.
- **Net leverage** — net debt measured against Adjusted EBITDA, i.e. roughly how many years of profit it would take to repay debt; here up from 0.54x to 1.18x in a year.
- **Bullet loan / bullet maturity** — a loan that pays no principal until one lump sum falls due at the end (here $400.0M on Mar-17-2030).
- **Say-on-pay** — the shareholder advisory vote on executive pay; the prior vote passed near 97.3%.

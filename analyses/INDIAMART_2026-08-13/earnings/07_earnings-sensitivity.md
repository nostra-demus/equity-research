# Earnings Sensitivity — INDIAMART

**Jurisdiction / currency / standard:** India (NSE: INDIAMART, BSE: 542726), Ind AS, consolidated, INR. Fiscal year ends 31 March ("FY26" = year ended 31-Mar-2026). All rupee figures below are in ₹ million unless a crore figure is quoted directly from a transcript (labelled inline), consistent with `01_historical-financials.md` and `02_revenue-drivers.md`. Base metric used throughout for EBITDA impacts: FY26 reported, consolidated EBITDA of ₹5,205.94mn [`01_historical-financials.md` §1, Capital IQ export, Income Statement tab, FY2026 column] — the company discloses no adjusted/non-GAAP EBITDA (`01_historical-financials.md` §4), so there is no reported-vs-adjusted ambiguity to separate here (CLAUDE.md §15).

## 1. Variable Selection

Six variables were selected from the upstream `02_revenue-drivers.md` and `03_margin-drivers.md` driver tables, chosen for carrying a "High" or the largest "Mid" magnitude rating in those tables, plus one variable pulled from the business-model `10_external-dependency.md` sensitivity disclosure that is not visible in either driver table because it sits below the operating line. Selected: (1) discretionary customer-acquisition opex (Advertisement + Outsourced sales cost) — named in `03_margin-drivers.md` §8 as "the single biggest margin driver"; (2) ARPU / price realization — rated High magnitude and the dominant realized-growth driver in `02_revenue-drivers.md` §3–4; (3) paying-subscriber net additions — rated High magnitude, the volume half of the revenue identity; (4) the investment-portfolio (treasury book) NAV sensitivity — the company's own disclosed market-risk sensitivity table in Note 31, surfaced via `10_external-dependency.md` §2, and the single largest number disclosed anywhere in the data pool; (5) customer support / trust-and-verification cost buildout — rated High magnitude (+191bps FY26) in `03_margin-drivers.md` §2; (6) the Accounting Software Services segment (Busy/Livekeeping) — rated High magnitude for its own growth and a quantifiable, disclosed segment P&L. A seventh candidate, stock-based compensation, was considered (Mid magnitude, +98bps FY26) but excluded — its FY26-to-Q1-FY27 growth deceleration (+95% YoY to +1.9% YoY) already reads as largely spent, and its absolute size (₹366.20mn FY26, 2.33% of revenue) is smaller than every variable retained. Two of the six (ARPU and subscriber net adds) are mechanically linked through the revenue identity (subscribers × ARPU) — see Section 5 for how the double-counting was avoided.

## 4. The Single Highest-Sensitivity Variable

By raw magnitude, the **investment-portfolio NAV / treasury-book sensitivity** is the single largest number in this table — a company-disclosed ±5% NAV move swings Profit Before Tax by ±₹1,426.29mn, equal to roughly 27% of FY26 EBITDA [`10_external-dependency.md` §2, FY26 Annual Report, Note 31]. It is a mix of external (market interest rates and bond/debt-fund prices, which the company does not control) and company-controlled (the decision to hold a ₹30bn+ treasury float in NAV-linked mutual funds, ETFs, bonds and debentures rather than fixed deposits). It would swing to the adverse case if Indian debt-fund and bond-market NAVs decline broadly — for example, an unexpected reversal of the rate-cut cycle or a credit-spread widening event — and this can happen independent of how the core marketplace business performs, which is exactly why `01_historical-financials.md` §3 already flags reported EPS as a noisier read than EBIT/EBITDA for this company. That said, this variable does not move the *operating* business: the single largest sensitivity affecting the company's core EBITDA is **discretionary customer-acquisition opex** (Rank 2, ₹744.4mn average impact, up to a ₹1,260.9mn EBITDA hit if Advertisement spend resumes to management's own guided ₹10 crore/quarter target) — and because that variable is management-controlled (a policy choice, per `03_margin-drivers.md` §8), it is the more actionable lever for anyone modelling the next 3–12 months of *operating* earnings.

## 5. Interaction Effects

ARPU and paying-subscriber net additions are mechanically linked — consolidated revenue in the Web segment is the product of the two (subscribers × ARPU, per `02_revenue-drivers.md` §2) — so this table deliberately held each variable's counterpart constant when flexing the other (Section 2), to avoid double-counting the same underlying revenue move twice. In practice they can move together in the same direction: management has directly attributed part of the recent net-add weakness to the Silver-tier price increase implemented at the end of Q2 FY26 [`02_revenue-drivers.md` §4, citing the Q4 FY26 Earnings Call: "1,000, 1,500 could have been because of the price"], meaning a further ARPU-raising bull case for pricing could itself push the subscriber-net-adds variable toward its bear case — a genuine trade-off, not two independent draws. There is also a plausible causal link between the customer-acquisition-opex variable and the subscriber-net-adds variable in the *other* direction: the discretionary opex pullback that is currently an EBITDA tailwind (Rank 2's bull case) is a candidate explanation for continued weak net adds, since management itself frames the current spend level as deliberately reduced while working through Silver-tier churn [`03_margin-drivers.md` §8]. A combined downside scenario — SME demand weakens (pushing subscriber net adds toward the bear case) and management responds by resuming customer-acquisition spend to defend the funnel (pushing opex toward its own bear case) — would compound two of this table's bear cases at once rather than occurring independently, and is not visible if each row is read in isolation.

## 6. Non-Linear Or Asymmetric Risks

- **Customer support / verification cost is a one-directional cost line, not a two-sided risk.** It has risen in every disclosed period (+116% YoY FY26, +168bps again YoY in Q1 FY27) with no observed decline anywhere in the pool — both the "bull" and "bear" cases in Section 2's table show a net EBITDA drag, only of different sizes. This is a structural, not cyclical, cost buildout (KYC, OTP verification, buy-lead verification, agentic call handling), and there is no evidence it mean-reverts.
- **Pricing changes hit subscriber losses with a 1–2 quarter lag, not immediately.** `03_margin-drivers.md` §3 notes the Silver-tier price increase implemented at the end of Q2 FY26 only showed up in subscriber losses by Q4 FY26. A further price increase in the ARPU bull case (Section 2) could therefore produce a delayed, not contemporaneous, hit to the subscriber-net-adds variable — the two rows in this table would not move on the same reporting date even though they are causally linked (Section 5).
- **Operating deleverage risk on the trust-and-safety cost line.** Customer support / verification costs scale with transaction and inquiry volume, not with the size of the paying-subscriber base — so if the subscriber-net-adds bear case materializes (a shrinking base), this cost line does not shrink proportionally, meaning the two adverse scenarios do not offset each other; they add.
- **Goodwill impairment is a discrete, one-sided tail risk, not modelled in Section 2.** ₹4,542.72mn of goodwill (Busy Infotech + Livekeeping) sits on the balance sheet and the FY26 statutory auditor named it a Key Audit Matter requiring judgement on revenue-growth, terminal-growth and WACC assumptions [`03_margin-drivers.md` §2, citing `03_segment-map.md` §1 and the FY26 Annual Report's Independent Auditor's Report]. If the Accounting Software Services segment's growth (Section 2, Rank 6) disappoints materially and persistently, an impairment charge would hit the Depreciation, amortisation and impairment expense line directly — and because that line sits inside IndiaMART's own EBITDA definition (unlike most companies), a goodwill impairment would cut EBITDA itself, not just EBIT. Not currently triggered; there is no upside equivalent to this risk.
- **The investment-portfolio NAV swing (Rank 1) is symmetric by disclosure, but sits below the operating line where the company's own reported EPS has already shown swings the operating variables in this table cannot explain** — e.g. diluted EPS fell from ₹31.24 (Q3 FY26) to ₹8.33 (Q4 FY26), a −72% move, on an EBITDA base that moved only from ₹1,342mn to ₹1,326mn (−1.2%) over the same two quarters [`01_historical-financials.md` §3]. A reader modelling EPS off Section 2's operating variables alone would materially misjudge quarter-to-quarter earnings volatility.

## 7. Earnings Volatility Score

**66/100 — inverted scale: higher = WORSE (more volatile / sensitive to small input changes).**

Band: 61–80, High volatility — multiple variables with large impact. Reason: at least three of the six variables in this table can independently swing earnings by 20%+ of their respective base metric in a single year — discretionary customer-acquisition opex (up to −24.2% of FY26 EBITDA if resumed to the company's own guided target), the investment-portfolio NAV (±27% of FY26 EBITDA on PBT, company-disclosed and exact), and the compounding downside scenario in Section 5 where two bear cases move together rather than independently — and reported EPS (the metric most readers will first look at) is already demonstrably more volatile than any single operating variable modelled here, swinging −72% quarter over quarter on a below-the-line item the operating drivers in this report do not touch (Section 6). The score is held below the 81–100 "extremely volatile" band because the company carries a structural net-cash balance sheet (`01_historical-financials.md` §1), the largest single lever (customer-acquisition opex) is management-controlled and can therefore be throttled rather than being a pure external shock, and the recurring subscription revenue base provides a floor that a project- or transaction-based business would not have.

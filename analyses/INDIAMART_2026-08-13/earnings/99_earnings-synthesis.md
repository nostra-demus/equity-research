# Earnings Module — INDIAMART (Synthesis)

## Abstract

IndiaMART's earnings are mixed, not accelerating: revenue growth has slowed every year since FY23 to 11.37% year-on-year in the latest quarter, the softest of the last eight, because growth now comes almost entirely from price — ARPU up 8.2% in FY26 — while paying-subscriber volume has been flat or negative in three of the last four quarters. The consensus bar is rated fair, but sell-side conviction is fading, with target-price cuts outnumbering raises sixteen to one over three months. The biggest risk is that the current 35.35% EBITDA margin, propped up by deliberately deferred acquisition spend management calls temporary, and the falling subscriber count are the same SME-demand weakness expressed twice, not two offsetting forces. Verdict: mixed earnings setup.

## 5. Earnings Setup Summary

### Revenue Setup

The current growth rate is sustainable in the sense that it rests on a recurring, structural lever — four straight years of ~8–9% ARPU growth on a subscription base — rather than M&A, FX, or an accounting change; there is no reported-vs-organic gap to name here because no acquisition or divestiture touches either compared period. But sustainability now depends entirely on one factor: whether paying-subscriber net additions turn positive again, because ARPU growth applied to a shrinking or flat subscriber base compounds against a smaller pool every quarter, and management itself has warned that pushing price further while renewal is weak risks making customer-acquisition cost rise faster than lifetime value. The single factor that would flip the direction of revenue growth is that subscriber net adds — negative in 3 of the last 4 quarters — either turn positive (confirming the current setup is a temporary soft patch) or stay negative through the "2, 3 quarter" window management itself flagged (confirming a genuine, not cyclical, demand problem). One further nuance worth flagging: the fast-growing Accounting Software Services segment, only 8% of FY26 revenue, supplied roughly a third (3.48 of 11.37 percentage points) of Q1 FY27's incremental growth — a segment mix shift that is real and disclosed, not a residual gap, but one riding on a much smaller, still loss-making base than the core marketplace.

### Margin Setup

Current margins are not sustainable through the next few quarters as reported — Q1 FY27's 35.35% EBITDA margin is a management-labelled temporary policy choice (deferred customer-acquisition spend), not a cyclical peak or trough in the traditional sense, and management has stated it plans to hold this stance for roughly "2, 3 more quarters" before resuming spend. The single driver that, moving adversely by 10–20%, takes the largest bite out of consolidated margins is discretionary customer-acquisition opex (Advertisement + Outsourced sales): resuming Advertisement spend to management's own guided ~₹10 crore/quarter run-rate would cost roughly a quarter of FY26 EBITDA on its own (up to −24.2% per 07_earnings-sensitivity's bear case). IndiaMART has no contractual or structural margin-protection mechanism in the traditional sense — there is no COGS line, no commodity pass-through, and no hedging, because the business has no input-cost exposure at all; the only lever management holds is its own discretion over acquisition spend, which is precisely the lever currently propping the margin up and the lever most likely to reverse it.

### Quality Check

The single largest gap between reported and economic earnings is the ~30% of FY26 Profit Before Tax and ~40% of diluted EPS that comes from mark-to-market and realised gains on the company's ~₹30 billion treasury book — a gap that is not narrowing; it has recurred at material size in every one of the last 5 fiscal years despite Capital IQ's own income-statement structure labelling it "Unusual Items." This is not an aggressive adjustment management is using to flatter results — it is fully disclosed under Ind AS fair-value accounting, is real, collected value, and IndiaMART does not publish any non-GAAP overlay of its own to compare it against. To model normalized earnings for next year, the correct starting point is GAAP EBIT/EBITDA, not GAAP PBT or diluted EPS, because EBIT/EBITDA already sit above the treasury-gains line in IndiaMART's own P&L structure and exclude this volatile, market-linked item entirely — while PBT and EPS embed it and have therefore swung far more than the operating business has (diluted EPS fell −72% quarter over quarter in one instance on an EBITDA base that moved only −1.2%).

### Consensus Bar

For IndiaMART to beat the current bar by a material margin, two things would need to happen roughly at once — paying-subscriber net adds turning positive meaningfully ahead of management's own "2, 3 quarter" timeline, and EBITDA margin holding near Q1 FY27's elevated level rather than reverting — which is the same conjunction risk 08_earnings-red-flags flags as the module's single biggest vulnerability, not two independent paths to a beat. The bar looks most likely mis-set on the EPS line specifically: consensus FY2027 EPS growth (+16.8%) outpaces consensus FY2027 EBITDA growth (+11.96%) by 4.8 percentage points, a gap that — given FY26's demonstrated EPS dependency on the volatile treasury-gains line (~30–40% of PBT/EPS) — plausibly embeds an unstated assumption that this non-operating item recurs or grows; if it does not, an EPS "miss" could occur even alongside healthy operating performance. The revenue bar, by contrast, looks correctly priced: it has been tightly calibrated to actual results (0% to +1.3% surprise) for four straight quarters, including an exactly in-line Q1 FY27 print, so a large surprise in either direction on the top line next quarter would be the exception, not the pattern.

## 5b. Leverage & Capital Structure

Leverage is within normal range and did not change materially during the period — no dedicated treatment required. IndiaMART carries no bank borrowings; its entire "Total Debt" per Capital IQ is lease liabilities (₹231.02mn at FY26-end), and the company has held a structural net-cash position on both the strict basis (total debt minus cash) and the broad basis (also netting short-term investments and the treasury book) throughout the full 5-year window reviewed, deepening from ₹122mn net cash (strict, FY23) to ₹573mn (strict, FY26) and exceeding ₹30 billion net cash on the broad basis in FY26 [01_historical-financials §1]. Neither Trigger A (net debt/Adjusted EBITDA > 3.0x) nor Trigger B (a material YoY leverage increase) fires.

## 9. Simple Summary

- Revenue is growing (+11.37% last quarter) but slowing every year, and almost all of it now comes from raising prices — the number of paying customers is flat to shrinking.
- Margins look good right now (35.35% EBITDA margin) mainly because management has deliberately cut back on customer-acquisition spending — management itself says this won't last more than "2, 3" more quarters.
- Earnings are not fully clean: about 30–40% of reported profit and per-share earnings comes from gains on a large investment portfolio, not from the core marketplace business — real money, but volatile and unrelated to operations.
- The Street's bar is roughly fair for revenue but the earnings-per-share number for next year looks set too high, because it seems to assume those investment gains keep recurring.
- Next quarter's setup is being called "balanced," but there's a real risk the margin boost and the customer decline are actually the same underlying weakness showing up twice — if so, both could disappoint at once.
- Cash generation is genuinely strong and has been for years — the company collects cash from customers before it recognizes the revenue, and that has never reversed.
- Earnings volatility is high — several single variables (discretionary spending, investment-portfolio swings) can each move earnings by 20%+ in a year.
- This module is useful: the data is complete and current, but the master synthesizer should treat the "balanced" and "fair" framings with real caution given the compounding-risk flag.

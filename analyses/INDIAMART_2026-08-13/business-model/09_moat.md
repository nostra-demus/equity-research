**Cross-check of the headline return figure (required by CLAUDE.md §15/§21 hygiene).** The table above uses Capital IQ's own "Return on Capital %" line as the primary figure. A hand-computed cross-check on the standard base (NOPAT ÷ average invested capital) for FY26, using **EBIT ₹5,015.93m** [Capital IQ Financials (1).xls, Income Statement tab] and the **normalized structural tax rate of 25.17%** (India's statutory corporate tax rate for domestic companies, per the FY26 Note 26 tax reconciliation — see below) against **average Total Capital (debt + equity) of ₹23,323.5m** (FY25 ₹24,234.77m and FY26 ₹22,412.26m) [Capital IQ Financials (1).xls, Capital Structure Summary tab], gives: NOPAT = 5,015.93 × (1 − 0.2517) = ₹3,753.4m; ROIC = 3,753.4 / 23,323.5 = **16.1%** — about 258 basis points (bps — hundredths of a percentage point) above CIQ's own reported 13.51% for FY26. This is a material divergence and is flagged, not silently resolved: it most likely reflects a different capital-base definition or averaging convention inside Capital IQ's proprietary "Return on Capital %" methodology that cannot be fully reconstructed from the disclosed export. Per the source hierarchy and the conservative default (CLAUDE.md §4), **this report uses the lower, vendor-reported CIQ figure (13.51% FY26 / 11.23% five-year average) as the primary anchor**, not the higher hand-computed 16.1%.

**Normalized tax rate, stated.** FY26's reported effective tax rate was 26.74%, up from 21.98% in FY25 [FY26 Annual Report (Ind AS), Note 26(c) — Reconciliation of tax expense]. The company's own reconciliation shows this is driven mainly by (i) a shrinking benefit from income taxed at lower rates (₹152.38m in FY26 vs ₹368.14m in FY25) and (ii) rising business losses at loss-making consolidated subsidiaries (Busy Infotech/Livekeeping) for which no deferred tax asset is recognised (₹206.99m in FY26 vs ₹167.27m in FY25) — both structural/subsidiary-consolidation effects, not core-marketplace operating economics. The **statutory rate of 25.17%** [FY26 Annual Report (Ind AS), Note 26(c)] is used here as the normalized structural rate — it sits close to the 5-year average reported effective rate (~24.5%: 23.77%, 23.55%, 26.50%, 21.98%, 26.74% for FY22–FY26) [Capital IQ, Income Statement tab] and strips out the subsidiary-consolidation and rate-mix noise that pushed FY26's reported rate above trend. This rate is the anchor for any downstream DCF (`valuation/04_intrinsic-dcf`) reconciling to the same normalized NOPAT base.

**Through-cycle, not peak-year.** `07_business-quality.md` (§4, Read) already flags FY25/FY26 as sitting near a multi-year high — Return on Capital was 15.40% (FY25) and 13.51% (FY26) vs 7.46%–10.26% in FY22–FY24 [Capital IQ, Ratios tab, cross-checked against the same figures cited in `07_business-quality.md`]. The single-year FY26 figure is therefore labelled a **near-peak year, not the through-cycle level**; the 5-year average of 11.23% is used for the economic-moat test below.

**Net-cash structure — gross-capital basis shown, net-of-cash not meaningful.** IndiaMART is a net-cash company: FY26 Total Cash & Short-Term Investments were ₹33,886.58m against Total Capital (debt + equity) of only ₹22,412.26m [Capital IQ Financials (1).xls, Capital Structure Summary tab] — cash and near-cash investments exceed the entire debt-plus-equity capital base. A net-of-cash invested-capital figure would therefore be **negative** (₹22,412.26m − ₹33,886.58m = −₹11,474.32m for FY26; also negative in FY25), making a net-of-cash ROIC mathematically undefined/not meaningful. The gross-capital basis (CIQ's reported "Return on Capital %," debt + equity, cash not netted) is therefore the only usable basis and is what is used throughout this section — it is already the more conservative of the two possible readings, since netting out the large cash pile would (if it produced a positive denominator) mechanically inflate the ratio.

**The economic moat test (required).**

> Return on capital **above** cost of capital, on the base-case estimate — but the margin is thin and not robust to a plausible change in the cost-of-capital assumption: **11.23% 5-yr avg ROIC (gross-capital basis)** vs **≈8.75% estimated WACC** (+~248 bps) — [CAPM estimate, inputs shown below; **Inference, not from filings**].

- **Cost-of-capital sourcing (priority per CLAUDE.md §4/report structure).** No company-disclosed WACC, cost of equity, or hurdle rate exists anywhere in the reviewed filings or transcripts. A CAPM estimate is therefore built and labelled as inference: risk-free rate = 6.85% (India 10-year G-Sec yield, ~Jul–Aug 2026) [Web: BusinessToday, 2026-08-05, "10 year G Sec yield climbs to 6.85% in July" — dated, unverified]; equity risk premium (India) = 7.31% [Web: Damodaran country equity-risk-premium dataset, July 2026 update — dated, unverified]; beta (5-year) = 0.26 [Capital IQ Public Company Profile export; corroborated independently in Company Comparable Analysis, Operating Statistics tab, as-of 2026-08-13]. Cost of equity = 6.85% + 0.26 × 7.31% = **8.75%**. Because Total Debt/Capital is only ~0.97% at FY26-end [Capital IQ Financials (1).xls, Ratios tab], WACC ≈ cost of equity ≈ **8.75–8.8%** — debt is too small to move the blended figure meaningfully.
- **Flagged sensitivity — the beta input is unusually low and the test is not robust to it.** A 5-year beta of 0.26 is low for a mid-cap, India-only, still-decelerating-growth internet/marketplace stock; more conventional betas used for comparable small/mid-cap growth internet names (roughly 0.8–1.0) would push the CAPM cost of equity to **≈12.7%–14.2%**. Under that more conventional assumption, the 5-year average ROIC of 11.23% would sit **at or below** the cost of capital, not above it — and even on the low-beta base case, one trough year in the window (FY23, ROIC 7.46%) already sits below the CAPM estimate. This sensitivity is material enough that the "above cost of capital" read cannot be treated as robust, and it is the deciding factor capping the verdict below Strong in §5.

## 4. Where The Company Sits

1. **Relative to peers.** Company sits at or near the **top** of the two named peers with disclosed financial data on operating margin — IndiaMART's LTM EBIT margin (32.0%) and gross margin (55.6%) both exceed Just Dial's (25.3% EBIT, 39.9% gross) [Company Comparable Analysis, Operating Statistics tab, as-of 2026-08-13]. On net income margin, Just Dial is higher (40.5% vs 30.6%), but that is driven by Just Dial's large net-cash treasury income (non-operating), the same distortion `08_competitive-map.md` §2 flags for that company — it does not reflect a superior core-marketplace business. A clean return-on-capital comparison against Just Dial is not possible: the only Just Dial figures available are wide, unverified web-aggregator ROE/ROCE ranges (3.4%–13.1%), not a filing-sourced, comparably-defined ROIC. TradeIndia and 1688.com/Alibaba.com disclose no comparable margin or return data at all. **Insufficient data to make a precise, filing-grade return-on-capital comparison against named peers**, though the operating-margin comparison against Just Dial is directionally favorable to IndiaMART.
2. **Absolute (the economic moat test).** IndiaMART earns a return on capital **modestly above** its estimated cost of capital on the base-case, low-beta CAPM estimate (11.23% 5-yr avg ROIC vs ≈8.75% WACC, +~248 bps) — but this result flips to **at or below** cost of capital under a more conventional beta assumption for a comparable small/mid-cap Indian internet stock (implying a ≈12.7%–14.2% WACC). The test is **not robustly assessable as "above"** given this sensitivity; it is closer to a borderline pass than a clear one.

## 5. Moat Verdict

**Narrow moat.**

The strongest evidenced advantages are Distribution and Scale relative to the closest true same-business-model rival, TradeIndia (a private, small competitor management itself has watched for years without observing material share shifts), plus a real but congestion-limited two-sided network effect. None of these clears the Strong band on its own, and the decisive economic test — return on capital versus cost of capital — passes only on a base-case CAPM estimate built on an unusually low disclosed beta (0.26); under a more conventional beta for a comparable small/mid-cap growth internet name, the 5-year average ROIC (11.23%) would sit at or below the estimated cost of capital, and even the base case shows a trough year (FY23) already below it. Combined with proven-weak switching costs (no contractual lock-in; a Silver-tier price rise in Q4 FY26 directly turned net supplier additions negative) and margin instability (EBITDA margin ranged 26.5%–40.9% over five years, per `07_business-quality.md`), this is a company with real, if partial, structural advantages over its named competitors that produce economics *close to* — not durably and robustly above — its cost of capital. The five-year test this moat would need to pass: sustaining a return on capital clearly and robustly above a properly-estimated cost of capital (not one resting on a fragile low-beta assumption) while the AI-search/agentic-commerce discovery layer above the core subscription mechanic — which `07_business-quality.md` itself flags as genuinely still in flux — plays out.

**Moat trajectory: stable, with a specific near-term erosion warning.** The 5-year return-on-capital trend is not falling — it rose from 10.26% (FY22) to a 15.40% peak (FY25) before easing to 13.51% (FY26) [Capital IQ, Ratios tab] — and `08_competitive-map.md`'s own competitive-position read is "Holding" (not clearly gaining or losing share against the named peers), based on management's own account that TradeIndia shows "no significant change" and Just Dial's paying-customer base only recently recovered from a COVID-era decline. That supports "stable" over "eroding" as the level call. But three specific, evidenced signals point toward erosion risk if they continue: (1) paying-supplier net additions turned negative in three of the last four disclosed quarters (Q3 FY26 −1,000, Q4 FY26 −1,200, Q1 FY27 −1,850) against an 8% five-year CAGR, tied directly to a Silver-tier price increase and a geopolitical shock [`04_unit-economics.md` §2–§4]; (2) standalone EBITDA margin compressed 284 bps in FY26 as employee/outsourced-sales cost and tech spend outgrew revenue [`07_business-quality.md`, Margin stability row]; and (3) the industry rate-of-change score sits in the Mixed band (45/100) specifically because the buyer-discovery layer (GenAI search, WhatsApp flows, ONDC) is named by management itself as genuinely unsettled [`07_business-quality.md`, Industry rate-of-change row]. None of these yet forces an "eroding" call on the data available, but they are the concrete triggers that would flip this call at the next re-score, and — per CLAUDE.md §24 Filter 5 — durability should be discounted accordingly given the discovery-layer instability sitting on top of an otherwise mature core mechanic.

## 2. Classification

**Capital allocation concerns.** The balance sheet is debt-free with net cash of roughly ₹31bn (FY26), working capital is structurally negative (customers pay upfront, no inventory), dividends are well covered, and the auditor, related-party, and board-process signals are individually clean or only mildly flagged. The one specific, recurring issue with how capital is spent is the pattern of deploying cash into a wide portfolio of small minority stakes in unrelated startups (fleet management, HR SaaS, legal tech, procurement, accounting software) on top of one sizeable control acquisition (Busy Infotech, $66.93mm) — a serial pattern with a mixed track record (one acquired subsidiary, Livekeeping Private Limited, was struck off in October 2024; Ten Times Online was divested in 2023 for a nominal $0.01mm after being bought for a nominal sum in 2020; a ₹23.81mn impairment and a ₹12.04mn diminution provision were both recorded against these investments in FY2025). This is the specific capital-allocation issue, set against otherwise clean governance.

## 3. Most Material Signal

The acquisition pattern is the single signal that would change this classification if it deteriorated further. Today the deals are individually small (mostly single-digit-to-low-double-digit-million-dollar minority checks), entirely self-funded from operating cash flow with zero debt drawn, and the one control deal (Busy Infotech, ~5% of the company's own ~$1.3bn equity value [Public Ownership Summary — market value ₹107,319.4mn at ₹1,784.21/share, Aug-11-2026]) is not large relative to the company's own value — so the acquisition-pattern row is scored 58/100, below the ≥70 threshold that would trigger the rejector-filter cap under CLAUDE.md §24 / Filter 4. If the company began funding deals with debt, wrote off a larger share of the roughly $250–300mm cumulative amount committed to this venture portfolio over ten years, or made a control acquisition priced near or above its own market value, this row would cross into serial-acquirer disqualifier territory and cap the Capital Allocation Score at 50/100 regardless of the otherwise clean balance sheet and governance signals.

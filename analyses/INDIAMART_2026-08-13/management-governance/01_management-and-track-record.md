## 3. Stated Strategy & Targets

IndiaMART's stated strategy is to grow the core marketplace by adding paying suppliers and moving existing suppliers up the Silver→Gold→Platinum tiers (category-based differential pricing), while scaling the Busy Infotech accounting-software subsidiary and deploying free cash flow into a portfolio of minority stakes in adjacent MSME-tech startups [`business-model/02_business-identity.md`, Section 2; `business-model/11_capital-allocation-governance.md`, Section 1]. **The company issues no formal, numeric, company-wide guidance for consolidated revenue, EBITDA, or EPS** — there is no investor-guidance deck in the pool, and quarterly board-outcome press releases carry no forward figures [`earnings/04_guidance-consensus.md`, Section 2]. The only checkable, dated targets in the data pool are qualitative statements made live on earnings calls: management's Q1 FY27 call target for Busy Infotech's revenue CAGR ("closer to anything between… 27% to 30%" over the next couple of years, with a longer 35–40% aspiration explicitly framed as "work in progress") and a target of resolving core-platform silver-tier supplier churn within "2, 3 quarters" [`earnings/04_guidance-consensus.md`, Section 2, citing Q1 FY27 transcript, Jul 21, 2026]. Because there is no formal numeric guidance today, this section's promise-vs-delivery test below relies on the specific, dated, numeric targets management stated live on prior-year calls — which is the correct application of the partial-data rule, since those targets exist and are checkable even though the company does not publish them as formal guidance documents.

On margin, the record is genuinely mixed: FY24 missed the guided range, but FY25 overshot the original 30%+ target by a wide margin — delivered later than promised, and through a different lever (cost control) than the one management had originally described.

**RF-MGT-003 (management changes a KPI after underperformance).** Triggered. Severity: Medium (this is a live-call aspiration repeatedly walked down in real time, not a formal published target quietly buried) — but it is a genuine, dated, quantified pattern, not an impression. Follow-up: does the ~9–10% band represent a durable new steady state, or will it be cut again if net supplier adds do not recover from the current near-zero/negative range (Section 4A)?

## 5. Management Read

The top of this company is as stable as management gets: two co-founders, 27 years in their executive seats, ~49% of the company owned directly since inception — real skin in the game, bought at founding rather than granted, and the one CFO transition in nine years was handled by retaining the outgoing CFO rather than losing him. But the promise-vs-delivery record on the metrics management chooses to talk about publicly is weak: the FY24 margin target was missed, the FY25 revenue-growth aspiration was missed by roughly half even after being downgraded live on the same call it was set, and the core paying-supplier net-add guidance (8,000–9,000/quarter) was abandoned within the same fiscal year it was given, with the CEO declining to offer a replacement number — a pattern that meets the bar for RF-MGT-003 (KPI changed after underperformance), not a one-off miss. Margin performance did eventually catch up and overshoot the original target, which is real credit, but it came a year late and via cost cuts rather than the growth story that was told when the target was set. The single biggest management signal is the combination of an unbroken 27-year founder tenure with zero disclosed succession plan, sitting directly on top of a core marketplace whose own management now openly admits the buyer-side funnel is stagnating — a genuine key-person and execution-quality risk that the record does not yet show this management team has a proven answer for.

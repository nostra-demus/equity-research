## 5. Minority-Shareholder Protection Read

The board clears the mechanical independence tests with room to spare — 62.5% independent, a fully independent audit committee and nomination/remuneration committee, no designated Chairman (so the CEO never chairs board meetings), no staggered board, no dual-class stock, and shareholders have used postal-ballot rights twice in the year under review with results published transparently, including one vote the resolution nearly failed to win comfortably. That machinery is real, not decorative. But two findings temper an otherwise clean read: a sitting Non-Executive Director held a personal paid-services contract with the company for six years running (Dhruv Prakash, ₹47 lakh in FY26 alone, only closed out when he left the board in June 2026), and the board asked shareholders to approve a paid position for a relative of the founder's family (Amit Agarwal) — a resolution that a genuine 21.72% of votes polled opposed, the clearest minority-dissent signal in this year's record. Neither item is large in rupee terms, and neither is undisclosed, but together they show the self-dealing channel that a founder-controlled board with no independent Chairman naturally creates: family and insider appointments still make it onto the agenda, and it took a real minority vote, not board self-policing, to register the objection. **Verdict: adequate** — the structural protections (independence ratios, committee composition, voting rights, absence of anti-takeover devices) are genuinely in place, but the RPT-vote outcome and the now-closed director service-agreement show minority holders had to exercise their rights actively rather than rely on the board alone to screen out insider appointments. This is not "strong protection" (that would require the board itself to have kept the Amit Agarwal appointment off the ballot, or for the vote to have been closer to unanimous), and it is well short of "weak/entrenched" (there is no dual-class stock, no staggered board, no blocked minority voting channel, and the promoter group holds only 49.17% — a minority-plus-institutional bloc of 50.88% retains real voting power if it coordinates). No cross-module `04_ownership-and-insider-behavior` finding of a structurally unaligned controlling owner (RF-OWN-004) exists to test against, since that agent has not yet run in this pipeline order; based on this agent's own read, the controlling group is the two co-founders with real, purchased-at-founding equity, not a government, a value-maximizing parent, or an unrelated conglomerate — so the §24 Filter 6 floor does not apply here.

No interest-rate or commodity-price sensitivity table is disclosed in Item 3 of the Q2 FY26 10-Q (that section covers FX only) — the interest-rate and commodity cost impact above is described qualitatively in the MD&A and earnings call, not quantified in a formal sensitivity table. This is a gap: "Not proven from available data" for a numeric interest-rate or commodity beta on margin.

## 3. Classification

**Partly externally driven.** Tesla is not a pure external-variable pass-through — pricing, vertical integration (in-house battery/cathode/lithium-refining capacity), cost-reduction programs, and product mix are real, demonstrated management levers, and the company explicitly credits these for holding automotive margin "approximately flat" ex one-off items in Q2 FY26 [Q2 FY26 transcript, prepared remarks]. But the exposure is material and only partly hedgeable: regulatory-credit revenue (a nearly pure-margin line) has been cut by government action outside Tesla's control (-67% YoY in Q2 FY26), interest-rate subvention costs move automatically with rate policy, roughly half of revenue sits in unhedged foreign currency, and the newer growth narratives (robotaxi, FSD, Megapack) are each gated by a regulator or a customer's own capex timeline rather than by Tesla's execution alone.

## 4. External Dependency Risk Score

**58/100** (inverted: higher = worse)

Rationale: the automotive core (~86.5% of FY2025 revenue) sits inside a self-acknowledged cyclical, rate-sensitive, policy-sensitive industry, and the regulatory-credit line — which is effectively a government subsidy dressed as revenue — has already been cut by more than half year-on-year under current policy. That combination (consumer-cycle + rate + policy exposure, all in the same segment) pushes the score above the "mixed" midpoint. It stays below the 61+ ("mostly externally driven") band because Tesla retains real, evidenced levers — vertical integration into lithium refining and cell manufacturing, pricing/mix management that held margin flat ex one-offs, and a service/software (FSD subscription) revenue stream that is not commodity- or rate-linked in the same way.

## 5. The Single Biggest Lever

A sustained further rollback of automotive regulatory-credit programs and EV/energy purchase incentives — a policy variable already cutting Q2 FY26 credit revenue by 67% YoY [Q2 FY26 10-Q, Item 2 MD&A] — would do the most damage if it moved another 20% adverse, because that revenue drops to the bottom line at close to 100% margin and the company has no substitute lever to replace it in the short run.

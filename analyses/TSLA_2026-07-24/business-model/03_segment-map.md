# Segment Map — TSLA

## 1. Segment Table

Tesla reports exactly **two** ASC 280 reportable segments: Automotive, and Energy Generation and Storage. "Services and other" is disclosed with its own revenue and cost-of-revenue lines but is folded into the Automotive segment for official segment reporting — it is shown below as a third, informational row, flagged as such, because management and the filings both treat it as an economically distinct, fast-growing business line.

**Note 1 (profit-share basis):** Tesla's segment note discloses **revenue, cost of revenue, and gross profit** by segment only. It does **not** allocate operating expenses (SG&A, R&D), operating income, or net income to either segment. Every "Profit Share" figure above is a gross-profit share, not an operating- or net-income share — flagged so downstream agents (`unit-economics`, `business-quality`) do not conflate the two.

## 2. Dominant Segment

**Automotive is the dominant segment**, by both revenue (86.5% of FY2025 revenue, 88.9% in Q2 FY2026) and gross profit (77.7% of FY2025 gross profit, 86.5% in Q2 FY2026) [`CIQ Financials_Annual export, Segments tab, FY2025`; `FY26 Q2 10-Q, Note 14`]. On a revenue basis alone, Tesla is close to the ">85% from one segment" single-segment threshold, so it is fair to call this an automotive company with a second, smaller energy business — not a diversified conglomerate.

The one trend worth flagging: Energy Generation and Storage's share of **gross profit** has nearly tripled in three years — from 6.5% of total gross profit in FY2023, to 15.1% in FY2024, to 22.2% in FY2025 [`CIQ Financials_Annual export, Segments tab`] — because its GAAP segment gross margin rose from 18.9% to 29.8% over the same period while Automotive's margin fell from 18.2% to 16.2%. Energy is still the smaller segment by a wide margin, but it is doing more of the profit-generating work each year, and it is the higher-margin, higher-quality-trend business of the two on the data disclosed so far.

## 3. Segment Disclosure Quality

Segment definitions have been consistent: the same two reportable segments (Automotive; Energy Generation and Storage) appear in the current `FY26 Q2 10-Q, Note 14` and in the CIQ annual segment history back through FY2017, with no reclassification of the segment structure itself in the periods reviewed. There is no "Other" or "Corporate" bucket — segment revenue and segment gross profit sum exactly to consolidated totals in both the Q2 FY2026 10-Q ($25,097M + $3,139M = $28,236M total revenue; $4,111M + $640M = $4,751M total gross profit) and the FY2025 annual data ($82,056M + $12,771M = $94,827M; $13,292M + $3,802M = $17,094M), so there is no unallocated residual to question. The bigger gap is depth, not consistency: Tesla discloses segment **revenue, cost of revenue, and gross profit only** — operating expenses (SG&A, R&D), operating income, and net income are never allocated to either segment, so no one outside the company can compute a true segment-level operating margin or return on capital; every profit-share figure in this report is explicitly a gross-profit share. A related gap is the sub-line "Services and other," which is economically distinct (record profitability, 46–50% YoY revenue growth in H1 FY2026) but reported only inside the Automotive segment, with no full-year cost-of-revenue breakout available in this pool — only single-quarter figures can be computed. Finally, the fast-growing forward bets Tesla talks about heavily in its shareholder letters — Robotaxi, Optimus, Cybercab, in-house AI inference chips — have **no separate segment or revenue-line disclosure at all**; whatever capital and operating expense they consume is invisible inside the Automotive segment's numbers, which is a real transparency gap given how much narrative weight management puts on these programs.

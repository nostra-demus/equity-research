# Business Model Reality Check — TSLA (Synthesis)

## Abstract

Tesla designs, builds, and sells electric vehicles directly to consumers, with a smaller battery-storage business and an unmonetized bet on self-driving software and robots bolted on. Vehicle sales dominate how it makes money, at 86.5% of FY2025 revenue and 77.7% of gross profit [CIQ Financials_Annual export, Segments tab, FY2025]. Its strongest positive is a $27.4 billion net-cash balance sheet (Jun-2026) funding continued vertical integration into battery cells and lithium refining [Capital IQ export, Capital Structure Summary tab]. Its strongest negative is that return on capital, at 2.75%–3.7% (LTM), sits roughly 770–885 basis points below an estimated 11.5% cost of capital, with margins compressing for three straight years and no proven moat. No disqualifier triggered, but persistent governance red flags surround CEO pay and related-party dealings. Verdict: a cyclical, technology-cycle bet, not a proven durable compounder — worth deeper work only with a strong timing edge.

**Module Disconfirmation (CLAUDE.md §8; fix F37).**

- **Single killer risk:** A court ruling that allows the federal securities-fraud class action (Autopilot/FSD/Robotaxi misrepresentation claims, motion to dismiss pending) to proceed to discovery would land directly on the unmonetized technology narrative the market's valuation appears to depend on, at the same time capex keeps running 1.4x–2.2x depreciation into that same bet while core automotive margin keeps compressing [`12_red-flags-sweep.md` §2–3; `11_capital-allocation-governance.md` §1].
- **Disconfirming evidence already visible:** the Q1–Q2 FY2026 automotive revenue rebound and the Energy segment's improving profit-share run counter to a simple "structural, monotonic decline" bear case — the multi-year trend confirms erosion, but the most recent two quarters do not confirm it in isolation [`08_competitive-map.md` §3].

## 4. Note To The Final Synthesizer

- Strongest business-model positive: a $27.4bn net-cash position (Jun-2026), funding real vertical integration (in-house lithium refining, cathode material, battery-cell production) and a ~55% North America FSD-subscription attach rate — real assets, but ones that have not yet lifted return on capital above cost of capital [`11_capital-allocation-governance.md` §1; `06_value-chain.md` §1; `09_moat.md` §2].
- Strongest business-model negative: operating margin has fallen every year for three straight years (16.8% FY2022 → 4.1% LTM) and return on capital has collapsed from an 18.6% cyclical peak (FY2022) to 2.75% (LTM), against an estimated ~11.5% cost of capital [`07_business-quality.md` §1; `09_moat.md` §3–5].
- Most important segment: Automotive (86.5% of FY2025 revenue, 77.7% of gross profit) — but Energy Generation and Storage is the higher-margin, faster-improving segment on a trend basis (gross-profit share 6.5% → 22.2% in three years) [`03_segment-map.md` §2].
- Cleanest unit-economics read: gross margin per vehicle only (~$6,639/vehicle, 15.7% of ASP, Q2 FY2026) — a true fully-loaded, per-vehicle economic-profit figure cannot be derived because Tesla never allocates R&D or SG&A by segment or by unit [`04_unit-economics.md` §2–3].
- Where the company sits vs named peers: highest gross margin among the three named comparables (18.9% vs Ford 7.1%, GM 10.2%), but its EBIT margin (4.1% LTM) now sits below GM's (5.5%) despite GM's far lower gross margin and far higher leverage (67% debt/capital vs Tesla's 15.5%); BYD, the actual global BEV volume leader, cannot be compared on margin or ROIC because it appears nowhere in Tesla's own filings in this pool [`08_competitive-map.md` §2; `09_moat.md` §4].
- Main external dependency: regulatory-credit rollback (-67% YoY, near-100%-margin revenue) plus unhedged FX exposure on roughly half of revenue and interest-rate-linked subvention costs that move automatically against margin [`10_external-dependency.md` §1, §4–5].
- Most important capital allocation or governance signal: the $132.3bn maximum-value, 423.7M-share CEO performance award layered on top of a 56% cumulative share-count rise since 2017 with zero buybacks — the signal most likely to worsen the governance classification further if additional tranches vest or a follow-on award is granted before this one is earned [`11_capital-allocation-governance.md` §3].
- Whether any automatic disqualifier triggered: No. All eight disqualifier tests read N, though the CEO share-pledging ratio carries a flagged, unresolved denominator ambiguity (28.9% vs. 50.2%) [`01_disqualifier-scan.md`].
- Which rejector filters tripped and the cap applied: Filter 5 (fast-changing industry) tripped — RF-BQ-005 propagated, Business quality capped at 65 (non-binding, actual score 33). ## 5. Simple Summary

- Tesla designs, builds, and sells electric vehicles direct to consumers, plus a smaller grid-battery business and an unmonetized bet on self-driving software and humanoid robots.
- It makes money mainly from `vehicles delivered × average selling price`, with a shrinking near-pure-margin government regulatory-credit line and a fast-growing usage-based services tail (used cars, Supercharging, paid FSD subscriptions).
- Each vehicle currently sells for about $6,639 more than it costs to build (Q2 FY2026), so units are not sold below cost — but no fully-loaded, unit-level economic-profit figure can be computed because R&D and SG&A are never allocated by segment or by vehicle.
- Automotive is by far the most important segment (86.5% of FY2025 revenue), though the smaller Energy segment is improving faster and now contributes 22.2% of gross profit, up from 6.5% three years ago.
- No moat is proven: Technology/IP is the strongest individual candidate (50/100) but return on capital (2.75%–3.7% LTM) sits well below an estimated ~11.5% cost of capital, and the gap has widened every year since FY2022.
- The business depends heavily on things it does not control — US regulatory-credit policy, unhedged foreign-currency swings on about half its revenue, and interest rates that move financing-subsidy costs automatically against margin.
- Capital allocation carries clean balance-sheet hygiene ($27.4bn net cash, near-zero acquisitions) alongside real governance red flags — a $132.3bn maximum CEO pay award, a 56% cumulative rise in share count with zero buybacks, and an unresolved securities-fraud class action tied to the company's core growth narrative.
- It deserves deeper work only with a strong timing edge — this is a cyclical, technology-cycle bet on EV competition and an unproven autonomous-driving story, not a durable compounder in its current, disclosed form.

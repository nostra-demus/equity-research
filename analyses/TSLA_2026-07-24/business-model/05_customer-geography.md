# Customer And Geography Map — TSLA

## 1. Customer Map

Tesla does not disclose named, revenue-percentage customer concentration in the filings available in the data pool. Its automotive and services revenue is generated from a large, diffuse base of individual retail vehicle buyers and individual/commercial energy-storage buyers — not from a small number of large institutional accounts. The clearest concentration-relevant disclosure available is a receivables concentration statement, which stands in for a customer-concentration test because Tesla does not publish a percent-of-revenue by customer table.

| Customer Type | Importance (% of revenue if disclosed) | Long-term Contract? (Y/N/Not disclosed) | Evidence | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Individual retail vehicle buyers (Automotive segment) | Majority of revenue — Automotive segment revenue was $69,526M of $94,827M total revenue in FY2025 (73.3%) | N — one-off retail purchase/lease, not a standing contract | [CIQ Financials_Annual export, Segments sheet, FY2025 (as filed FY25 10-K data)] | Low single-customer risk given diffusion; high sensitivity to aggregate consumer demand, financing rates and incentive policy (demand risk, not concentration risk) |
| Other automobile manufacturers (buyers of automotive regulatory credits) | Small — regulatory credits revenue was $146M in Q2 2026, down 67% y/y, versus $28,236M total Q2 2026 revenue (~0.5%) | N — credit sales are transactional and policy-dependent, not long-term contracts | [FY2025 10-Q (filed Jul-23-2026), MD&A, p. re: Automotive regulatory credits] | Revenue line is shrinking and policy-dependent: "Recent governmental and regulatory actions have restricted certain regulatory credit programs tied to our products" [FY2025 10-Q, MD&A] |
| Commercial / utility / data-center energy-storage customers (Energy Generation and Storage segment) | Energy segment revenue was $12,771M in FY2025 (13.5% of total) and $3,139M in Q2 2026; management describes deployments as "lumpy" and "tied to customer time lines" | Not disclosed at the individual-customer level; some framework agreements disclosed (e.g., SPIE multi-year BESS framework agreement across France/Belgium/Netherlands, extendable to Poland/Germany) | [Key Developments, Client Announcement, 2025-12-15]; [Q2 2026 earnings call, Jul-22-2026, CFO prepared remarks] | Segment-level lumpiness tied to a handful of large individual deployments (utility/data-center scale) is a real risk even though no single customer's revenue share is disclosed |
| Named individual large counterparties in CIQ relationship data (banks, auto retailers, energy developers, SpaceX/X.AI, Rivian, etc.) | Not disclosed as % of revenue — these are relationship-type records (borrower, lessee, distributor, licensee), not revenue-ranked customers | Not disclosed | [CIQ Customers export, Tesla Inc NasdaqGS TSLA Customers.xls] | None quantifiable; useful only as a qualitative list of counterparties, not a concentration signal |

Not proven from available data: a named "Customer A accounts for X%" disclosure. Tesla's business model (millions of individual vehicle and product buyers) makes single-customer concentration structurally unlikely, and the only quantitative concentration test the filings provide (receivables, not revenue) confirms this — see Section 3.

## 2. Geography Map

Tesla reports revenue by three geographic buckets: United States, China, and Other International (Europe, rest of Asia-Pacific, and rest of world combined) [FY2025 10-Q (filed Jul-23-2026), Note 14 — Segment Reporting and Information about Geographic Areas; CIQ Financials_Annual export, Segments sheet].

| Geography | % of Revenue | Trend (Growing / Stable / Declining / Unknown) | Evidence | Risk |
|---|---:|---|---|---|
| United States | 50.2% (FY2025: $47,627M of $94,827M); 47.2% in H1 2026 ($23,885M of $50,623M) | Growing on a full-year basis (48.8% in FY2024 → 50.2% in FY2025); roughly stable in H1 2026 vs FY2025 | [CIQ Financials_Annual export, Segments sheet, FY2024–FY2025]; [FY2025 10-Q, Note 14, six months ended Jun-30-2026 vs Jun-30-2025] | Largest single-country exposure; sensitive to US interest rates (loan/lease subvention costs), US vehicle-emission-credit policy, and US import tariffs |
| China | 22.1% (FY2025: $20,962M of $94,827M); 17.5% in H1 2026 ($8,859M of $50,623M) | Declining share (21.4% FY2024 → 22.1% FY2025 → 17.5% H1 2026) | [CIQ Financials_Annual export, Segments sheet]; [FY2025 10-Q, Note 14] | Geopolitical, tariff, and local-competition risk (BYD and other domestic EV makers); China is also a manufacturing/export base (Gigafactory Shanghai), so the country carries both revenue and asset exposure |
| Other International (Europe, APAC ex-China, rest of world) | 27.7% (FY2025: $26,238M of $94,827M); 35.3% in H1 2026 ($17,879M of $50,623M) | Growing sharply in H1 2026 (27.7% FY2025 → 35.3% H1 2026); management cited sequential Q2-2026 delivery growth of 27% (APAC) and 12% (EMEA) | [CIQ Financials_Annual export, Segments sheet]; [FY2025 10-Q, Note 14]; [Q2 2026 earnings call, Jul-22-2026, CFO prepared remarks] | Aggregated bucket masks country-level concentration within Europe/RoW — filings do not break this out further |

Long-lived assets follow a similar US-weighted pattern: United States $35,847M at Dec-31-2025 versus Other International $9,400M (a roughly 79%/21% split); at Jun-30-2026, United States $42,213M versus Other International $9,552M (about 82%/18%) [FY2025 10-Q, Note 14]. Production and physical-asset concentration in the US is even higher than the revenue split, which increases the exposure of a US-specific shock (tariff, regulation, natural disaster) relative to a pure revenue read.

Geographic shares sum to ~100% in each period shown; "Other International" is a single combined bucket that the filings do not split further by country.

## 3. Concentration Flags

| Concentration Flag | Triggered (Y/N) | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| One customer >20% of revenue | N | No customer is named with a revenue percentage in the filings. The closest available quantitative test — accounts-receivable concentration — shows: "As of June 30, 2026 and December 31, 2025, no entity represented 10% or more of our total receivables balance." [FY2025 10-Q, Note — Concentration of Risk]. This is a receivables test, not a revenue test, but it is the only concentration figure Tesla discloses and it is well under 20%. |
| Top 3 customers >40% of revenue | N | Not disclosed, and structurally implausible given a retail-diffuse buyer base across automotive and most of energy storage; no evidence of institutional buyer concentration at this scale. |
| One geography >50% of revenue | Y | United States was 50.2% of FY2025 revenue ($47,627M of $94,827M) [CIQ Financials_Annual export, Segments sheet, FY2025]. On a trailing-six-month basis (H1 2026) the US share was 47.2%, just under the 50% line [FY2025 10-Q, Note 14], so the trigger is annual-period-dependent and sits right at the threshold rather than deep past it. |
| One customer or geography >30% with no long-term contract disclosed | Y | United States revenue (>30% in every period shown) is generated through one-off retail vehicle sales and services, not long-term supply or offtake contracts [FY2025 10-Q, Note 14; CIQ Financials_Annual export, Segments sheet]. This reflects organic consumer-demand dependency on the home market, not a counterparty-contract risk — there is no single anchor customer behind the US number. |

## 4. Read

Tesla's concentration risk sits almost entirely on the geography side, not the customer side: the filings show no entity holding 10% or more of receivables [FY2025 10-Q, Note — Concentration of Risk], and the business model (millions of individual buyers) makes customer concentration structurally unlikely. Geographically, the United States crossed 50% of FY2025 revenue [CIQ Financials_Annual export, Segments sheet] and carries an even larger share of long-lived assets (~79–82%) [FY2025 10-Q, Note 14], while China's revenue share fell from about 22% (FY2025) to about 17.5% (H1 2026) as "Other International" (Europe/APAC ex-China/RoW) grew to over a third of revenue [FY2025 10-Q, Note 14; Q2 2026 earnings call]. None of this geographic revenue is secured by long-term contracts — it is organic, demand-driven, and swings with regional deliveries, incentives, and tariffs each quarter. The single biggest dependency the synthesizer should carry forward: Tesla now earns roughly half its revenue and over three-quarters of its long-lived assets in one country (the United States), with no contractual protection against a US-specific demand, tariff, or policy shock.

## What This Module Found

Tesla is barely borrowed on any normal measure. Net debt — total debt minus cash, the strict basis — is just $861 million against $10,849 million of yearly EBITDA (earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortization, a rough proxy for operating cash profit). That is a leverage ratio of 0.08x. On broader but still-standard readings the company is net cash by $5,877 million to $34,182 million (it holds more cash and short-term investments than it owes) [`01`, §4].

The real risk is not the size of the debt but its timing. On a true-date (contractual) basis, 78.2% of the debt ($7,306 million) comes due within 12 months, and almost all of that is one $5,888 million China working-capital loan (money borrowed to fund day-to-day operations in China). That loan is booked on the balance sheet as long-term based on management's stated intent and ability to refinance it — an accounting judgment, not a signed replacement deal [`02`, §1a–§1c]. That promise, not a shortage of cash, is the single thing to watch.

The cushion against that wall is large. Usable liquidity of $48,238 million (cash, unrestricted short-term investments, and an undrawn committed revolver — a pre-agreed credit line) covers the 12-month contractual wall about 6.6 times over, and gives a runway of about 28 months even without counting any operating cash flow [`03`, §3]. The stress test shows no covenant breach and no cash shortfall until EBITDA falls 92–99% — essentially total loss of operating earnings. Tesla has already lived through a comparable 39.1% peak-to-trough EBITDA drop (FY2022→FY2025) with no balance-sheet strain [`06`, §2–§3].

The single biggest solvency risk: that China facility is 63% of total gross debt on the narrow basis, is unhedged, sits in a different market from Tesla's US-dollar cash, and has no signed replacement on file [`01`, §7].

## The Specialists, Briefly

- **Data triage:** Sufficient — recent balance sheet, instrument-level debt note, and cash flow statement all present; the one real gap is no quantified covenant threshold.
- **Capital structure & leverage:** Near-zero leverage on every basis; canonical net debt $861M / EBITDA 0.08x. About 99.98% of on-balance-sheet debt is non-recourse to the parent (issued by subsidiary vehicles or the offshore China facility).
- **Maturity wall & refinancing:** "Refinanceable in most markets" — not self-funded. GAAP shows 15.2% due within 12 months, but the true-date figure is 78.2%.
- **Liquidity runway:** About 28 months on the conservative basis; does not depend on free cash flow showing up.
- **Coverage & covenants:** Interest coverage is extreme (32.48x) but reflects a tiny debt base, not proven resilience; covenant headroom not assessable.
- **Off-balance-sheet & contingencies:** Largest quantified exposure is a $4.07B resale-value guarantee cap (5.07% of equity — below the spike trigger). Five active litigation/regulatory matters carry no disclosed dollar figure.
- **Downside stress test:** Survives every haircut modeled (−30%/−40%/−60% and the −39.1% historical) with a >$26B liquidity surplus and no breach.

**Disagreement resolved by the synthesis:** the maturity wall reads as 15.2% due within a year on the GAAP balance sheet but 78.2% on a true contractual-date basis. The synthesis leads with the contractual 78.2% as the operative number, because the long-term booking of the China loan rests on an accounting judgment, not a signed agreement (conservative-default rule). No numerical disagreements between specialists were left unresolved.

## What Would Change This Read

**Would strengthen it:**
- A signed, disclosed replacement facility or bond issue for the China loan ahead of its Sep-2026–Mar-2027 maturity.
- A disclosed covenant threshold showing genuinely wide numeric headroom.
- The five open litigation matters resolved or quantified at immaterial amounts.

**Would weaken it:**
- A failed or materially more expensive re-draw of the China facility.
- Chinese bank credit and US capital markets tightening at the same time (the joint-closure scenario the pool does not model).
- A real covenant surfacing that is tighter than the labeled assumption used here.
- A large unquantified litigation loss crystallizing.

**Data needed:** the credit agreement/indenture text (actual covenant thresholds), the full FY2025 10-K commitments/contingencies note, and any signed refinancing agreement for the China facility.

---

## Bottom Line

- Verdict is **Solid, not Fortress** — leverage is near-zero (net debt $861M / EBITDA 0.08x) and the company is net cash on every broader reading, but the wall and disclosure gaps are real.
- Biggest reason it could be **better than it looks:** liquidity covers the 12-month contractual wall ~6.6x without needing any operating cash flow, and the China loan has already been expanded and re-drawn three times in 18 months ($2,740M → $4,288M → $5,888M).
- Biggest reason it could be **worse than it looks:** 78% of debt is contractually due within a year in one unhedged China loan with no signed replacement — a management refinancing promise, not a deal.
- **Missing evidence:** no disclosed covenant threshold (only "in material compliance"); no full FY2025 10-K contingency notes; a single stale credit rating (S&P BBB/Stable, action dated Oct-2022). Both caps reflect disclosure gaps, not evidence of actual fragility.
- **One thing to watch next:** whether Tesla discloses a signed refinancing or replacement for the $5,888M China facility before its Sep-2026–Mar-2027 maturity.

## Plain-English Glossary

- **EBITDA** — earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortization; a rough proxy for operating cash profit.
- **Net debt (strict basis)** — total debt minus cash; here $861M.
- **Net cash** — holding more cash and investments than debt owed.
- **Contractual (true-date) maturity** — when a loan actually falls due by its terms, regardless of how it is classified on the balance sheet.
- **Committed revolver (RCF)** — a pre-agreed credit line the company can draw on; here undrawn.
- **Liquidity runway** — how many months the company can meet obligations from cash and committed funding without operating cash flow.
- **Covenant** — a condition in a loan agreement the borrower must meet; Tesla's thresholds are undisclosed.
- **Covenant headroom** — the distance between current numbers and the level that would break a covenant; here "Not assessable" because no threshold is disclosed.
- **Non-recourse debt** — debt lenders can claim only against specific assets, not the parent's general assets.
- **Free cash flow** — operating cash flow minus capital spending; flagged unreliable here against the guided capex ramp.

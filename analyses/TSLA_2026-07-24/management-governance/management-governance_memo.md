## What This Module Found

The core read: this management team has not turned invested money into value per share, and the board has narrowed the tools minority shareholders could use to push back. From FY2021 to FY2025, invested capital (the money tied up in the business) grew 141% while operating profit (EBIT) fell 33% and diluted per-share earnings fell 70% from their FY2022 peak [`02_capital-allocation-scorecard.md` §5]. The company bought back zero shares in any year since at least FY2017 even though it generated positive free cash flow (cash left after running and investing in the business) every year, and the share count rose 27.4% [RF-CAP-002].

The single most important driver is the entrenchment sequence: the board reincorporated the company from Delaware to Texas (2024), adopted a rule that you must own 3% of shares to bring a shareholder lawsuit (2025), kept a classified board (directors elected in staggered groups, hard to replace at once), and holds a 66⅔%-of-shares supermajority bylaw that already blocked a majority-backed 2024 vote to unstagger the board — each step timed around the multi-year court fight over the CEO's own pay [`05_board-and-shareholder-rights.md` §4, §4A]. Two of these facts (the supermajority bylaw and the 3% suit threshold) rest on dated web sources, not a primary filing in the pool, which is why confidence is held at 78.

The single most important risk is an unresolved federal securities-fraud class action naming Tesla and the CEO personally over Autopilot/FSD/Robotaxi claims, with a motion to dismiss pending — on the exact autonomy subject where this module independently found two dated 2025 promises were missed [RF-MGT-005]. It is unproven, but if it proceeds it is the fact pattern most capable of breaking the trust the autonomy-led bull case rests on.

There are real counterweights. The CEO took $0 cash and $0 realized pay in 2025 despite a $158.4bn accounting headline, holds a large 20.3–29.9% stake, and Tesla avoided the serial-acquirer trap with near-zero M&A [`03_incentives-and-compensation.md` §4; `02` §2]. But the stake is mostly award-derived rather than bought, and no CEO pay milestone uses a per-share or return-on-capital target.

## What Would Change This Read

**Would strengthen the verdict:** dismissal of the securities-fraud class action on the merits; a board-initiated reversal of the 3%-derivative-suit threshold and/or the supermajority bylaw; a per-share or ROIC metric added to a future CEO/NEO award; a return to positive incremental ROIC with EPS growth; a buyback funded from the existing net-cash cushion.

**Would weaken it:** an adverse ruling or settlement in the securities-fraud case; a further mega-grant to the CEO before the 2025 award is even partly earned; a widening GAAP-to-Adjusted-EBITDA gap; more capital committed to CEO-controlled related parties without an independent return hurdle.

**Data needed:** the current bylaws exhibit (to confirm the supermajority and 3%-threshold provisions); the original FY2025 10-K auditor's opinion letter; a tabulated AGM vote count; the FY2026 actual capex/EBIT outcome against the reaffirmed >$25bn capex floor.

## Bottom Line

- **Verdict: Serious governance concerns**, Governance Rating Weak (43/100). No hard disqualifier, but 2 Critical red flags and a §24 integrity filter tripped, capping the overall investment rating at "Watchlist."
- **Could be better than it looks:** pay is genuinely at-risk ($0 cash, $0 realized in 2025), the CEO owns 20.3–29.9%, the company avoided value-destroying M&A, and the CFO discloses bad news candidly — the form of alignment is present.
- **Could be worse than it looks:** the entrenchment sequence and the unresolved fraud suit both concentrate around defending the CEO's position and pay, and no counterweight in the record is strong enough to offset that pattern.
- **Missing evidence:** the two most consequential entrenchment facts (66⅔% supermajority bylaw, 3% suit threshold) rest on dated web sources, not a primary filing — the single highest-value next request is the current bylaws exhibit.
- **Watch next:** the ruling on the securities-fraud motion to dismiss, and whether FY2026 commentary explicitly reconciles the two missed 2025 promises.

## Plain-English Glossary

- **EBIT (operating profit):** what the business earns from operations before interest and tax.
- **Diluted EPS:** per-share earnings counting all shares that could exist if options convert.
- **Free cash flow (FCF):** cash left after running the business and paying for capital spending.
- **Invested capital:** the total money tied up in running the business.
- **Incremental ROIC:** the profit earned on each extra dollar invested; negative here means new money lost value.
- **Buyback:** the company using cash to repurchase its own shares, lifting per-share value.
- **Classified/staggered board:** directors elected in separate groups over several years, so they cannot all be replaced at one meeting.
- **Supermajority bylaw:** a rule requiring far more than a simple majority (here 66⅔% of all shares) to change certain provisions.
- **Related-party transaction:** a deal between the company and an entity its own insiders control.
- **GAAP-to-Adjusted-EBITDA gap:** the distance between official accounting profit and the company's own adjusted profit measure; a wide gap flags aggressive add-backs.
- **Net-cash cushion:** cash on hand above total debt, available to fund buybacks or counter-cyclical moves.

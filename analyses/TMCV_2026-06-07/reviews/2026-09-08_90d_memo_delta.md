## 3. Did the original thesis play out?
The original call was Watchlist, capped by two Critical/High red flags (RF-CAP-004 serial-acquirer pattern; TMF Holdings related-party overhang), with a cautious base case (-4.4% probability-weighted expected return, risk/reward -0.23x). Neither named binary risk has resolved. Price has moved sharply against the base case's implied direction (+26% vs. an expected roughly flat-to-down 12-month path), driven by an earnings beat and rising EPS consensus rather than resolution of the governance or leverage risk. This is not a clean "right for the wrong reason" (luck) or "wrong despite good process" case — the specific risks the cap actually rests on are still open, so `decision_quality` is again marked **not assessable**, though the case for revisiting that call strengthens as the price gap widens without resolution.

## 6. Stage-One sheet comment
TMCV is up about 26% since our June Watchlist call, roughly 25 points ahead of the Nifty 50 and 18 points ahead of the auto sector index. That is a big gap and it has grown since the 30-day check-in (then about 14 points). None of it comes from the two things we said would move our rating: the Iveco Group deal is still not closed (the cash tender offer just opened September 7 and is expected to finish in early November, later than our original window), and we still cannot confirm from public sources how the TMF Holdings-related shareholder vote actually went. The rally instead reflects a strong quarterly profit beat (net profit up 83%) and analysts raising, not cutting, next year's earnings estimate — the opposite of what our forecast ledger predicted on that specific point. One piece of good news: the related-party investment loss we worried about did not repeat this quarter; it was a gain instead. We are not chasing the rally and not changing the rating. The real test is the Iveco deal's actual financing mix when it closes, expected before our next checkpoint.

## 3. Disclosure Transparency

Burlington reports one consolidated operating segment, so it cannot provide separate segment economics; its FY2025 10-K nevertheless identifies net income, Adjusted Net Income, Adjusted EBITDA, Adjusted EBIT, comparable-store sales, gross margin, inventory and liquidity as key measures, and the Q2 call gave the tariff-refund amount, reinvestment timing, inventory mix, store count and operating drivers. [S02: FY2025 Form 10-K, Note 2—Segment Information; Results of Operations] [S05: Q2 FY2026 earnings call, prepared remarks]

The same principal KPI set is visible across FY2022–FY2025 annual disclosures and the Q1/Q2 FY2026 calls, and no KPI was identified as redefined or dropped after deterioration. The evidence does not demonstrate the five full years needed for the A7-03 Green band, and category- or region-level profitability is not disclosed; the latter is a limitation of a one-segment issuer, not evidence of a transparency reduction. [S06: FY2024 Annual Report, MD&A—Key performance and non-GAAP measures] [S02: FY2025 Form 10-K, Results of Operations] [S04: Q1 FY2026 earnings call, prepared remarks] [S05: Q2 FY2026 earnings call, prepared remarks and Q&A]

## 4. Tone in Bad Times

The two supplied verbatim calls cover quarters that beat guidance; the closest recent stress test is the Q3 FY2026 guide, where management set out the lower $1.60–$1.70 Adjusted EPS range, the 60–80bp operating-margin decline, the 40%/60% tariff-refund reinvestment split, and the weather and consumer risks rather than calling the impact immaterial. [S03: Q2 FY2026 earnings release, Outlook] [S05: Q2 FY2026 earnings call, prepared remarks]

In Q&A, management answered directly on tariff-refund uncertainty, cannibalisation, weather exposure, consumer pressure and the trade-off between sales and margin. The language is promotional in a good quarter (for example, calling underlying momentum “extremely robust”), but the numerical disclosures and direct risk answers mean no evasiveness was identified; a real delivered bad-quarter transcript is not in the admitted record. [S04: Q1 FY2026 earnings call, Q&A] [S05: Q2 FY2026 earnings call, Q&A]

## 5. Candor Read

**Mixed.** Management was specific about the $55m tariff refund, removed it from its underlying-Q2 presentation, and explained why it lowered Q3 margin and EPS while keeping the full-year effect neutral. The counterweight is recurring exclusions labelled non-GAAP adjustments and no actual management miss or bad-result call in the available sample, so the evidence does not support a candid / high-trust conclusion.

The single biggest candor signal is the explicit treatment of the tariff refund: $55m received in Q2, about 40% to be reinvested in Q3 and 60% in Q4, with a stated neutral full-year impact. The recurring lease-cost, impairment and litigation exclusions are a medium-severity caution, not evidence that the Q2 result was concealed.

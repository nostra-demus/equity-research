## 6. Seasonality Read

Seasonality is mildly helpful for judging Q3 but makes Q4 more important. Q3 represented an average 23.6% of annual revenue in FY2023–FY2025, versus 31.5% in Q4; therefore, the Q3 print can test whether price investment and inventory flow are working, but it will not settle the much larger holiday-quarter result. The company also calls the Fall transition important, with weather a direct demand and markdown risk. [data/BURL/Burlington Stores Inc NYSE BURL Financials_Quarterly.xls, Income Statement, FY2023–FY2025 quarterly columns; data/BURL/Burlington Stores, Inc., Q2 2027 Earnings Call, Q&A, 2026-08-27]

## 7. Historical Pattern

The vendor's last four reported quarters show normalized EPS beats in all four, with a +13.1% average and +7.3% median surprise, but revenue was two beats and two misses, with only a +0.3% average surprise. This is judgment informed by four observations, not an empirical probability: it should receive limited weight, especially because the latest Q2 results included a $0.64-per-share tariff refund that management will reinvest rather than retain. [data/BURL/BurlingtonStores,IncNYSEBURLEstimatesReport.xls, Surprise sheet, CIQ FQ3 2026–FQ2 2027; data/BURL/Burlington Stores, Inc., Q2 2027 Earnings Call, prepared remarks, 2026-08-27]

## 8. Setup Verdict

**Setup is balanced.** The most important factor is whether mature-store demand responds to the Q3 value investment: new stores supply clear capacity, but Q2 comparable-store growth was only +2% and transactions were relatively flat. The biggest risk that could flip the setup is weather-driven weak Fall traffic, which would turn the planned price investment and higher inventory into a margin cost rather than a sales benefit. [data/BURL/Burlington_Stores_Inc_-_Form_10-Q(Aug-27-2026).doc, MD&A — Results of Operations and Inventory, filed 2026-08-27; data/BURL/Burlington Stores, Inc., Q2 2027 Earnings Call, prepared remarks and Q&A, 2026-08-27]

## 9. Second-Quarter Look-Ahead

The Q4 FY2026 setup is more consequential because Q4 has averaged 31.5% of annual revenue, compared with 23.6% for Q3. It also carries about $33m of the planned tariff-refund price investment and a guided 40–60 bps operating-margin decline, versus Q3's 60–80 bps decline; a Q3 beat without a credible Q4 sales and margin outlook would therefore be incomplete. [data/BURL/Burlington Stores Inc NYSE BURL Financials_Quarterly.xls, Income Statement, FY2023–FY2025 quarterly columns; data/BURL/Burlington Stores, Inc., Q2 2027 Earnings Call, prepared remarks, 2026-08-27]

## 10. Pre-Mortem

If this setup fails, the most likely error is giving too much weight to Q2's new-store growth and refund-aided earnings while assuming that lower prices will revive mature-store traffic. The evidence does not quantify the demand response to the price investment or the weather sensitivity, so a warm Fall and weak transactions could produce a sales-plus-margin miss even while total stores keep growing.

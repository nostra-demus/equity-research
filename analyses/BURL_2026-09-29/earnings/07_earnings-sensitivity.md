# Earnings Sensitivity — BURL

All figures are USD. Burlington reports under U.S. GAAP; “adjusted EBITDA” and “adjusted EBIT” are company-defined non-GAAP measures. The quantified operating rows below use a Q3 FY2026 13-week revenue midpoint of $2,981.5m, unless stated otherwise. They are sensitivities, not forecasts. A 1-basis-point move on that revenue base is about $0.298m. The company has not disclosed sales, weather, freight, merchandise-margin, or SG&A sensitivity coefficients; those rows use the disclosed Q2 observations as a historical range and are therefore not company guidance.

## 1. Variable Selection

The six variables come from the high/mid-magnitude revenue and margin driver tables: merchandise margin, SG&A leverage, comparable-store sales, store rollout, and the planned tariff-refund price investment. Interest rates are included because they are the only variable with a company-disclosed market-risk coefficient. The business-model dependency review also flags consumer spending, weather and tariff policy; consumer demand and weather feed the comparable-sales and markdown rows rather than being treated as independently additive variables. ## 4. The Single Highest-Sensitivity Variable

Merchandise margin and markdown execution is the largest supportable two-sided earnings sensitivity: a repeat or reversal of the 70bp Q2 underlying contribution changes a Q3-sales-midpoint adjusted-EBITDA read by about $20.9m before tax. It is partly company-controlled through buying, pricing and markdown decisions, but depends on the availability and mix of off-price merchandise. The adverse case needs weaker demand or less favourable goods availability to force more markdowns or remove the recent margin gain; neither the filing nor the call quantifies that probability or a recovery rate. ## 5. Interaction Effects

The variables are linked. Warm Fall weather or lower-income consumer pressure can reduce traffic and comparable sales, leave more inventory for markdowns, and remove SG&A leverage at the same time. Conversely, the planned Q3/Q4 price investment may support customer value and comparable sales while deliberately reducing margin. Store growth adds sales capacity and may improve fixed-cost absorption, but it also raises depreciation from stores and supply-chain infrastructure. Interest rates are separate from EBITDA but can worsen household budgets and the small direct cash-interest exposure together. These links mean the table is not additive. ## 6. Non-Linear Or Asymmetric Risks

The adverse demand case is likely more severe than the static comparable-sales calculation: lower traffic can create both missed sales and higher markdowns while store and infrastructure costs do not fall proportionally. Burlington states it may not be able to offset a comparable-sales decline with savings initiatives. Weather can compound this risk because cold-weather apparel demand and store traffic remain partly weather-dependent, while inventory was $1,541.3m at Q2 end and comparable-store inventory was up 11% year on year. The tariff item is also asymmetric: the $55.5m benefit was a recognised Q2 event, while management plans to give it back through customer value; the available pool does not establish a repeat refund or the current tariff terms. ## 7. Earnings Volatility Score

**58 /100 — higher = worse.** Earnings have material sensitivity to merchandise margin, demand and operating leverage, and Q2 includes a non-recurring tariff refund; however, the disclosed interest-rate coefficient is modest and flexible sourcing, swaps and price/value actions provide partial but unmeasured offsets. The absence of company-disclosed demand, weather and merchandise-cost coefficients limits confidence in any more precise volatility score.

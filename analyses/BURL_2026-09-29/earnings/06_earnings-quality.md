The TTM reported FCF includes a $55.5m tariff-refund benefit to Q2 FY2026 operating cash flow. On the disclosed assumption that the full refund was cash received in the period, normalised operating FCF is about $357.0m ($412.5m − $55.5m), versus reported $412.5m. The filing says operating cash flow benefited by $55.5m but does not separately present the refund’s cash timing, so $357.0m is a conservative normalisation, not a separately reported measure. [Q2 FY2026 Form 10-Q, p.20 and MD&A Liquidity and Capital Resources, p.30]

## 2. Cash Conversion Assessment

CFO tracked the CIQ-standard EBITDA well: conversion was 91.6%, 102.5%, 82.4% and 99.2% in FY2022–FY2025, respectively. It was above 70% in every year and below 50% in none, so the record does not show a cash-conversion breakdown. [CIQ Financials Annual workbook, Cash Flow and Income Statement, annual columns; FY2025 Form 10-K, pp.45–46]

The FY2024 dip to 82.4% coincided with a $126.1m working-capital cash use and $880.4m of capex, rather than with a collapse in CFO. FY2025 CFO recovered to $1,231.4m, but FCF remained only $171.6m because cash capex rose to $1,059.8m. This distinguishes cash-backed earnings from the separate question of how much cash is retained after the current investment programme. [FY2025 Form 10-K, pp.45–46]

FY2025 ending receivables rose 18.4% while revenue rose 9.4%; inventory rose 15.0% while COGS rose 7.9%. Those point-in-time comparisons require attention, but the days-based evidence points the other way: DSO rose only 0.1 day and DIO fell 3.4 days year on year. The 10-K attributes the inventory increase to comparable-store and new-store inventory following 104 net new stores, while reserve inventory fell to 40% of inventory from 46%. This does not prove there is no inventory risk, but it does not support a channel-stuffing conclusion. ## 9. Earnings Quality Score

**73 /100 — Mostly clean but some working-capital and adjustment noise.** The key support is cash conversion above 80% in each of the last four fiscal years and a small FY2025 reported-to-adjusted gap. The score is below the top band because reported FCF is volatile during a high-capex build, several “one-off” adjustments recur, and the latest TTM cash flow includes the $55.5m tariff-refund benefit. [FY2025 Form 10-K, pp.29, 45–46; Q2 FY2026 Form 10-Q, p.20]

## 10. The Single Biggest Quality Concern

The largest current-quality risk is that Q2 FY2026 results contain a tariff refund that is real cash but not repeatable operating performance. The $55.5m reduction in cost of sales added $41.3m after tax to Q2 net income of $184.3m, about 22%, and the 10-Q says it also lifted six-month CFO. This is not evidence of an accounting error, and the earlier FY2022–FY2025 cash record remains cash-backed. It does mean the latest reported TTM FCF and gross margin should not be extrapolated without removing the refund; the filing provides no evidence that an equivalent future refund will recur. [Q2 FY2026 Form 10-Q, pp.20, 30]

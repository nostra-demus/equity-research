# Margin Drivers — BURL

Burlington reports under U.S. GAAP in USD. Fiscal 2026 ends January 30, 2027; the latest reported period is Q2 FY2026, the 13 weeks ended August 1, 2026. [Q2 FY2026 Form 10-Q, Note 1 — Fiscal Year]

## 1. Segment Decomposition Status

Business-model inputs are available. Burlington has one U.S. off-price retail reportable segment, representing 100% of FY2025 revenue and profit. It does not disclose margins by merchandise category, store cohort, or channel, so this is a consolidated analysis; a product-category margin split is not assessable from the filings. [FY2025 Form 10-K, Note 1 — Segment Information; Q2 FY2026 Form 10-Q, Note 1 — Segment Reporting]

Sector overlay applied: Retail / consumer — margin analysis uses comparable-store sales, gross margin, inventory/reserve mix, store rollout, and product-sourcing/SG&A leverage. Sales per square foot and numerical inventory turns are not disclosed. [FY2025 Form 10-K, Item 1 — Business and Our Off-Price Sourcing and Merchandising Model; frameworks/SECTOR_OVERLAYS.md, Retail / consumer row]

**Cycle position: mid-cycle, not a normalised margin run rate.** This is an inference, not from filings: Q2 comparable-store sales were +2% after +5% a year earlier, while the reported 46.2% gross margin includes a +185 bps tariff-refund effect that management will reinvest in the second half. The business is seasonally weighted to back-to-school and holiday demand, and management identifies weather and low-income consumer spending as material variables. [Q2 FY2026 Form 10-Q, MD&A — Comparable Store Sales and Gross Margin; Q2 FY2026 earnings call, 2026-08-27, prepared remarks; FY2025 Form 10-K, Item 1 — Seasonality]

The reported gross-margin change calculates to +242 bps: `(2,997.778 − 1,614.011) ÷ 2,997.778 = 46.160%` versus `(2,701.026 − 1,519.629) ÷ 2,701.026 = 43.739%`. The refund alone was `$55.5m ÷ $2,997.8m = 1.851%`, or +185 bps, leaving +57 bps not separately allocated by the filing; the filing attributes that residual qualitatively to merchandise margin. This is not a realised price pass-through measure. [Q2 FY2026 Form 10-Q, pp.3, 20, MD&A — Gross Margin]

Management's underlying Q2 view removes the refund and bankruptcy-acquired-lease costs: gross margin was 44.3%, up 60 bps; adjusted EBIT margin rose 100 bps to about 7%. Management attributes the underlying gross-margin change to +70 bps merchandise margin partly offset by +10 bps freight. It expects the tariff-refund effect to be neutral for the full year because it will reinvest the refund into customer value in Q3 and Q4. [Q2 FY2026 earnings call, 2026-08-27, prepared remarks]

## 4. Margin Walk — Which Margin Level Matters Most?

Underlying adjusted EBIT margin matters most. Gross margin isolates buying, markdowns and freight, but it misses the operating leverage from 149 net new stores and the D&A increase from store and distribution-centre investment. Adjusted EBIT captures those effects and is the metric management guides: excluding planned refund reinvestment, its fall guidance assumed a 10–30 bps improvement. The measure is non-GAAP, so reported gross margin and the refund-adjusted bridge remain necessary checks. [Q2 FY2026 earnings call, 2026-08-27, prepared remarks]

Other: `reported total +100 bps − (+70 − 10 + 20 + 50 bps) = −30 bps`. This is the residual, not an attribution. It can include the D&A step-up and other items management did not size within this bridge. The basis matches throughout: Q2 FY2026 versus Q2 FY2025, company-defined adjusted EBIT margin, both excluding tariff refunds and bankruptcy-acquired-lease costs.

Sum of specifically reported components = `+70 − 10 + 20 + 50 = +130 bps`; `+130 bps + (−30 bps residual) = +100 bps` total. The named merchandise-margin driver accounts for 70% of the +100 bps underlying move, so it clears the threshold for the largest disclosed Q2 component. The residual is still material and prevents a more granular causal claim.

## 8. The Single Biggest Margin Driver

For the next two reported quarters, the planned tariff-refund reinvestment is the largest known mover of reported margin. Management says it will use about 40% of the refund in Q3 and 60% in Q4 to offer sharper value, and guides operating margin down 60–80 bps in Q3 and 40–60 bps in Q4 because of that choice. This is a deliberate, finite headwind rather than proof that underlying costs have worsened: excluding that reinvestment, management's fall adjusted-EBIT-margin assumption remains +10–30 bps. Within Q2's underlying bridge, merchandise margin is the largest disclosed component at +70 bps. [Q2 FY2026 earnings call, 2026-08-27, prepared remarks]

## 9. Investment Spend — Both Signs

FY2025 capital expenditure was $1,104.1m, up from $843.9m in FY2024, so this section applies. The FY2025 total comprises $482.0m for stores, $464.0m for supply-chain initiatives, and the remainder for IT and other initiatives; it is an aggregate of different projects but all are capital spend in the same fiscal year. [FY2025 Form 10-K, Item 7 — Capital Expenditures]

| Reading | What it would show | Evidence here |
|---|---|---|
| Spend as a future **cost** | Store and distribution-centre spend becomes D&A and can squeeze EBIT margin before revenue fully matures. | Q2 D&A was $114.0m, up from $94.8m; D&A rose from 3.5% to 3.8% of sales (+29 bps), mainly from new/non-comparable stores and supply-chain infrastructure. [Q2 FY2026 Form 10-Q, pp.3, 27] |
| Spend as a **demand signal** | Store growth should yield incremental sales and fixed-cost absorption. This is evidence from open stores, not contracted backlog. | The 149 net new stores opened since Q2 FY2025 produced $254.8m of Q2 net-sales growth; management reported 45 net new stores opened in Q2. [Q2 FY2026 Form 10-Q, MD&A — Results of Operations; Q2 FY2026 earnings call, 2026-08-27, prepared remarks] |

**Current read:** Both signs matter. The Q2 sales contribution from new stores supports the demand reading for store investment, while the D&A rise is already a measurable margin cost. The evidence does not prove that all supply-chain infrastructure will earn its cost, because Burlington gives no backlog or distribution-centre return disclosure. The observable that would flip the current read is store-led sales growth and adjusted EBIT margin failing to absorb the continuing D&A increase; a numerical threshold is not proven from available data.

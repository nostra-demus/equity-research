# Earnings Module — BURL (Synthesis)

## Abstract

Burlington’s earnings picture is mixed: Q2 sales and underlying operating margin improved, but annual sales growth had slowed and the quarter was helped by a finite tariff refund. New and non-comparable stores drove $254.8 million, or about 86%, of Q2’s sales increase, while transactions at mature stores were relatively flat. The next-quarter revenue bar sits inside management’s range and looks fair, while the EPS comparison is not definition-matched. The biggest risk is that the $22.2 million Q3 value investment fails to revive traffic and instead raises markdown pressure. The verdict is a mixed earnings setup. [FY2025 10-K, p.43; Q2 FY2026 10-Q, pp.3,20 and MD&A—Net Sales; Q2 FY2026 call, prepared remarks and Q&A; CIQ Estimates, FQ3 FY2027, latest change 2026-09-22]

## 5. Earnings Setup Summary

### Revenue Setup

The current total-sales trajectory can continue while the store programme adds capacity, but it is capital-dependent rather than a one-time M&A, foreign-exchange or accounting effect. The reported-versus-underlying gap is material: new and non-comparable stores supplied 9.4 percentage points of Q2’s 11.0% growth, while comparable stores supplied 1.6 points. [Q2 FY2026 Form 10-Q, MD&A—Net Sales] The single factor that would flip the revenue direction is mature-store traffic: transactions were relatively flat in Q2, so weaker Fall visits could overwhelm the benefit from a larger store base. [Q2 FY2026 earnings call, Q&A] The one-time H2 value investment may help traffic, but its sales response is not quantified and should not be treated as recurring growth. [Q2 FY2026 earnings call, prepared remarks]

### Margin Setup

Q2’s 46.2% reported gross margin is not sustainable as a run rate because about 185 basis points came from the refund; the better current anchor is the 44.3% refund-excluded margin, up 60 basis points year on year. [Q2 FY2026 Form 10-Q, pp.3, 20; Q2 FY2026 earnings call, prepared remarks] This is a mid-cycle, not proven peak-or-trough, margin read because H2 has the planned reinvestment, seasonal weather risk and a larger holiday quarter still ahead. Burlington has one segment and no category profit disclosure, so a 10%–20% adverse move in any segment cannot be measured; the largest supportable operating test is instead a 70-basis-point merchandise-margin reversal, which would reduce Q3-like adjusted EBITDA by about $20.9 million under the specialist’s zero-mitigation bound. [Earnings Sensitivity, Sections 2–4] No contractual merchandise-cost pass-through or quantified realised offset is proven, although flexible sourcing, pricing, reserve inventory and supply-chain actions provide partial but unmeasured mitigation. [FY2025 Form 10-K, Item 1—Sourcing and Merchandising Model; Q2 FY2026 earnings call, prepared remarks]

### Quality Check

The largest gap between reported and economic earnings is the non-recurring $55.5 million refund: reported trailing FCF was $412.5 million, versus about $357.0 million after a conservative full-refund normalisation; whether that gap narrows cannot be proven until the H2 cash timing is reported. [Q2 FY2026 Form 10-Q, pp.20, 30; Earnings Quality, Section 1] Lease, impairment and litigation adjustments recur, but the FY2025 adjusted-versus-GAAP net-income gap was only 2.5%. [FY2025 Form 10-K, p.29] A next-year model should start from GAAP earnings, remove the tariff refund and assess recurring adjustments individually rather than use non-GAAP earnings as a blanket replacement.

### Consensus Bar

A material Q3 beat requires several linked outcomes: continued store ramp, mature-store sales near or above management’s range, and less margin loss than planned from the value investment. [Beat / Miss Setup, Sections 2–4] The revenue bar appears correctly set rather than low: it is 0.37% above management’s midpoint but inside the range, while the apparent EPS gap cannot be judged because CIQ normalized EPS and company Adjusted EPS are not definition-matched. [Capital IQ Estimates Report, Consensus sheet, CIQ FQ3 2027, latest logged change 2026-09-22; Q2 FY2026 earnings release, Outlook] The latest Q2 surprise was helped by the refund, but Q3 normalized EPS was reset from $2.04 to $1.73 and the degree to which consensus still relies on that one-off is not measurable. [Capital IQ Estimates Report, Trends sheet, CIQ FQ3 2027, latest logged change 2026-09-22]

## 8. Note To The Final Synthesizer

- **Dominant trend and driver:** The setup is mixed. Total sales and underlying margin improved, but most Q2 growth came from store capacity rather than mature-store traffic. [Q2 FY2026 Form 10-Q, MD&A—Net Sales]
- **Cash backing:** Historical earnings are cash-backed, but the latest reported cash read must be reduced for up to $55.5 million of non-recurring refund benefit; several adjusted items also recur. [FY2025 Form 10-K, pp.29, 45–46; Q2 FY2026 Form 10-Q, pp.20, 30]
- **Consensus bar:** Revenue is fairly set inside guidance; the EPS bar is not cleanly comparable because vendor normalized EPS and company Adjusted EPS use unmatched definitions. [Capital IQ Estimates Report, Consensus sheet, latest logged change 2026-09-22; Q2 FY2026 earnings release, Outlook]
- **Next-quarter and look-ahead:** Q3 is balanced, but even a Q3 beat will not settle Q4, which has averaged 31.5% of annual revenue and carries about $33 million of the planned value investment. [CIQ Financials Quarterly workbook, FY2023–FY2025; Q2 FY2026 earnings call, prepared remarks]
- **Top sensitivity:** Merchandise margin improved 70 basis points in Q2; a reversal is the largest supportable two-sided operating sensitivity, while its durability is unproven. [Q2 FY2026 earnings call, prepared remarks; Earnings Sensitivity, Sections 2–4]
- **Partial-data caps:** None of the formal caps applies. Missing price elasticity, store-cohort returns and operating coefficients still limit precision, especially on the downside.
- **Red-flag severity verdict: Material concerns.**
- **High flag — capacity-led growth:** About 86% of Q2’s dollar sales increase came from new and non-comparable stores while mature-store transactions were relatively flat. [Q2 FY2026 Form 10-Q, MD&A—Net Sales; Q2 FY2026 earnings call, Q&A]
- **High flag — reported margin is not recurring:** The $55.5 million Q2 tariff refund will be reinvested in H2, so the reported Q2 margin cannot be extrapolated. [Q2 FY2026 Form 10-Q, p.20; Q2 FY2026 earnings call, prepared remarks]
- **High flag — Q3 upside is conjunctive:** A beat needs store ramp, mature-store demand and price-investment conversion to work together; weak traffic or weather creates the simpler miss path. [Beat / Miss Setup, Sections 2–3]
- **High flag — Q4 remains unresolved:** Q4 is the larger seasonal quarter and carries roughly 60% of the planned refund reinvestment, so a Q3 beat alone cannot upgrade the setup. [Beat / Miss Setup, Sections 5–6, 9]
- **High flag — latest cash quality is inflated:** Reported trailing FCF includes up to the $55.5 million refund cash benefit; the normalised read is about $357.0 million versus $412.5 million reported. [Q2 FY2026 Form 10-Q, pp.20, 30; Earnings Quality, Section 1]
- **High flag — correlated downside is unquantified:** Demand, weather, markdown and selling-cost leverage can worsen together, but the company discloses no operating coefficients for that chain. [Earnings Sensitivity, Sections 1–2, 5–7]
- **High flag — acceleration framing is too strong:** Capacity-led growth, flat mature-store transactions and refund-aided reported results support a mixed verdict, not proven earnings acceleration. [Revenue Drivers, Sections 4, 6–7; Margin Drivers, Sections 3, 8]
- **RF-EQ-001 (rising accruals divergent from cash earnings)**
- **Biggest missing data point:** The transaction response to the Q3 value investment; without it, price elasticity remains unproven.
- **What changes the verdict:** Upgrade only when transaction-led mature-store demand and refund-excluded margin hold through the larger Q4; downgrade on weaker transactions, markdown pressure and a full-year guidance cut.

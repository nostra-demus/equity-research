## 3. Runway

| Metric | Value |
|---|---:|
| Total committed liquidity | 1,645.7 |
| Annual FCF (or proxy) | 357.0 normalised operating FCF; 412.5 reported LTM FCF |
| Basis used (net-of-FCF / gross-obligations) | Net-of-FCF, using normalised FCF |
| Annual net cash burn (on the stated basis — see step 4) | **(336.9) — annual surplus, not burn** |
| Monthly net cash burn (annual burn ÷ 12) | **(28.1) — monthly surplus, not burn** |
| **Liquidity runway (months) = liquidity ÷ monthly net cash burn** | **No finite runway on this run-rate basis** |

FCF means CFO less total cash capex. Reported LTM FCF is `$1,415.5m CFO − $1,002.9m cash capex = $412.5m`. The source-bound CIQ facts sidecar confirms the $1,415.5m LTM CFO read; its $107.4m “levered FCF” is a different after-interest definition and is not used. The Q2 filing says CFO benefited by a $55.5m tariff refund, so the more conservative normalised operating FCF is `$412.5m − $55.5m = $357.0m`; this is an inference from the filing, not a reported company measure. [CIQ Financials→Cash Flow `Cash from Ops.`, LTM 2026-08-01 — vendor export; CIQ facts sidecar, `ltm_ocf_m`, status present; CIQ Financials→Cash Flow `Levered Free Cash Flow`, LTM 2026-08-01 — vendor export; FY2025 Form 10-K, pp.45–46; Q2 FY2026 Form 10-Q, pp.5–6, 20, 30]

Formula on the net-of-FCF basis: `annual net cash burn = (12-month maturities $20.1m + committed dividends/buybacks $0.0m) − normalised FCF $357.0m = $(336.9)m`. Cash interest and capex are already inside CFO/FCF, so adding the $73.1m historical cash-interest line or a capex proxy again would double-count. The resulting run-rate annual surplus is $336.9m, or $28.1m per month; it is not a forecast. Normalised FCF covers the stated 12-month debt maturities 17.7x, while cash alone covers them 34.9x. [Q2 FY2026 Form 10-Q, Note 3, p.10; Note 4, p.12; earnings/01_historical-financials; earnings/06_earnings-quality]

### Seasonality / Peak Liquidity Need (Hard Check)

BURL is seasonal: Q4 generated an average 31.5% of annual revenue in FY2023–FY2025, and management says its retail business is seasonal. The filing reports working capital of $380.3m at 1 August 2026, but does not disclose a peak seasonal working-capital build or peak-quarter cash use that can be matched to a runway re-run. **Peak working-capital need not disclosed — runway may be overstated.** The $528.8m six-month cash use is not a peak-working-capital proxy because it also includes $537.7m of investing outflows and $325.7m of financing outflows. [FY2025 Form 10-K, Item 1—Seasonality, p.5; Q2 FY2026 Form 10-Q, Item 2—Liquidity and Capital Resources, p.30; earnings/01_historical-financials]

## 4. Sources & Uses Bridge

Internal sources cover the stated next-12-month debt use without a refinancing: reported consolidated cash of $703.7m is 34.9x the $20.1m maturity, and the normalised FCF run rate leaves a $336.9m annual surplus after that maturity. This read depends on $357.0m of FCF materialising; it is not all cash already in hand, and the filing does not quantify Parent-level cash restrictions. The additional $942.0m ABL availability is committed external draw capacity, not needed on this base case and subject to its borrowing base. [Q2 FY2026 Form 10-Q, p.5; Note 3, p.10; Note 4, pp.13–14; p.20; p.30]

## 5. Liquidity Read

There is no finite liquidity-runway end on the normalised LTM FCF basis: $1.646bn of committed liquidity and a $357.0m normalised FCF run rate sit against only $20.1m of stated financial-debt maturities over 12 months. The conclusion relies on operating cash continuing through a seasonal retailer’s peak inventory period; the $55.5m tariff refund is removed, but the peak working-capital build is not disclosed. The single biggest liquidity risk is a consumer, weather, or inventory shock that turns FCF negative while it also reduces ABL availability; $2.559bn of disclosed purchase commitments has no disclosed timing and could intensify that working-capital call, though it represents merchandise purchases rather than a disclosed loss. [Q2 FY2026 Form 10-Q, p.20; Item 2—Liquidity and Capital Resources, p.30; Note 11—Purchase Commitments, p.20; business-model/10_external-dependency]

## 3. Classification

**Partly externally driven.** BURL's outcomes are materially exposed to lower-income discretionary spending, seasonal weather and overseas production and trade policy. Management has real levers: it buys much merchandise close to need, can shift purchases between suppliers and categories, uses interest-rate swaps, and says it has reduced weather exposure through mix and localization. Those levers reduce exposure but do not make it company-controlled. The $55.5m Q2 FY26 tariff refund is not an ongoing earnings benefit in management's stated full-year plan: it intends to reinvest the amount in customer value across the second half. [FY25 10-K, Business — Off-Price Sourcing and Merchandising Model, p.3]; [Q2 FY26 earnings call, 2026-08-27, prepared remarks]

## 4. External Dependency Risk Score

**55 / 100 — inverted: higher = worse.**

This is a material-exposure, mixed-mitigation score. Consumer spending and weather are the main operating variables; tariffs and overseas supply add cost and availability risk. The score is below the mostly-externally-driven range because the filing documents flexible procurement and category/supplier shifts, while the company has an interest-rate hedge. No filing quantifies the protection from those operating levers against tariffs, weather or consumer weakness. [FY25 10-K, Item 7 MD&A — Inflation; Item 1A, pp.9–11; Item 7A, pp.39–40]

## 5. The Single Biggest Lever

**Consumer cycle:** a sustained 20% adverse hit to the discretionary-spending capacity of BURL's low-income core customer would likely cause the most damage through traffic and comparable sales; the company discloses no numerical sensitivity, and value trade-down is a partial counterforce rather than a proven offset. [Q2 FY26 10-Q, MD&A — Uncertainties and Challenges, p.22]

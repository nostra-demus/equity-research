# Moat — BURL

## 1. Named Competitors

- **Ross Stores, Inc. (NasdaqGS: ROST):** U.S. off-price apparel and home-fashion retailer operating Ross Dress for Less and dd’s DISCOUNTS; 2,267 stores at January 31, 2026. [Competitive Map — BURL, 2026-09-29, §2; Ross FY2025 Form 10-K, Item 1 — Business]
- **The TJX Companies, Inc. (NYSE: TJX):** its directly relevant U.S. Marmaxx segment operates T.J. Maxx, Marshalls and Sierra, with 2,748 locations and $36.585bn FY2026 sales. [Competitive Map — BURL, 2026-09-29, §2; TJX FY2026 Form 10-K, Item 7 — U.S. Segments]

**The economic moat test: Not assessable.** Through-cycle return on capital is **7.0% ROIC** (simple mean of CIQ’s 9.5%, 4.7%, 6.2%, 7.2% and 7.4% annual reads from Jan. 29, 2022 to Jan. 31, 2026; latest LTM is 7.8%), but a matching group WACC is not determinable from the available evidence. The 10-K’s 6.2% weighted-average lease discount rate is a collateralized lease incremental-borrowing-rate input, not group WACC; CIQ supplies a 1.42 five-year beta but no source-bound equity-risk premium or market-return input. Constructing CAPM from that missing input would be an unsourced inference, so no cost of capital is invented. [CIQ Financials → Income Statement + Ratios, retrieved 2026-09-29 — vendor export; FY2025 Form 10-K, Leases note; CIQ Comparable Analysis → Operating Statistics, as of 2026-09-28 — vendor export]

The BURL LTM ROIC is a reconciled vendor read: the deterministic `ciq_facts.json` reports 7.8% with `status: present`, and the underlying CIQ ratios sheet shows 7.8259%; there is no material gap. [CIQ Financials → Income Statement + Ratios (multi-year columns), retrieved 2026-09-29 — vendor export]

**ROIC cross-check and tax basis.** Management does not disclose a group ROIC headline. For FY2025, reported operating income of $864.3m × (1 − 25.3% normalized tax rate) gives $645.7m NOPAT (after-tax operating profit). The 25.3% rate is the simple average of FY2024’s 25.4% and FY2025’s 25.2%; Q2 FY2026’s lower interim rate is excluded because the filing says it benefited from purchased federal energy tax credits. On a gross-capital basis, average FY2024–FY2025 debt-equivalent operating leases plus equity were $8,391.6m and $9,497.4m, respectively: ($8,391.6m + $9,497.4m) / 2 = $8,944.5m; $645.7m / $8,944.5m = **7.2%**. That is 20bp below CIQ’s 7.4% FY2025 ROIC, so the conservative calculated result is used as the cross-check. [FY2025 Form 10-K, Item 7 — Results of Operations / Income Taxes; Q2 FY2026 Form 10-Q, Note 7 — Income Taxes; CIQ Financials → Balance Sheet and Ratios, retrieved 2026-09-29 — vendor export]

## 4. Where The Company Sits

**Relative to peers:** Insufficient data to compare against named peers on both margin and capital efficiency. On the only directly comparable profitability line supplied by competitive-map, BURL’s 7.8% LTM EBIT margin is below Ross’s 12.7% and TJX’s 12.2%; peer ROIC is not disclosed and gross-margin definitions are not safely comparable. [Competitive Map — BURL, 2026-09-29, §2; FY2025 Form 10-K, Item 7 — Gross Margin]

**Absolute (the economic moat test):** Not assessable — BURL’s through-cycle ROIC is 7.0% (latest LTM 7.8%), but cost of capital is not determinable from available data without inventing a WACC. [CIQ Financials → Income Statement + Ratios, retrieved 2026-09-29 — vendor export; FY2025 Form 10-K, Leases note]

## 5. Moat Verdict

**Narrow moat — structural, not yet proven economic.** The strongest evidence is the 60/100 distribution asset, reinforced by an expanding but still sub-scale store base and flexible in-season sourcing. It has not translated into peer-leading EBIT economics, and no valid cost-of-capital comparison is available to prove a sustained economic spread; over the next five years, the durability test is whether gross-capital ROIC and EBIT margin rise through the consumer and weather cycle while store growth avoids dilution and competitive response. [FY2025 Form 10-K, Item 1 — Distribution and Warehousing / Off-Price Sourcing and Merchandising Model; Competitive Map — BURL, 2026-09-29, §§2–3]

**Moat trajectory: not assessable (mixed — direction not established).**

| Metric (same 3–5 year window) | Start | Latest | Direction | Supports / contradicts the stated trajectory |
|---|---:|---:|---|---|
| Gross margin | 41.6% (Jan. 29, 2022) | 44.1% (LTM) | Up 250bp | Supports a better merchandise/freight outcome, but BURL’s gross-margin classification limits peer comparison. |
| EBITDA margin | 11.0% (Jan. 29, 2022) | 11.1% (LTM) | Roughly flat, +20bp | Neutral. |
| EBIT or operating margin | 8.5% (Jan. 29, 2022) | 7.8% (LTM) | Down 70bp | Contradicts widening. |
| Return on capital (ROIC / ROCE) vs cost of capital | 9.5% ROIC (Jan. 29, 2022) | 7.8% ROIC (LTM); cost of capital not determinable | Down 170bp; spread not assessable | Contradicts widening; it is the decisive unresolved row. |
| Market share (or the closest disclosed volume/position proxy) | 927 stores (FY2022) | 1,287 stores (Q2 FY2026) | Up 38.8% | Supports greater physical reach, not a proven revenue-share gain. |
| Cash conversion (CFO ÷ EBITDA) | 81.6% (FY2022: $833.2m / $1,020.6m) | 104.0% (LTM: $1,415.5m / $1,360.7m) | Up 22.4pp | Supports improved cash conversion, though it does not establish price power. |
| Pricing / realised price or mix | Not disclosed | Not disclosed | Not assessable | Neither. Q2 had a higher basket and flat transactions, but no realised-price or mix series is disclosed. |

The panel is split rather than confirming either widening or erosion. Gross margin rose from 41.6% to 44.1%, cash conversion rose from 81.6% to 104.0%, and store count rose from 927 to 1,287; these support the operating platform. In contrast, EBIT margin fell from 8.5% to 7.8% and ROIC fell from 9.5% to 7.8%, while market share and realised-price data are not disclosed. The improved gross margin does not overturn those two capital-efficiency rows because BURL itself says its gross-margin classification is not comparable across retailers, and Q2 FY2026 reported gross margin also included $55.5m of tariff refunds that management intends to reinvest; therefore this is not confirmed erosion, but neither is it a widening moat. [CIQ Financials → Income Statement, Cash Flow and Ratios, retrieved 2026-09-29 — vendor export; BURL FY2024 Annual Report, Item 1 — Store Expansion; Q2 FY2026 Form 10-Q, MD&A — Gross Margin / Tariff Refunds; Q2 FY2026 earnings call, prepared remarks]

The single next data point most likely to flip this read is **FY2026 gross-capital ROIC**, calculated from reported GAAP operating income, normalized tax and year-end capital in the FY2026 Form 10-K: a rise above FY2025’s 7.4% CIQ read on the same basis would support improvement; a decline would reinforce erosion. *Inference, not management guidance:* it should arrive with the annual filing in roughly March 2027 based on the prior filing cadence; the exact date is not announced. [CIQ Financials → Ratios, retrieved 2026-09-29 — vendor export; FY2025 Form 10-K, filed 2026-03-19]

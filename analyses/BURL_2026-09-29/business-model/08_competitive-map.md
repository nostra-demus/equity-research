# Competitive Map — BURL

## 1. Dominant Segment

Burlington is a single-segment U.S. off-price retailer: the Burlington store operation produced 100% of FY2025 reported revenue and profit. [data/BURL/Burlington_Stores_Inc_-_Form_10-K-Mar-19-2026.txt (FY2025 Form 10-K), Note 1 — Segment Information; data/BURL/Burlington_Stores_Inc_-_Form_10-Q-Aug-27-2026.txt (Q2 FY2026 Form 10-Q), Note 1 — Segment Reporting]

## 2. Named Competitors

### Competitor A — Ross Stores, Inc.

- **Ticker / listing:** NasdaqGS: ROST.
- **Where they compete:** U.S. off-price apparel and home fashion through Ross Dress for Less and dd’s DISCOUNTS. Ross had 1,904 Ross stores and 363 dd’s stores at January 31, 2026; its merchandise and value proposition overlap Burlington’s apparel, footwear, accessories and home categories. [Ross FY2025 Form 10-K (SEC), Item 1 — Business]
- **Scale:** $24.512bn LTM revenue in the latest Capital IQ peer filing data (filed September 1, 2026); FY2025 reported sales were $22.751bn. [data/BURL/Company-Comparable-Analysis-Burlington-Stores-Inc.xls, Financial Data, as of 2026-09-28 — vendor export; Ross FY2025 Form 10-K (SEC), Item 7 — Results of Operations]
- **Profitability / return on capital:** Latest LTM EBIT margin was 12.7%. FY2025 operating margin was 11.9%. Calculated FY2025 return on equity (ROE, net earnings divided by average beginning-and-ending stockholders’ equity) was 36.7%: $2.145bn / (($6.187bn + $5.509bn) / 2). This is an equity return, not ROIC. [data/BURL/Company-Comparable-Analysis-Burlington-Stores-Inc.xls, Operating Statistics, as of 2026-09-28 — vendor export; Ross FY2025 Form 10-K (SEC), Item 7 — Results of Operations; Consolidated Statements of Comprehensive Income and Balance Sheets]
- **Source named in:** Capital IQ’s BURL comparable-company export includes Ross; Burlington’s own filing names off-price retailers as a competing class but does not name individual companies. [data/BURL/Company-Comparable-Analysis-Burlington-Stores-Inc.xls, Business Description, as of 2026-09-28 — vendor export; data/BURL/Burlington_Stores_Inc_-_Form_10-K-Mar-19-2026.txt (FY2025 Form 10-K), Item 1 — Competition]
- **One-line read:** *Inference, not from filings:* Ross is the closest national, U.S.-only off-price peer in this named set. Its $24.512bn latest LTM revenue is about twice Burlington’s $12.209bn, and it also operates the lower-price dd’s format. [data/BURL/Company-Comparable-Analysis-Burlington-Stores-Inc.xls, Financial Data and Business Description, as of 2026-09-28 — vendor export; Ross FY2025 Form 10-K (SEC), Item 1 — Business]

### Competitor B — The TJX Companies, Inc.

- **Ticker / listing:** NYSE: TJX.
- **Where they compete:** Its U.S. Marmaxx segment operates T.J. Maxx, Marshalls and Sierra. It sold $36.585bn of apparel- and home-led merchandise in FY2026 through 2,748 U.S. locations, making it the direct TJX business to compare with Burlington rather than the full international group. [TJX FY2026 Form 10-K (SEC), Item 7 — U.S. Segments]
- **Scale:** $62.363bn group LTM revenue in the latest Capital IQ peer filing data; the directly relevant U.S. Marmaxx segment was $36.585bn in FY2026. [data/BURL/Company-Comparable-Analysis-Burlington-Stores-Inc.xls, Financial Data, as of 2026-09-28 — vendor export; TJX FY2026 Form 10-K (SEC), Item 7 — U.S. Segments]
- **Profitability / return on capital:** Latest group LTM EBIT margin was 12.2%. Marmaxx’s FY2026 segment-profit margin was 15.1%; it is a pre-tax segment measure before corporate expense and net interest, so it is not the same basis as group EBIT. Calculated FY2026 group ROE was 59.1%: $5.494bn net income / (($10.190bn + $8.393bn) / 2 average shareholders’ equity). This is an equity return, not ROIC. [data/BURL/Company-Comparable-Analysis-Burlington-Stores-Inc.xls, Operating Statistics, as of 2026-09-28 — vendor export; TJX FY2026 Form 10-K (SEC), Item 7 — U.S. Segments; Consolidated Statements of Income and Balance Sheets]
- **Source named in:** Capital IQ’s BURL comparable-company export includes TJX; Burlington’s own filing names off-price retailers as a competing class but does not name individual companies. [data/BURL/Company-Comparable-Analysis-Burlington-Stores-Inc.xls, Business Description, as of 2026-09-28 — vendor export; data/BURL/Burlington_Stores_Inc_-_Form_10-K-Mar-19-2026.txt (FY2025 Form 10-K), Item 1 — Competition]
- **One-line read:** T.J. Maxx and Marshalls give TJX a much larger U.S. off-price footprint, while Burlington remains a store-only model and TJX says Marmaxx e-commerce, together with Sierra.com, represented about 2% of Marmaxx sales in FY2026. [data/BURL/Burlington_Stores_Inc_-_Form_10-K-Mar-19-2026.txt (FY2025 Form 10-K), Item 1A — Competition; TJX FY2026 Form 10-K (SEC), Item 7 — U.S. Segments]

## 3. Competitive Position

**Position: Gaining store footprint (inference); revenue-market share is not disclosed.** Burlington added 149 net stores in the year to August 1, 2026, taking the chain to 1,287; that is 13.1% growth from the implied 1,138 a year earlier. As a directional reach proxy, this was faster than Ross’s 81 net additions (2,186 to 2,267) and Marmaxx’s 68 (2,680 to 2,748), though the peer reporting windows do not match exactly. [data/BURL/Burlington_Stores_Inc_-_Form_10-Q-Aug-27-2026.txt (Q2 FY2026 Form 10-Q), MD&A — Liquidity and Capital Resources / Store Openings; Ross FY2025 Form 10-K (SEC), Item 7 — Store Openings; TJX FY2026 Form 10-K (SEC), Item 7 — U.S. Segments]

The mature-store signal does not establish a sales-share gain: Burlington’s comparable sales rose 4% in the six months to August 1, 2026, broadly matching TJX Marmaxx’s 4% in FY2026 and below Ross’s 5% in FY2025. Those are different reporting windows, so this is neither a matched comparison nor a market-share series. [data/BURL/Burlington_Stores_Inc_-_Form_10-Q-Aug-27-2026.txt (Q2 FY2026 Form 10-Q), MD&A — Six Month Period Ended August 1, 2026; TJX FY2026 Form 10-K (SEC), Item 7 — U.S. Segments; Ross FY2025 Form 10-K (SEC), Item 7 — Results of Operations]

## 4. Competitive Shape

**Fragmented.** Burlington describes U.S. retail apparel and home-furnishings markets as “highly fragmented and competitive” and identifies competition not only from off-price chains but also department, specialty, online, discount, wholesale-club and outlet retailers. Ross independently uses the same fragmented-market description for apparel and home fashion. The admitted evidence contains no like-for-like market-share, top-four-share or HHI series, so the two named large operators do not justify an oligopoly claim. [data/BURL/Burlington_Stores_Inc_-_Form_10-K-Mar-19-2026.txt (FY2025 Form 10-K), Item 1 — Competition; Ross FY2025 Form 10-K (SEC), Item 1 — Business]

## 5. Caveat

Burlington’s Competition discussion names competitor categories rather than corporate names and does not provide an off-price market-share series. Ross and TJX are credible named rivals because the Capital IQ BURL comparable export identifies them and their own audited filings confirm the directly overlapping U.S. off-price businesses, but BURL revenue share is not proven from available data. A same-boundary U.S. off-price apparel-and-home sales panel, with retailer shares over time, would resolve whether the faster Burlington store rollout is translating into sales-share gains.

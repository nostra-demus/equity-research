## 3. Value Creation Read

**Verdict: Unclear.** FY2025 implied gross profit per average store rose to $4.36m from $4.34m, but gross profit is not store contribution because the filing puts store payroll, occupancy and other selling costs below it. The $9.96m implied average-store sales figure fell 0.8% even as comparable-store sales rose 2%; the measures cover different populations, because comparable sales begin roughly one year after grand-opening activity while $691.4m of FY2025 sales came from new and non-comparable stores. [FY2025 Form 10-K, fiscal year ended January 31, 2026, Item 7 — Key Performance and Non-GAAP Measures / Results of Operations; Item 1 — Store Expansion and Real Estate Strategy] The $482.0m aggregate store-capex pool cannot be matched to a new-store contribution stream or a build cost, so the test that lifetime contribution exceeds acquisition cost cannot be completed. The single most valuable missing disclosure is a new-store cohort schedule showing net build cost, store contribution after occupancy and payroll, and cash payback by vintage.

## 4. Sensitivity

Sales per store is the largest observable sensitivity. Holding the FY2025 43.8% gross margin constant, a 20% fall in the $9.96m implied sales per average store would reduce gross profit by about $0.87m per store (20% × $9.96m × 43.8%), before any cost response; it is not a contribution-margin forecast because the lower store cost base is undisclosed. Comparable-store sales were +4%, +4% and +2% in FY2023–FY2025, and +4% in the first six months of FY2026, so the available history does not show a 20% sales move. The latest six-month gross margin of 45.1% includes $55.5m of tariff refunds, so it is not clean evidence that the store’s normalised economics have risen by the full reported amount.

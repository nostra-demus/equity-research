## What This Module Found

Burlington is a U.S. off-price retailer selling branded apparel, footwear, home goods, and other merchandise through 1,287 physical stores. Its single retail segment produces all reported revenue, with growth coming from new stores and from transactions and basket size at mature stores. [FY2025 Form 10-K, Item 1 — Business and Note 1 — Segment Information; Q2 FY2026 Form 10-Q, MD&A — Overview]

The main positive is flexible in-season sourcing: Burlington can shift among suppliers and categories, and no brand exceeded 5% of net purchases in FY2023–FY2025. FY2025 gross margin rose 60 basis points—a basis point is one-hundredth of a percentage point—to 43.8%. [FY2025 Form 10-K, Item 1 — Our Off-Price Sourcing and Merchandising Model; Item 7 — Gross Margin]

The central weakness is unproven store-level value creation. FY2025 capital expenditure, or capex—spending on stores, supply chain, and other long-lived assets—was $1,104.1m, equal to 9.5% of revenue and 2.6 times depreciation and amortisation, but the filings do not disclose store contribution, net build cost, or cohort payback. [FY2025 Form 10-K, Item 7 — Capital Expenditures; Unit Economics — BURL, §2–3]

The moat evidence is mixed rather than clearly improving or eroding. From the FY2022 anchor, gross margin rose 250 basis points, cash conversion rose 22.4 percentage points, and store count rose 38.8%, while EBIT margin fell 70 basis points and return on invested capital, or ROIC—the profit earned on the capital put into the business—fell 170 basis points. [CIQ Financials → Income Statement, Cash Flow and Ratios, retrieved 2026-09-29 — vendor export; Q2 FY2026 Form 10-Q, MD&A — Gross Margin]

The biggest operating risk is merchandise-flow disruption during the higher-sales second half. Six distribution centres handled more than 99% of FY2025 units, split between New Jersey and Southern California, while supply is not contractually assured. [FY2025 Form 10-K, Item 1 — Seasonality and Distribution and Warehousing, p.2; Item 1A — Distribution Network Risk]

## Bottom Line

- **Verdict:** Average business — worth deeper work only if valuation is cheap. No automatic disqualifier or rejector-filter cap applies.
- **Why it could be better than it looks:** Flexible in-season buying lets Burlington shift among suppliers and categories, no brand exceeded 5% of net purchases in FY2023–FY2025, and FY2025 gross margin rose 60 basis points to 43.8%. [FY2025 Form 10-K, Item 1 — Our Off-Price Sourcing and Merchandising Model; Item 7 — Gross Margin]
- **Why it could be worse:** FY2025 capex was $1,104.1m without filed cohort returns, while six distribution centres handled more than 99% of merchandise units. [FY2025 Form 10-K, Item 7 — Capital Expenditures; Item 1 — Distribution and Warehousing, p.2]
- **What is missing:** A filed new-store cohort schedule, comparable peer ROIC, a source-bound BURL cost of capital, and named-customer concentration.
- **One thing to watch next:** Whether audited evidence shows new stores earning higher contribution and cash payback after occupancy and payroll, not merely adding sales.

## Plain-English Glossary

- **Moat:** A durable advantage that protects a business from competitors.
- **EBIT margin:** Operating profit before interest and tax as a share of sales.
- **External dependency risk:** How much results depend on factors the company does not control.
- **Capital allocation:** How management directs cash among stores, supply chain, acquisitions, debt, and shareholder returns.
- **Capital expenditure (capex):** Cash spent on stores, supply chain, and other long-lived assets.
- **Cash conversion:** How effectively reported profit becomes operating cash.
- **Basis point:** One-hundredth of a percentage point.
- **ROIC:** Return on invested capital, or the profit earned on the capital put into the business.
- **Unit economics:** The revenue, costs, contribution, and payback of one store or store cohort.
- **Payback:** The time needed for an investment's cash returns to recover its initial cost.

# Value Chain Position — BURL

## 1. Stages Occupied

| Value Chain Stage | Company Role (1 sentence) | Bargaining Power vs Upstream | Bargaining Power vs Downstream | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Distributor / wholesaler | Burlington receives merchandise from vendors and routes it through six distribution centers that shipped more than 99% of FY2025 merchandise units to its own stores; the small remainder was drop-shipped by vendors. | Mid | Strong | [FY2025 Form 10-K, Item 2 — Properties] |
| Retailer / end-customer-facing brand | Burlington sells a changing assortment of branded apparel, home and other goods direct to consumers through Burlington stores at an everyday discount. | Mid | Weak | [FY2025 Form 10-K, Item 1 — Business and Our Off-Price Sourcing and Merchandising Model] |

The upstream rating is **Mid**, not Strong. Burlington can buy a majority of merchandise in season and move purchases among suppliers and categories, but it has no contractual assurance of supply, price, or access to product. The downstream rating is **Weak** because its value proposition requires a discount to other retailers' prices in a fragmented market where rivals can mark down to similar prices. [FY2025 Form 10-K, Item 1 — Business; Item 1A — Risk Factors]

## 2. Input Cost Pass-Through

| Axis | What it asks | Verdict | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Contractual pass-through** | Is there an escalator, indexed-pricing, or cost-plus clause that moves price automatically? | **No automatic customer clause disclosed** — the filings disclose no retail cost-plus or indexed-pricing mechanism. Burlington's supplier purchases are generally purchase-order based, with no long-term purchase contracts; that supports flexibility upstream but does not prove that no customer contract could exist. | [FY2025 Form 10-K, Item 1A — Risk Factors: merchandise supply; Item 1 — Our Off-Price Sourcing and Merchandising Model] |
| **Realised recovery** | Of a given input-cost increase, how much did the company actually recover through price, mix, hedging, re-sourcing, or a cost programme? | **Not computable from the pool.** The filing does not disclose the gross pre-mitigation tariff or other input-cost increase, nor a separately measured price/mix recovery. | [Q2 FY2026 Form 10-Q, Note — Tariff Refunds; MD&A — Gross Margin] |

The prescribed calculation cannot be completed: `realised recovery = 1 − (observed margin impact ÷ disclosed gross pre-mitigation impact)`. Q2 FY2026 gross margin rose 250 basis points, from 43.7% to 46.2%; $55.5 million of tariff refunds reduced cost of sales. Against $2,997.8 million of Q2 sales, the refund equals about 185 basis points (`55.5 ÷ 2,997.8`), leaving about 65 basis points of the reported gross-margin increase attributed only to improved merchandise margin. The unreported gross tariff/input-cost impact makes neither the 250 basis points nor the 65-basis-point residual a recovery rate. The refund is also a customs recovery, not customer price pass-through. [Q2 FY2026 Form 10-Q, Note — Tariff Refunds; MD&A — Results of Operations and Gross Margin]

Burlington's disclosed mitigation is procurement and cost control, not an automatic price escalator: it buys most merchandise in season, shifts purchases among suppliers and categories, and expects better supplier terms to offset rising goods costs. Management also said it pivoted away from highly tariff-exposed categories in the second half of FY2025, particularly Home; this reduced tariff exposure but pressured sales. Product-sourcing costs improved by 20 basis points in Q2 and 30 basis points for the first half as a share of sales from supply-chain initiatives — cost absorption, not evidence that shoppers paid more. Conversely, management intends to reinvest the roughly $55 million Q2 tariff refund into lower prices / sharper value in the second half, so the latest explicit pricing action is a planned price investment, not a price increase. [FY2025 Form 10-K, Item 1 — Business; Q2 FY2026 Form 10-Q, MD&A — Gross Margin; Q2 FY2026 earnings call, prepared remarks and Q&A]

**Supplier / input concentration.** No individual **brand** accounted for more than 5% of net purchases in FY2025, FY2024, or FY2023; that is not the same as a vendor-concentration disclosure. The company says it is not dependent on one supplier, but it does not disclose the largest supplier's share of purchases or cost of sales, the top-three share, or a sole-source critical input. It had $2,558.5 million of purchase commitments for goods not yet received at August 1, 2026, but the filing does not allocate those commitments by supplier. The underlying risk is merchandise availability rather than a named raw material: vendors can limit retail channels, revise terms, or reduce excess inventory; although Burlington buys a majority from U.S.-based vendors, production occurs primarily overseas and is exposed to tariff changes. [FY2025 Form 10-K, Item 1 — Our Off-Price Sourcing and Merchandising Model; Item 1A — Risk Factors: merchandise supply and international operations; Q2 FY2026 Form 10-Q, Commitments]

## 3. Customer Pricing Power

Burlington has not shown that it can raise prices without a volume cost. The FY2025 10-K says it must keep branded merchandise discounted to remain competitive, and that full-price and specialty retailers can mark down to prices near Burlington's. Management explicitly plans to put the roughly $55 million Q2 tariff refund into lower prices / sharper value, not raise prices; this is the one concrete pricing action in the admitted last-24-month evidence. Q2 comparable-store sales rose 2%, driven mainly by basket size while transactions were roughly flat, but that print predates the planned tariff-refund price investment and does not show a consumer reaction to a price increase or decrease. [FY2025 Form 10-K, Item 1A — Competition and Margin Pressure; Q2 FY2026 earnings call, prepared remarks and Q&A]

## 4. Economic Control Verdict

**Mixed.** Burlington has real procurement flexibility: no brand exceeded 5% of net purchases in each of the past three fiscal years, it can buy in season and shift categories or suppliers, and it runs its own distribution path to stores. But its economics remain conditional on a continuing pool of discounted branded goods and on maintaining a visible consumer price gap; vendors have no obligation to provide supply or price, and shoppers have many alternatives. [FY2025 Form 10-K, Item 1 — Our Off-Price Sourcing and Merchandising Model; Item 1A — Risk Factors and Competition]

## 5. The Single Biggest Bargaining Risk

**Availability of attractive discounted branded merchandise from third-party vendors:** if vendors restrict channels, raise terms, or reduce excess inventory, Burlington can lose the product-value gap that brings shoppers into stores before it can replace that supply. [FY2025 Form 10-K, Item 1A — Risk Factors: merchandise supply]

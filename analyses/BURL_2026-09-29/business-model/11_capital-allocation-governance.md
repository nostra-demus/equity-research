## 2. Classification

**Standard professional management** — the evidence shows formal governance controls, stable senior leadership, repurchases that exceed equity issuance on the reported share-count measures, no disclosed related-person transactions, and no serial-acquirer pattern. The counterweight is an unusually large organic capex program, a longer cash-conversion cycle, and substantial lease commitments; these make the allocation outcome dependent on store and supply-chain returns rather than on a governance failure already visible in the filings. [FY2026 Proxy, Corporate Governance; Certain Relationships and Related Person Transactions, p.74; FY2025 10-K, Item 7 — Capital Expenditures, p.33]

## 3. Most Material Signal

The most material signal is capex intensity versus the cash the business generates. FY2025 operating cash flow was $1,231.4m against reported capex of $1,104.1m (including accrued capex), leaving $127.3m on that reported basis; in H1 FY2026, $334.6m of operating cash flow versus $426.7m reported capex (also including accruals) was a $92.1m shortfall. The half-year comparison is seasonal and therefore not a forecast, but a persistent shortfall after a full fiscal year, or no improvement in cash conversion as new stores and distribution centres mature, would move the classification to **Capital allocation concerns**. [FY2025 10-K, Item 7 — Cash Flows and Capital Expenditures, p.33; Q2 FY2026 10-Q, Item 2 — Cash Flow and Capital Expenditures, p.30]

## 4. Capital Allocation Score /100

**66 /100 (higher = better).** This is the weighted inverse of the signal severities, placing extra weight on capex, debt including leases, off-balance-sheet commitments, and working capital. No serial-acquirer cap applies: the acquisition-pattern severity is 8/100, not 70/100 or higher. The score is supported by clean disclosed related-person and audit reads, stable leadership, and a declining reported diluted share count, but held in the standard-management band by the $1.104bn FY2025 organic investment program, cash conversion that lengthened through FY2025, and the $3.993bn operating-lease liability.

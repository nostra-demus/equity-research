# Business Model Reality Check — BURL (Synthesis)

## Abstract

Burlington Stores is a U.S. off-price retailer that sells branded apparel, footwear, home goods and other merchandise through 1,287 physical stores. Its sole retail segment produces all reported revenue, with growth coming from new stores and from transactions and basket size at mature stores. The strongest positive is flexible in-season sourcing and an FY2025 gross-margin gain of 60 basis points to 43.8%. The central weakness is that store-level value creation remains unproven while FY2025 capital spending reached $1.104 billion, or 9.5% of revenue. No automatic disqualifier triggered, but the evidence supports an average business worth deeper work only if valuation is cheap.

### Module Disconfirmation

- **Strongest bear point:** FY2025 capital expenditure was $1,104.1m, or 9.5% of revenue and 2.6 times depreciation and amortization, yet the filings do not provide store contribution, build cost or cohort payback. [FY2025 Form 10-K, Item 7 — Capital Expenditures; Unit Economics — BURL, §2–3]
- **Strongest bull point:** Burlington can buy much of its merchandise in season and shift among suppliers and categories; no brand exceeded 5% of net purchases in the last three fiscal years. [FY2025 Form 10-K, Item 1 — Our Off-Price Sourcing and Merchandising Model]
- **Single killer risk:** A disruption to merchandise flow during the higher-sales second half could combine supplier availability risk with a concentrated network in which six distribution centres handled more than 99% of FY2025 units. [FY2025 Form 10-K, Item 1 — Seasonality and Distribution and Warehousing, p.2; Item 1A — Distribution Network Risk]
- **Disconfirming evidence already visible:** Gross margin rose 250 basis points, cash conversion rose 22.4 percentage points and store count rose 38.8% from the FY2022 anchor, but EBIT margin fell 70 basis points and ROIC fell 170 basis points; this split prevents either a widening-moat or confirmed-erosion conclusion. [CIQ Financials → Income Statement, Cash Flow and Ratios, retrieved 2026-09-29 — vendor export; Q2 FY2026 Form 10-Q, MD&A — Gross Margin]

## 3. Reconciliation

- **FY2025 revenue:** The audited filing reports $11,566.910m, while the CIQ segment and geographic read reports $11,559m, a $7.910m or 0.07% difference. The filing governs; both sources still show one segment and 100% U.S. revenue. [FY2025 Form 10-K, Note 1 — Segment Information; CIQ Financials → Segments, 12 months ended 2026-01-31 — vendor export]
- **Supplier-graph row count:** Data triage says the graph holds zero disclosed relationships, while customer-geography, value-chain and capital-allocation consistently report 33 recently disclosed supplier-view rows, including 31 outside parties and zero intra-group rows. This synthesis uses the value-chain specialist's scoped row-level read because that specialist owns the graph; it treats the 33 rows only as proof of named relationships, never as a full supplier census or evidence of materiality. [CIQ Suppliers export (BURL), retrieved 2026-09-29 — vendor export, scope notes]
- **New-store economics:** The business-identity report carries management's estimate that recent new stores can exceed $7m of annual sales with payback under two years, while unit-economics says payback is not disclosed in the filings. These are different evidence levels, not a clean contradiction: the estimate remains a transcript claim, and filed unit-level value creation is unproven without cohort build cost and contribution after occupancy and payroll. [Q2 FY2026 earnings call, Q&A; FY2025 Form 10-K, Item 1 — Store Expansion and Real Estate Strategy; Item 7 — Capital Expenditures]
- **Margin and moat direction:** FY2022-to-LTM gross margin, cash conversion and store count improved, but EBIT margin and ROIC fell. The adverse capital-efficiency measures prevent the operating gains from being called a widening moat; the positive measures prevent the declines from being called confirmed erosion. The reconciled trajectory is mixed and not assessable. [CIQ Financials → Income Statement, Cash Flow and Ratios, retrieved 2026-09-29 — vendor export]
- **Reported margin bases:** FY2025 audited gross margin of 43.8%, Q2 FY2026 reported gross margin of 46.2%, and CIQ LTM gross margin of 44.1% cover different periods and bases. Q2 includes a $55.5m tariff-refund benefit, so it is not substituted for the audited annual or LTM reads. [FY2025 Form 10-K, Item 7 — Gross Margin; Q2 FY2026 Form 10-Q, MD&A — Gross Margin; CIQ Financials → Income Statement, retrieved 2026-09-29 — vendor export]

## 4. Note To The Final Synthesizer

- **Strongest business-model positive:** Burlington can buy much merchandise in season and shift among suppliers and categories; no brand exceeded 5% of net purchases in FY2023–FY2025. [FY2025 Form 10-K, Item 1 — Our Off-Price Sourcing and Merchandising Model]
- **Strongest business-model negative:** The business is investing heavily without filed store-cohort returns: FY2025 capex was $1,104.1m, while store contribution, net build cost and payback are undisclosed. [FY2025 Form 10-K, Item 7 — Capital Expenditures; Unit Economics — BURL, §2–3]
- **Most important segment:** The sole U.S. off-price retail segment accounts for all reported revenue and profit; there is no separate product, channel or store-cohort P&L. [FY2025 Form 10-K, Note 1 — Segment Information]
- **Cleanest unit-economics read:** Implied gross profit per average store rose to $4.36m from $4.34m, but that is not contribution because payroll, occupancy and other store costs sit below gross profit; value creation per new store cannot be established. [FY2025 Form 10-K, Item 7 — Key Performance and Non-GAAP Measures / Results of Operations]
- **Position versus named peers:** BURL's LTM EBIT margin of 7.8% trails Ross at 12.7% and TJX at 12.2%. BURL's latest LTM ROIC is 7.8%, but comparable peer ROIC and a source-bound BURL cost of capital are unavailable, so relative capital efficiency and the economic moat test remain unproven. [CIQ Comparable Analysis — Operating Statistics, as of 2026-09-28 — vendor export; CIQ Financials → Ratios, retrieved 2026-09-29 — vendor export]
- **Main external dependency:** Discretionary spending by the low-income core customer is the main operating lever, with weather and tariff policy adding demand and cost volatility; no quantified operating sensitivity is disclosed for those variables. [Q2 FY2026 Form 10-Q, MD&A — Uncertainties and Challenges, p.22; FY2025 Form 10-K, Item 1A, pp.6–11]
- **Most important capital-allocation signal:** The expansion program consumed $1,104.1m in FY2025, versus $1,231.4m of operating cash flow, leaving only $127.3m on the reported basis before buybacks and financing; governance itself shows no serial-acquirer or related-party pattern. [FY2025 Form 10-K, Item 7 — Cash Flows and Capital Expenditures, p.33; FY2026 Proxy, Certain Relationships and Related Person Transactions, p.74]
- **Automatic disqualifier:** None triggered. The largest-customer test remains not assessable because no customer share is disclosed; that gap is not evidence of concentration. [FY2025 Form 10-K, Item 1 — Business; Note 1 — Revenue Recognition]
- **Rejector filters and caps:** Crooks/integrity did not trip; serial acquirer did not trip because acquisition-pattern severity is 8/100; fast-changing industry did not trip because rate-of-change scores 45/100, above the ≤40 threshold. No rejector-filter cap applies. [FY2025 Form 10-K, Report of Independent Registered Public Accounting Firm; FY2025 Form 10-K, Item 7 — Capital Expenditures; FY2025 Form 10-K, Item 1A — Competition]
- **Mandatory red flag — distribution-network concentration (severity 55/100; higher = worse):** Six distribution centres shipped more than 99% of FY2025 units, with three in New Jersey and three in Southern California. Georgia and Arizona capacity may mitigate this, but the current unit split is not disclosed. [FY2025 Form 10-K, Item 1 — Distribution and Warehousing, p.2; Item 1A — Distribution Network Risk]
- **Biggest missing data point:** A new-store cohort schedule showing net build cost, contribution after occupancy and payroll, and cash payback by vintage.
- **Why deeper work is justified:** The model is clear, current filings are sufficient and no hard disqualifier is visible, but the business only clears the average-quality bar; valuation must compensate for unproven cohort returns, weaker peer margins and external dependence.
- **What would change the answer:** Audited cohort returns and a sustained rise in gross-capital ROIC and EBIT margin would support a higher-quality view. Persistent full-year operating cash flow below capex, falling ROIC as stores mature, or a material distribution disruption would move the view toward low-quality. [FY2025 Form 10-K, Item 7 — Cash Flows and Capital Expenditures; CIQ Financials → Ratios, retrieved 2026-09-29 — vendor export; FY2025 Form 10-K, Item 1A — Distribution Network Risk]

## 5. Simple Summary

- Burlington sells branded apparel, footwear, home goods and other merchandise at discounted prices through physical stores. [FY2025 Form 10-K, Item 1 — Business Overview]
- It makes money by opening stores and increasing transactions and basket size at mature stores; Q2 FY2026 comparable sales rose 2%, mainly from basket growth while transactions were roughly flat. [Q2 FY2026 earnings call, prepared remarks and Q&A]
- Whether each new store creates value is not proven from filed data because store contribution, net build cost and cohort payback are missing. [FY2025 Form 10-K, Item 7 — Capital Expenditures]
- The single U.S. off-price retail segment is the whole business and produces all reported revenue and profit. [FY2025 Form 10-K, Note 1 — Segment Information]
- The moat is narrow: distribution and sourcing flexibility help, but BURL's margin trails Ross and TJX and customers face no switching cost. [FY2025 Form 10-K, Item 1 — Distribution and Warehousing / Competition; CIQ Comparable Analysis — Operating Statistics, as of 2026-09-28 — vendor export]
- The main outside variables are low-income consumer spending, weather, tariffs, freight and overseas merchandise availability. [FY2025 Form 10-K, Item 1A, pp.6–11; Q2 FY2026 Form 10-Q, MD&A — Uncertainties and Challenges, p.22]
- Capital allocation is clean in form but demanding in substance: organic capex, leases and working capital absorb large amounts of cash. [FY2025 Form 10-K, Item 7 — Capital Expenditures; Q2 FY2026 Form 10-Q, Condensed Consolidated Balance Sheets]
- It deserves deeper work only if valuation pays for average quality, unproven store-level returns and a concentrated distribution network.

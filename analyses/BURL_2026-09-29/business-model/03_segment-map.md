## 2. Dominant Segment

Burlington is effectively a single-segment business: its U.S. off-price retail operation is 100% of reported FY2025 revenue and profit, so it dominates by both measures. Apparel, footwear, home and the other merchandise categories are product categories within that one economic segment, not separately reported businesses. The Q2 FY2026 filing retains the same one-segment definition. [FY2025 Form 10-K, Note 1 — Segment Information; Q2 FY2026 Form 10-Q, Note 1 — Segment Reporting]

## 3. Segment Disclosure Quality

Disclosure is consistent in the two annual filings available for FY2024 and FY2025, and in the Q2 FY2026 10-Q: each reports one segment. There is no separate `Other` or `Corporate` segment; `other segment expenses` is a cost line within the sole segment — $2,965.3m, or 25.6% of FY2025 total revenue — covering store-related costs, store payroll, corporate costs, marketing and other store/selling costs. The CODM receives net income and total assets for the one segment, but Burlington does not disclose profit, assets, capital expenditure or margins by product category, channel or store cohort; those economics are therefore not assessable from the filing. The frozen pool contains no investor deck. The Capital IQ facts sidecar's source-bound read also shows one `Retail - Apparel` segment at $11,559m (100%); this is $7.9m (0.07%) below the filing's $11,566.910m total revenue. The filing governs; the extractor does not explain the small vendor-to-filing difference, and it does not change the 100% share conclusion. No change is visible across FY2024, FY2025 and Q2 FY2026, but a FY2023 filing is not in the frozen pool, so a full three-year change test is not proven from available data.

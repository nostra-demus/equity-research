## 2. What the Company Does

Delivery Hero runs local food-delivery and quick-commerce apps in about 70 countries — talabat, Baemin, foodpanda, Glovo, and PedidosYa. Two things earn money:

1. It takes a cut of each restaurant order placed through its apps (a commission), plus a growing slice of advertising revenue.
2. It increasingly buys groceries itself and resells them to you (quick-commerce), so it earns a retail margin, not just a commission.

Rough revenue model: **orders × average order value × take-rate (its cut), plus its own grocery sales, plus ad income.**

The one fact that dominates everything: on 2026-07-16 Uber announced an offer to buy Delivery Hero, guided to close in the second half of 2027. Most of what has happened to the share price since is about that deal, not about the underlying business.

## 5. Why It Could Fail — Bear Case & The Killer Risk

The risks that most push the thesis down:

1. **The offer price is unverified.** Every base- and bull-case number inherits one fragile assumption — the ~€41.50 price — which is web-sourced only and appears in no primary document [Claim Quality Level 0]. If the true price is lower, or is cash-plus-stock rather than all cash, the expected return falls further.
2. **Auditor going-concern warning on Glovo Spain.** KPMG's audit report carries a dedicated section, "Material Uncertainty about the Ability of Subsidiaries to Continue as a Going Concern," tied to a Spanish rider-classification case, contingent liability €440–770m [FY24 Annual Report, Independent Auditor's Report]. The FY2025 cash outflow for this matter (€524.0m) already exceeded the FY2024 provision (€492.2m) — evidence the cost is real and growing.
3. **Debt already past its own ceiling on an audited basis.** Net debt is 2.78x adjusted earnings but 8.24x on audited (GAAP) reported earnings — already above the analyst's illustrative 6.0x refinancing ceiling. Cash conversion collapsed to 8.8% in FY2025, so the friendlier adjusted figure is not backed by cash.
4. **Poor-quality, cash-light earnings.** Earnings quality scored 36/100. The "+€250m free cash flow" headline for FY2025 leaves out an implied ~€600–650m cash outflow tied to the same Spain exposure, and that exclusion method is now FY2026 guidance policy [`earnings/06`, `08`]. Two Critical-severity red flags sit here.
5. **A history of value-destroying deals.** About €2,054m of cumulative goodwill write-offs (2021–2025) against the Woowa and Glovo mega-deals; share count up 49.6% over five years with zero buybacks; book value per share down 75.4% [`management-governance/02`].

**The killer risk, in one sentence:** a blocked or broken Uber deal — no confirmed price, no disclosed break fee — sends the stock back toward standalone risk while the Glovo Spain going-concern liability (up to €770m) and 8.24x audited-basis leverage remain unresolved.

## 14. Plain-English Glossary

- **Merger arbitrage / arbitrage spread** — buying a takeover target below the offer price to pocket the gap when the deal closes; the spread is that gap (here ~11.6%). Only real money if the deal completes at the assumed price.
- **Going-concern warning** — an auditor's formal note that there is real doubt a business can keep operating and pay its bills; here it is on the Spanish subsidiary, not the whole group.
- **Contingent liability** — a possible future bill that depends on an outcome (here, a Spanish court/regulator ruling); up to €770m.
- **Net debt (strict basis)** — total debt minus cash (€4,625.5m − €2,112.7m = €2,512.8m); how much debt is left if all cash were used to pay it down.
- **Leverage / net debt to EBITDA (8.24x GAAP vs 2.78x adjusted)** — debt measured against yearly earnings; 8.24x means debt is about eight years of audited earnings. GAAP = audited-accounts basis; adjusted = the company's own add-back version.
- **EBITDA** — earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation; a rough proxy for operating cash earnings before financing and accounting charges.
- **Cash conversion (CFO/adjusted EBITDA, 8.8%)** — how much of reported earnings actually shows up as operating cash; 8.8% means almost none did in FY2025.
- **Free cash flow (FCF)** — operating cash flow minus capital spending; the cash left over for debt, dividends, or buybacks.
- **Take-rate** — the cut Delivery Hero keeps from each order routed through its apps.
- **Margin of safety** — the cushion between the price you pay and estimated fair value; bigger cushion, more room to be wrong.
- **Reverse-DCF ("what's priced in")** — working backwards from the share price to find the growth rate the market must be assuming (here ~16.5% a year for seven years).
- **Fair value (bull/base/bear)** — an estimate of what a share is worth under good, middle, and bad assumptions; a range, not a single number.
- **Moat** — a durable competitive advantage that protects profits; "no moat proven" means the evidence does not show one at group level.
- **ROIC / cost of capital** — the profit earned on each €100 put into the business (0.8%–1.6% best year) versus what that money costs to raise (10.7%–13.7%); earning less than the cost destroys value.
- **Goodwill impairment** — writing down the premium paid for an acquisition when it turns out to be worth less; €2,054m written off over 2021–2025.
- **Squeeze-out / domination agreement** — German rules that let a controlling owner buy out or override remaining minority shareholders once it crosses ownership thresholds.
- **Change-of-control vesting** — a pay term where executives' unvested shares fully vest if they leave after a takeover; a personal reason to favour the deal closing.
- **Convertible bonds** — debt that can turn into shares; if it converts, it dilutes existing shareholders (~23% of market cap here).
- **Break fee** — a penalty one side pays if it walks away from an agreed deal; none is disclosed here.
- **Basis points (bps)** — hundredths of a percent; 100 bps = 1%.

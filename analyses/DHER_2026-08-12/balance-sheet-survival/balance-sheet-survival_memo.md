## What This Module Found

Delivery Hero carries real debt: €2,512.8m net (total debt minus cash, strict basis) against €4,625.5m gross. How heavy that looks depends entirely on which profit measure you use. It is 2.78x net debt to the company's own adjusted profit (Adjusted EBITDA, €903.0m — the company's preferred earnings measure before certain costs), which looks manageable, but 8.24x its GAAP profit (Reported EBITDA, €304.9m — the audited accounting measure). That is a €598.1m gap between the two, and the GAAP number matters because cash generation was weak: cash conversion fell to 8.8% in FY2025, and operating cash flow (€79.5m) covered only 0.32x — less than a third — of the €246.5m interest actually paid in cash.

The single most important positive: the debt due in the next two years (€877.4m, 18.97% of the total) has already been repaid after the report date, using a new $1.4bn term loan due 2032 plus cash, executed by April 2026. That near-term wall is handled. The real unaddressed risk sits in 2029, where 46.38% of the debt (€2,145.4m) clusters in a single year, secured, floating-rate, and not repayable in euros — with no refinancing plan disclosed yet.

The single most important risk is a compound one, not a simple profit drop. The company survives even a full one-year wipeout of profit on cash grounds alone. But the disclosed Spanish rider-reclassification dispute (a labor-law matter) carries an unrecognized potential cost of €520m–€860m, on top of €492.2m already provisioned and €524.0m already paid. The auditor added a going-concern warning naming the Spanish subsidiary (Glovoapp Spain Platform S.L.U.) directly. The stress test shows a 40% profit decline plus the top of the Spain range flips a €507.8m liquidity surplus into a €352.2m shortfall.

## The Specialists, Briefly

- **Solvency-data-triage:** Sufficient data — recent balance sheet, instrument-level debt note, and full cash flow all present; two narrow gaps (numeric covenant threshold, revolver mechanics). No FY2025 audited Annual Report PDF in the pool.
- **Capital-structure-and-leverage:** Net leverage 2.78x (Adjusted) / 8.24x (Reported); DHER is not net cash — it carries substantial debt, and the read is roughly 3x basis-dependent.
- **Maturity-wall-and-refinancing:** Near-term wall small and already covered by the executed refinancing; the real structural risk is the 2029 cluster (46.38% of debt), plus a possible Uber-deal change-of-control put.
- **Liquidity-runway:** ~18.3 months, resting almost entirely on cash already on the balance sheet, not on future cash flow — a conservative and, here, favorable stance given FY2025 normalized free cash flow was negative (−€246.3m).
- **Coverage-and-covenants:** Coverage is weak on GAAP (EBITDA/interest 0.80x) and thin on Adjusted (2.36x); cash coverage of interest was only 0.32x.
- **Off-balance-sheet-and-contingencies:** Largest live exposure is the Spain matter (€520m–€860m unrecognized); RF-OBS-001 fired.
- **Downside-stress-test:** No mechanical break on a profit decline alone even at −100%; first breaks a 6.0x leverage ceiling at −53.6% (Adjusted basis) — already breached today on the Reported basis.

**Most important disagreement:** Reported vs Adjusted EBITDA is a real basis disagreement, not rounding — it swings net leverage from 2.78x to 8.24x. Per doctrine (audited/GAAP numbers over management-adjusted), the synthesis treats the Reported-EBITDA basis as the conservative anchor and set the verdict (Stretched, not Adequate) with that read carrying real weight. The independent 8.8% cash-conversion finding corroborates that the Adjusted figure is not fully cash-backed.

## What Would Change This Read

**Would strengthen it:** a confirmed numeric covenant threshold showing wide, certifiable headroom; a disclosed refinancing plan for the 2029 cluster; sustained positive normalized free cash flow over 2+ quarters, closing the 8.8% cash-conversion gap; resolution of the Spain matter without further cash outflows above what is provisioned.

**Would weaken it:** a further large Spain-related payment beyond the €520m–€860m range; a change-of-control put triggered by the Uber deal pulling forward convertible-bond maturities; sustained continuation of FY2025-style negative operating free cash flow; confirmation that covenant "liquidity" excludes the pledged cash pool, narrowing true headroom.

**Data needed:** the FY2025 audited Annual Report (the pool holds only the FY2024 vintage); the exact numeric covenant threshold and its definition; guarantor-level detail for the Dollar/KRW Term Facilities; and explicit change-of-control clause text for the convertible bonds.

## Bottom Line

- **Verdict: Stretched.** Meaningfully levered, survives on cash, but already past a safe leverage line on GAAP terms.
- **Why it could be better than it looks:** the near-term debt wall is already repaid, the revolver (€461.8m undrawn) is excluded from the liquidity figure so the true cushion may be larger, and a Taiwan disposal expected to bring ~€520m at closing is earmarked mainly for debt reduction.
- **Why it could be worse than it looks:** on the GAAP profit measure, leverage is 8.24x — already above the 6.0x ceiling the stress test uses as a break point — and cash covered only 0.32x of the interest bill.
- **What evidence is missing:** the FY2025 audited Annual Report — needed for the exact covenant threshold, updated Spain figures, and guarantor detail on the secured term loans.
- **The one thing to watch next:** the Spanish rider-reclassification dispute — a further cash payment beyond the disclosed range, especially if it lands alongside a profit downturn, is the realistic path from surplus to breach.

## Plain-English Glossary

- **Net debt (strict basis):** total borrowings minus cash on hand.
- **Adjusted EBITDA:** the company's own profit measure before certain costs it treats as one-off; flatters the leverage ratio.
- **Reported (GAAP) EBITDA:** the audited accounting profit measure; the conservative anchor here.
- **Net leverage:** net debt divided by a year's profit — how many years of profit the debt equals.
- **Cash conversion:** how much of reported profit actually shows up as operating cash (8.8% here means very little did).
- **Maturity wall:** a large chunk of debt all coming due in a short window.
- **Covenant / covenant headroom:** a rule in the loan agreement the company must keep meeting; headroom is the safety margin before it breaks.
- **Revolver:** a standing backup credit line the company can draw on.
- **Change-of-control put:** a clause letting lenders demand early repayment if the company is taken over.
- **Normalized free cash flow:** operating cash minus capital spending, after stripping out one-offs; negative here means the business burned cash.

## 2. Triggered Disqualifiers — Detail

**#1 — Auditor going-concern note (subsidiary-level, opinion unmodified).** KPMG AG's Independent Auditor's Report on the FY2024 consolidated financial statements contains a standalone section headed "Material Uncertainty about the Ability of Subsidiaries to Continue as a Going Concern." [FY24 Annual Report, Independent Auditor's Report, p.222] It states that at subsidiary Glovoapp Spain Platform S.L.U. ("Glovo Spain"), ongoing Spanish social-security investigations into the legal classification of riders under an earlier business model could result in back-payment of social insurance contributions and fines; a contingent liability of €440–770 million for a further investigation is disclosed in the notes, and "these events and conditions indicate considerable uncertainty that may cast significant doubt on the subsidiary's ability to continue its business activities... within the meaning of Section 322 (2) sentence 3 HGB." [FY24 Annual Report, p.223] The auditor explicitly states the Group-level opinions on the consolidated financial statements and the combined management report "have not been modified in this regard," so this is not a qualified opinion and not a whole-Group going-concern qualification. It is nonetheless a hard, named, sourced fact that meets the literal disqualifier-scan trigger ("qualified opinion OR going-concern note"): the auditor itself, not just management, flagged a going-concern risk tied to a real (and large — up to €770m) contingent liability at a material operating subsidiary (Glovo). Per this module's rules, a hard-evidence trigger of this kind locks the verdict regardless of business quality; the synthesizer should note the trigger's precise scope (subsidiary-level, unmodified Group opinion) alongside the lock.

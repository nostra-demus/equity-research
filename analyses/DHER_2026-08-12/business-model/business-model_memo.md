# Business Model Module Memo — DHER

**Verdict: Low-quality business — avoid deeper work** — the outside auditor flagged a going-concern-level "material uncertainty" at the Glovo Spain arm tied to up to €770m of rider-pay liability, which locks the module here regardless of how good the Middle East business looks [FY24 Annual Report, Independent Auditor's Report, p.222–223].

*Memo date: 2026-08-12. Source: `99_business-model-synthesis.md` (adjudicated).*

Delivery Hero runs local food-delivery and quick-commerce apps (talabat, Baemin, foodpanda, Glovo, PedidosYa) in roughly 70 countries. It takes a cut of restaurant orders and, increasingly, buys and resells groceries itself from its own small warehouses. This is a shareable condensation of the module synthesis — no new numbers or analysis.

## What This Module Found

The controlling fact is the auditor's report. KPMG attached a standalone "Material Uncertainty about the Ability of Subsidiaries to Continue as a Going Concern" note to Glovo Spain, tied to a Spanish social-security investigation into whether riders are freelancers or employees, with a contingent liability (a possible future bill that isn't yet on the books) of €440–770m [FY24 Annual Report, Auditor's Report, p.222–223]. The group-level audit opinion itself is clean, but this note trips disqualifier #1 and locks the verdict.

The bull point the synthesis credits is real but narrow: the Middle East (MENA) business, run mostly through the 80%-owned talabat, produced 68.3% of the group's FY2024 Adjusted EBITDA — €472.9m of €692.5m — at a 13.4% segment margin, up from 11.3% the year before, even as sales volume (GMV, the total value of orders) grew 28.8% [FY24 Annual Report, "Key Figures," p.4]. Adjusted EBITDA is the company's own measure of operating profit before interest, tax, depreciation and several one-off items.

The overriding negative is that the whole group earns less on its money than that money costs. Return on capital (ROIC — the profit earned on each €100 invested) was only about 0.8%–1.6% in its best year (FY2025) and about −6.1% averaged over five years, against the company's own stated 10.7%–13.7%+ cost of capital (WACC — what its funding costs to raise). A business earning below its cost of capital destroys value even before the governance and disqualifier problems [`09_moat.md`].

The single biggest business-model risk is rider reclassification. The same freelance-vs-employee issue behind the going-concern note runs through 93.7% of group delivery expense (€5,644.3m of €6,022.2m) — the largest single cost in the business. A shift from freelance to employed riders across the EU or Latin America would turn a flexible cost into a regulator-set one [`06_value-chain.md`, §5].

## The Specialists, Briefly

- **data-triage** — Sufficient, but no audited FY2025 report or interim filing exists; DHER is a live Uber acquisition target (deal expected to close H2 2027), so this is not a clean standalone read.
- **disqualifier-scan** — Verdict-locked: auditor going-concern note on Glovo Spain, rider liability €440–770m.
- **business-identity** — A two-sided app marketplace turning itself into an owned-inventory grocery retailer, plus a fast-growing ad business (~€1.5bn revenue run rate). Own-delivery rose from 67% to 78% of orders in one year.
- **segment-map** — MENA dominates profit (68.3%) though Asia is bigger by revenue; a €683.0m unallocated gap sits between segment profit and the audited group loss.
- **unit-economics** — Cannot be built at the per-order level: DHER discloses no order count, average order value, or cost to win a customer.
- **customer-geography** — Concentrated by geography, not customer: South Korea alone is 23.8% of FY2024 revenue.
- **value-chain** — Price-setter in MENA and ads, price-taker in Asia, grocery, and over rider labor; 93.7% of delivery cost runs through freelance riders.
- **business-quality** — 34/100 (weak); industry rate-of-change scored 26, tripping Filter 5; FY2025's first-ever operating profit is a fragile, recent inflection.
- **competitive-map** — Holding/gaining share in MENA, losing share in Asia; Uber is both its closest Middle East rival and its pending acquirer.
- **moat** — No moat proven; ROIC below cost of capital every year for five years.
- **external-dependency** — 52/100 (inverted): two High-rated dependencies (currency/inflation across ~85% of non-euro revenue, and rider regulation) plus the Uber merger-approval overlay.
- **capital-allocation-governance** — 42/100, capped at 50 by Filter 4: serial-acquirer pattern (~€2.05bn of goodwill write-downs 2021–2025), €770m off-balance-sheet contingent liability, heavy board/management turnover including a seven-month CFO vacancy.
- **red-flags-sweep** — 8 disclosed data breaches in FY2024 (all personal data, 35,343 users, severity 42); one-off gains (€220.9m Uber breakup fee, €99.3m debt-modification gain) flattered FY2024 profit.

**Most important disagreement:** two specialists cited different FY2024 Adjusted EBITDA figures — €692.5m vs €791.3m. The synthesis reconciled to **€692.5m** because four of five reports converge on it and it sums exactly to the five segments (MENA €472.9m + Asia €385.1m − Integrated Verticals €98.7m − Europe €77.0m + Americas €10.3m). The €791.3m figure and its leverage ratios should be treated as unreconciled; the corrected figure pushes net-debt-to-Adjusted-EBITDA leverage modestly higher (roughly 2.7x–3.6x), still moderate, not distressed.

## What Would Change This Read

The synthesis says the answer to "does this deserve deeper work?" is **No, not at present** — the disqualifier lock governs. What would change it:

- **Primary evidence the Glovo Spain going-concern matter is resolved** — a favorable court ruling, a capped settlement, or removal of the auditor's material-uncertainty language in a later audited filing, with the contingent liability materially reduced.
- **Resolution of the live Uber acquisition** — a completed deal turns this from a standalone equity question into a merger-arbitrage one.
- Absent those, even without the lock, weak business quality (34), no proven group moat, and the Filter-4-capped capital-allocation score would keep this in "avoid deeper work" territory.

---

## Bottom Line

- **Verdict: Low-quality business — avoid deeper work.** The auditor's going-concern note on Glovo Spain locks it, whatever the Middle East strength.
- **Could be better than it looks:** MENA/talabat earns a 13.4% margin, expanding while volume grew 28.8%, and beats the ~7% margin Uber's own CEO put on Uber's comparable Middle East business.
- **Could be worse than it looks:** the group earns below its own cost of capital every year for five years, and a broader rider-employment shift would hit 93.7% of delivery cost on top of the €440–770m Spain liability.
- **Missing evidence:** no audited FY2025 report, and no order count or average order value anywhere — so true per-order economics cannot be proven.
- **Watch next:** any court ruling or settlement on the Glovo Spain rider case, and merger-control approval of the Uber deal.

## Plain-English Glossary

- **Going-concern / material uncertainty note** — an auditor's warning that a business unit's ability to keep operating is in doubt.
- **Contingent liability** — a possible future bill that isn't yet recorded on the balance sheet because it depends on an outcome (here, a court case).
- **Adjusted EBITDA** — the company's own measure of operating profit before interest, tax, depreciation, and several one-off items; not an official accounting figure.
- **GMV (gross merchandise value)** — the total value of orders placed through the apps.
- **Margin** — profit as a percentage of sales.
- **ROIC (return on capital)** — the profit earned on each €100 of money invested in the business.
- **Cost of capital (WACC)** — what the company's funding costs to raise; earning below it destroys value.
- **Moat** — a durable competitive edge that lets a company keep earning above-average returns.
- **Net debt** — total borrowings minus cash (here cited on a cash-netted "broad" basis).
- **Goodwill impairment** — a write-down admitting the company overpaid for a business it bought.

# Capital Allocation & Governance — DHER

**Context note:** Delivery Hero SE (Frankfurt Prime Standard, XTRA:DHER; SE — Societas Europaea, IFRS as adopted by the EU, reporting currency EUR, fiscal year end Dec-31) is, as of this report's date, the subject of a live, announced acquisition offer from Uber Technologies (call held Jul 16, 2026), with the reference shareholder Prosus/Naspers having "irrevocably committed to tender its stake" [Uber Technologies, Inc., Delivery Hero SE - M&A Call, Jul 16 2026, transcript p.4]. This is not a steady-state standalone company at the current date, and the findings below should be read with that overlay in mind. The most recent audited source in the pool is the FY2024 Annual Report (published Apr 25, 2025); FY2025 figures come from the Mar 26, 2026 earnings-call transcript (Tier 6) and Capital IQ workbook exports (Tier 5), not an audited filing.

## 2. Classification

**Governance red flags.** The signal table shows elevated severities that are not confined to a single line item: a historically serial, debt- and stock-funded acquisition program that has produced roughly €2.05bn of cumulative goodwill write-downs across nearly every acquired unit (severity 75); a large unrecognized contingent liability of up to €770m tied to Spain rider-classification risk sitting alongside a subsidiary-level "material uncertainty" going-concern emphasis-of-matter from the auditor (severities 65 and 45); heavy management and Supervisory Board churn concentrated in a single year, including a seven-month CFO vacancy at the Management Board level (severity 55); and ~50% dilution in shares outstanding over five years with no offsetting group-level buyback (severity 58). Capex discipline, related-party exposure, and the dividend stance are the cleaner rows. Layered on top, the company is now the subject of a live takeover approach from Uber with the reference shareholder Prosus already committed to tender — a fact that does not itself worsen historical governance, but confirms this is not a steady-state, standalone capital-allocation picture at the current date.

## 3. Most Material Signal

The acquisition pattern is the single most material signal, and — per the rejector-filter rule (CLAUDE.md §24, Filter 4) — it caps the Capital Allocation Score below regardless of the cleaner rows. Delivery Hero built its current group through a multi-year run of large, debt- and stock-funded acquisitions (Woowa Brothers, Glovo built up to 99.4% ownership over three years, various country-level bolt-ons), and roughly €2.05bn of goodwill has been written off against those same deals from 2021 to 2025, spread across nearly every acquired cash-generating unit — Glovo platform, Glovo Dmart, LatAm platform, Europe platform, Europe Dmart, Asia Delivery, and Yemeksepeti. That is direct evidence the company paid more for these businesses than they turned out to be worth, and the pattern is close to textbook "serial acquirer" value destruction rather than one-off bad luck. If this pattern resumed — for example, if the strategic review referenced on the FY2025 earnings call ("we are being very diligent... methodology that has made us successful in the past" [Delivery Hero SE, 2025 Earnings Call transcript, Mar 26 2026, p.12]) turned into a new debt-funded acquisition spree rather than continued consolidation/divestiture (Foodpanda Taiwan sale, Digital Services SG stake sale), it would confirm rather than break the pattern and would argue for a still lower score.

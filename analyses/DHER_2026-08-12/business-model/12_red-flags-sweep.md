## 3. Most Severe New Flag

The most material new flag is the pattern of disclosed data breaches: 8 separate incidents in FY2024 alone, all involving personal data, affecting 35,343 users, on a platform that processes payment and location data for millions of consumers across 70+ countries with materially varying data-protection regimes. This sits on top of — and is a distinct exposure from — the regulatory and litigation risks already flagged upstream (rider classification, EU antitrust); a GDPR enforcement action or a larger-scale breach would be a new, separate cost and reputational event, not a continuation of the risks other specialists already priced in. The synthesizer should weight this as a standing operational-security tail risk that current disclosure shows is recurring, not isolated, even though the company's own risk rating for it eased year over year.

## 4. Cross-Cutting Patterns

Three items in this sweep and in the upstream capital-allocation-governance report point the same direction: a business whose reported statutory profitability is being smoothed by non-operating, one-off items running in both directions across periods — the €220.9m Uber Taiwan breakup-fee gain and €99.3m debt-modification gain in FY2024, against the €259.7m goodwill impairment in FY2025 and the historical €2.05bn of cumulative goodwill write-downs from the acquisition program (already flagged upstream, Filter 4). None of these individually is hidden — each is disclosed and explained — but together they mean DHER's FY2024–FY2025 statutory net-loss trajectory and "path to profitability" narrative should be read through normalized, one-off-adjusted figures, not the headline statutory numbers, when assessing whether the underlying operating business has actually turned a corner.

## 2. Input Cost Pass-Through

Delivery Hero's largest input cost — delivery expenses (external riders, third-party logistics, and DH's own rider fleet) — rose to 67.2% of total cost of sales in FY2024, up from 64.5% in FY2023, and cost of sales grew 28.6% year-over-year versus revenue growth of 23.7% [FY24 Annual Report, p.108–109]. The filing does not describe any contractual pass-through mechanism, escalator, or indexed-pricing clause tying commission or delivery-fee rates to rider cost inflation; instead, management's stated levers are mix shift (own-delivery capturing the fee itself) and operational efficiency ("order stacking levels" and logistics centralization "help to bring down the cost per order") [FY24 Annual Report, p.135]. Where a cost shock was regulatory rather than commercial, DH did not pass it through — it absorbed it: the Europe segment's FY2024 Adjusted EBITDA miss versus the Group's own 2024 target range (€692.5m actual vs. €725–775m guided) was specifically attributed to "additional expenses recognized for rider-related reclassification risks in Italy," and Europe's segment margin stayed negative (−4.1% Adjusted EBITDA/segment revenue) [FY24 Annual Report, p.4, p.106; Segment Map, §1]. The one demonstrated lever that functions like a price increase is discount reduction: vouchers fell from 8.1% to 6.9% of Total Segment Revenue in FY2024 while GMV still grew 7.7%, showing DH can pull back subsidies without an immediate, filing-visible volume loss at group level [FY24 Annual Report, p.109].

**Supplier / input concentration (quantify).** The dominant, quantified input dependency is on external/freelance labor for delivery, not on a raw-material supplier: 93.7% of FY2024 delivery expense (€5,644.3m of €6,022.2m) went to external riders and third-party logistics providers rather than DH's own employed fleet (€377.9m, 6.3%) [FY24 Annual Report, p.109]. This is a material single-category dependency — the company runs its core last-mile function overwhelmingly through a workforce it does not directly employ, which is precisely the population subject to the reclassification risk described in §1 and §5. For the Dmarts principal business, no supplier concentration is disclosed: there is no figure in the pool for the largest goods supplier's share of Dmarts cost of sales, no single-source input identified, and no wholesale-terms disclosure — "Not proven from available data," and this is itself a disclosure gap rather than evidence of low concentration.

## 3. Customer Pricing Power

The evidence is regionally split rather than uniform. In DH's strongest market, MENA (talabat), the company both raised its margin and pulled back discounting: MENA's Adjusted EBITDA/segment revenue rose to 13.4% in FY2024 from 11.3% in 2023, and management called the segment's performance "significantly better than expected" [FY24 Annual Report, p.4, p.105]. Loyalty tools reinforce this — subscription penetration reached up to 61% of GMV in Saudi Arabia by Q1 2026, and in Korea the newly relaunched subscription program is "nearly 50% of total order volumes" [Q1 2026 Trading Statement Call; FY2025 Earnings Call, Mar 26, 2026, Q&A]. Set against that, the Asia segment's GMV fell 7.7% in FY2024 "mainly driven by rising competition in certain regions," and Adjusted EBITDA there stayed flat at a lower 9.5% margin despite 9.2% revenue growth — direct evidence that DH lost volume rather than holding price when a rival undercut it in a specific market [FY24 Annual Report, p.4, p.105–106]. No explicit list-price or commission-rate increase in the last 24 months is disclosed in the pool with an associated volume read; the only quantified pricing-adjacent action is the voucher pull-back described in §2, which management ties to "improved" margins without a stated volume cost.

## 4. Economic Control Verdict

**Mixed.**

Delivery Hero sets price and captures rising margin in its dominant regional market (MENA, 13.4% Adjusted EBITDA/segment revenue and rising) and has built a fast-growing, apparently price-setting advertising layer (nearing a €1.5bn run rate) that management explicitly uses to reduce dependence on restaurant commissions [FY24 Annual Report, p.4, p.105; FY2025 Earnings Call, Mar 26, 2026]. But it is a price-taker in its second-largest segment by revenue (Asia, where GMV fell 7.7% on competition), in its fastest-growing new business line (Dmarts/quick commerce, still at a −3.6% segment margin), and — structurally — over the labor input that makes up two-thirds of its cost of sales, where 93.7% of delivery spend runs through external/freelance riders now facing an active, multi-jurisdiction reclassification threat [FY24 Annual Report, p.4, p.106, p.109, p.126–128].

## 5. The Single Biggest Bargaining Risk

Rider employment reclassification: with 93.7% of delivery expense running through external/freelance riders and a disclosed Spain contingent liability of €440.0m–€770.0m on top of an already-triggered Italy provision, a broader shift from freelance to employed rider status across the EU or Latin America would convert DH's largest, currently most controllable cost line into a much less flexible, regulator-set one [FY24 Annual Report, p.109, p.126–128, p.203–204].

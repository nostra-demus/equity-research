# External Dependency Check — DHER

Note on scope: Delivery Hero SE (XTRA:DHER, Prime Standard/Frankfurt, IFRS as adopted by the EU, reporting currency EUR, fiscal year end Dec-31) is currently the subject of a live, announced acquisition offer from Uber Technologies, disclosed on the Jul 16, 2026 M&A call [Uber Technologies, Inc., Delivery Hero SE - M&A Call, Jul 16, 2026]. That deal's completion depends entirely on merger-control approvals the company cannot control, which is itself the largest current external dependency and is treated separately in Section 3/5 below, on top of the standalone-business dependency table.

## 3. Classification

**Partly externally driven.**

The standalone business retains real management levers — subscription penetration (up to 61% of GMV in Saudi Arabia), multi-vertical bundling (Dmarts/quick commerce alongside core food delivery), AdTech monetization, FX hedging (blue-chip swaps in Argentina, USD forwards in Türkiye, term-facility redenomination to KRW), and pricing/market-mix decisions — none of which are available to a pure pass-through commodity or macro name. But the exposure is not background noise either: FX/hyperinflation (Argentina, Türkiye, Laos under IAS 29) and rider-classification regulatory risk (up to €440m–€770m contingent liability in Spain alone) are both rated "High" by the company's own risk framework, and ~85% of revenue (inference from segment mix) is earned in non-EUR currencies before translation into the reporting currency.

On top of that standalone read, DHER is currently a live M&A target: whether shareholders realize Uber's offer is now a function of merger-control approval across multiple jurisdictions — a variable entirely outside Delivery Hero management's control. This does not change the standalone classification, but it means the single most consequential external dependency right now is not FX or regulation of the operating business — it is deal-approval risk (see Section 5).

## 4. External Dependency Risk Score

**52 / 100** (0–100 scale; **higher = worse** — this score is inverted per CLAUDE.md §12/§15).

Rationale: two High-rated dependencies (FX/hyperinflation, rider-classification regulation) with real but partial mitigants (hedging, subscription mix, litigation defenses with company-assessed "more likely than not" outcomes in most jurisdictions) place this above the 41–60 "material exposure, mixed mitigation" midpoint rather than in the 61–80 "mostly externally driven" band, because the company retains functioning commercial and hedging levers and most contingencies are assessed by the company as not probable. The live Uber-acquisition regulatory-approval overlay is a material aggravating factor not fully captured in a steady-state operating score, and is called out separately in Section 5 rather than pushing the operating-business score itself into the "mostly externally driven" band.

## 5. The Single Biggest Lever

Right now, the single most consequential external variable is not a 20%-adverse commodity/FX/rate move on the standalone business — it is whether merger-control regulators across the jurisdictions where Uber and Delivery Hero overlap approve, delay, or block the announced Uber acquisition (guided close: H2 2027); a block or material delay would remove the deal premium that is currently the dominant driver of the stock's near-term outcome, a risk entirely outside Delivery Hero management's control [Uber Technologies, Inc., Delivery Hero SE - M&A Call, Jul 16, 2026 transcript]. For the standalone operating business absent the deal, the equivalent 20%-adverse-move lever would be a synchronized depreciation of the Turkish lira, Argentine peso and South Korean won against the euro, which would compound with existing IAS 29 hyperinflation accounting on ~85% of non-EUR revenue (inference from segment mix, not from filings).

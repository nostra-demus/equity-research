**Through-cycle read (required for the economic-moat test, not a single peak year).** DHER's business is not classically cyclical (`07_business-quality.md` scores cyclicality 42/100, "moderate," not a boom-bust commodity cycle) — but it has spent five straight years climbing out of deep structural losses, and using only FY2025's near-breakeven year would read as a cyclical-peak error in the opposite direction (treating the best year in the company's history as the norm). CIQ's own Return on Capital % series: FY2021 −14.5%, FY2022 −9.2%, FY2023 −5.3%, FY2024 −2.1%, FY2025 +0.8% [Delivery Hero SE XTRA DHER Financials.xls, Ratios tab]. The five-year average is **≈ −6.1%** — this is the number the economic-moat test below is anchored to, not the FY2025 single-year 0.8–1.6%.

**The economic moat test (required).**

> Return on capital **below** cost of capital: **0.8%–1.6% ROIC (FY2025, single best year) / ≈ −6.1% ROIC (FY2021–FY2025 average, through-cycle)** vs **10.7% WACC** (company-disclosed floor) **to 13.7%+ WACC** (company-disclosed for higher-risk CGUs) — a gap of roughly **900–1,000+ basis points in the best year, and roughly 1,700+ basis points through-cycle**. Cost-of-capital source: Delivery Hero's own FY2024 Annual Report goodwill-impairment testing (IAS 36) discloses a company-specific WACC of **10.7%** for the Woowa platform CGU (its largest goodwill CGU by far, €3,950.6m of €5,132.7m total goodwill), built from a 2.50% euro-area risk-free rate, a CGU-specific risk premium of 8.07%–24.09%, and a uniform entity-specific beta factor of 1.2 — with higher-risk CGUs (e.g., the Asia-ex-Korea group impaired in 2024) discounted at 13.7% [FY24 Annual Report, Note F.1.b "Impairment of Goodwill," p.168–169]. This is a Tier-1, company-disclosed cost-of-capital figure — no CAPM estimate or invented number was needed.

## 4. Where The Company Sits

1. **Relative to peers.** Company sits at the **bottom** of named peers on group-level margin and return on capital: DHER's FY2025 EBIT margin (0.7%) trails both DoorDash (5.3%, GAAP-profitable for the first time in FY2025) and Uber (10.7%) by a wide margin, and DHER's group ROIC (0.8%–1.6%, best year) is a fraction of Uber's ROE (~35.8%, though inflated by a one-off) and below DoorDash's ROE (~9.3%) [`08_competitive-map.md`, §2]. The one place DHER does NOT sit at the bottom is its own MENA segment specifically: its 13.4% segment Adjusted EBITDA margin exceeds the ~7% EBITDA margin Uber's CEO placed on Uber's comparable Middle East business, and Jahez (the smallest named peer) reported a 61.1% YoY net-profit decline on "elevated promotional activity" in the same period DHER's MENA margin expanded [`08_competitive-map.md`, §2–3] — a segment-level, not group-level, position of strength.
2. **Absolute (the economic moat test).** The company earns a return on capital **below** its cost of capital — 0.8%–1.6% (best year) to roughly −6.1% (through-cycle average) versus a company-disclosed 10.7%–13.7%+ WACC. This is the decisive test, and it fails by a wide margin at the group level.

## 5. Moat Verdict

**No moat proven.**

The strongest candidate for an advantage — scale and distribution density in the MENA segment via talabat, evidenced by a 13.4% segment margin that beats the closest comparable Uber Middle East figure (~7%) and appears to be pressuring smaller rival Jahez on price — is real but confined to one of four segments and has not translated into a group-level return on capital anywhere close to Delivery Hero's own disclosed 10.7%–13.7% cost of capital. Per the hard rule in this agent's mandate: a return on capital at or below cost of capital caps the verdict below "Strong moat" regardless of peer-relative superiority — DHER's group ROIC of roughly 0.8%–1.6% in its best-ever year, and roughly −6.1% through the last five years, is not merely below WACC, it is negative through-cycle, which rules out even "Narrow moat" at the group level. This is the textbook **moat in structure, not economics** case: a genuine scale/distribution asset in one segment (MENA) that the group's overall capital returns do not confirm. The durability test this would need to pass over the next five years is straightforward and hard: can the MENA segment's 13.4% margin keep expanding while own-delivery/Dmarts mix-shift (which has already dragged group gross margin from 29.9% to 24.4% over two years) and Asia's outright GMV decline (−7.7% on "rising competition") stop offsetting it — all inside an industry `07_business-quality.md` scores 26/100 on rate-of-change/disruption risk (≤40, triggering the CLAUDE.md §24 Filter 5 discount), where the winners are still being sorted by live M&A (DoorDash–Deliveroo, Uber–Delivery Hero itself) rather than by demonstrated, defensible economics.

**Moat trajectory — widening (narrow, segment-specific; group-level picture is mixed).** The quantitative trend that exists is a five-year climb in return on capital from −14.5% (FY2021) to +0.8% (FY2025) [Delivery Hero SE XTRA DHER Financials.xls, Ratios tab], and MENA segment margin specifically expanded from 11.3% to 13.4% over the same broad period even as GMV kept growing [FY24 Annual Report, p.4] — both are genuine widening signals, not noise. But this is not a clean, one-directional read: group gross margin has been falling (29.9% → 24.4%, FY2023–FY2025) as mix shifts toward lower-margin Dmarts/quick-commerce [Delivery Hero SE XTRA DHER Financials.xls, Ratios tab], Asia GMV fell outright (−7.7%) on rising competition in the same window DHER's own risk disclosures rate as "High" [FY24 Annual Report, p.105], and `07_business-quality.md` itself calls the FY2025 operating-profit inflection "fragile" and "not a cyclical peak to anchor against." The honest read is that the group's capital returns are rising off a very low, deeply negative base — evidence of a widening trend, but nowhere near evidence of a moat yet, and Asia is the clear counter-example showing the same trend running in reverse in a different segment of the same company.

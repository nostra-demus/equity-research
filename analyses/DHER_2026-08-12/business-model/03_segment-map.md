**Revenue-share note:** the five segment-revenue figures above sum to €13,141.0m — 102.7% of the disclosed "Total Segment Revenue" of €12,796.4m. The 2.7-point excess is the company's own disclosed intersegment-consolidation adjustment of −€344.5m ("mainly due to intersegment consolidation adjustments for services charged by the Platform segment to the Integrated Verticals segment") [FY24 AR, p.4, "Notes"]. This is disclosed, not an extraction error — flagged rather than silently forced to 100%. A cleaner, IFRS-clean cross-check is the company's own "Segment share of Group GMV" pie chart, which sums to exactly 100% (Asia 48%, MENA 26%, Europe 18%, Americas 8%; Integrated Verticals GMV is embedded within the four platform segments' GMV and shown separately "for illustrative purposes only," so it is excluded from this specific chart) [FY24 AR, p.4–5]. Profit shares (Adj. EBITDA) sum to 100.0% with no such elimination issue.

**"Total Segment Revenue" vs. IFRS "Revenue":** the segment table above uses Total Segment Revenue (€12,796.4m for FY2024), the company's own primary segment KPI, defined as revenue before the reduction of vouchers and adjusted for certain IFRS reclassification effects. The audited IFRS "Revenue" line in the Consolidated Statement of Profit or Loss is €12,294.7m for FY2024 (vouchers of −€884.1m and other reconciliation effects of +€382.4m bridge the two) [FY24 AR, p.108, "Reconciliation Total Segment Revenue to Revenue"]. This IFRS revenue figure is disclosed only at Group level, not broken out by segment.

## 2. Dominant Segment

**MENA is the dominant segment on profit — the metric that matters — even though Asia is larger on revenue.** MENA generated €472.9m of Adjusted EBITDA in FY2024, 68.3% of the Group's €692.5m total and the single largest segment contributor, at the best margin of any segment (13.4% Adj. EBITDA/segment revenue, up from 11.3% in 2023) [FY24 AR, "Key Figures" p.4]. Asia is larger by revenue (31.8% vs. MENA's 27.6% of Total Segment Revenue) but contributes less profit (55.6% vs. 68.3%) at a lower and flatter margin (9.5%, roughly unchanged from 10.3% in 2023) because of the competitive pressure described above [FY24 AR, p.4, p.105]. Per this module's rule, when revenue and profit shares disagree, the dominant segment is named by profit: **MENA**.

MENA does not clear the 85% threshold for "effectively single-segment" (it is 68.3% of profit and 27.6% of revenue), so Delivery Hero remains a genuinely multi-segment business — the other four segments together still generate 31.7% of Group Adj. EBITDA (and Asia alone is 55.6%, a large second contributor), and two segments (Europe, Integrated Verticals) are net drags on Group profit.

**FY2025 directional cross-check (unaudited, Tier 5/6 — not the primary basis for this finding):** per a Capital IQ workbook export tied to the Mar-26-2026 FY2025 earnings call, MENA's dominance appears to have widened in FY2025 — segment Adj. EBITDA of €546.0m (60.5% of the €902.8m segment-level sum) vs. Asia's €333.1m (36.9%), with Asia's Adj. EBITDA actually declining year-over-year [Delivery Hero SE XTRA DHER Financials.xls, Segments tab, Dec-31-2025 column]. This is consistent with, but not proof beyond, the FY2024 audited finding above; no audited FY2025 segment note is present in this pool to confirm it at Tier-1 quality.

## 3. Segment Disclosure Quality

Segment definitions are broadly consistent year-over-year — the same five segments (Asia, MENA, Europe, Americas, Integrated Verticals) appear across FY2020–FY2024 in both the Annual Report and the Capital IQ financials export — but the underlying historical figures have been restated or reclassified repeatedly: the Capital IQ segment history is labeled "Restated" for FY2020–FY2022 and "Reclassified" for FY2023, meaning the segment revenue/Adj. EBITDA figures reported for a given historical year have changed across successive annual filings [Delivery Hero SE XTRA DHER Financials.xls, Segments tab, column headers]. There is no discrete "Other" or "Corporate" segment row in the reportable-segment table itself, but a large centralized reconciling bucket sits between segment profit and Group statutory profit: "Unallocated Management Adjustments" (−€511.9m in FY2024) plus "Unallocated Expenses for Share-Based Compensation" (−€171.1m in FY2024) total −€683.0m — almost exactly offsetting the €692.5m of segment-level Adjusted EBITDA — which is why the Group's audited IFRS operating result was actually a loss of −€341.3m and net result a loss of −€881.7m in FY2024, despite segment Adjusted EBITDA showing a €692.5m profit [Delivery Hero SE XTRA DHER Financials.xls, Segments tab; FY24 AR, p.107, "Consolidated Statement of Profit or Loss"]. This is the single biggest disclosure-quality issue: **profit is disclosed at segment level only on a heavily adjusted, non-IFRS basis** (Adjusted EBITDA is defined by management to exclude share-based compensation, legal/antitrust and courier-reclassification expenses, restructuring costs, corporate-transaction costs, and IFRS 16 right-of-use depreciation [FY24 AR, p.101, footnote 5]), and the bridge back to audited IFRS profit is a large, unallocated, Group-level item that the segment table gives no way to attribute to any one segment. A second disclosure gap specific to MENA: the segment consolidates 100% of talabat's results even though DH holds only an 80% economic interest following talabat's December 2024 Dubai listing, so the segment table alone cannot be used to size DHER shareholders' actual claim on MENA profit without a further minority-interest adjustment [FY24 AR, p.106]. No FY2025 audited annual report or segment note is present in this pool; FY2025 segment figures used above are sourced only from a Capital IQ workbook export tied to a Mar-26-2026 earnings-call transcript (Tier 5/6), not an audited filing.

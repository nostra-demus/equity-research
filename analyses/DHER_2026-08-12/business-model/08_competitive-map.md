# Competitive Map — DHER

Upstream dependencies confirmed present: `02_business-identity.md` and `03_segment-map.md` both exist in this run folder and are used below. No missing-upstream caveat applies.

## 1. Dominant Segment

MENA is Delivery Hero's dominant segment by profit — 68.3% of FY2024 Group Adjusted EBITDA (€472.9m of €692.5m) at the best segment margin in the group (13.4% Adjusted EBITDA/segment revenue) — even though Asia is larger by revenue [FY24 Annual Report, "Key Figures" p.4; `03_segment-map.md`, Section 2]. The MENA segment is run almost entirely through talabat (80%-owned by Delivery Hero since its Dec-2024 Dubai listing) [FY24 Annual Report, p.4, footnote].

## 2. Named Competitors

### Competitor A — Uber Technologies, Inc.

- **Ticker / listing:** NYSE:UBER
- **Where they compete:** Global — Uber Eats competes with foodpanda (Asia), Glovo/foodora/efood (Europe) and PedidosYa (Americas). In the Middle East specifically — Delivery Hero's dominant segment — Uber's own CEO directly compared Uber's Middle East delivery-and-mobility business to talabat: "when you look in the Middle East and compare Uber and talabat, both last year grew about 30% in terms of gross bookings ... They have a very large kind of consumer ecosystem and ... both have 8 million monthly active users. And then both have about really attractive margins as well, about 7% EBITDA margins as well. That is both businesses on a stand-alone basis." [Uber Technologies, Inc., Delivery Hero SE - M&A Call, Jul 16, 2026, Dara Khosrowshahi (CEO) remarks]. Uber Technologies has also agreed to acquire all of Delivery Hero SE, expected to close H2 2027 — Uber is simultaneously DHER's largest global competitor and its pending acquirer [same source].
- **Scale:** FY2025 total revenue $52.017bn (Mobility + Delivery + Freight, company-wide) [Uber Technologies Q4 & FY2025 earnings release, Feb 4, 2026 (web, dated)]. Converted at the FY2025 average EUR/USD rate of 1.1306, Delivery Hero's FY2025 revenue of €14,059.8m is approximately $15.9bn [Delivery Hero SE XTRA DHER Financials.xls, Segments tab; FX: 2025 average EUR/USD 1.1306, X-Rates.com, accessed 2026-08-12] — Uber is roughly 3.3x DHER's scale group-wide.
- **Profitability / return on capital:** FY2025 GAAP operating margin 10.7% ($5.565bn operating income / $52.017bn revenue); GAAP net margin 19.3% ($10.053bn net income / $52.017bn revenue) — the net margin is materially inflated by a one-off item (a large deferred-tax benefit disclosed in Uber's FY2025 results) and should not be read as a clean operating comparison; return on equity ~35.8% ($10.053bn net income / $28.083bn total stockholders' equity, Dec-31-2025), same one-off caveat applies; Adjusted EBITDA margin 4.5% of Gross Bookings ($8.730bn / $193.454bn) [Uber Technologies Q4 & FY2025 earnings release, Feb 4, 2026 (web, dated); balance-sheet figure per Uber FY2025 10-K, cited via SEC EDGAR filing index (web, dated)]. Segment-comparable to DHER's dominant segment: Uber's own Middle East delivery-and-mobility business runs at "about 7% EBITDA margins," which the CEO placed side-by-side with talabat's own ~7% margin in the same remark — both are described on a "stand-alone basis," i.e. before the deal's synergies [Uber Technologies, Inc., Delivery Hero SE - M&A Call, Jul 16, 2026]. This is lower than the 13.4% Adjusted EBITDA/segment-revenue margin DHER discloses for its full MENA segment [FY24 Annual Report, p.4] — the two metrics are not on an identical basis (Uber's figure covers mobility+delivery combined; DHER's covers the marketplace-plus-Dmarts MENA segment), so the gap is directional evidence, not a clean like-for-like.
- **Source named in:** S&P Capital IQ Competitors export (Uber Technologies, Inc. matched against Delivery Hero SE (XTRA:DHER), sourced to DoorDash, Inc.'s 2024 Form 10-K) [`Delivery Hero SE XTRA DHER Competitors.rtf`]; directly and repeatedly in Uber's own M&A call announcing the acquisition of DHER [Uber Technologies, Inc., Delivery Hero SE - M&A Call, Jul 16, 2026].
- **One-line read:** The company that most directly mirrors talabat's own growth rate, user base and margin in the Middle East is about to become DHER's owner — competitive rivalry and consolidation are the same event here.

### Competitor B — DoorDash, Inc.

- **Ticker / listing:** NasdaqGS:DASH
- **Where they compete:** DoorDash acquired Deliveroo plc (closed Oct 2, 2025) and Wolt (2022), giving it direct delivery operations in the UK, Ireland, France, Italy, Belgium, Hong Kong, Singapore, and — via Deliveroo's Gulf footprint — the UAE, Kuwait and Qatar, putting it head-to-head with both DHER's Europe segment and, in the Gulf, with talabat in DHER's dominant MENA segment [Company Comparable Analysis, Business Description tab: "As of October 2, 2025, Deliveroo plc operates as a subsidiary of DoorDash, Inc."; countries per Deliveroo's own description in the same source].
- **Scale:** FY2025 total revenue $13.717bn [DoorDash Q4 & FY2025 earnings release, Feb 18, 2026 (web, dated)] versus DHER's FY2025 revenue of approximately $15.9bn (€14,059.8m at the 2025 average EUR/USD rate of 1.1306) — DoorDash is roughly 0.86x DHER's scale, comfortably inside the ~5x comparable-scale bound [Delivery Hero SE XTRA DHER Financials.xls, Segments tab; FX: X-Rates.com, accessed 2026-08-12].
- **Profitability / return on capital:** FY2025 GAAP operating margin 5.3% ($723m operating income / $13.717bn revenue); GAAP net margin 6.8% ($935m net income attributable to DoorDash common stockholders / $13.717bn revenue) — DoorDash's first full year of GAAP profitability; return on equity approximately 9.3% ($935m net income / $10.033bn total stockholders' equity, Dec-31-2025) [DoorDash Q4 & FY2025 earnings release, Feb 18, 2026 (web, dated); balance sheet per DoorDash FY2025 10-K, cited via SEC EDGAR (web, dated)]. By contrast, DHER's audited FY2024 IFRS operating result was a loss of −€341.3m and net result a loss of −€881.7m [FY24 Annual Report, p.107] — on a clean GAAP/IFRS basis (not the Adjusted EBITDA DHER prefers to headline), the largest scale peer DoorDash is already profitable while DHER's group is not.
- **Source named in:** S&P Capital IQ Competitors export (DoorDash, Inc. matched against Delivery Hero SE (XTRA:DHER), sourced to DoorDash, Inc.'s own 2026 Form 10-K) [`Delivery Hero SE XTRA DHER Competitors.rtf`]; DoorDash also appears as the acquirer of Deliveroo plc, itself listed in Delivery Hero's own S&P Capital IQ default comparable-company set [`Company-Comparable-Analysis-Delivery-Hero-SE__Business-Description.txt`].
- **One-line read:** The closest scale peer globally, and the only one of the three profiled here that is both GAAP-profitable and expanding into DHER's own regions (Europe, Gulf) through acquisition rather than organic build-out.

### Competitor C — Jahez International Company for Information Systems Technology

- **Ticker / listing:** Tadawul (SASE:6017)
- **Where they compete:** Saudi Arabia food-delivery platform (Jahez brand, plus the Snoonu and other regional apps it has folded in), directly named as a competitor to talabat and to talabat's own HungerStation subsidiary — both live inside DHER's dominant MENA segment [S&P Capital IQ Competitors export: Jahez International matched against Talabat Holding plc (DFM:TALABAT), sourced to Talabat Holding plc's own filing; also matched against HungerStation, Ltd.].
- **Scale:** FY2025 Group GMV SAR 7.2bn (~$1.92bn at the SAR/USD peg of 3.75) and Net Revenue SAR 2.3bn (~$613m) [Jahez FY2025 results, reported via Zawya, "Jahez's net profits decrease in 9M-25" and mid-east.info, "Jahez Reports FY2025 Results With 10.8% GMV Growth", 2026 (web, dated, unverified — Jahez's own results release was not in the data pool)]. Against DHER's FY2025 revenue of approximately $15.9bn, Jahez is roughly 26x smaller — well outside the ~5x comparable-scale bound, included here as a clear regional/segment-specific rival per the selection criteria rather than a scale peer.
- **Profitability / return on capital:** FY2025 net income attributable to shareholders of SAR 73m on SAR 2.3bn net revenue is a net margin of approximately 3.2%, down sharply from the prior year — reported as a 61.1% year-on-year decline in net profit, which the company attributes to "a deliberate and measured trade-off to invest in retaining customers during a period of elevated promotional activity across the industry" [Zawya and mid-east.info, 2026 (web, dated, unverified)]. Jahez states it "maintained double-digit EBITDA margins in KSA Platforms of 11.9%" at the segment level in the same release [mid-east.info, 2026 (web, dated, unverified)]. Return on invested capital / return on equity: not disclosed in any source available to this agent — not public / not disclosed.
- **Source named in:** S&P Capital IQ Competitors export, "Named by Company," sourced to Talabat Holding plc's own filing [`Delivery Hero SE XTRA DHER Competitors.rtf`].
- **One-line read:** A much smaller, Saudi-only pure-play whose FY2025 margin compression — explicitly blamed on "elevated promotional activity across the industry" — is direct third-party evidence of the price competition DHER's own MENA segment disclosure has not (yet) had to absorb at the same intensity; DHER's MENA Adjusted EBITDA margin actually rose, from 11.3% to 13.4%, over the same broad period [FY24 Annual Report, p.4].

## 3. Competitive Position

Position vs peers in the dominant segment (MENA): **Holding to gaining**, on the evidence available — DHER does not disclose a market-share percentage for MENA, so this rests on proxies. First, MENA's own Adjusted EBITDA margin expanded from 11.3% to 13.4% in FY2024 even as GMV kept growing, the opposite of what margin compression under intensifying competition would look like [FY24 Annual Report, p.4]. Second, Uber's own CEO placed talabat's Middle East gross-bookings growth (~30% in the prior year) and EBITDA margin (~7%, on a business definition that spans mobility and delivery) essentially level with Uber's own Middle East business, describing talabat as "the leading food delivery player in the market" [Uber Technologies, Inc., Delivery Hero SE - M&A Call, Jul 16, 2026]. Third, Jahez — a smaller, Saudi-only rival — reported FY2025 GMV growth of only 10.8% and a 61.1% net-profit decline tied to "elevated promotional activity across the industry," consistent with talabat pressuring smaller players on price rather than the reverse [mid-east.info, 2026 (web, dated, unverified)]. This reads as DHER holding or gaining ground in MENA specifically. The picture is different in the second-largest segment, Asia, which is explicitly **losing** share on the company's own words: "GMV of the Asia segment declined (−7.7%), mainly driven by rising competition in certain regions," with Adjusted EBITDA "flat year-over-year, mainly due to the effects of the competitive environment" [FY24 Annual Report, p.105–106].

## 4. Competitive Shape

The Middle East market — DHER's dominant segment — reads as a **consolidated oligopoly**: a handful of platforms (talabat, Uber Eats/Careem, Jahez, Noon Food, Snoonu, HungerStation — the last two already owned respectively by talabat and Jahez) contest each Gulf country, with talabat itself described by Uber's own CEO as "the leading food delivery player in the market" [Uber Technologies, Inc., Delivery Hero SE - M&A Call, Jul 16, 2026]. No independent HHI or top-N market-share disclosure exists in this data pool to size the concentration precisely — this read rests on the small, named set of platforms above, not a calculated index. The global on-demand delivery industry, by contrast, is actively consolidating through M&A rather than organic competition alone: DoorDash bought Wolt (2022) and Deliveroo (2025), and Uber has now agreed to buy the whole of Delivery Hero (announced Jul 16, 2026, expected close H2 2027) [Company Comparable Analysis, Business Description tab; Uber Technologies, Inc., Delivery Hero SE - M&A Call, Jul 16, 2026]. Once that deal closes, the number of large independent multi-country delivery platforms globally shrinks further, reinforcing the oligopoly read rather than a fragmented one.

## 5. Caveat

No DHER-disclosed market-share percentage exists for any segment in this data pool — all "position vs peers" reads above rely on GMV/EBITDA-margin trend proxies and on statements made by a competitor's (Uber's) own management on an M&A call, not on an independent third-party share study. Jahez's financial figures come from web-sourced press coverage of its own results release (Zawya, mid-east.info), not from a primary filing in the data pool — labeled web/unverified accordingly and used only for the smallest, most segment-specific of the three named competitors. No public MENA peer other than Jahez (Careem, Noon, Snoonu are all privately held or subsidiaries that do not separately disclose margins) was available to further anchor MENA-segment profitability comparisons; this would be resolved by a Careem or Noon standalone financial disclosure, which does not exist publicly today.

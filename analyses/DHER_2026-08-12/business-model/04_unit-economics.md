# Unit Economics — DHER

Basis: Delivery Hero SE (XTRA:DHER), Prime Standard / Frankfurt Stock Exchange, IFRS as adopted by the EU, EUR, fiscal year ending Dec-31. Figures below are FY2024 (year ended Dec-31-2024) from the audited FY2024 Annual Report (published Apr 25, 2025) unless stated otherwise. FY2025 figures are explicitly labeled Capital IQ workbook / earnings-transcript sourced (Tier 5/6, per `analyses/DHER_2026-08-12/business-model/00_data-triage.md`) — no audited FY2025 annual report or `ciq_facts.json` sidecar exists in this pool.

Upstream input used: `03_segment-map.md` (present) identifies **MENA** as the dominant segment on profit — 68.3% of Group Adjusted EBITDA (€472.9m of €692.5m) at the best segment margin (13.4% Adj. EBITDA/Segment Revenue, up from 11.3%), even though Asia is larger by revenue [FY24 Annual Report, "Key Figures" p.4]. This report builds the unit-economics table for MENA.

## 1. Natural Unit

The natural economic unit for MENA is **the order** — a single food-delivery or quick-commerce transaction placed through a MENA platform brand (talabat, Hungerstation, InstaShop, or Yemeksepeti) [FY24 Annual Report, Combined Management Report, A.3 "Segments", p.100–101]. Delivery Hero itself frames its economics this way: "GMV is influenced by the number of orders, as well as average basket size per order" [FY24 Annual Report, p.5081-region text].

A second, structurally different unit exists in the Integrated Verticals segment: the **Dmart (quick-commerce) order**, where Delivery Hero acts as principal — buying and reselling inventory through its own warehouses rather than earning a commission — giving that unit a merchandise-margin economic structure instead of MENA's commission-based one [FY24 Annual Report, p.101].

## 3. Value Creation Read

**Unclear at the true per-order level — but the one disclosed proxy points toward value creation, not destruction.** MENA's Adjusted EBITDA / GMV margin rose from 3.1% to 3.7% in FY2024 while segment GMV grew +28.8% [FY24 Annual Report, "Key Figures" p.4] — the only segment combining double-digit volume growth with an already meaningfully positive relative margin (Asia's is 1.6%, and Europe/Integrated Verticals are negative). That is consistent with each incremental order adding profit rather than diluting it. But Delivery Hero discloses no order count, no average order value, and no acquisition cost tied to a single order or a single new customer for MENA (or the Group), so the contribution-margin-×-lifetime-vs-acquisition-cost math this section is meant to test cannot actually be built from filings — only inferred from a GMV-normalized EBITDA margin, which is a coarser proxy. **The single most valuable missing disclosure is MENA-segment (or even Group-level) absolute order count and average order value**, which would let revenue-per-order and a real per-order contribution margin be computed directly instead of inferred.

**Peak-cycle ring-fence:** FY2024's MENA acceleration (+28.8% GMV, +55.3% Adj. EBITDA, margin +0.6pp) coincided with talabat's Dec-2024 Dubai Financial Market listing and management's own comment that MENA "performed significantly better than expected" [FY24 Annual Report, p.4]. The FY2025 cross-check (Tier 5, unaudited, Capital IQ workbook) shows MENA revenue growth decelerating sharply to +14.3% (€3,527.8m → €4,033.7m) while the Adj. EBITDA/revenue margin only held flat (13.4% → ~13.5%), not expanding further [Delivery Hero SE XTRA DHER Financials.xls, Segments tab]. Read the 3.1%→3.7% GMV-margin improvement as a strong-year result that stabilized rather than compounded further in FY2025 — not yet proven as a structurally rising margin.

## 4. Sensitivity

The single input most likely to swing this read if it moved 20% is **delivery cost per order** (the largest and most volatile cost-of-sales line). Group delivery expenses grew 33.9% in FY2024 (€4,496.0m → €6,022.1m) — faster than the 23.7% growth in Group revenue — and management attributed part of the excess directly to "intensified competition in certain regions, which led to increased logistic expenses" [FY24 Annual Report, B.3 "Results of Operations", p.106]. Delivery expenses represent 67.2% of Group cost of sales (2023: 64.5%) [FY24 Annual Report, p.106], so a 20% adverse move in per-order delivery cost — the same dynamic already visible in Asia, where GMV fell −7.7% under competitive pressure and margin stayed flat despite revenue growth [FY24 Annual Report, p.105–106] — would compress MENA's 3.7% Adj. EBITDA/GMV margin far more than an equivalent move in marketing spend (€484.8m, already falling) or IT costs (1.1% of GMV). A second, structural sensitivity specific to MENA: the segment table consolidates 100% of talabat's results even though DH holds only an 80% economic interest post-listing [FY24 Annual Report, p.106] — so roughly a fifth of any MENA per-unit profit improvement accrues to talabat's public minority shareholders, not to DHER shareholders directly, which the GMV-margin proxy above does not adjust for.

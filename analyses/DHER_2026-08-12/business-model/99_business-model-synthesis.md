# Business Model Reality Check — DHER (Synthesis)

## Abstract

Delivery Hero runs local food-delivery and quick-commerce apps (talabat, Baemin, foodpanda, Glovo, PedidosYa) in roughly 70 countries, taking a commission on restaurant orders while increasingly buying and reselling groceries itself through its own warehouses. Its Middle East business, run mostly through the 80%-owned talabat, produces most of the group's profit — 68.3% of FY2024 Adjusted EBITDA (€472.9m of €692.5m) at a 13.4% segment margin [FY24 Annual Report, "Key Figures," p.4] — while group-wide return on capital still sits far below the company's own 10.7%+ disclosed cost of capital (roughly 0.8%–1.6% in the best year, FY2025, and about −6.1% averaged over the last five years). The controlling negative: KPMG's audit report carries a going-concern-level "material uncertainty" note tied to the Glovo Spain subsidiary and up to €770m of rider-classification liability [FY24 Annual Report, Auditor's Report, p.222–223], which locks this review at avoid deeper work regardless of the Middle East segment's relative strength.

**Module Disconfirmation (CLAUDE.md §8):**

- **Strongest bear point:** Group return on capital sits below the company's own disclosed cost of capital in every one of the last five years — roughly 0.8%–1.6% in FY2025 (the best year on record) against a −6.1% five-year average, versus a company-disclosed 10.7%–13.7%+ WACC [`09_moat.md`, §3]. A business earning below its cost of capital through-cycle destroys value even before governance and disqualifier issues are counted.
- **Strongest bull point (steelman):** The MENA segment, run through the 80%-owned talabat, generates 68.3% of Group Adjusted EBITDA at a 13.4% margin that expanded from 11.3% the prior year even as GMV grew 28.8% — and that margin exceeds the ~7% EBITDA margin Uber's own CEO placed on Uber's comparable Middle East business on the M&A call, with talabat described by Uber's CEO as "the leading food delivery player in the market" [`08_competitive-map.md`, §2–3; `09_moat.md`, §4].
- **Single killer risk specific to the business model:** A broader shift from freelance to employed rider status across the EU or Latin America — the same regulatory dynamic already producing the auditor's going-concern note — would convert 93.7% of Group delivery expense from a flexible, DH-controlled cost into a regulator-set, much less flexible one, on top of the disclosed €440–770m Spain contingent liability [`06_value-chain.md`, §5].
- **Disconfirming evidence already visible:** FY2025 delivered the Group's first-ever positive full-year operating result (€93.7m, 0.7% margin) [`07_business-quality.md`], and MENA's margin trend has been genuinely widening for two straight years — evidence the group-level picture is not uniformly deteriorating, even though `07_business-quality.md` itself calls this inflection "fragile" and not yet a proven structural trend.

## 3. Reconciliation

**FY2024 Group Adjusted EBITDA — €692.5m vs. €791.3m, a real disagreement.** `03_segment-map.md`, `07_business-quality.md`, `08_competitive-map.md` and `09_moat.md` all consistently cite Group Adjusted EBITDA of **€692.5m** for FY2024, sourced to the FY24 Annual Report's "Key Figures" page (p.4) and matching the sum of the five reportable segments' Adjusted EBITDA in the segment table. `11_capital-allocation-governance.md`, in its debt-trajectory row, instead cites Adjusted EBITDA of **€791.3m** for FY2024, sourced to the same Annual Report but page ~112. Neither report explains the ~€99m gap, and no reconciling note (e.g., a different adjustment basis, or a post-close restatement) is identified in either source citation. **Reconciled view for this synthesis:** the €692.5m figure is used as authoritative, both because it is the figure four of five relevant specialist reports converge on independently and because it reconciles arithmetically to the segment-level table (MENA €472.9m + Asia €385.1m + Integrated Verticals −€98.7m + Europe −€77.0m + Americas €10.3m = €692.5m) — a check the €791.3m figure does not pass. The €791.3m figure and its implied leverage ratios (2.3x–2.8x net debt/Adjusted EBITDA) in `11_capital-allocation-governance.md` should be treated as unreconciled and re-verified before being used downstream; using the reconciled €692.5m figure instead would push net-debt/Adjusted-EBITDA leverage modestly higher (roughly 2.7x–3.6x on the same net-debt figures), still within a moderate, non-distressed band but not identical to the module's own stated ratio.

No other material disagreements were found between specialists — segment dominance (MENA on profit), the going-concern trigger, the serial-acquirer pattern, and the FX/regulatory dependency reads are all mutually consistent across the reports that touch them.

## 4. Note To The Final Synthesizer

- **Strongest business-model positive:** MENA/talabat's segment economics — 13.4% Adjusted EBITDA/segment revenue in FY2024 (up from 11.3%), expanding while GMV grew 28.8%, and matching or beating the comparable Middle East margin Uber's own CEO cited for Uber's own business (~7%) [`08_competitive-map.md`, §2; `09_moat.md`, §4].
- **Strongest business-model negative:** Group return on capital sits below the company's own disclosed cost of capital in every one of the last five years (~0.8%–1.6% best-year, ~−6.1% five-year average, vs. a 10.7%–13.7%+ WACC) — this is a "moat in structure, not economics" business at the group level [`09_moat.md`, §3–5].
- **Most important segment:** MENA — 68.3% of Group Adjusted EBITDA on a reconciled €692.5m FY2024 base, though the segment table consolidates 100% of talabat despite DHER holding only an 80% economic interest post-listing, overstating DHER shareholders' actual claim on that profit [`03_segment-map.md`, §1–2].
- **Cleanest unit-economics read (or why it cannot be derived):** Cannot be derived at the true per-order level for any segment — DHER discloses no order count, average order value, or per-order acquisition cost anywhere in the pool. The best available proxy, MENA Adjusted EBITDA/GMV (3.1%→3.7% FY2023→FY2024), points to per-unit value creation but is a coarser, GMV-normalized substitute, not a real contribution-margin calculation [`04_unit-economics.md`, §2–3].
- **Where the company sits vs named peers on margin / ROIC:** Group-level, DHER sits at the bottom of its three named peers — FY2025 EBIT margin 0.7% vs. DoorDash's 5.3% and Uber's 10.7%; DHER's ROIC (0.8%–1.6%) is a fraction of DoorDash's ROE (~9.3%) and Uber's ROE (~35.8%, inflated by a one-off tax benefit). Segment-level, MENA alone outperforms the comparable Uber Middle East business on margin [`08_competitive-map.md`; `09_moat.md`, §3–4].
- **Main external dependency:** FX/hyperinflation exposure across roughly 85% of revenue earned outside the eurozone (inference from segment mix), compounded by rider-classification regulatory risk already producing a going-concern note and an active EU antitrust investigation — External dependency risk scored 52/100 (inverted, higher=worse) [`10_external-dependency.md`, §3–4]. Layered on top: the Uber deal's completion is contingent on merger-control approvals entirely outside DHER's control.
- **Most important capital allocation or governance signal:** The serial-acquirer pattern (Filter 4, severity 75) — ~€2.05bn of cumulative goodwill impairments 2021–2025 spread across nearly every acquired unit (Glovo platform, Glovo Dmart, LatAm platform, Europe platform, Europe Dmart, Asia Delivery, Yemeksepeti) — is direct evidence DHER paid more for these businesses than they turned out to be worth [`11_capital-allocation-governance.md`, §3].
- **Whether any automatic disqualifier triggered:** Yes — disqualifier #1, auditor going-concern note tied to Glovo Spain rider-classification risk (contingent liability €440–770m). This locks the verdict at "Low-quality business — avoid deeper work" regardless of the MENA segment's relative strength.
- **Which rejector filters tripped, and the cap each applied:** Filter 4 (serial acquirers) — tripped, Capital allocation & governance capped at 50 (module scored 42), Overall usefulness capped at 70 by rule (superseded here by the disqualifier lock). Filter 5 (fast-changing industry) — tripped, Business quality capped at 65 (non-binding; module's own aggregate of 34 is already lower); thesis flagged as a sector/technology-cycle bet, not a durable compounder (`RF-BQ-005`). Filter 1 (crooks/integrity) — not tripped; no fraud evidence found, distinct from the going-concern disqualifier that drives the lock.
- **Biggest missing data point:** An audited FY2025 annual report — the only FY2025 figures in the pool come from an earnings-call transcript (Tier 6) and Capital IQ workbook exports (Tier 5), not an audited filing, and no independent order-count or average-order-value disclosure exists at any segment for any period, which is what blocks a real unit-economics build.
- **Whether the business deserves deeper work, and what would change the answer:** No, not at present — the disqualifier lock governs. What would change the answer: primary evidence that the Glovo Spain going-concern matter has been resolved or the contingent liability materially reduced/settled (e.g., a favorable court ruling, a capped settlement, or removal of the auditor's material-uncertainty language in a subsequent audited filing), combined with resolution of the live Uber acquisition (since a completed deal changes this from a standalone equity-research question to a merger-arbitrage one).
- **Mandatory red-flag propagation (severity ≥40 from `12_red-flags-sweep.md`):** Disclosed data breaches — 8 separate incidents in FY2024, 100% involving personally identifiable information, affecting 35,343 users, severity 42 — is propagated here per rule; it sits on top of, and is distinct from, the regulatory/litigation risks already flagged elsewhere. (The one-off-gains flag, severity 35, falls below the mandatory threshold; it is summarized in Section 2's roll-up and in the Reconciliation-adjacent commentary rather than repeated here in full.)

## 5. Simple Summary

- **What it does:** Runs local food-delivery and quick-commerce apps (talabat, Baemin, foodpanda, Glovo, PedidosYa) in about 70 countries, plus its own small warehouses (Dmarts) that it stocks and sells from directly.
- **How it makes money:** A cut of restaurant/shop orders (commission) as an agent in the marketplace business, and close to the full sale price as the seller of record in quick commerce, plus a fast-growing advertising layer (~€1.5bn revenue run rate) sold to restaurants and brands.
- **Whether each new unit creates value:** Cannot be proven from filings — DHER discloses no order count, average order value, or per-order acquisition cost for any segment; the best available proxy (MENA Adjusted EBITDA/GMV) points toward value creation in MENA specifically, not group-wide.
- **Which segment matters most:** MENA (via talabat), which generates 68.3% of Group Adjusted EBITDA at the group's best and improving margin, even though the larger Asia segment brings in more revenue.
- **Whether it has a moat, and against whom:** No group-level moat proven — return on capital sits below the company's own cost of capital every year for the last five years. A real, but narrow and segment-confined, cost/scale edge exists in MENA against Uber's own Middle East business and against smaller rival Jahez.
- **What external variables it depends on:** Currency swings and hyperinflation accounting across roughly 85% of non-euro revenue, and rider-employment regulation across multiple jurisdictions — the same exposure behind the auditor's going-concern note — plus, right now, whether merger-control regulators approve the announced Uber acquisition.
- **Whether capital is allocated well:** No — a serial-acquirer pattern has produced ~€2.05bn of cumulative goodwill write-downs since 2021, shares outstanding rose ~50% over five years with no offsetting buyback, and a large unrecognized contingent liability (up to €770m) sits off the balance sheet.
- **Whether it deserves deeper work:** No — the auditor's going-concern note on the Glovo Spain subsidiary locks this at "avoid deeper work" regardless of the Middle East segment's relative strength.

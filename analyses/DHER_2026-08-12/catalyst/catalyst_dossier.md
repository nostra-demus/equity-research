## 2. The Nearest Dated Catalyst

The nearest proven-dated catalyst is the **FQ2 2026 results release on 2026-08-27**, 15 days from this report's date. Q2 is DHER's seasonally weakest quarter, and the print will show whether Adjusted EBITDA — the metric management actually guides to, and the metric with a three-year in-line-or-better track record — lands in the upper half of the €910m–€960m FY2026 range as management signaled on 2026-04-30, or whether the unreconciled gap between management's "+18%" revenue-growth framing and the vendor-computed +5.8% reported-currency trend (visible in both FY2025 and Q1 2026) finally shows up as a headline miss. Both outcomes are live: EBITDA has tracked guidance closely for three straight years, but EPS has missed Street's estimate in every one of the last five fiscal years and free cash flow has a disclosed history of large "extraordinary item" swings against guidance (a -€366m gap in FY2025 alone). One live risk to weighing this catalyst at all: if Uber-deal news dominates trading around 2026-08-27, the market's reaction to the underlying operating beat/miss may not show up cleanly in the stock.

## 3. The Single Most Important Catalyst

The Uber acquisition's **closing / change of control, guided for H2 2027**, is the single most important catalyst — dated in the sense that management has given a window, but not proven in the sense of a contractual closing date, and its timing sits entirely outside DHER's control (multi-jurisdiction merger-control approval). The bullish path is straightforward: regulators clear the deal on or near the guided timeline, and the deal completes at the disclosed €41.50/share cash offer (**Web, unverified**) or higher if a competing bid re-emerges — DoorDash had separately explored acquiring Delivery Hero, including specifically its MENA/Talabat business, before Uber's agreement was signed. The bearish path is a regulatory block or a materially extended delay in one of the "several well-defined steps" Uber's own CFO described as complex; because this report's own standalone base-case fair value (€39.65/share) sits close to but below the disclosed offer price, a broken deal would not just remove an unrelated premium — it would reprice the stock toward a standalone read that itself carries real downside (bear case €18.59/share under an adverse operating and regulatory scenario). A buyer cannot precisely time this catalyst: there is no contractual close date, no disclosed break fee in this pool, and the guided window spans roughly 12–17 months from today.

## 4. Negative / Bearish Catalysts

- **Reported free cash flow miss on "extraordinary" items** — a live risk at every remaining FY2026 print given the FY2025 precedent (-€366m swing against the guided figure), tied to legal/contingency outflows and the Talabat minority dividend that the company's own FCF guide excludes but the reported "Actual" figure includes.
- **Adverse EU Platform Work Directive transposition (~Nov-2026 deadline)** — could extend Spain's employment-based rider model across additional EU markets, intersecting directly with the auditor-flagged Glovo Spain contingent liability (€440m–€770m, unprovisioned).
- **Uber deal delay or block (H2 2027 guided close)** — regulatory risk entirely outside company control; the current price already carries an ~11.6% arbitrage spread to the disclosed €41.50/share offer, meaning the market is not treating completion as a certainty.
- **Post-closing squeeze-out / domination agreement** — a live, evidenced forward risk to any remaining minority holders (`management-governance/05`, Finding 05-021, Red) once Uber's stated economic ownership crosses 50%; the terms of any compression are set largely by the new controlling shareholder, not by today's dispersed holder base.
- **Convertible-bond change-of-control acceleration** — inference-labelled, but a real possibility that a completed deal pulls forward 2027–2030-dated maturities into a single redemption event, layered on top of the already-large, unaddressed 2029 maturity cluster.
- **The 2029 debt-maturity cluster, beyond this calendar's 12-month window but material and dated** — €2,145.4m (46.4% of total debt) concentrated in a single year: Convertible Bonds III-B €480.9m due **2029-03-10** (proven date), Dollar Term Facility €1,110.3m due **2029-12-01** (proven date), and the KRW Term Facility €496.1m due sometime in 2029 (year only, not itemized). None of this has been addressed by any disclosed refinancing action; management's own language ("visibility all the way into 2028") stops short of it [`balance-sheet-survival/02_maturity-wall-and-refinancing.md §2, §4`].
- **DHER's own serial-acquisition history, if the deal breaks** — Inference, not from filings, and explicitly undated, so it is not a table row: management's FY2025 call referenced an ongoing "strategic review... methodology that has made us successful in the past." Given `business-model/11_capital-allocation-governance.md` independently scores DHER's acquisition pattern at severity 75/100 (§24 Filter 4 — a proven historical serial-acquirer pattern with ~€2.05bn of cumulative goodwill impairment against its own past deals), any resumption of debt-funded M&A if the Uber deal fails would not be a conviction-lifting catalyst and should be read with that filter, not as a fresh growth story.

## 5. Calendar Read

This calendar is not thematic — it carries one hard-proven date (FQ2 2026 results, 2026-08-27) and a cluster of real, evidenced windows (Taiwan close, EU policy deadline, tender-offer mechanics, deal close), but almost nothing beyond the next earnings print is a fixed date; even the calendar's single largest item, the Uber acquisition close, is a guided window contingent on regulators outside the company's control. Near-term (the next 3–4 months), the calendar skews toward routine operating-execution risk — DHER's five-year EPS miss streak and its history of FCF guidance-versus-reported swings argue for caution on the Aug-27 print, even though the metric management actually guides to (Adjusted EBITDA) has a clean three-year track record. The single most important timing caveat for the whole calendar: the dominant catalyst (deal completion) has no contractual close date, no disclosed break fee in this pool, and its price-relevant term (the €41.50/share offer) was found only via a web search, not in the data pool itself — a genuine gap the next data refresh should close, since the entire post-2026 calendar for this stock now runs through that one, largely un-timeable event.

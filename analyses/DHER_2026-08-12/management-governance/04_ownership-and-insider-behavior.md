# Ownership & Insider Behavior — DHER

**Context note (carried from `00_governance-data-triage.md` and `01_management-and-track-record.md`).** Delivery Hero SE is, as of this report's date, the subject of a live, announced acquisition offer from Uber Technologies (M&A call held Jul 16, 2026; expected close H2 2027). Uber states it already has "economic exposure to 37% of Delivery Hero" and that reference shareholder Prosus (via Naspers Limited / MIH Food Holdings B.V.) has "irrevocably committed to tender its stake, which brings our economic ownership position to over 50% following a successful offer" [Uber Technologies, Inc., Delivery Hero SE — M&A Call, Jul 16, 2026, p.4/7]. This is a live corporate-action overlay, not a steady-state ownership picture — the read below separates the static FY2024 pool disclosure from this in-progress control-structure event. Separately, `business-model/01_disqualifier-scan.md` found no promoter/insider share-pledging disclosure anywhere in the pool, and confirmed Naspers/MIH is the only >10%-voting-rights holder disclosed, with no controlling promoter.

## 5. Ownership / Insider Read

Skin in the game inside the company is thin and entirely compensation-derived: the whole Management Board together held 0.38% of the company at 2024 year-end (1,098,191 of 287,385,940 shares), built through LTIP, STI, and Hero Grant equity awards rather than open-market purchase — so the founder-CEO narrative rests on tenure, not on money Niklas Östberg himself put at risk buying stock. There is no pledging of insider or strategic-holder shares, and no dual-class or super-voting structure — on those two points, minority holders are protected. The dominant fact right now is not steady-state ownership at all: the pool shows Uber Technologies already holding economic exposure to 37% of Delivery Hero and reference shareholder Naspers/Prosus irrevocably committed to tender its remaining stake, pushing Uber's position "over 50%" if the offer succeeds — a live control-structure event, not a governance signal to score on its own terms. The single biggest gap for this read is data, not conduct: no PDMR (insider) transaction log sits in the pool, so recent insider buying/selling cannot be verified from primary sources, and the richer, more concerning story visible only in unverified web coverage — an activist fund building to ~15% and pressuring the founder-CEO to step down, shortly before a premium takeover bid emerged — has to be flagged as context only, not evidence, until a pool-sourced filing confirms it.

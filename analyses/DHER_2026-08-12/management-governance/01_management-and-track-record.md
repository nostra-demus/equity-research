## 3. Stated Strategy & Targets

Management describes the strategy as defending and growing local-delivery category leadership while "becoming an everyday app": expanding quick commerce (Dmarts) beyond food into grocery, health and beauty; scaling AdTech toward a stated "€1.5 billion revenue run rate"; migrating remaining brands onto a single global technology stack; and applying AI to cut cost-to-serve (a stated target of automating 85% of first-line customer-service contacts) [FY2025 Earnings Call, Mar 26 2026, prepared remarks]. The explicit, checkable FY2026 targets management has set are: Group GMV growth of 8%–10% like-for-like; Adjusted EBITDA of €910m–€960m; and Free Cash Flow of more than €200m, the last two stated on a basis that excludes "extraordinary" items such as legal costs, the Talabat minority dividend, and related interest [FY2025 Earnings Call, Mar 26 2026, prepared remarks; Q&A, CFO Marie-Anne Popp]. No formal revenue or EPS figure is guided.

The five-year Street EPS miss streak is a further caution against taking the calm, low-activity current FY2026 estimate revisions (`earnings/04_guidance-consensus.md` §4–5) as reassurance.

## 5. Management Read

Niklas Östberg has run this company for fifteen years as founder-CEO with no leadership discontinuity, and his and COO Vandepitte's contracts were both extended to 2029 in early 2026 — a real continuity signal, not narrative. Set against that: a seven-month partial Management Board vacancy at CFO after Emmanuel Thomassin's 2024 departure, and roughly half the Supervisory Board turning over in the same year, are stability warnings that should not be waved away by the founder's tenure. On delivery, management has hit its own guided Adjusted EBITDA target three years running — real, checkable execution on the metric it controls — but the Free Cash Flow guidance has swung from a large headline beat to a large headline miss largely on non-operating items, and the Street has missed on EPS every year for five straight years; the "path to profitability" story is therefore proven on one company-chosen metric and still unproven on the metric (statutory operating profit/cash flow) that ultimately matters to a shareholder. The single biggest management signal is the split character of the record: a stable, tenured founder who reliably hits his own guided operating target, running a company whose reported bottom-line and cash-flow numbers remain fragile, one-off-dependent, and one year — not several — into an inflection that has not yet been proven durable.

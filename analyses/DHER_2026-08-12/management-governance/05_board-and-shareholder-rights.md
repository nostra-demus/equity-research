# Board & Shareholder Rights — DHER

**Context carried from `management-governance/00_governance-data-triage.md` and `business-model/01_disqualifier-scan.md`.** Delivery Hero SE (Frankfurt Prime Standard, XTRA:DHER; German Societas Europaea under the two-tier board system — a Management Board that runs the company and a Supervisory Board that appoints and oversees it) is, as of report date, the subject of a live, announced acquisition offer from Uber Technologies (M&A call, Jul 16, 2026), with reference shareholder Prosus/Naspers "irrevocably committed to tender its stake," which Uber says "brings our economic ownership position to over 50% following a successful offer... we will evaluate the most appropriate ownership structure based on the outcome of the tender offer and the options available under German corporate law" [Uber Technologies, Inc., Delivery Hero SE - M&A Call, Jul 16 2026, p.8]. That last phrase is a direct signal that a post-closing squeeze-out or domination agreement — mechanisms that can compress or extinguish remaining minority holders' economic position under German law — is under consideration; this is read as the current, forward-looking centre of gravity for minority-shareholder protection, layered on top of the historical governance record below. Separately, `business-model/01_disqualifier-scan` triggered a hard disqualifier (an auditor-flagged, subsidiary-level "Material Uncertainty about the Ability of Subsidiaries to Continue as a Going Concern" tied to Glovo Spain's rider-classification investigation, contingent liability €440–770 million, Group opinion unmodified) [FY24 Annual Report, Independent Auditor's Report, p.222–223]. Per MODULE_RULES.md Disqualifier Deference, this agent reports that fact verbatim and does not re-adjudicate it; it is not itself a board-structure or shareholder-rights defect, but the downstream synthesis must apply the Governance-risk floor and cap the overall stewardship verdict at no better than "Serious governance concerns" regardless of the board-quality read below.

**Read on takeover defenses.** Formally, DHER has almost no entrenchment machinery — single-class voting, no poison pill, majority (not plurality) director voting, and a Management Board that (per the WpÜG neutrality rule and the absence of any disclosed defensive-issuance authorization) has limited legal room to block a bid unilaterally. The live Uber process is proceeding without disclosed board resistance. The one real minority-relevant feature is the change-of-control equity acceleration: it gives the Management Board a personal financial incentive to see the Uber deal complete on favorable terms to itself, which is a conflict of interest to weigh against management's public support for the transaction, though not evidence of entrenchment.

## 5. Minority-Shareholder Protection Read

On paper, the architecture is genuinely clean: one share, one vote, no dual class, no poison pill, majority (not plurality) director elections, immaterial related-party leakage (≈0.2% of revenue, individually disclosed and Audit-Committee-approved), and a functioning four-committee Supervisory Board that documents its work in detail. Set against that: roughly half the Supervisory Board turned over in a single year (2024), the FY2024 Audit Committee chair was the operating partner of the Company's sole >10% reference shareholder while self-assessed "independent," shares outstanding grew ~50% in five years against zero buybacks, and the AGM is virtual-only, which narrows real-time shareholder engagement even though it is legal under Section 118a AktG. None of this amounts to classic entrenchment — management is not blocking a bid, has not adopted defensive share issuance, and is publicly cooperating with the Uber process. The single biggest risk to minority holders is forward-looking, not historical: Uber's own stated plan to "evaluate the most appropriate ownership structure" once its economic ownership (with Prosus/Naspers's irrevocably tendered stake) crosses 50% raises the live possibility of a post-closing squeeze-out (Section 327a AktG, a 95% ownership threshold) or a domination agreement (a ~75% AGM threshold) that would set the terms on which any remaining minority stake is compressed or cashed out — a process where a newly dominant Uber, not today's dispersed shareholder base, would control the outcome. Combined with a Management Board whose equity fully vests on a change of control (a real, if modest, conflict of interest in supporting the deal), the verdict is **adequate**: today's formal rights are intact and better than a US dual-class or promoter-controlled peer, but the protection minority holders actually get from here depends heavily on how the pending change-of-control process plays out, not on anything the board can now guarantee. Separately, and independent of this board-quality read, the auditor-flagged Glovo Spain going-concern note (`business-model/01_disqualifier-scan.md`) caps the Group's overall stewardship verdict at no better than "Serious governance concerns" downstream, regardless of the board architecture assessed here.

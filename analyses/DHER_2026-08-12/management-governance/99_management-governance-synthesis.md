# Management-Governance Module — DHER (Synthesis)

## Abstract

Delivery Hero's stewardship record does not clear the bar for a trusted steward of shareholder capital: a founder-CEO with fifteen years of continuity has hit his own guided Adjusted EBITDA target three years running, but that operating discipline sits on top of a capital-allocation record that destroyed per-share value — €2,054m of cumulative goodwill impairment against two debt-and-stock-funded mega-deals (Woowa, Glovo), a 49.6% five-year share-count increase with zero buybacks, and book value per share down 75.4%. Incentives are only partly aligned — a genuine FY2024 redesign put 70% of the long-term plan on relative total shareholder return, but a large legacy option pool rewarded pure revenue growth through years of billion-euro losses, and insiders own just 0.38% of the company, entirely compensation-derived. The single biggest governance risk is an auditor-flagged, subsidiary-level going-concern note on Glovo Spain (a €440–770m contingent liability) that hard-locks this verdict regardless of the operating record, compounded by a live Uber tender offer in which management's equity fully vests on a change of control — a personal incentive detached from the price other shareholders receive. Verdict: serious governance concerns.

## 1A. Module Disconfirmation

- **Strongest bear point:** Capital allocation destroyed per-share value on the record, not on inference — €2,054m of cumulative goodwill impairment FY2021–FY2025 against two debt-and-stock-funded mega-deals (Woowa ~$4.0bn, Glovo €2.3bn), book value per share down 75.4% (€22.32→€5.50), tangible book value per share negative in every year of the window, and share count up 49.6% with zero buybacks in any year — the clearest evidence that per-share value was not the organizing principle of the last five years [`02_capital-allocation-scorecard.md`, Findings 02-009, 02-020, 02-021].
- **Strongest bull point (steelman):** The FY2024 incentive redesign is a real, cited improvement — 70% of the Long-Term Incentive now weights relative Total Shareholder Return (a genuine per-share, market-tested metric), malus/clawback was extended to cover both STI and LTI for the first time, and management has hit its own guided Adjusted EBITDA target within 1–2 percentage points in each of FY2023, FY2024, and FY2025 — a real, checkable three-year execution record on the metric it controls [`03_incentives-and-compensation.md`, Findings 03-007, 03-012; `01_management-and-track-record.md`, Findings 01-008–01-010].
- **Single killer risk (stewardship-specific):** The live Uber tender offer, layered on a change-of-control provision that fully and unconditionally vests all Management Board members' unvested STI/LTI on resignation within three months of a change of control — a direct personal financial incentive to see the deal close, independent of the per-share price achieved for other shareholders — combined with Uber's own statement that it will "evaluate the most appropriate ownership structure" once its economic ownership exceeds 50%, which raises real post-closing squeeze-out/domination-agreement risk for any minority holder who does not tender [`03_incentives-and-compensation.md`, Finding 03-018; `05_board-and-shareholder-rights.md`, Finding 05-021].
- **Disconfirming evidence already visible:** Management answered pointed, direct analyst questions candidly when challenged (the FCF-exclusion mechanics, the Glovo Spain contingent-liability range, and the "it's obviously a clear problem for us... it's not our decision" answer on the Uber overhang) rather than evading [`06_candor-and-disclosure-quality.md`, Findings 06-008, 06-010]; say-on-pay support was 93.05%; there is no dual-class structure, no promoter/insider pledging, and related-party transactions sit at ≈0.2% of revenue, immaterial and individually disclosed [`04_ownership-and-insider-behavior.md`, Finding 04-005; `05_board-and-shareholder-rights.md`, Findings 05-010, 05-013].

## 3. Reconciliation

Two disagreements are worth stating explicitly, both resolved conservatively:

1. **Capital allocation vs. incentive alignment.** `02_capital-allocation-scorecard` scores the historical record 40/100 (value-destructive), while `03_incentives-and-compensation` scores the FY2024-forward incentive framework 57/100 (mixed, improving). These are not contradictory once the timelines are separated: the capital-allocation score is a five-year backward look (FY2021–FY2025) dominated by the Woowa/Glovo mega-deal write-offs and the legacy revenue-CAGR option plan, while the incentive score credits a genuinely better system adopted starting FY2024 that has not yet had a full four-year cycle to prove itself. The reconciled view: the NEW framework is a real improvement but is one year old and untested, and it does not retroactively repair the value already destroyed under the OLD framework whose legacy options continue vesting through 2029.

2. **Board/shareholder-rights formal architecture vs. forward risk.** `05_board-and-shareholder-rights` finds the formal minority-protection architecture "genuinely clean" (one-share-one-vote, no poison pill, immaterial RPT) yet still scores the module's headline read as "adequate" rather than clean, because the pending Uber tender's squeeze-out/domination-agreement exposure is the dominant live risk. `04_ownership-and-insider-behavior` similarly declines to score the Uber process as a governance positive or negative on its own terms, treating it as a live event to monitor rather than a verdict input. The reconciled, more conservative view carried into this synthesis: today's formal architecture is real and better than a promoter-controlled or dual-class peer, but it provides no assurance about the terms on which any remaining minority stake would be compressed or cashed out if the deal closes without a full squeeze-out — that outcome is controlled by Uber and German takeover law, not by DHER's current board.

No other material disagreements exist between specialists; all six converge on describing FY2024 as an inflection point (better incentive design, better malus/clawback coverage, better financial-linkage in the STI) that is real but recent, sitting on top of a longer record (M&A, dilution, candor, board churn) that has not yet been offset by it.

## 5. Stewardship Summary

This management team has delivered on the promise it controls most tightly — Adjusted EBITDA — hitting its own guided target within a percentage point or two in each of the last three fiscal years, a real and checkable record. It has not delivered per-share value: five years of capital deployment, dominated by two debt-and-stock-funded mega-acquisitions (Woowa, Glovo), left the company with €2,054m of cumulative goodwill write-offs, book value per share down more than three-quarters, tangible book value negative in every year, and nearly 50% more shares outstanding with no offsetting buyback. Incentives point in a better direction than they did — the FY2024 redesign ties 70% of the long-term plan to relative shareholder return and extended clawback to cover both annual and long-term pay for the first time — but that framework is one year old, sits behind a large legacy option pool that rewarded pure top-line growth through years of billion-euro losses, and insiders hold just 0.38% of the company, entirely from compensation rather than purchase. Minority shareholders' formal rights are real (single share class, no poison pill, immaterial related-party leakage) but the protection those rights deliver now depends on how a pending third-party acquisition plays out, not on anything the current board can guarantee, and management's own equity vests in full on a change of control — a conflict of interest layered directly on top of the live process. The single most important reason not to extend this team the benefit of the doubt with new capital is structural, not behavioral: an auditor-flagged, subsidiary-level going-concern note on Glovo Spain, tied to a contingent liability of up to €770m, sits underneath a five-year capital-allocation record that already shows the company overpaid for the businesses that created that liability.

# Capital-Allocation Scorecard — DHER

**Context carried from `01_management-and-track-record.md` and `business-model/11_capital-allocation-governance.md`:** Delivery Hero SE (Frankfurt Prime Standard, XTRA:DHER; German Societas Europaea, IFRS as adopted by the EU, reporting currency EUR, fiscal year end 31-Dec) is, at this report's date, the subject of a live, announced acquisition offer from Uber Technologies (M&A call, 16-Jul-2026), with reference shareholder Prosus/Naspers "irrevocably committed to tender its stake" [Uber Technologies, Inc., Delivery Hero SE – M&A Call, 16-Jul-2026, transcript p.4]. This is a look-back scorecard on capital already spent by a still-independent, standalone board — it is not affected by the pending deal, but the pending deal is itself a data point about where five years of that capital allocation left the company (Section 5). The most recent audited source in the pool is the FY2024 Annual Report (published 25-Apr-2025); FY2025 figures come from the 26-Mar-2026 earnings-call transcript (management-stated) and the Capital IQ workbook export, cross-checked, not from an audited filing — flagged wherever it matters. This module deepens and supersedes `business-model/11`'s quick-read per `MODULE_RULES.md`.

## 1. Uses of Capital (5 years, FY2021–FY2025)

Currency: **EUR million**, cumulative FY2021–FY2025 (5 fiscal years), reporting standard IFRS as adopted by the EU [Delivery Hero SE XTRA DHER Financials.xls, Cash Flow tab; `earnings/01_historical-financials.md` §1].

**Denominator note (§15 hygiene):** cumulative CFO over this window is **−€892.9m** and cumulative FCF is **−€2,329.6m** — both negative, because the company was cash-flow-negative in three of the five years (FY2021–FY2023) before turning CFO-positive in FY2024–FY2025. **Serial-acquirer + opportunity-cost test (CLAUDE.md §24, Filter 4) — applied.** DHER built its current group through two debt-and-stock-funded mega-deals (Woowa ~$4.0bn, Glovo €2.3bn headline valuation, together the majority of the group's €4.4–5.0bn goodwill balance across FY2021–FY2025) plus a string of smaller bolt-ons and NCI buy-ins. Against those same deals, the group has recognized **€2,054.0m of cumulative goodwill impairment from FY2021–FY2025** (€85.9m + €760.9m + €857.8m/€894.6m + €89.7m + €259.7m, see the goodwill-impairment red-flag table already established in `business-model/11_capital-allocation-governance.md`) — direct, audited evidence that the group paid more for these businesses than they turned out to be worth. The opportunity cost is not confined to the write-downs themselves: to close the Woowa deal, DHER had to divest its own pre-existing Korean business as an antitrust condition (a forced divestiture directly caused by the acquisition, web-sourced); Glovo's rider-classification legal exposure (up to €770m unrecognized, Section 4) came bundled with the acquisition and now carries a subsidiary-level going-concern emphasis-of-matter; and the balance-sheet and per-share damage from the acquisition-and-dilution program (Section 3, Section 4) left the company financially stretched enough that, as of this report's date, its reference shareholder has agreed to sell the entire company to Uber rather than continue standalone — the ultimate foregone option is continued independence itself. **RF-CAP-004 is triggered.** Per `MODULE_RULES.md` Score Cap Rules, this caps the M&A-discipline component near the floor regardless of the two genuinely value-accretive moves in the period (the Hungerstation buy-in ahead of the Talabat listing, and the Talabat IPO itself) — those offset the M&A discipline score modestly upward from the floor but do not come close to lifting it out of the weak band. Bare "expand into Asia" / "strengthen the platform" language in the disclosed rationales is treated as narrative, not evidence, consistent with the rule that synergy language alone does not prove value creation.

This module runs BEFORE valuation, so buyback timing is **not** judged against intrinsic/fair value here — the valuation module has not run at the time of this report, so "Intrinsic-value discipline not assessable from this module" for the (non-existent) equity buybacks.

**A distinct, real capital-structure action must not be confused with an equity buyback.** In 2024–2025 DHER repurchased and cancelled roughly **€452m (2024) and €895.9m (2025)** face value of its own **convertible bonds** (debt instruments, not common shares) through tender offers and private/open-market purchases — €409.2m + €33.2m + €9.5m in 2024, and €23.5m + €537.5m + €334.9m in the Feb-2025 tender [FY24 Annual Report, Corporate Financing section, p.94–95]. These purchases were funded largely by new term-loan and add-on-facility issuance, not by discretionary FCF, and their purpose was maturity extension and (for the converts specifically) removing future conversion overhang before it dilutes shareholders — a modestly shareholder-protective act, but it is debt-liability management, not a capital-return program, and it does not appear as "Buybacks" in Section 1's uses-of-capital table (it is embedded in the "Debt repaid" line). Calling this a "buyback" without the distinction would overstate shareholder-return discipline that did not occur.

**Net effect for a holder of DHER common stock:** shares outstanding rose 49.6% over five years (199.3m → 298.2m) with **zero** offsetting mechanism at the parent level. Talabat (an 80%-owned subsidiary) separately announced its own subsidiary-level buyback of Talabat shares, into which DHER (as majority owner) explicitly declined to sell [Delivery Hero SE, 2025 Earnings Call transcript, 26-Mar-2026, p.11] — that is Talabat managing its own share count, not DHER managing DHER's, and does not change the parent-level dilution finding above. **RF-CAP-002 (buybacks not reducing share count) does not technically trigger** because there were no buybacks to test — the substantive concern (uncompensated dilution) is captured directly in the Per-Share Value Creation score (Section 4 below and the scorecard).

## 5. Capital-Allocation Read

Over FY2021–FY2025, Delivery Hero spent money it did not have from operations (cumulative CFO −€892.9m) on a mega-deal acquisition program (Woowa ~$4.0bn, Glovo €2.3bn headline) that has since absorbed €2,054.0m of cumulative goodwill impairment — direct, audited proof the company overpaid — while share count grew 49.6% with zero offsetting buyback in any year, driving book value per share down 75.4% and tangible book value per share negative in every single year of the window. The best use of capital in the period was the Talabat 20% IPO placement (Dec-2024, ~€1.8bn realized while retaining an 80% controlling stake) — a genuine, market-tested value-recycling event, alongside a defensible zero-dividend policy given persistent net losses and disciplined, below-D&A capex; the worst use was the acquisition program itself, whose opportunity cost extended beyond the write-downs to a forced Korean-unit divestiture (Woowa antitrust condition) and an inherited, unresolved up-to-€770m Spain rider-classification legal exposure now sitting alongside a subsidiary-level going-concern note. The single biggest capital-allocation signal is that a five-year record of serial, debt-and-stock-funded M&A plus uncompensated dilution left the company financially stretched enough that, as of this report's date, its reference shareholder has agreed to tender the entire company to Uber rather than continue standalone — the clearest evidence available that per-share value was not the organizing principle of the last five years' capital deployment. **Verdict: value-destructive / size-driven.**

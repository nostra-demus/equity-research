# Incentives & Compensation — DHER

**Context carried from `00_governance-data-triage.md` and `01_management-and-track-record.md`:** Delivery Hero SE is currently the subject of a live, announced acquisition offer from Uber Technologies (M&A call held Jul 16, 2026); reference shareholder Prosus/Naspers is "irrevocably committed to tender its stake" [Uber Technologies, Inc., Delivery Hero SE - M&A Call, Jul 16 2026, p.4]. `business-model/01_disqualifier-scan` also triggered a hard disqualifier (auditor-flagged, subsidiary-level going-concern note on Glovo Spain, €440–770m contingent social-security liability). This agent does not re-adjudicate that lock, but it bears on how the compensation read below should be weighted downstream. The compensation disclosure below is drawn from the FY2024 Annual Report's Compensation Report (Section 162 AktG, KPMG-audited) — the German proxy-equivalent — published Apr 25, 2025 and therefore ~15.6 months old at report date; no FY2025 Compensation Report is in the pool, so the FY2024 structure and figures below are the most current audited comp data available, and the live change-of-control provisions triggered by the pending Uber deal (Section 3 below) are read against that FY2024-dated contractual language.

Tranches granted 2018–2023 are still outstanding and vesting through exercise windows extending to 2029 [FY24 Annual Report, p.52].

**Read on the metrics.** The FY2024 redesign is a real, evidenced improvement over the pre-2024 regime: TSR at 70% of the LTI is a genuine per-share metric, and malus/clawback provisions were extended to cover the STI/LTI for the first time in FY2024 (see Section 3). But the STI's growth leg remains a menu of absolute-size options, the exact FY2024 selection is not disclosed in this filing, and a large stock of legacy options — granted purely for hitting a 20% revenue-CAGR bar during the company's heaviest loss-making and most dilutive years — continues to vest through 2029. The new system has one year of history; it has not yet been tested through a full four-year LTI cycle.

## 4. Incentive Alignment Read

The FY2024 redesign is a real, cited improvement: the Long-Term Incentive now weights relative Total Shareholder Return at 70% — a genuine per-share, market-tested metric — and malus/clawback was extended to cover both the STI and LTI for the first time. Set against that: the STI's growth leg is still chosen from three absolute-size options (revenue, GMV, gross profit) with the FY2024 selection undisclosed in this filing, a large book of legacy stock options — granted purely for clearing a 20% revenue-CAGR bar with no profitability or per-share test, comfortably beaten every year through FY2023 while the Group posted billions of euros in net losses and heavy share issuance — continues vesting through 2029, and the live Uber change-of-control provisions hand Management Board members full, unconditional vesting of all unvested equity on resignation, a direct personal incentive to see a deal close that is not tied to the per-share price achieved for other shareholders. The single biggest incentive signal is this split character: a genuinely well-designed new framework, only one year old and untested through a full vesting cycle, layered on top of a multi-year legacy of pure growth-for-growth's-sake option grants that are still working their way through the share count. **Conclusion: mixed.**

## What This Module Found

Tesla's calendar has a few genuinely dated, evidenced financial events sitting under a much larger story that carries no date at all.

The nearest proven event is the China Working Capital Facility's contractual refinancing window, which opens Sep-2026 (about six weeks out) and runs to Mar-2027. This is where $5,888 million of borrowing — 63.0% of Tesla's total gross debt (all the money it owes, before netting cash) — comes due. It is booked as long-term debt purely on management's stated "intent and ability to refinance," with no signed replacement facility disclosed anywhere in the filings [Q2 FY26 10-Q, Note 8 (Debt)]. That is the single most important driver on the dated calendar, and it is a concentration risk, not a positive trigger.

The single most important catalyst for the stock is different and undated: whether the market keeps pricing Tesla's unmonetized robotaxi, Optimus, and FSD potential near its historical multiple, or re-prices toward the roughly $32/share that the reverse-DCF's triangulated base case supports. (A reverse-DCF works backwards from today's price to show what growth the price already assumes.) This resolves gradually through hedged operational updates, not on a scheduled date, so a buyer cannot time it [valuation/07_scenario-and-fair-value.md].

The single most important risk is that same China facility window. It is the largest single-name concentration on the calendar, floating-rate, non-US, with no committed replacement financing on file [balance-sheet-survival/02_maturity-wall-and-refinancing.md].

There is a real counter-point: the facility has been expanded and successfully re-drawn three times in 18 months ($2,740mm → $4,288mm → $5,888mm since Dec-2024) — a dated track record of market access, not just hope [Q2 FY26 10-Q, Note 8].

## What Would Change This Read

- **China facility (Sep-2026–Mar-2027):** A disclosed, signed replacement or successful re-draw would be bullish. A failed or materially more expensive re-draw — especially if Chinese bank credit tightens alongside US-side liquidity strain — would be bearish.
- **Q3 FY2026 print (~Oct-21-2026):** A fifth straight revenue beat with operating profit recovering toward the FY2025 4.6% margin trend (Q2 2026 was 1.41%) would help. A revenue miss, or a repeat >30% operating-profit miss, would hurt.
- **Robotaxi ramp / Terafab / AI5 / Tesla Semi:** A dated, quantified, and met milestone would add real evidence. Continued slippage or hedged "soon/probably" language extends the pattern and changes nothing.
- **CEO Performance Award:** A new tranche certified "probable" would trigger a large non-cash margin miss with little warning — a repeat of the mechanism already seen on the current $9.82bn tranche.
- **Governance votes:** Lower votes-against on a future pay/capital item, or a declassification proposal reaching the ballot, would ease the overhang; a repeat of high (>20%) opposition or a fresh large equity award would worsen it.

## Plain-English Glossary

- **Gross debt:** All the money the company owes, before subtracting any cash it holds.
- **Refinancing window:** The contractual period in which a maturing loan must be paid off or replaced with a new one.
- **Floating-rate:** A loan whose interest cost moves with market rates, rather than being fixed.
- **Reverse-DCF:** A valuation check that works backwards from today's share price to reveal what future growth the price already assumes.
- **FCF (free cash flow):** The cash a business generates after paying for its capital spending.
- **Non-cash margin miss / SBC:** A drop in reported profit caused by stock-based compensation (paying staff/executives in shares), which lowers earnings without spending cash.

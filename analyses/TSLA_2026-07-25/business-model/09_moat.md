# Moat — TSLA

## 1. Named Competitors

(Inherited from `08_competitive-map.md`, which itself flags that Tesla's own filings in this data pool never name a specific competitor — only a Part III-only Form 10-K/A is present, with no Item 1A Competition section, and neither in-pool earnings-call transcript names a rival by name [`08_competitive-map.md`, §2, §5].)

- **BYD Company Limited** (HKEX:1211 / SZSE:002594) — battery-electric and plug-in-hybrid passenger vehicles globally; the volume leader that overtook Tesla as the world's top BEV seller in calendar 2025 (2.26 million BEV units vs Tesla's 1.64 million); named only in dated, unverified web trade press, not in any Tesla filing [`08_competitive-map.md`, §2].
- **Ford Motor Company** (NYSE:F) — US/global light-vehicle manufacturer with a growing EV lineup (Mustang Mach-E, F-150 Lightning); LTM revenue 1.83x Tesla's, but LTM net-income margin is −3.2% (a net loss) [`08_competitive-map.md`, §2, citing Capital IQ Comparable Analysis export].
- **General Motors Company** (NYSE:GM) — US/global light-vehicle manufacturer (Chevrolet, Cadillac, GMC, Buick) with its own EV lineup and a large China joint-venture presence; LTM revenue 1.79x Tesla's, profitable at the operating line but well below Tesla on gross and net margin, and carrying far more balance-sheet leverage (67% debt/capital vs Tesla's 15.5%) [`08_competitive-map.md`, §2].

**Own computed ROIC, cross-checked against the CIQ vendor figure.** The Capital IQ Ratios tab reports "Return on Capital %" of 2.94% (FY2025) and 2.745% (LTM Jun-30-2026) [`Tesla Inc NasdaqGS TSLA Financials_Annual.xls`, Ratios tab] — a vendor-computed figure whose internal tax assumption is not disclosed (back-solving implies something close to a flat ~35% rate, not Tesla's actual effective rate). Recomputing on a standard base — NOPAT = EBIT × (1 − normalized tax rate), ÷ average invested capital — using the **normalized structural tax rate of ~26%** (LTM effective rate 25.9%, FY2025 27.0%; both exclude the FY2023 anomaly, where Income Tax Expense was **−$5,001M**, a one-time $5.9bn deferred-tax valuation-allowance release that made the FY2023 "Effective Tax Rate %" line NM/negative and is not representative of Tesla's structural tax burden [`Tesla Inc NasdaqGS TSLA Financials_Annual.xls`, Income Statement tab, "Effective Tax Rate %" and "Total Deferred Taxes" rows]):

- **Net-of-cash (strict) basis:** invested capital = Total Debt + Total Equity − Cash & Equivalents, averaged Dec-31-2025 ($81,071M) and Jun-30-2026 ($88,380M) → average $84,726M [`Tesla Inc NasdaqGS TSLA Financials_Annual.xls`, Balance Sheet tab; `Financials_Quarterly.xls`, Balance Sheet tab]. NOPAT (LTM EBIT $4,278M × (1 − 0.259)) = $3,168M. **ROIC (net-of-cash) = 3.74%.**
- **Gross-capital (pre-cash-netting) basis, shown because Tesla is net-cash:** invested capital = Total Debt + Total Equity only, averaged over the same two dates → average $100,592M. **ROIC (gross-capital) = 3.15%.**

The computed figures (3.15%–3.74%) diverge modestly from the CIQ vendor figure (2.75%) — both point to the same conclusion, and the gap is flagged rather than averaged away. The more conservative of the two bases available (CIQ's own 2.75% headline, and the gross-capital 3.15% computed figure) is used for the economic-moat test below.

**Through-cycle read (required, not a single peak year).** Tesla's own CIQ-reported Return on Capital series: FY2021 10.52%, **FY2022 18.58% (cyclical peak** — pandemic-era supply shortage plus a near-monopoly EV position, not repeatable [`07_business-quality.md` §4]), FY2023 8.90%, FY2024 5.97%, FY2025 2.94%, LTM 2.75% [`Tesla Inc NasdaqGS TSLA Financials_Annual.xls`, Ratios tab]. The 5-year average (FY2021–FY2025) is **~9.4%**; including LTM, **~8.3%**. Even on this through-cycle basis — which is far more favorable to Tesla than the latest LTM figure — the return sits below the cost-of-capital estimate developed next.

**The economic moat test (required).**

> Return on capital **below** cost of capital: **~2.75%–3.7% ROIC** (LTM, CIQ vendor / own computed range) and **~8.3%–9.4% ROIC** (5-year through-cycle average) vs **~11.5% WACC** (gap of roughly **770–885 basis points** on the LTM read, and **~210–320 basis points** even on the more favorable through-cycle average) — [Tesla Inc NasdaqGS TSLA Financials_Annual.xls, Ratios tab; own NOPAT/invested-capital calculation above; WACC is a CAPM estimate, **Inference, not from filings**, basis below].

**Cost-of-capital basis (no company-disclosed WACC or hurdle rate exists in this pool).** Tesla does not state a cost of equity, WACC, or hurdle rate anywhere in the filings or transcripts in this data pool — this is a CAPM estimate, clearly labelled inference: cost of equity = risk-free rate (~4.3%, approximate 10-year US Treasury yield, mid-2026) + beta (1.80, the disclosed 5-year beta [`Tesla-Inc-NasdaqGS-TSLA-Public-Company-Profile.txt`; Capital IQ Comparable Analysis export, Operating Statistics tab]) × equity-risk-premium (~5.0%, a standard long-run US estimate) ≈ **13.3%**. Pre-tax cost of debt ≈ interest expense ÷ average total debt (LTM $334M ÷ ~$15,400M ≈ 2.2%; Tesla's disclosed debt carries unusually low coupons relative to its rating, a fact not independently explained in this pool), after-tax at the 25.9% normalized rate ≈ 1.6%. Weighting at the LTM debt/capital mix (15.5% debt / 84.5% equity [`Tesla Inc NasdaqGS TSLA Financials_Annual.xls`, Ratios tab, "Total Debt/Capital %"]): **WACC ≈ 11.5%.** *Inference, not from filings.*

## 4. Where The Company Sits

1. **Relative to peers.** Tesla sits at the **top** of the three named competitors on gross margin (18.9% vs Ford's 7.1% and GM's 10.2%) [Capital IQ Comparable Analysis export, Operating Statistics tab] but the **bottom** among the profitable pair on EBIT margin trend direction — Tesla's EBIT margin has fallen every year for three straight years (16.8% FY2022 → 4.6% FY2025 → 4.1% LTM [`07_business-quality.md` row: Margin stability]) while GM's LTM EBIT margin (5.5%) currently exceeds Tesla's (4.1%) in absolute terms despite GM's much lower gross margin. BYD's return-on-capital cannot be compared at all — not disclosed anywhere in this pool, only a web-derived, unverified net-margin estimate exists [`08_competitive-map.md` §2].
2. **Absolute (the economic moat test).** The company earns a return on capital **below** its cost of capital: **~2.75%–3.7%** LTM (and **~8.3%–9.4%** even on the more favorable 5-year through-cycle average) **vs ~11.5%** estimated WACC — the decisive test that any structural advantage identified in §2 has not translated into economic value creation.

## 5. Moat Verdict

**No moat proven.**

The strongest candidate is **Technology / IP** (strength 50/100) — proprietary battery-cell/lithium-refining vertical integration, the NACS charging standard now licensed to rival OEMs, and a paid FSD-subscription attach rate near 55% of North America deliveries [`06_value-chain.md` §1] — followed by Distribution (45/100, direct-to-consumer with no dealer network) and Brand (40/100, evidenced by the equity multiple, not by pricing power). But **CLAUDE.md's hard rule applies**: return on capital sits below cost of capital on every basis computed here — LTM (2.75%–3.7% vs ~11.5% WACC) and even the more forgiving 5-year through-cycle average (8.3%–9.4% vs ~11.5% WACC) — so no combination of these structural advantages can be scored a Strong or even a middling Narrow moat; this is a **moat in structure, not economics**: real scale, distribution, and technology assets that have not lifted the return on capital to or above what raising that capital costs. Durability over the next 5 years would need proof that automotive gross margin (16.2% FY2025, falling every year [`07_business-quality.md` row: Margin stability]) stabilizes as the shrinking regulatory-credit line fully runs off, and that FSD/robotaxi/Optimus — still a zero-disclosed-revenue-line item — converts a technology asset into a return-on-capital improvement rather than a growing capital drag; per `07_business-quality.md`'s "fast-changing industry" flag (rate-of-change score 30, ≤40), any such durability claim is discounted further because the winners in EV, battery-chemistry, and autonomous-driving competition are not knowable in advance (CLAUDE.md §24, Filter 5).

**Moat trajectory — eroding.** Return on capital has fallen every year for three straight years and the gap to cost of capital has widened, not narrowed: from **above** WACC in FY2021–FY2022 (10.5%–18.6% vs ~11.5% WACC) to materially **below** WACC every year since (8.9% FY2023, 6.0% FY2024, 2.9% FY2025, 2.75% LTM) [`Tesla Inc NasdaqGS TSLA Financials_Annual.xls`, Ratios tab]. Pricing power is falling in parallel — automotive gross margin fell every year FY2023–FY2025 with management crediting only mix, FX, and a shrinking regulatory-credit line, not sticker-price increases, for holding margin up at all [`06_value-chain.md` §3] — and global BEV volume share was lost to BYD on a full calendar-year-2025 basis (Tesla 8.8% vs BYD 12.1% [`08_competitive-map.md` §3]). Entry/disruption pressure is rising, not falling, per `07_business-quality.md`'s Competitive intensity (28/100) and rate-of-change (30/100) rows. The one genuine counter-signal — automotive-sales revenue up 23–27% year-on-year in Q1–Q2 FY2026 after a Q4 FY2025 decline [`08_competitive-map.md` §3] — is a volume rebound, not evidence the margin or capital-return trend has turned; it does not change the trajectory call.

## 5. Minority-Shareholder Protection Read

The board is independent on paper — 77.8% of directors, a separate independent Chair, and four all-independent standing committees, with a real Audit Committee RPT-review process — but its practical record is one of ratifying the CEO's own terms rather than constraining them: it approved a record-setting pay package that both ISS and Glass Lewis recommended against and that roughly a quarter of voted shares opposed, and it has not implemented a board-declassification change that a majority of voted shares supported in 2024, citing a supermajority bylaw threshold. The single biggest self-dealing and entrenchment risk to minority holders is not any one number in the related-party table (all currently below the module's High/Critical thresholds) — it is the sequence of structural changes since 2024: reincorporating from Delaware (where a nine-share investor forced judicial review of Musk's 2018 pay package) to Texas, then adopting a 3%-of-outstanding-shares threshold for derivative suits that only Musk himself and the largest index funds can individually clear, layered on a classified board that a majority of shareholders has already voted, unsuccessfully, to unwind. **Verdict: weak / entrenched.** Two of the most consequential facts in this section (the supermajority bylaw threshold and the 3% derivative-suit bylaw) could not be independently confirmed against a primary document inside this data pool and are corroborated only by dated web reporting — a follow-up to pull the current bylaws exhibit is the single highest-value next step for this specific finding.

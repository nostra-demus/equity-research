## 2. What This Module Found

The core read: this management team has not turned shareholder money into value per share. Invested capital (the money put into the business) grew 141% from FY2021 to FY2025, but operating profit (EBIT) fell 33% and per-share earnings (diluted EPS) fell 70% from their FY2022 peak [`02_capital-allocation-scorecard.md` §5]. The company bought back zero shares in any year since at least FY2017, even though it produced positive free cash flow (the cash left after running and investing in the business) every year, and its share count rose 27.4% [RF-CAP-002].

The single most important driver of the verdict is a coordinated entrenchment sequence — the board reincorporated the company from Delaware to Texas (2024), then set a 3%-of-shares minimum to bring a derivative suit (a shareholder lawsuit on the company's behalf), on top of a classified board (directors elected in staggered groups, so the whole board can't be replaced at once) and a 66⅔%-of-shares supermajority bylaw that has already blocked a majority-backed 2024 vote to undo the staggered board. Each step narrows a tool minority shareholders had used, and the timing lines up with the multi-year court fight over the CEO's own pay [`05_board-and-shareholder-rights.md` §4, §4A; RF-SHR-002].

The single most important risk is an unresolved federal securities-fraud class action naming Tesla and CEO Musk personally over Autopilot/FSD/Robotaxi claims — the exact autonomy subject where this module independently found two dated 2025 promises were missed. A motion to dismiss is pending; if it proceeds, this is the fact pattern most capable of invalidating the autonomy-led bull case [`01_management-and-track-record.md` §4A; RF-MGT-005].

Two genuine positives sit against this. The CEO took $0 cash and $0 realized pay in 2025 despite a $158.4bn accounting headline, so the pay is genuinely at-risk, and Tesla avoided the serial-acquirer trap entirely (near-zero M&A) [`03_incentives-and-compensation.md` §4; `02_capital-allocation-scorecard.md` §2]. But "at-risk" is not the same as "aligned": none of the 12 milestone tranches in either CEO award (2018 or 2025) uses a per-share, return-on-capital (ROIC), or EPS metric — all reward absolute scale (deliveries, subscriptions, robots, Adjusted-EBITDA dollars), and the market-cap hurdle is diluted by the award's own share issuance [`03_incentives-and-compensation.md` §3].

## 3. The Specialists, Briefly

- **Governance data triage** — Sufficient; all six specialists could run, no data caps bind. The 10-K/A Part III works as a full proxy-equivalent covering pay, ownership, board, and related-party transactions.
- **Management & track record (46/100)** — Two dated 2025 promises (return to delivery growth; FSD launch in Europe/China) were missed, on the exact subject now under the securities-fraud class action.
- **Capital allocation (39/100)** — Value-destructive: incremental ROIC (profit on each new dollar invested) is negative on both an EBIT (−3.8%) and after-tax NOPAT (−4.6%) basis; EPS −70% from peak; invested capital +141%; zero buybacks.
- **Incentives & compensation (53/100)** — $0 realized CEO pay in 2025 is real, but no per-share, ROIC, or EPS metric appears anywhere in either CEO award.
- **Ownership & insider behavior (73/100)** — CEO stake is large (20.3% on the 10-K/A basis, rising to 29.9% after a June-2026 option exercise) but overwhelmingly award-derived, not bought; one genuine ~$1.0bn open-market purchase (Sep-2025) is the exception. A Voting Agreement hands the CEO progressively more voting control (423.7M shares) as tranches vest.
- **Board & shareholder rights (42/100)** — The entrenchment sequence above; narrows minority tools step by step.
- **Candor & disclosure (45/100)** — Split by speaker: the CFO owns bad quarters with five named, quantified miss-drivers; the CEO called the worst recent quarter (Q2 2026, EPS miss −38.9% vs. consensus) "a great quarter" with no reference to the miss.

**Most important disagreement, and how it was resolved:** ownership scored well (73) while board/rights scored weak (42) on the same concentrated CEO stake. The synthesis reconciled these conservatively — large insider ownership is a genuine positive for skin-in-the-game but, inside this specific control structure, compounds rather than offsets the entrenchment risk to other shareholders. Shareholder friendliness is anchored to the board/rights read (42), not averaged upward.

## 4. What Would Change This Read

**Would strengthen the verdict:** dismissal of the securities-fraud class action on the merits; a board-initiated reversal of the 3%-derivative-suit threshold and/or the supermajority bylaw; a per-share or ROIC metric added to a future CEO/NEO award; a return to positive incremental ROIC with EPS growth; a buyback funded from the existing net-cash cushion.

**Would weaken it:** an adverse ruling or settlement in the securities-fraud case; a further mega-grant to the CEO before the 2025 award is even partly earned; a widening GAAP-to-Adjusted-EBITDA gap (the gap between reported profit and management's adjusted figure); further capital committed to CEO-controlled related parties with no independent return hurdle.

**Data most needed:** the current Tesla bylaws exhibit (to confirm the supermajority and 3%-threshold provisions directly, rather than via dated web sources); the original FY2025 10-K auditor's opinion; a tabulated AGM vote count; and the FY2026 actual capex/EBIT outcome against the reaffirmed >$25bn capex floor.

## 5. Bottom Line

- **Verdict: Serious governance concerns.** Governance Score 43/100, "Weak" rating, capped at no better than Weak by the Critical-red-flag rule. §24 integrity filter tripped; conviction ceiling "Watchlist" on the overall investment rating.
- **Biggest reason it could be better than it looks:** the CEO holds a real 20.3–29.9% stake, took $0 cash and $0 realized 2025 pay, and the company avoided the serial-acquirer trap — the *form* of alignment and capital discipline is present.
- **Biggest reason it could be worse:** the entrenchment sequence and the unresolved fraud suit both concentrate on defending the CEO's position and pay, not protecting the shareholders whose capital funds it — and no counterweight in the record offsets that pattern.
- **What evidence is missing:** the two most consequential entrenchment facts (the 66⅔% supermajority bylaw and the 3%-of-shares derivative-suit threshold) rest on dated web sources, not a primary bylaws document in this pool.
- **The one thing to watch next:** the ruling on the motion to dismiss in the securities-fraud class action.
- **Handoff:** this module supersedes the business-model quick-read on governance; the master synthesizer should treat this verdict, the 43/100 score, and the red-flag register as the primary governance read for TSLA.

## 6. Plain-English Glossary

- **EBIT (operating profit):** profit from running the business, before interest and tax.
- **EPS (diluted earnings per share):** company profit divided by all shares, counting shares that options could create.
- **Free cash flow:** cash left after running the business and paying for capital spending.
- **Buyback:** company using cash to repurchase its own shares, raising each remaining share's slice.
- **Invested capital:** the total money put into the business to run and grow it.
- **Incremental ROIC (return on invested capital):** the profit earned on each new dollar invested; negative here means new money lost value.
- **NOPAT:** operating profit after tax — an after-tax version of the same profit measure.
- **Derivative suit:** a lawsuit a shareholder brings on the company's behalf, here against insiders.
- **Classified / staggered board:** directors elected in separate groups over several years, so the whole board can't be replaced in one vote.
- **Supermajority bylaw:** a rule requiring far more than half the shares to pass certain changes.
- **Related-party transaction:** a deal with an entity the CEO or an insider controls, where terms may not be arm's-length.
- **GAAP-to-Adjusted-EBITDA gap:** the distance between official reported profit and management's own adjusted profit figure; a wide gap invites scrutiny.
- **Net-cash cushion:** cash on hand above total debt, available to fund buybacks or counter-cyclical moves.

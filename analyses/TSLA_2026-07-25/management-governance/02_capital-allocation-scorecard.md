## 2. M&A Scorecard

No material controlling-acquisition M&A occurred in the FY2021–FY2025 period: cash acquisitions were $0 in four of the five years and $64M in FY2023 only [Financials_Annual.xls, Cash Flow tab, "Cash Acquisitions" row]. Cash acquisitions were also $0 in FY2017, FY2020, FY2021, FY2022, FY2024, FY2025 and LTM, with small amounts ($115M FY2017, $18M FY2018, $45M FY2019, $13M FY2020) only in the pre-2021 tail. Tesla is not a serial acquirer on this record, and `business-model/11_capital-allocation-governance.md` independently reaches the same conclusion (acquisition-pattern severity scored 20/100, well below the Filter-4 trigger threshold) [`business-model/11_capital-allocation-governance.md` §1, §Acquisition pattern test].

**Serial-acquirer + opportunity-cost test (CLAUDE.md §24, Filter 4).** Tesla does not show a serial-acquirer pattern — cash M&A has been at or near zero for the entire FY2021–FY2025 window, so RF-CAP-004 is **not triggered** and no Filter-4 score cap applies to the M&A-discipline component. The SpaceX equity investment is not an operating acquisition and is not charged against the Filter-4 test, but it is charged against M&A discipline on its own terms: it is a $2.0bn capital commitment to a related party controlled by the company's own CEO, with a governance conflict-of-interest dimension that ordinary M&A discipline (arm's-length price discovery, an independent return hurdle, disclosed synergy) cannot address by design — a controlled party cannot negotiate at arm's length with itself. That conflict, not deal size, is the reason this scores as a **red flag** rather than a clean pass (RF-CAP-001 below).

**RF-CAP-001 (Large acquisition with vague rationale).** Triggered on the SpaceX equity investment: $2.0bn committed to a related party with no disclosed independent return hurdle, synergy quantification, or arm's-length negotiation process — only "upon receiving the applicable regulatory approvals." Severity: Medium (2.4% of cumulative FY2021–FY2025 CFO; not large enough to be High/Critical on the MODULE_RULES related-party materiality scale on its own, but the governance-conflict dimension raises it above Low). Follow-up: track whether Tesla discloses an independent valuation, a board process separate from the CEO, or any performance milestone tied to this investment in future filings.

**Incremental ROIC methodology and figures.** Invested capital is proxied by CIQ's "Total Capital" (Total Debt + Total Common Equity + Total Minority Interest) [Financials_Annual.xls, Capital Structure Summary tab]: FY2021 $40,456M → FY2022 $51,646M → FY2023 $73,182M → FY2024 $87,303M → FY2025 $97,584M — invested capital grew 141% over four years. Against that, GAAP operating income (EBIT) fell from $6,523M (FY2021) to $4,355M (FY2025) [`earnings/01_historical-financials.md` §1], and NOPAT (EBIT × (1 − effective tax rate), using CIQ's reported effective tax rates of 11.0% FY2021 and 27.0% FY2025 [Financials_Annual.xls, Income Statement tab, "Effective Tax Rate %" row]) fell from $5,804M to $3,181M over the same span.

- **Cumulative incremental ROIC, EBIT basis, FY2021→FY2025:** ΔEBIT = $(2,168)M ÷ ΔInvested Capital $57,128M = **−3.8%**. Every dollar of net new capital invested over the period is associated with a small negative change in operating profit, not a return.
- **Cumulative incremental ROIC, NOPAT basis, FY2021→FY2025:** ΔNOPAT = $(2,623)M ÷ ΔInvested Capital $57,128M = **−4.6%**.
- This is directionally consistent with — and cross-checks against — CIQ's own reported "Return on Capital %" level (not incremental, a different metric): 18.6% (FY2022 cyclical peak) → 8.9% (FY2023) → 6.0% (FY2024) → 2.9% (FY2025) → 2.75% (LTM Jun-2026) [Financials_Annual.xls, Ratios tab, "Return on Capital %" row; already cited cross-module in `business-model/07_business-quality.md`]. The level has collapsed and the incremental (marginal) return on the capital added since FY2021 is negative.
- **Reinvestment intensity:** capex has run 1.4x–2.2x depreciation & amortization every year since 2021 (FY2025: $8,527M capex vs $6,148M D&A = 1.39x; LTM: $12,923M vs $6,477M = 2.00x) even as FY2025 revenue fell 2.9% and operating margin compressed from 7.8% to 4.6% [`business-model/11_capital-allocation-governance.md` §1, "Capex intensity vs depreciation" row]. Capital is being deployed at an accelerating rate into a business whose marginal return on that capital, by the calculation above, is negative.

**FY2023 tax-rate caveat.** FY2023's reported effective tax rate is not meaningful (a $5,001M one-off tax benefit from a valuation-allowance release, not a cash tax outcome) [Financials_Annual.xls, Income Statement tab, "Income Tax Expense" row, FY2023 = $(5,001)M]; this scorecard therefore anchors the incremental-ROIC calculation on the FY2021 and FY2025 endpoints rather than including FY2023 as an intermediate NOPAT data point, to avoid a distorted mid-period read. Year-by-year EBIT-basis increments (all negative or small): FY2021→FY2022 +64.1% (the cyclical FY2022 peak — capital added and profit rose faster); FY2022→FY2023 −22.3%; FY2023→FY2024 −8.7%; FY2024→FY2025 −32.1%. Three of the last four annual increments are negative.

## 5. Capital-Allocation Read

Capital has not been allocated to create per-share value over FY2021–FY2025: invested capital grew 141% while EBIT fell 33% and diluted EPS fell from a FY2022 peak of $3.62 to $1.08 in FY2025 (down further to $1.08 TTM, −35.3% year-on-year) [`earnings/01_historical-financials.md` §1, §2], and the incremental return on the $57 billion of capital added since FY2021 is negative on both an EBIT and NOPAT basis (−3.8% / −4.6%, §4 above). The best use of capital in the period is the one that did not happen: Tesla avoided the serial-acquirer trap entirely (near-zero M&A) and this is a genuine credit, not a hedge — most acquisition-hungry companies destroy value, and Tesla simply did not spend on that channel. The worst use of capital is the combination of rising, low-marginal-return capex (1.4x–2.2x depreciation, funding an unproven AI/robotics/Robotaxi bet per `business-model/07_business-quality.md`) with zero capital returned through buybacks or dividends despite a growing net-cash cushion on a broad basis, while share count rose every single year (27.4% over the FY2021–LTM window) with no offsetting repurchase activity. The single biggest capital-allocation signal is the FY2025 share-count jump of 535 million shares tied to CEO Performance Award mechanics, landing on top of a $132.3 billion maximum-value new award (Sept-2025) and a $2.0 billion related-party equity investment in a company the CEO personally controls (Mar-2026) — capital and share-count decisions are increasingly concentrated around the CEO's own compensation and outside interests rather than routed through arm's-length capital-return or M&A discipline. **Conclusion: value-destructive / size-driven** — the size-driven element runs through organic capex and share-count growth rather than through M&A, but the per-share arithmetic (rising invested capital, falling EBIT, falling EPS, rising share count, zero capital returned) is the same failure mode CLAUDE.md §24 exists to catch.

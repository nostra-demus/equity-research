## 3. Stated Strategy & Targets

Management frames Tesla's next growth phase around bringing AI into the physical world: scaling Full Self-Driving (Supervised) subscriptions, expanding the Robotaxi ride-hailing service beyond Austin, and ramping the Optimus humanoid robot, layered on top of the existing vehicle and energy-storage businesses [Q2 FY2026 10-Q, Item 2 MD&A, "We are focused on bringing artificial intelligence into the real world"]. The only quantified, checkable target in the current data pool is a capital-expenditure floor for FY2026 — "more than $25 billion" — reaffirmed on both the Q1 and Q2 2026 calls [Q2 2026 Earnings Call transcript, Jul 22, 2026, prepared remarks, CFO Vaibhav Taneja]. Tesla issues no formal point guidance for revenue, EBITDA, or EPS [`earnings/04_guidance-consensus.md` §2], so beyond that capex floor, forward "targets" are qualitative (energy gross margin "normalizing" in the "mid- to low 20%" range long-term; Robotaxi fleet expansion "throughout the year") rather than dated, numeric commitments this agent can score.

## 5. Management Read

Musk's tenure (18 years as CEO) and the fully-vested, hard-milestone 2018 pay package are real evidence this management can deliver against a numeric bar — but the two specific, dated promises checkable in this data pool for 2025 (a return to delivery growth; an FSD Supervised launch in Europe and China) were both missed, and the one autonomy promise that was kept (U.S. Robotaxi) arrived a month late in a single city with the safety monitor removed only "on a limited basis." Below the top two seats, the bench has thinned — four senior operating/policy/robotics leaders, including the Optimus engineering chief, left within the last ~14 months, and CEO succession planning is gated behind the two least-likely pay tranches and is a framework Musk himself is tasked with writing. The single biggest signal is the unresolved federal securities-fraud class action alleging Tesla and Musk overstated the same Autopilot/FSD/Robotaxi capabilities this agent independently found were missed on their own stated 2025 timeline — an unproven but unignorable overlap between what management promised, what it delivered, and what it is now being sued over.

**Read:** across both CEO performance-award generations (2018 and 2025) and the entire NEO population, Tesla has never used a per-share or capital-returns metric (ROIC, ROCE, EPS, FCF/share) in a bonus or long-term incentive plan. The two available proxies for shareholder value — the market-cap hurdles (aggregate, diluted by the awards themselves) and stock-option price appreciation (undiluted per-share exposure, but with no scaled target) — are the closest things to per-share alignment in the entire structure, and neither is a true per-share metric.

## 4. Incentive Alignment Read

Tesla's pay design gets one thing structurally right and one thing structurally wrong. What is right: cash pay is functionally zero across all three named executives relative to total opportunity, there is no annual cash bonus of any kind for anyone, and the CEO realized $0 in 2025 despite a $158.4 billion accounting-basis headline — nothing is guaranteed, and everything is multi-year, at-risk equity with 5-to-10-year lockups. What is wrong: the metrics that actually unlock that equity are size, not per-share value — vehicle deliveries, FSD subscription counts, bots delivered, robotaxis in service, and absolute-dollar Adjusted EBITDA thresholds, gated behind an aggregate market-capitalization ladder that the awards themselves dilute (423.7 million new shares from the 2025 award alone, roughly 10.7% of shares outstanding at grant, stacked on the 2018 award's 304.0 million shares) — there is no ROIC, ROCE, EPS, or FCF-per-share metric anywhere in either CEO performance award or in the CFO/SVP option grants. The single biggest incentive signal is that a $132.3 billion award (and its $26.1 billion now-forfeited predecessor) can be earned by hitting absolute scale and an aggregate valuation the company's own share issuance helps inflate, with zero requirement that per-share economics — earnings per share, cash flow per share, or return on invested capital — improve at all. Combined with ~30% shareholder opposition at the last say-on-pay vote and a multi-year Delaware court fight over the prior award's validity, this is a pay structure built to reward Tesla getting bigger, not Tesla getting better per share. Conclusion: **mixed** — genuinely at-risk, long-dated equity design (a real positive) delivered against metrics that are overwhelmingly size-based rather than per-share (a real negative), with no per-share metric present anywhere to offset it.

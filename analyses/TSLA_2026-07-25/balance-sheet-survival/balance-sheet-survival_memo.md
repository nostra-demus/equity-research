## What This Module Found

- Tesla is barely borrowed. Net debt (total debt minus cash) is just $861 million on the strictest reading, against $10,849 million of yearly EBITDA (earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortization — a rough proxy for operating cash earnings) — a leverage ratio of 0.08x. On broader but still-standard readings the company is net cash by $5,877 million up to $34,182 million [`01`, §4].
- The real risk is *concentration*, not size. On a true-due-date (contractual) basis, 78.2% of debt ($7,306 million) comes due within 12 months, and 63% of that is a single $5,888 million China working-capital loan [`02`, §1b].
- That China loan is booked on the balance sheet as long-term based on management's stated "intent and ability to refinance" it — an accounting judgment, not a signed replacement agreement. That promise, not a shortage of cash, is the single thing to watch [`02`, §1c].
- The cushion is large. Usable liquidity (cash, short-term investments, and an undrawn revolving credit line — a pre-committed backup borrowing facility) is $48,238 million, covering the contractual 12-month wall about 6.6 times over without needing any operating cash flow to show up. That gives a runway of at least 28 months on the most conservative math [`03`, §1, §3].
- The stress test shows no covenant breach and no cash shortfall until EBITDA falls 92–99% — essentially total loss of operating earnings. A normal recession-scale drop of 30–40% is comfortably survived, and Tesla already lived through a comparable 39.1% peak-to-trough EBITDA fall (FY2022→FY2025) with no balance-sheet strain [`06`, §2–§3].
- The main limits are disclosure gaps, not evidence of fragility: no covenant number is disclosed, and five open lawsuits/investigations carry no dollar estimate [`04`, §2; `05`].

## The Specialists, Briefly

- **Data triage:** Sufficient — recent balance sheet, instrument-level debt note, and cash flow all exist; the one real gap is no quantified covenant threshold.
- **Capital structure & leverage:** Near-zero leverage on every basis (net debt $861M / 0.08x); 99.98% of on-balance-sheet debt is non-recourse to the parent — issued by subsidiary vehicles or the offshore China facility.
- **Maturity wall & refinancing:** "Refinanceable in most markets," not self-funded — GAAP shows 15.2% of debt due within a year, but the contractual figure is 78.2%, driven by the China facility.
- **Liquidity runway:** ≈28.2 months on the conservative basis, not dependent on free cash flow; usable liquidity is 6.6x the contractual 12-month wall.
- **Coverage & covenants:** Interest coverage is extreme (32.48x) but reflects a tiny debt base, not proven resilience; covenant headroom not assessable.
- **Off-balance-sheet & contingencies:** Largest quantified exposure is a $4.07B vehicle resale-value guarantee cap (5.07% of equity, below the spike trigger); five active litigation/regulatory matters carry no disclosed dollar figure.
- **Downside stress test:** Survives every haircut modeled with a >$26B liquidity surplus; break points (92–99% EBITDA decline) reflect the near-debt-free balance sheet, not a downturn-proof operating business (EBIT margin fell from 16.8% in FY2022 to 4.6% in FY2025).

The most important is the maturity wall: GAAP-current classification (15.2% due within 12 months) versus the contractual view (78.2%). The synthesis leads with the contractual 78.2% as the operative figure, because the long-term booking of the China facility rests on an accounting judgment, not a signed agreement (conservative-default rule, §4). ## What Would Change This Read

**Would strengthen it (§6):**
- A disclosed, signed replacement facility or bond issue for the China loan ahead of its Sep-2026–Mar-2027 maturity.
- A disclosed covenant threshold showing genuinely wide numeric headroom.
- Resolution of the five open litigation matters at immaterial amounts.

**Would weaken it (§6):**
- A failed or materially more expensive re-draw of the China facility.
- Chinese bank credit and US capital-markets access tightening at the same time (the single killer risk — not modeled in this pool, §1A).
- Disclosure of a real covenant tighter than the labeled assumption used here.
- A large unquantified litigation loss crystallizing.

**Data needed:** the credit agreement/indenture (actual covenant thresholds); the full FY2025 10-K commitments/contingencies note; any signed refinancing or extension for the China facility.

## Bottom Line

- Verdict: **Solid, not Fortress** — well-cushioned, but the maturity wall and disclosure gaps are real.
- Why it could be better than it looks: the China facility has been expanded and re-drawn three times in 18 months ($2,740M → $4,288M → $5,888M), and liquidity covers the near-term wall ~6.6x without needing any operating cash flow [`02`, §4; `03`, §1].
- Why it could be worse than it looks: 78% of debt is contractually due within a year, sitting almost entirely in one unhedged China loan whose long-term booking rests on a promise, not a signed deal [`02`, §1b].
- What's missing: no disclosed covenant number (so "how close to breaking" can't be answered with real data), no full FY2025 contingency notes, and a single stale credit rating (S&P BBB/Stable, Oct-2022).
- One thing to watch next: whether Tesla discloses a signed refinancing or replacement for the China working-capital facility before its Sep-2026–Mar-2027 maturity.

This module assigns no probabilities and issues no rating; the master synthesizer's "Balance Sheet and Survival Test" should defer to it, using the 92–99% EBITDA break point as the downside input (§7).

## Plain-English Glossary

- **Net debt:** all borrowings minus cash on hand. A negative figure ("net cash") means more cash than debt.
- **EBITDA:** earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortization — a rough proxy for operating cash earnings.
- **Leverage ratio (net debt / EBITDA):** how many years of earnings it would take to repay net debt; lower is safer.
- **Contractual (true-date) basis:** when debt actually falls due by its terms, versus how it is classified on the balance sheet.
- **Liquidity runway:** how many months the company can meet obligations from cash and committed facilities alone.
- **Revolving credit line (revolver / RCF):** a pre-committed backup borrowing facility the company can draw on.
- **Covenant / covenant headroom:** a lender's rule the borrower must obey (e.g. a maximum leverage limit) and how far it is from breaking it. Here, no such rule's number is disclosed.
- **Interest coverage:** how many times earnings cover interest owed; higher is safer.
- **Non-recourse debt:** borrowing that lenders can claim only against specific assets, not the parent company's general assets.

# Beat / Miss Setup — NU

## 1. Next Reporting Period Context

Nu is expected to file FQ3 2026, the standalone three months ending 30 September 2026, on 12 November 2026; the date is a Capital IQ calendar field, not a company commitment. **FQ3 standalone bar = FQ3 standalone vendor estimate: US$5,936.74m revenue and US$0.22164 GAAP EPS. No addition is required.** The bar implies 42.3% revenue growth and 39.0% EPS growth versus Q3 2025 on the same standalone-quarter basis. [Capital IQ Estimates — Consensus, Fiscal Quarters and current fiscal-year header, FQ3 2026; Capital IQ Estimates — Surprise, Fiscal Quarters, Q3 2025]

The available record does not support a three-year, like-for-like Q3 seasonality conclusion. The Q3 revenue bar has risen 7.0% in 90 days, and Nu gives no numeric revenue or EPS guidance against which to test it. [Capital IQ Estimates — Trends, FQ3 2026; Q2 2026 earnings call, Q&A, 2026-08-13, pp. 8–11]

Likelihood labels below are judgment based on the cited current drivers, not empirical probabilities; the four-quarter surprise history is too small to measure a repeatable beat rate.

## 6. Seasonality Read

Seasonality neither helps nor hurts the FQ3 setup on the available evidence: the historical-financials review found insufficient like-for-like primary quarterly history for a three-year seasonality table. Management has described Q4 as seasonally higher for purchase volume and revenue, but that is a Q4 observation, not evidence that Q3 carries a seasonal advantage. [Historical Financials — NU, §5, 2026-08-30; Q2 2026 earnings-call transcript, Q&A, p. 9]

## 7. Historical Pattern

The last four reported quarters show no stable beat signal. Revenue beat in Q3 2025 (+3.3%), Q4 2025 (+3.0%) and Q2 2026 (+0.6%), but missed in Q1 2026 (−1.8%); EPS beat in Q3 and Q2 but missed in Q4 and Q1. This is a four-observation descriptive record, so it should receive little weight and is not an empirical probability of an FQ3 beat or miss. [Capital IQ Estimates — Surprise, Fiscal Quarters, Q3 2025–Q2 2026]

## 8. Setup Verdict

**Setup favors miss.** The single most important factor is the raised revenue bar: it requires 42.3% YoY growth after the FQ3 estimate increased 7.0% in 90 days, without a numeric company guide, while management says originations are normalising. The biggest risk to this conclusion is that earlier credit growth and the unsecured mix continue to mature into interest income fast enough to exceed the bar despite that normalisation. [Capital IQ Estimates — Consensus and Trends, FQ3 2026; Q2 2026 earnings-call transcript, prepared remarks and Q&A, pp. 6, 9]

## 9. Second-Quarter Look-Ahead

The FQ4 2026 setup may differ because management describes Q4 purchase volume and revenue as seasonally higher; that could help card-related activity, but it is not a quantified FQ4 earnings forecast. Visibility is otherwise limited: no FQ4 consensus bar is carried in the guidance-consensus output, and the durability of risk-adjusted NIM, credit cost and the approximately 20% FY2026 efficiency objective remains the key issue. [Q2 2026 earnings-call transcript, Q&A and prepared remarks, pp. 8–11]

## 10. Pre-Mortem

If this setup fails, the most likely error is treating normalising originations as an immediate slowdown in reported revenue when the existing credit book can still produce a delayed Q3 interest-income lift. The opposite error is also possible: reading the Q2 risk-adjusted-NIM bridge as durable despite the disclosed early-delinquency and allowance effects from intentional risk expansion. [Q2 2026 earnings-call transcript, prepared remarks and Q&A, pp. 6–9; Q2 2026 Earnings Presentation, slides 16, 18]

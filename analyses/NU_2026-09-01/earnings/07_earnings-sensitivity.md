# Earnings Sensitivity — NU

Nu Holdings reports under IFRS Accounting Standards in US dollars. It does not report EBITDA. Where a filing permits a dollar impact below, it is therefore shown as an allowance, pre-tax-income, or financial-instrument fair-value measure and is never relabelled as EBITDA or EPS.

## 1. Variable Selection

The five variables below come from the High drivers in the revenue and margin tables: credit-book growth/yield, credit cost, and the consumer-credit/rates dependencies, plus the disclosed transaction-cost line. Interest rates and Brazilian consumer credit were also included from the business-model external-dependency review. AWS and Mastercard are named third parties, but their recent-relationship export covers only disclosed relationships and gives no spend, contractual rate, or concentration; it cannot support a quantified sensitivity. [business-model external-dependency, §§1–2, 2026-08-30; Capital IQ Suppliers/Customers export, retrieved 2026-08-30 — vendor export, scope notes]

## 4. The Single Highest-Sensitivity Variable

**Macroeconomic scenario weighting and the associated credit-loss allowance** is the largest disclosed range. It is mostly external: Brazilian consumer conditions, rates and employment affect borrowers' ability to pay, although underwriting and collections are company levers. The current read is mixed, not cleanly improving: the Q2 credit-cost bridge was favourable by 115bp QoQ, yet reported ECL was US$1.482bn, 46.4% above Q2 2025, and risk expansion raised early delinquency by 24bp. The adverse disclosed state is a 100% downside macro weighting, which raises the allowance by US$504.700m from its weighted-average level. It is a sensitivity, not a prediction of a downturn. [H1 FY2026 Unaudited Interim Condensed Consolidated Financial Statements, Notes 3(a) and 7, pp.15–16; Q2 2026 Earnings Presentation, slides 16 and 18]

## 5. Interaction Effects

Credit-book growth, unsecured mix, yield and credit cost are not independent. The Q2 bridge assigns +178bp to credit income and +115bp to lower credit cost, while the same risk expansion raised early delinquency and the allowance; adding those two bps effects to the macro ECL range would double count related credit-cycle outcomes. Higher rates can also lift interest income while increasing funding cost, reducing credit demand and worsening defaults, so the disclosed DV01 must not be added to the credit-income or credit-cost rows. [Q2 2026 Earnings Presentation, slides 15–18; H1 FY2026 Unaudited Interim Condensed Consolidated Financial Statements, Note 32, p.42]

## 6. Non-Linear Or Asymmetric Risks

The credit-loss range is asymmetric around the current weighted allowance: the downside state adds US$504.700m while the upside state releases US$418.759m. That asymmetry follows the disclosed weighted starting point, whose scenario weights are not provided; it should not be extrapolated linearly. There is also operating leverage in the wrong direction for a credit shock: the revenue benefit from higher-yield unsecured lending arrives before the associated losses are fully known, while management already reported a 24bp rise in early delinquency from risk expansion. Transaction costs are also not a pure price shock: the US$49.505m historical movement can be accompanied by activity and fee-revenue changes, so its cost-only bounds cannot be added to a revenue case. [H1 FY2026 Unaudited Interim Condensed Consolidated Financial Statements, Note 3(a), p.15; Q2 2026 Earnings Presentation, slides 16 and 18; H1 FY2026 Unaudited Interim Condensed Consolidated Financial Statements, Note 6(d), p.15]

## 7. Earnings Volatility Score

**68/100 — INVERTED: higher = worse.** Credit conditions, yield and funding all materially affect a lender whose disclosed downside macro allowance is US$504.700m above the weighted case, while the latest credit expansion also carries higher early delinquency. The score is below the extreme band because the filing discloses hedging and because an earnings-per-bp or earnings-per-credit-cost coefficient is not proven from available data. [H1 FY2026 Unaudited Interim Condensed Consolidated Financial Statements, Notes 3(a) and 32, pp.15 and 42; Q2 2026 Earnings Presentation, slides 16 and 18]

# Business Model Reality Check — NU (Synthesis)

## Abstract

Nu is a mobile bank for consumers and small businesses in Brazil, Mexico, and Colombia. Its only reportable segment earns mainly from credit and investment interest and gains—85.2% of FY2025 revenue—plus fees. [FY2025 Form 20-F, Items 4.B–5; Note 34] Reach is the clearest positive: Q2 2026 had 139 million customers and 83.5% activity. [Q2 2026 earnings presentation, slide 6] Concentration is the main weakness: Brazil supplied 91.3% of the narrower FY2025 geographic-revenue subtotal, while full cohort contribution and payback remain undisclosed. [FY2025 Form 20-F, Note 34; Item 5] No automatic disqualifier triggered; the verdict is an average business worth deeper work only if valuation is cheap. [Disqualifier Scan — NU, §3, 2026-09-01]

### Module Disconfirmation

- **Strongest bear point:** Brazil supplied 91.3% of the narrower FY2025 geographic-revenue subtotal, leaving the group exposed to one country's consumer-credit, policy, rate, regulatory, and currency conditions. [FY2025 Form 20-F, Note 34, F-97; Item 3.D, country-risk factors]
- **Strongest bull point:** NU had 139 million customers, an 83.5% activity rate, and US$17.1 monthly ARPAC—average revenue per active customer—in Q2 2026, evidence of reach and repeat use even though the revenue is not contracted. [Q2 2026 earnings presentation, slides 6 and 28]
- **Single killer risk:** A weaker Brazilian credit cycle that raises expected credit loss while funding costs stay high would hit the dominant measured revenue base; the filing does not quantify that combined scenario. [FY2025 Form 20-F, pp.131–132, 166–171]
- **Disconfirming evidence already visible:** Q2 2026 NIM—net interest income relative to interest-earning assets—rose to 22.9% from 18.8% a year earlier, risk-adjusted NIM rose to 12.4% from 9.9%, and both disclosed delinquency measures improved sequentially. These readings argue against an immediate deterioration call but do not establish through-cycle economics. [Q2 2026 earnings presentation, slides 15, 17–19]

## 3. Reconciliation

- **Revenue scope:** Segment Map uses US$15.775bn of consolidated IFRS revenue, Customer and Geography Map uses the US$12.084bn geographic-revenue subtotal defined in Note 34, and Capital IQ labels US$6.991bn as “Banking” revenue. The vendor figure equals US$15.775bn less US$4.579bn of interest and other financial expenses and US$4.205bn of expected credit loss, but it is a reclassified vendor line, not audited total revenue. This synthesis uses US$15.775bn only for consolidated revenue and US$12.084bn only as the geography denominator. [FY2025 Form 20-F, Consolidated Income Statement, F-7; Note 34, F-97–F-98; CIQ Financials→Segments, latest annual column — vendor export]
- **Brazil share:** Customer and Geography Map reports 91.3%, External Dependency rounds the same filing inputs to 91.4%, and CIQ displays 91%. The synthesis uses the filing's US$11,038.313m divided by US$12,083.766m, or 91.3% to one decimal; CIQ's whole-number display is reconciled rounding. [FY2025 Form 20-F, Note 34, F-97; CIQ Financials→Segments, Geographic Segment → Revenues, FY2025 — vendor export]
- **Operating cash flow:** The audited FY2025 filing reports positive US$3.500bn cash from operations, while CIQ reports negative US$10.305bn for LTM June 2026. The periods and vendor basis differ, so the audited annual series governs the four-year disqualifier test; the later rolling vendor read remains unreconciled and is not relabelled as a filing figure. [FY2025 Form 20-F, Cash Flows, F-14; CIQ Financials→Cash Flow, “Cash from Ops.”, LTM June 2026 — vendor export]
- **Moat score versus verdict:** The 65/100 score is the strongest individual moat source—cost advantage—not a claim that the total moat is equally strong. The moat specialist's overall verdict remains narrow and its trajectory not assessable because gross margin fell 150 basis points while pre-tax margin, customer count, and ARPAC improved, and no matching Group cost of equity is available. [Moat — NU, §§2–5, 2026-09-01]

No material disagreement remains after preserving these period, scope, source, and definition differences.

## 4. Note To The Final Synthesizer

- **Strongest business-model positive:** Scale is paired with frequent use: Q2 2026 had 139 million customers, 83.5% activity, and US$17.1 monthly ARPAC. [Q2 2026 earnings presentation, slides 6 and 28]
- **Strongest business-model negative:** Brazil supplied 91.3% of the narrower FY2025 geographic-revenue subtotal, so country-specific credit, rate, policy, regulatory, and FX shocks can affect most of that measured revenue base at once. [FY2025 Form 20-F, Note 34, F-97; Item 3.D, country-risk factors]
- **Most important segment:** Nu Group is the sole reportable segment and represents 100% of disclosed segment revenue and profit; Brazil is the economic concentration, not a separately reported segment. [FY2025 Form 20-F, Note 34, F-97–F-98]
- **Cleanest unit-economics read:** US$17.1 monthly ARPAC less US$1.0 cost to serve equals US$16.1 per active customer, but this is revenue minus partial service cost, not contribution margin, because financial-service costs and expected credit loss are excluded. [Q2 2026 earnings presentation, slide 28; FY2025 Form 20-F, p.171]
- **Versus named peers on margin and return:** Not assessable on a matched basis. NU's 17.2% loss-inclusive FY2022–FY2025 IFRS ROE is a multi-year average; Itaú's 24.3%, Bradesco's 16.2%, and Santander Brasil's 12.5% are current 2Q26 management-defined returns, while matched gross and EBIT margins are unavailable. [FY2025 Form 20-F, pp.155, 157; Itaú 2Q26 Form 6-K; Bradesco 2Q26 Form 6-K, p.7; Santander Brasil 2Q26 Form 6-K, p.3]
- **Main external dependency:** Brazilian consumer-credit conditions, followed by interest rates and regulation; specified hedges do not remove household-demand or default risk. [FY2025 Form 20-F, pp.53, 131–132, 166–167; H1 2026 interim financial statements, Notes 20 and 32]
- **Capital allocation and governance:** Audit history is clean and no serial-acquirer pattern is proven, but David Vélez's 74.4% voting power gives the founder, CEO, and chair decisive control over outside Class A holders. [FY2025 Form 20-F, pp.193, 203]
- **Contingent credit exposure:** US$38.164bn of unused card limits was off balance sheet but included in expected-credit-loss measurement; it is a disclosed lending commitment, not an undisclosed financing vehicle. [Q2 2026 interim financial statements, Note 32, p.40]
- **Automatic disqualifier:** None triggered. Pledged shares and related-party sales and purchases remain not assessable rather than zero. [Disqualifier Scan — NU, §1, 2026-09-01]
- **Rejector filters and caps:** Crooks/integrity, serial acquirers, and fast-changing industry did not trip. No rejector-filter cap or capital-structure transaction cap was applied. [Disqualifier Scan — NU, §§3–4, 2026-09-01; Capital Allocation & Governance — NU, §§1 and 4, 2026-09-01; Business Quality — NU, §1, 2026-09-01]
- **Deferred-tax flag (severity 62/100; higher = worse):** The US$991.046m H1 2026 deferred-tax benefit was 51.3% of US$1.933bn net income, and the US$3.649bn net deferred-tax asset was 27.5% of equity. Future taxable profit must support realization; the filing's support means this is estimate dependence, not evidence of improper accounting. [Q2 2026 interim financial statements, pp.7–8; Note 30, pp.36–37]
- **Legal and tax provision flag (severity 42/100; higher = worse):** The provision balance rose US$13.423m, or 43.4%, in six months to US$44.343m, with H1 additions of US$27.370m versus US$14.474m a year earlier. The balance is small beside group earnings, but the direction is adverse and cash timing is uncertain. [Q2 2026 interim financial statements, Note 25, pp.32–33; Cash Flows, p.12]
- **Biggest missing data point:** A new-customer cohort table showing revenue, funding cost, expected credit loss, full variable cost, retention, lifetime contribution, and payback. Without it, NU's low CAC and high ARPAC do not prove that each new customer creates value. [FY2025 Form 20-F, p.157; Q2 2026 earnings presentation, slide 28]
- **Deeper-work decision:** Yes, but only as a valuation-gated average business. A full cohort-profit disclosure and Group IFRS ROE staying above a sourced, matching Group cost of equity through a weaker credit period would move the answer toward high quality; failure to realize the deferred-tax asset, or credit losses overwhelming the improved spread, would move it toward low quality. [Moat — NU, §§3–5, 2026-09-01; Q2 2026 interim financial statements, Note 30, pp.36–37]

## 5. Simple Summary

- Nu is a mobile-first retail bank for consumers and small businesses in Brazil, Mexico, and Colombia. [FY2025 Form 20-F, Item 4.B, pp.13–15]
- Interest and financial-instrument gains supplied 85.2% of FY2025 revenue; card and other fees supplied the rest. [FY2025 Form 20-F, Item 5, pp.157–158]
- NU has not shown whether each new customer creates value: ARPAC is revenue, and cost to serve excludes funding cost and credit loss. [Q2 2026 earnings presentation, slide 28; FY2025 Form 20-F, p.171]
- The group reports one segment. Brazil supplied 91.3% of the narrower FY2025 geographic-revenue subtotal. [FY2025 Form 20-F, Note 34, F-97–F-98]
- The moat is narrow and its direction is not assessable; it rests mainly on low-cost digital acquisition and service rather than direct network effects, against Itaú, Bradesco, and Santander Brasil. [FY2025 Form 20-F, Item 3.D; Item 5.A, p.157; Moat — NU, §5, 2026-09-01]
- Rates, regulation, and the Brazilian consumer-credit cycle matter more than commodities or industrial demand. [FY2025 Form 20-F, pp.53, 131–132, 166–167]
- Capital allocation has no proven serial-acquirer pattern, but the founder, CEO, and chair controls 74.4% of the vote. [FY2025 Form 20-F, pp.193, 203]
- No automatic disqualifier fired. NU deserves deeper work only if valuation compensates for Brazil concentration, incomplete cohort economics, and estimate-heavy H1 tax benefits.

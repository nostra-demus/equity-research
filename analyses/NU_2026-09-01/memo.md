## What the Company Does

Nu is a digital bank and lender serving consumers and small businesses in Brazil, Mexico, and Colombia. It reports one financial-services segment. The simple revenue formula is: customer balances and credit use generate interest and gains on financial instruments, with the remaining reported revenue lines added on top. Interest and financial-instrument gains supplied 85.2% of FY2025 revenue. Credit losses, funding costs, operating costs, and tax then determine how much of that revenue becomes profit. [data/NU/Filings/Nu_Holdings_Ltd_-_Form_20-F(Apr-08-2026).pdf, FY2025, Item 5 pp.157–158]; [analyses/NU_2026-09-01/business-model/99_business-model-synthesis.md, full synthesis]

The operating idea is straightforward. Add customers, keep them active, and earn more from each active customer without letting credit losses and costs consume the gain. In Q2 2026, Nu reported 139 million customers, an 83.5% activity rate, and monthly ARPAC of US$17. ARPAC means average revenue per active customer. [data/NU/Filings/Nu_Holdings_Ltd_-_(Aug-13-2026).pdf, Q2 2026 earnings presentation, slides 6 and 15–16]

That model can produce fast growth, but this memo separates business progress from the price paid for the shares. The investment question is not whether Nu had a good quarter. It is whether the future earnings and credit outcomes needed to justify US$14.30 are proven.

## The Variant Perception

### What everyone already knows

Nu is a fast-growing Latin American digital bank. Q2 2026 revenue rose 50.3%, and diluted EPS — earnings per share after allowing for potential share dilution — rose 66.3%. Customer count, activity, and ARPAC were also high at 139 million, 83.5%, and US$17. These are disclosed results, not the engine's estimate. [data/NU/Filings/Nu_Holdings_Ltd_-_(Aug-13-2026).pdf, Q2 2026 earnings presentation, slides 6 and 15–16]; [data/NU/Filings/Nu_Holdings_Ltd_-_Form_Interim_Report(Aug-14-2026).pdf, Q2/H1 2026, p.5]

### What is probably priced in

The US$14.30 share price and the US$18.78 mean sell-side target both reflect continued high growth and returns. A clean reverse-DCF — a model that asks what future results the current price requires — is unavailable because the reverse specialist changed both the intrinsic model's rate and its starting value. Conditional on that specialist's 16.51% rate for a Brazil Investments cash-generating unit, US$14.30 requires either 47.36% terminal ROE, meaning return on common equity, or 80.89% FY2027–30 profit growth. The same earnings path reconciles at a 9.78% rate. These are conditional sensitivities, not a clean Group-level market expectation. ### What the engine thinks the market may be missing

Current spread and revenue gains depend heavily on credit income before the final credit-loss outcome is visible. H1 2026 net income also included a US$991.046m deferred-tax benefit, equal to 51.3% of H1 net income. Deferred tax moves accounting tax between periods; it is not the same as cash earned from customers. This is disclosed timing and estimate dependence, not evidence of improper accounting. It is disproved by a clean Group valuation at or above US$14.30 and a material Q3 revenue-and-EPS beat with stable or better delinquency, credit cost, and expected-credit-loss coverage. ## Why It Could Work — Bull Case

The best bull case is that the valuation model asks for too high a return and assumes Nu's extra profits fade too quickly.

1. **Scale and customer use are still rising.** Nu had 139 million customers, an 83.5% activity rate, and US$17 monthly ARPAC in Q2. Those figures support the claim that the platform is adding users and earning more from active customers. They do not, on their own, prove returns after funding, credit losses, and capital. [data/NU/Filings/Nu_Holdings_Ltd_-_(Aug-13-2026).pdf, Q2 2026 earnings presentation, slides 6 and 15–16]

2. **The latest growth was fast.** Q2 revenue grew 50.3%, while diluted EPS grew 66.3%. This makes it possible that the engine's residual-income fade is too cautious. Residual income is profit left after charging shareholders' required return against the equity used by the business. One quarter, however, does not prove a lasting trend. [data/NU/Filings/Nu_Holdings_Ltd_-_Form_Interim_Report(Aug-14-2026).pdf, Q2/H1 2026, p.5]

3. **The latest spread bridge improved.** Risk-adjusted NIM reached 12.4%. NIM, or net interest margin, is the lending spread after funding costs; the risk-adjusted version also reflects credit cost. The 294-basis-point sequential improvement was explained by 178 basis points from credit income, 115 from lower credit cost, and a 1-basis-point residual. A basis point is one-hundredth of a percentage point. Both headline delinquency measures also improved sequentially. The arithmetic supports the improvement, although it does not prove durability. [data/NU/Filings/Nu_Holdings_Ltd_-_(Aug-13-2026).pdf, Q2 2026 earnings presentation, slides 15–18]; [analyses/NU_2026-09-01/earnings/99_earnings-synthesis.md, §§3–5]

4. **Current returns can support a premium if they last.** Nu's NTM ROTE proxy was 39.4%, compared with an 18.6% peer median. NTM means next twelve months; ROTE means return on tangible equity, or profit relative to common equity after removing intangible assets. A scope-matched Group required return materially below the model's 12.16% build, combined with durable credit outcomes, could support the US$18.78 mean sell-side target. That is the positive tail in the scenario model, not the engine's fair value. [analyses/NU_2026-09-01/valuation/03_relative-valuation-peers.md, §§2–5]; [data/NU/NuHoldingsLtdNYSENUEstimatesReport.xls, Consensus, Target Price, export modified 2026-08-29 — Capital IQ vendor]

This bull case defeats the Avoid only when a model-consistent Group rate and repeated operating evidence support it. A target price or one good quarter is not enough.

## Why It Could Fail — Bear Case and the Killer Risk

1. **The shares are above every engine value.** At US$14.30, the price exceeds the US$11.101 bull, US$8.472197 base, and US$2.952 bear values. Even the bull implies a −22.3706% return. The base implies −40.7539%, and the bear implies −79.3566%. [analyses/NU_2026-09-01/valuation/99_valuation-synthesis.md, §§1–7, 2026-09-01]

2. **Credit improvement may not last.** Risk expansion added 24 basis points to early delinquency and US$170m to the allowance bridge. The implied credit-cost rate was 10.5%, compared with 8.9% a year earlier. This does not erase the 294-basis-point sequential margin improvement, but it prevents that improvement from being called a proven new trend. [data/NU/Filings/Nu_Holdings_Ltd_-_(Aug-13-2026).pdf, Q2 2026 earnings presentation, slides 15–18]; [analyses/NU_2026-09-01/earnings/99_earnings-synthesis.md, §§3–8]

3. **Q3 has a raised bar.** Frozen vendor consensus is US$5.93674bn of revenue and US$0.22164 of GAAP EPS, requiring 42.3% and 39.0% year-on-year growth. The revenue estimate rose 7.0% over 90 days. The engine assigns a 55% judgment probability to revenue finishing below consensus and a separate 55% judgment probability to EPS finishing below consensus. The forecasts are correlated and must not be multiplied into a joint miss probability. The filing-built last-twelve-month cash-from-operations figure is negative US$1.382bn, versus negative US$10.305bn from Capital IQ; the US$8.923bn definition gap is unresolved. 5. **The downside risks can arrive together.** A weaker borrower cycle can reduce EPS, lower the price-to-earnings multiple investors will pay, and pressure prudential buffers at the same time. **Killer risk to the Avoid:** the residual-income duration or required return is wrong. Sustained residual earnings or a lower Group cost of equity could make the US$8.47 base value too low. That is why this is an Avoid rather than a Short Candidate.

## What Would Change Our Mind

The Avoid must be reconsidered if any of these observable tests passes:

- A model-consistent Group residual-income and reverse valuation, using one scope-matched required return, one earnings path, the same fade and share count, values NU at or above US$14.30. The Brazil Investments cash-generating-unit rate is not a substitute for the Group rate.
- Q3 2026 standalone revenue exceeds US$6.11484bn and GAAP EPS exceeds US$0.23272 while early delinquency, credit cost, and expected-credit-loss coverage are stable or better year on year. This exact test did not pass in Q1 or Q2 2026.
- Two consecutive standalone quarters materially beat both frozen revenue and EPS consensus while the same-basis credit measures remain stable or improve year on year. The Q1/Q2 2026 pair did not pass.
- A matched peer and sector history supports NU's current 14.76x NTM P/E and 5.7x P/TBV through a credit cycle on comparable forward ROE, growth, and risk. No such matched history was available for the prior two periods.

Two narrower forecast-ledger tests remain open for Q3. The engine assigns a 55% judgment probability to revenue below US$5.93674bn; revenue at or above that level falsifies that forecast. It separately assigns a 55% judgment probability to GAAP diluted EPS below US$0.22164; EPS at or above that level falsifies the EPS forecast. Both are due in the 5–20 November 2026 window. ## Bottom Line

- Nu is a digital bank and lender whose FY2025 revenue was 85.2% interest and financial-instrument gains.
- The latest quarter was good: Q2 revenue rose 50.3%, diluted EPS rose 66.3%, and the risk-adjusted spread improved by 294 basis points.
- The price is the problem: US$14.30 is above the US$11.101 bull, US$8.472197 base, and US$2.952 bear values.
- The main operating risk is that current credit income arrives before later credit losses; the main model risk is that the Group required return is too high or excess returns last longer than assumed.
- Filing and vendor evidence support the growth, price, consensus, and governance facts. Bank-wide prudential evidence, matched peer calls, cohort economics, and a sourced Group cost of equity are missing.
- **Do not buy now. Wait with no position. Do not infer short authority from the Avoid.**
- Watch Q3 revenue, EPS, delinquency, credit cost, and expected-credit-loss coverage together. A clean dual beat with stable or better credit would weaken the Avoid.

## Plain-English Glossary

- **Bull / base / bear cases** — the favorable, central, and adverse outcomes used to show a range rather than one precise forecast.
- **Probability-weighted target** — each scenario's price multiplied by its probability, with those results added together.
- **Expected return** — the probability-weighted gain or loss from today's share price.
- **Downside risk** — the loss from today's price to the bear-case value; higher is worse here.
- **Risk/reward** — the expected gain or loss compared with the distance to the bear value; the signed result is negative in this memo.
- **Margin of safety** — the price cushion below estimated fair value; a negative figure means the market price is above that value.
- **Cost of equity / required return** — the return shareholders demand for providing risk-bearing capital.
- **Solvency** — the ability to absorb losses and keep meeting financial and regulatory obligations.
- **FX** — foreign-exchange movements between currencies.
- **ARPAC (average revenue per active customer)** — the revenue Nu earns from one active customer in a period.
- **Diluted EPS (earnings per share)** — profit for each share after allowing for options or other instruments that could increase the share count.
- **Reverse-DCF** — a valuation run backward to estimate what future performance the current price appears to require.
- **Cash-generating unit** — the accounting-defined part of a business tested separately for whether its assets retain their value.
- **ROE (return on equity)** — profit compared with common shareholders' equity.
- **Terminal ROE** — the ROE assumed after the detailed forecast period ends.
- **Deferred tax** — accounting tax recognized now for a tax effect expected to reverse or be used in another period.
- **Variant perception** — an evidence-backed view that differs from what investors already know or appear to price in.
- **Residual income** — profit left after charging the required return on shareholders' equity.
- **Fade** — the assumed decline of unusually high returns toward a more normal level over time.
- **NIM (net interest margin)** — lending income after funding cost, measured against the relevant earning-asset base; the risk-adjusted version also reflects credit cost.
- **Basis point** — one-hundredth of one percentage point.
- **Credit cost** — the charge taken for expected losses on lending.
- **Expected credit loss / ECL** — the accounting estimate of credit losses expected from loans and similar assets.
- **NTM (next twelve months)** — a forecast covering the coming twelve months.
- **ROTE (return on tangible equity)** — profit relative to common equity after removing intangible assets.
- **Sell-side target** — a share-price target published by brokerage or investment-bank analysts; it is not the engine's fair value.
- **Cash from operating activities / CFO** — cash generated or used by the reported operations during the period; for a lender, it needs bank-specific interpretation.
- **Price multiple** — a market price stated relative to earnings, book value, or another financial measure.
- **Short / short sale** — a position intended to gain if the share price falls; it needs borrow or suitable instruments and can lose when the price rises.
- **Options** — contracts whose value depends on the share price and whose terms and cost affect whether a trade can be executed.
- **Debt/EBITDA** — corporate debt compared with earnings before interest, tax, depreciation, and amortization; the dossier says this is not a valid bank-solvency test for Nu.
- **Interest coverage** — earnings available to meet interest expense; the dossier says the corporate version is not the right bank-survival test for Nu.
- **FCF (free cash flow)** — cash from operations after capital spending; the dossier says a corporate FCF runway is not a valid bank-solvency test for Nu.
- **Risk-weighted assets** — bank assets adjusted for the regulatory risk assigned to them.
- **Prudential return / buffer** — a regulator-facing bank report, or the capital and liquidity held against required levels and risk exposures.
- **Share-based compensation** — pay delivered in shares or share-linked awards.
- **Adjusted earnings** — a company-selected profit measure that removes specified reported costs or gains.
- **Fair value** — the model's estimate of what the shares are worth under stated assumptions.
- **P/TBV (price-to-tangible-book value)** — the share price compared with tangible common equity per share.
- **P/E (price-to-earnings)** — the share price divided by earnings per share.
- **Sum of the parts** — valuing separate business units and adding their values together.
- **GAAP** — the accounting rules used for the reported figure in question.
- **Hedge ratio** — the size of an offsetting position needed against the main investment exposure.
- **Cohort economics** — revenue, costs, credit losses, and capital tied to a defined group of customers or products over time.
- **Beta** — an estimate of how a share has moved relative to a chosen market index, used in some required-return models.

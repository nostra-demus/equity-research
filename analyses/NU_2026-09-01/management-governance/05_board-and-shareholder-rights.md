# Board & Shareholder Rights — NU

**Scope and standard.** Nu Holdings Ltd. is a Cayman Islands company and a foreign private issuer (FPI) listed on the NYSE. This is therefore a Cayman/NYSE-FPI rights review, not an Indian LODR review. “Independent” below means the company’s disclosed designation; it is not a conclusion that the director is independent of the controlling shareholder. The latest primary board disclosure is nine directors as of 8 April 2026 and the 6 August 2026 AGM re-elected those nine. A Capital IQ roster dated 29 August adds Roberto Campos Neto as a director, which conflicts with both primary disclosures and the vendor’s own disclosed maximum board size of nine. I do not treat that vendor row as proof of a tenth current director. The current post-AGM board count therefore needs a company confirmation.

**Verdict: Weak / entrenched.** The Audit and Risk Committee is fully independent and a lead independent director exists, but David Vélez’s 74.3% voting power, 20:1 Class B votes, right to nominate up to five directors, and consent rights over major decisions mean the Class A vote cannot independently constrain the controller. The overall governance finding is not an integrity allegation.

## 2. Tenure & Refreshment

The official board has one founder, seven company-designated independent directors, and one director not designated independent. The newest director joined in April 2026. The board’s disclosed median tenure is about 5.3 years, while Leone has served since 2016. Neither the long-serving director nor the controller link is, by itself, proof of compromised judgment. The concern is structural: the founder can nominate up to five directors while holding at least 40% of the vote, and he currently holds 74.3%. [FY2025 Form 20-F, Item 6A, pp.187–194; Item 7A, p.203]

Director attendance, individual commitment time, age/retirement policy, evaluation results, and reasons for any recent board exits are not disclosed. These are real evidence gaps. Capital IQ’s current roster conflict also means the board count after the AGM should be reconciled to an official Nu disclosure before treating the roster as fully current. [Capital IQ Board Members export, as of 2026-08-29 — vendor]

## 4. Takeover Defenses & Voting Rights

Nu has a dual-class structure: each Class A share has one vote and each Class B share has 20 votes. At 1 March 2026, Vélez held 88.3% of Class B shares and 74.3% of the combined voting power. [FY2025 Form 20-F, Item 7A, p.203]

The shareholder agreement gives the founder, while he holds at least 40% of voting power, the right to designate up to five directors. At at least 10%, it also requires his prior written consent for material debt, related-party transactions involving officers/directors other than him, major business changes, a rights plan, dividends, a change of control, liquidation, share-capital changes, asset sales above 20% of net equity, and director/officer compensation. With 74.3% voting power, he exceeds both thresholds. These rights are more restrictive than simple voting control: they give a contractual consent layer over board and shareholder decisions. [FY2025 Form 20-F, Item 6A “Shareholders Agreement”, pp.193–194]

Class B holders, but not Class A holders, have preemptive rights on future ordinary-share issuance to maintain proportional ownership. Nu also has authorized blank-check preferred shares, with none outstanding, that the board may issue without a shareholder vote. These features can dilute Class A holders or entrench control, although no preferred issuance or active poison pill was disclosed. [FY2025 Form 20-F, Item 10B “Ordinary Shares—Preemptive or Similar Rights”; Capital IQ Takeover Defenses export, as of 2026-08-29 — vendor]

There is one mitigating legal feature: if a merger, consolidation, or sale of all/substantially all assets occurs, the Class A and Class B shares must receive equal treatment. That does not remove the founder’s power to decide whether such a transaction proceeds. [FY2025 Form 20-F, Item 10B “Equal Status”]

### 4A. Shareholder Voting & Dilution

Directors are elected annually while the founder remains above the classifying threshold; the founder’s vote can determine the outcome. Shareholders require 50% of voting power to requisition a special meeting. If the founder ceases to hold a majority, the articles provide that shareholders may not requisition a meeting. Written resolutions require unanimity of shareholders, and there is no cumulative voting. These provisions make a Class-A-led challenge impractical in the present structure. [FY2025 Form 20-F, Item 10B “General Meetings of Shareholders” and “Voting Rights”; Capital IQ Corporate Governance export, as of 2026-08-29 — vendor]

Amendments to the memorandum and articles require a two-thirds special resolution. That ordinarily constrains unilateral board action, but the founder’s 74.3% voting interest can meet that threshold. The board cannot itself amend the charter according to the vendor export; this is a formal protection whose practical value is limited by control concentration. [Capital IQ Corporate Governance export, as of 2026-08-29 — vendor; FY2025 Form 20-F, Item 7A, p.203]

## 5. Minority Protection Read

**Conclusion: weak / entrenched.** Nu’s formal board design has positive features: seven of nine directors are company-designated independent, the lead independent director sits on the Audit & Risk Committee, that committee is fully independent, and its chair is the disclosed financial expert. [FY2025 Form 20-F, Item 6A, pp.187–190; Item 6C, pp.199–200]

Those safeguards are outweighed for Class A holders by the controller’s current 74.3% vote, 20:1 Class B votes, ability to nominate a board majority, and broad contractual consent rights. The absence of a separate nomination committee, the presence of a non-independent member on the Compensation & People Committee, Class-B-only preemptive rights, and missing attendance/evaluation/dissent detail further weaken outside holders’ ability to test board accountability. [FY2025 Form 20-F, Item 6A, pp.193–194; Item 7A, p.203; Item 6C, pp.199–201]

The critical watch item is not a proven abuse. It is whether Nu can demonstrate actual independent challenge: current official board composition; meeting and attendance records; independent-session occurrence; named approval and recusal for the director-linked commercial relationship; and AGM vote results by nominee and proposal.

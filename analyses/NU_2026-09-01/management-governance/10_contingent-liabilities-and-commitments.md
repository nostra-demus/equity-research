Nu Holdings Ltd. is a Cayman-incorporated NYSE foreign private issuer that reports under IFRS in U.S. dollars. Amounts below are US$ millions unless noted otherwise; the latest balance-sheet date is 30 June 2026. `balance-sheet-survival/05_off-balance-sheet-and-contingencies.md` is not available for this run, so this is this module's own filing-based read and does not attempt the survival module's maximum-exposure analysis.

## 1. Headline Exposure (A7a-01)

The quantified off-P&L litigation amount is US$9.675m: US$4.784m civil plus US$4.891m labor claims classified as possible. The US$44.343m provision is already recognized in liabilities, so it is shown separately rather than mislabelled as off-P&L. The computed US$54.018m “total disclosed legal exposure” is therefore a transparent build: US$44.343m provided + US$9.675m quantified possible. It excludes the Colombian GMF class action because the filing gives the US$2.5bn claim for all 52 defendants but says Nu Colombia's share cannot be quantified; allocating a share would be invention. It is also not established whether that case is included in the US$4.784m possible-civil line. ## 11. Read

Quantified current legal exposure is US$54.018m — US$44.343m already provided plus US$9.675m quantified possible — only 0.41% of US$13.252bn net worth, so it clears the size band on the amounts Nu can actually quantify. The biggest unresolved item is the Colombian GMF class action: the FY2025 filing calls the US$2.5bn all-defendant claim possible and says Nu Colombia's share cannot be quantified, so it cannot honestly be sized against equity or assumed harmless. The provision record is mixed but not evasive on available evidence: it separately states the probable/possible policy, booked provisions grew with reconciled additions, reversals and payments, and the FY2022 PIS/COFINS provision crystallized through a disclosed 2023 settlement; no serial appellate losses carried unprovided are shown. A rational minority holder should demand the next filing state the GMF allocation, current court status, and whether it is included in the possible-civil subtotal, and should require a complete issued-guarantee, recourse-financing, statutory-dues and capital-commitments schedule.

## What This Module Found

NU appears operationally competent but only partly aligned with outside shareholders, producing the Standard / mixed stewardship read. [Management-Governance Synthesis — NU, Abstract and §5, 2026-08-30]

The central risk is David Vélez's 18.6% economic ownership carrying 74.4% of votes—a 55.8-percentage-point wedge—through 20-for-1 Class B shares while he serves as CEO and chair and holds nomination and consent rights; this proves entrenchment, not misconduct. [FY25 Form 20-F, Items 6A, 7A and 10B]

Capital allocation—how the company uses capital—is mixed: diluted EPS—earnings per share after potential dilution—rose 175.6% from FY23 to FY25, and US$500.393m repurchased 40.660m Class A shares at an implied US$12.31, lowering end-period shares 0.5%; weighted diluted shares still rose 0.2%, and acquisition and reinvestment returns are undisclosed. [FY25 Form 20-F, pp.F6–F7; H1 FY26 Interim Report, Notes 9, 10 and 31]

Incentives are mixed: annual equity-award burn—awards granted relative to shares—was 0.775%, overhang—potential dilution from outstanding awards—was 1.543%, and 88% of employees received awards, while a three-year clawback helps but executive measures, weights, and individual payouts remain absent. [FY25 Form 20-F, Item 6B; Note 10]

The strongest offsets are three unqualified FY23–FY25 issuer audits, effective FY25 internal control over financial reporting (ICFR), fully independent Audit/Risk membership, a lead independent director, and no proven leakage or hard disqualifier. [FY25 Form 20-F, auditor report, Items 6A, 15.B–D and 16C; Business-Model Disqualifier Scan — NU, §1]

The main reason for caution beyond founder control is incomplete external coverage: all 24 people were Clean on their own records, but every grade is coverage-limited, 18 entity branches were unexpanded, and the legal sweep did not complete court, NYSE, rating, sanctions, and person-level axes. [People & Network Integrity Dossiers — NU, §§1, 5 and 7, 2026-08-30; Regulatory, Legal & Compliance — NU, 2026-08-30]

## Plain-English Glossary

- **Capital allocation:** How a company uses its capital.
- **Diluted EPS:** Earnings per share after potential dilution.
- **Buyback:** A company purchase of its own shares.
- **Incentive alignment:** Whether management pay rewards outcomes that benefit shareholders.
- **Clawback:** A rule allowing previously awarded pay to be recovered.
- **Economic ownership:** The share of the financial interest owned, separate from voting power.
- **Super-vote:** A share carrying more votes than an ordinary share.
- **Adjusted profit:** Management's profit measure after its stated exclusions or additions.
- **RPT:** Related-party transaction, involving a connected person or entity.
- **Contingent liability:** A potential obligation whose amount or outcome is uncertain.
- **Accounting forensics:** Checks for signs of weak or manipulated financial reporting.
- **ECL:** Expected credit loss, the amount a lender estimates it may not collect.
- **Managerial P&L:** Management's profit-and-loss presentation rather than the statutory statement.
- **Confidence-Adjusted Governance Score:** The governance score reduced for evidence uncertainty.
- **Share-based compensation:** Pay awarded in shares.
- **Burn:** Equity awards granted during the period relative to shares.
- **Overhang:** Potential dilution from outstanding equity awards.
- **ICFR:** Internal control over financial reporting.
- **Provision:** An amount recognized for an expected obligation or loss.
- **Basis points:** A unit used for changes in percentage rates.
- **M&A:** Mergers and acquisitions.
- **Gatekeeper exit:** A departure by an auditor, finance, compliance, or similar control role.
- **Beneficial owner:** The person who ultimately owns or controls an entity or holding.

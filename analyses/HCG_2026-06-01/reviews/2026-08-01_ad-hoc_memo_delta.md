## 3. The finding is our forecast, not the company

We set the FQ1 FY27 revenue forecast to resolve **"by 2026-07-30"**. The company's board does not meet to approve those results until **2026-08-06** — a week *after* our window closed.

**The forecast could never have been settled inside its own window.**

That is a construction fault, and a specific one. Check AO already tests that a forecast *can* be settled — that its trigger pins a number or names a settleable document. It does **not** test that the forecast's **window contains the settling event**. This one named the right document and then gave it a deadline that arrives before the document does.

Judged only on what was knowable at 2026-06-01 (no hindsight): an Indian listed company's June-quarter results are customarily approved in late July or August under SEBI LODR timelines. A 30 July window for a June-quarter print was optimistic on the calendar at the time of writing.

**Recorded `still open`, not `not assessable`.** The distinction matters for scoring: *not assessable* means the evidence may never exist. Here it is scheduled, dated and six days away. Marking it not-assessable would quietly drop a resolvable forecast out of the scoring population.

## What This Module Found

Uber trades close to fair value. The base-case fair value is $74.91 versus a $68.18 price — a 9.9% gap that lands just inside the "fairly valued" band, at its edge [§1, synthesis]. The bull case is $104.89; the bear case (a 12-month cyclical downturn) is $40.23; and a deeper structural-reset floor of $31.88 (24–36 months) is carried separately, not blended into the headline [§1].

The single most important driver is the peer-comparison read, which carries 68% of the base-case weight. Against its four closest economics-matched peers (Lyft, DoorDash, DiDi, Grab), Uber has the highest EBITDA margin (a measure of core operating profit before interest, tax, and accounting charges) at 13.5% of revenue, yet trades at a 17.5% discount on forward EV/EBITDA (enterprise value — the whole company including debt — divided by that EBITDA) [§2, relative-valuation row]. The synthesis judges that discount only partly warranted.

The single most important risk is the "warranted-multiple" question: the modest upside depends on the market granting Uber a higher EV/EBITDA multiple (13.25x versus today's 11.89x) that assumes its recent margin gains are durable. The business-model module's moat work found "no moat proven" — Uber's return on capital (the profit earned on each dollar invested) has cleared its ~8.1% cost of capital (what that money costs to raise) for only two of the last three years, not a full cycle [§1]. If the market keeps treating the current margin as a peak rather than a new normal, most of the modest upside disappears.

The reverse-DCF (working backward from the price to ask what growth it assumes) is reassuring: the price only requires 14.7% free-cash-flow growth and a 12.8% steady-state margin — both below what Uber already delivers (12.1% LTM EBIT margin — operating profit as a share of revenue) and below consensus [§1]. The bar looks achievable, not stretched. But the setup is lopsided: the cushion to fair value is thin (+9.0%) while the drop to the bear case is 41.0%, and to the structural floor 53.2% [§1].

- **Multiples vs own history** — Zero-weighted (illustrative only): the window is just ~17 months. Multiples sit at the trough, but about half of that is EBITDA/EBIT roughly doubling, not price falling.
- **Relative valuation (peers)** — Base $73.39 (+7.6%). Uber has the highest EBITDA margin (13.5%) among its four core peers yet trades at a 17.5% forward EV/EBITDA and 38.5% forward P/E discount to their median — only partly warranted.
- **Intrinsic DCF** — Base $82.87 (+21.5%), but terminal-value-dominated (77.1% of enterprise value, over the 75% limit); a stricter financeable-growth check implies a more conservative $64–$75.
- **Reverse DCF** — The price requires only 14.7% free-cash-flow growth and a 12.8% margin, both below Uber's own recent trend and consensus.
- **Sum-of-the-parts** — Swings nearly 2x on comparable choice: $95.73 (+40.4%) on favourable comps, $51.15 (-25.0%) on conservative comps — "cannot cleanly call" the stock.
- **Scenario and fair value** — Base $74.91 (+9.9%), Bull $104.89, Bear (cyclical) $40.23, structural floor $31.88; margin of safety a modest 9.0%, downside to the headline bear an asymmetric 41.0%.

**Most important disagreement:** the DCF base ($82.87) and the sum-of-the-parts conservative case ($51.15) disagree by 62% — a >40% cross-method gap. The synthesis did not average it away: it resolved the gap as an artefact of which comparable is credited to each segment (Lyft vs Grab for Mobility, DoorDash vs Grab for Delivery), layered on the DCF's separately-flagged terminal-value fragility — not a real disagreement about Uber's consolidated economics. The module trusts the peer-relative read most.

## What Would Change This Read

**Would make it cheaper (more upside):**
- A third straight year of return on capital clearing the ~8.1% cost of capital — closing the "no moat proven" gap and supporting a fuller re-rating toward the raw 14.42x peer median.
- A longer (3–5 year) own-multiple history showing today's level is genuinely cheap versus a durable norm, not just a 17-month window.
- Resolution of the sum-of-the-parts comp uncertainty (e.g. Uber Delivery earning DoorDash's growth premium rather than Grab's more moderate multiple).

**Would make it more expensive (less upside):**
- The SG&A-cost-leverage tailwind reversing beyond the +63bps already seen, confirming margin gains are cyclical, not structural.
- Integration costs or dilution from the debt-funded Delivery Hero acquisition materializing (per the serial-acquirer flag RF-CAP-004).
- A driver-classification regulatory ruling in a major market — the structural-reset trigger already priced into the $31.88 floor.

**Data that would sharpen the call:** a primary 10-K/10-Q filing (currently absent); a 3–5 year own-multiple history (only 17 months exists); segment-level forward guidance to reduce the sum-of-the-parts comp sensitivity.

## Bottom Line

- **Verdict:** Fairly valued, at the edge of modestly undervalued — base fair value $74.91 vs a $68.18 price (+9.9%).
- **Biggest reason it could be better than it looks:** the price bakes in a conservative bar (14.7% free-cash-flow growth, 12.8% margin), below what Uber already delivers and below consensus, and Uber posts the highest margin of its four closest peers while trading at a discount to them.
- **Biggest reason it could be worse:** the modest upside needs a re-rating from 11.89x to 13.25x EV/EBITDA that assumes today's margin is a durable new normal — but "no moat proven" (return on capital cleared its cost of capital for only two of three years), and the downside is lopsided (41.0% to the cyclical bear, 53.2% to the structural floor).
- **What's missing:** a primary 10-K/10-Q filing (every figure is a vendor export, tier 5) and a 3–5 year own-multiple history (only 17 months exists). Also unquantified: the serial-acquirer risk on the $14.8bn Delivery Hero deal (RF-CAP-004).
- **One thing to watch next:** whether return on capital clears the ~8.1% cost of capital for a third straight year — that is what turns the assumed re-rating from "evidenced but not earned" into earned.

## Plain-English Glossary

- **EBITDA** — core operating profit before interest, tax, and non-cash accounting charges; a rough proxy for cash the operations generate.
- **EV / EV-EBITDA** — enterprise value is the whole company's price including debt; EV/EBITDA divides it by EBITDA to compare companies on the same footing.
- **P/E** — share price divided by earnings per share.
- **EBIT margin** — operating profit as a share of revenue.
- **Free-cash-flow growth** — the rate at which the cash left after running and investing in the business grows.
- **Return on capital / cost of capital** — the profit earned on each dollar invested, versus what that money costs to raise; earning above the cost creates value.
- **Margin of safety** — the discount of the price to estimated fair value; a cushion against being wrong.
- **DCF / reverse-DCF** — a discounted-cash-flow model values a company from its projected future cash; a reverse-DCF runs it backward to reveal what growth the current price already assumes.
- **Terminal value** — the value a DCF assigns to all years beyond the explicit forecast; when it dominates, the estimate is fragile.
- **Net debt (strict)** — total debt minus cash.
- **Sum-of-the-parts** — valuing each business segment separately, then adding them up.
- **Basis points (bps)** — hundredths of a percent; 63bps = 0.63 percentage points.

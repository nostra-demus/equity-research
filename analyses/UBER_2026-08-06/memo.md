## 2. What the Company Does

Uber runs an app that connects three groups with independent workers: riders with drivers (Mobility), food and grocery customers with couriers (Delivery), and shippers with truckers (Freight). It owns the software and the payment rails — not the cars, restaurants, or trucks.

The way it makes money is simple:

> **Uber's revenue ≈ (gross bookings — the total dollars flowing through the app) × (take rate — the slice Uber keeps as commission).**

Mobility is the engine. In FY2025 it was 57.0% of revenue but 90.5% of all profit before interest, tax, and non-cash charges ($7,899mm of $8,730mm). Freight loses money at that same profit line and is shrinking (revenue down 27% over three years). Uber has also placed a roughly $10bn bet spread across six-plus self-driving-car partners — it is buying into that technology, not building its own.

## 3. The Variant Perception

This is where we ask whether we actually see something the market does not.

- **What everyone already knows:** Uber is the largest global ride-hailing and delivery marketplace, has turned durably profitable (operating income moved from $(1,832)mm in FY2022 to $5,565mm in FY2025), is buying back stock, and signed a large debt-funded deal to buy Delivery Hero (announced 2026-07-16, fully public). The regulatory risk around whether drivers are contractors or employees, and the self-driving-car strategy, are widely discussed.

- **What is probably already priced in:** A cautious, not aggressive, growth case. A reverse-DCF — working backwards from today's $68.18 price to ask what growth the price already assumes — shows the price needs only 14.7% annual free-cash-flow growth and a 12.8% long-run profit margin. Both are below Uber's own consensus case (18.6% growth, 16.0% margin) and below what it already earns (12.1% operating margin over the last twelve months). The market is also already discounting the "no moat proven" finding and the deal risk — which is part of why Uber trades 17.5% cheaper than its four closest peers on enterprise value to profit (EV/EBITDA), despite having the highest profit margin in that group.

- **What the engine thinks the market may be missing:** (1) The Delivery Hero loan's covenant squeeze is not visible in any reported number today, because the debt has not been drawn yet — leverage today is a modest 1.32x. (2) The largest single earnings lever, SG&A spending as a share of revenue, has already started moving the wrong way (20.50% over the last twelve months versus a 19.87% low in FY2025), ahead of any headline miss. (3) The roughly 10-percentage-point gap between bookings growth (+22%) and reported revenue growth (+12.2%) is still unexplained against any primary filing.

- **What would prove the edge is real:** If the FQ3 2026 results (guided 2026-08-05, reported ~late October 2026) show the SG&A ratio still climbing past 20.50% AND revenue still lagging bookings with no filed reconciliation, the margin story is more fragile than the market's already-cautious bar assumes. Conversely, if the Delivery Hero loan terms come out showing real safety margin, the balance-sheet risk we flag is refuted. **Edge score: 40/100 — evidenced and testable, but below the 50-point bar, so conviction is capped at 60 regardless of anything else.** The honest read: this is a narrow, data-dependent edge, not a strong one.

## 4. Why It Could Work — Bull Case

1. **Margin lead may be a real, widening advantage.** Mobility's profit margin reached 26.6% in FY2025 (up from 19.2% in FY2020), against near-zero for Lyft and negative for DiDi. Return on capital has risen five years running. What would confirm it: a third straight year of return on capital clearing the ~8.1% cost of capital.

2. **The beat streak.** Adjusted profit and per-share earnings have beaten the top of guidance twice in a row, and cash generation is clean — operating cash flow was 115.7% of adjusted profit in FY2025. Confirmation: a third straight beat at or above the $2,960mm guidance high.

3. **Room to re-rate.** Uber trades 17.5% cheaper than peers on EV/EBITDA despite the best margin in the group, and the bar the price implies is achievable, not stretched. Confirmation: the multiple moving from 11.89x toward 13.0x+.

4. **Levers to cut debt if needed.** Today's balance sheet is genuinely clean (1.32x leverage, no point at which it runs out of cash), and Uber holds ~$3.8bn of self-driving-partner equity stakes it could sell. Confirmation: the Delivery Hero loan terms including a real paydown schedule.

5. **Buyback could restart.** Uber paused a buyback that had been running near 50% of free cash flow, redirecting $4bn to the Delivery Hero stake. A restart would be a clean positive. Confirmation: an announced resumption with size and cadence.

The single most credible way to argue against our own Watchlist call: the Delivery Hero risk has been public since 2026-07-16, and the stock still trades at a peer discount — so the market may already be pricing the risk the cap is penalizing. The one piece of evidence that would move us there is the loan's actual covenant terms showing real headroom.

## 5. Why It Could Fail — Bear Case & The Killer Risk

1. **The Delivery Hero loan.** Once the deal closes and the ~€14bn bridge loan is drawn, leverage runs 5.0x–6.6x on a mid-cycle profit base. An assumed 3.5x covenant would breach on an EBITDA fall of just 0.9% (full draw) to 25.5% (consideration-only) — a normal 30–40% recession breaches it in three of four modeled cases. The covenant terms themselves are undisclosed.

2. **Cost base stiffening into a slowdown.** SG&A as a share of revenue has already reversed +63bps off its low; if bookings decelerate while costs cannot flex down, profit falls faster than a straight line would suggest.

3. **The revenue-vs-bookings gap.** Reported revenue growth (+12.2%) was the second-slowest in eight quarters and missed the Street twice; management's "optical, UK accounting" explanation is unverified against any filing.

4. **Driver reclassification.** A ruling in a major market that gig drivers are employees would reprice the contractor cost base across both Mobility and Delivery at once — a UK reclassification already cut roughly 500bps off Mobility's take rate this quarter.

5. **Self-driving disruption.** Uber does not own the technology and cannot predict who wins; this is a fast-changing industry (rate-of-change scored 35/100), which is why it is flagged as a sector/technology bet, not a settled compounder.

**The killer risk, in one sentence:** the Delivery Hero deal closes and draws its loan without adequate disclosed covenant headroom, turning an ordinary recession into a covenant crisis at the exact moment leverage discipline is least proven (RF-CAP-004).

## 6. Avoid-Big-Risks Check (§24)

Two of the six standing risk filters tripped on evidence:

- **Filter 4 — serial acquirers (RF-CAP-004, Critical).** At least 7 bolt-on deals in ~18 months, culminating in the $14.8bn debt-funded Delivery Hero purchase — 10.6% of Uber's own market value — funded by a ~€14bn bridge loan, requiring a forced $1.6bn divestiture, with $4bn pulled from a promised buyback to help fund the stake. This is the cap: it holds the rating at Watchlist with no bypass.

- **Filter 5 — fast-changing industry (RF-BQ-005, Medium).** The ~$10bn spread across six-plus unproven self-driving partners. This flags Uber as a technology-cycle bet; it does not lower the score further (business quality already sits below the cap at 47/100).

Survival read: no hard disqualifier fired. The board is genuinely independent (90% independent, no poison pill, no dual-class stock, zero disclosed related-party deals above $120,000, from a primary SEC filing). Today's balance sheet is clean at 1.32x leverage with no point at which it runs out of cash. The risk is entirely forward — it arrives with the loan draw, not before.

## 10. What Would Change Our Mind

- Delivery Hero loan discloses a covenant with <10% headroom after the draw, or a breach/waiver appears within 4 quarters of drawing → forward leverage risk confirmed (exit trigger).
- Revenue misses the Street a 3rd straight quarter AND SG&A ratio keeps rising past 20.50% → the margin/re-rating case is genuinely fragile.
- A further large debt-funded deal is announced before Delivery Hero even closes, or leverage overshoots the (unverified) <2x target → the serial-acquirer pattern repeats.
- A driver-classification ruling in the US or EU reclassifies gig drivers as employees → the contractor cost base is repriced.
- The multiple compresses toward Lyft's 7.94x instead of re-rating → the valuation case is refuted.

Hard exit conditions: a confirmed covenant breach, a leverage overshoot past management's own (unverified) <2x target, or a major-market employee-reclassification ruling.

## 14. Plain-English Glossary

- **Gross bookings** — the total dollars flowing through the app before Uber's cut.
- **Take rate** — the slice of each booking Uber keeps as commission.
- **EBITDA / adjusted EBITDA** — profit before interest, tax, and non-cash charges; "adjusted" strips out further items management chooses to exclude, so watch what is excluded.
- **Operating income / operating margin** — profit from the core business before interest and tax; the margin is that profit as a share of revenue.
- **EV/EBITDA** — enterprise value (the whole business, debt included) divided by EBITDA; a cheaper-or-dearer gauge across companies. Uber's 11.89x means you pay $11.89 per $1 of that profit.
- **Return on capital (ROIC)** — the profit the business earns on each $100 put into it; compare it against the cost of capital.
- **Cost of capital (~8.1%)** — what the money Uber uses costs to raise; earning above it creates value, below it destroys value.
- **Reverse-DCF** — working backwards from today's price to find the growth the price already assumes.
- **Terminal value** — in a DCF, the chunk of value assumed to come after the explicit forecast years; a large share (here 77.1%) means most of the answer rests on far-off guesses.
- **Margin of safety** — the discount to fair value that cushions you if your estimate is wrong (here 9.0%).
- **Net debt / leverage** — total debt minus cash (strict basis); leverage of 1.32x means debt is 1.32 times annual EBITDA.
- **Covenant / covenant headroom** — a promise in a loan agreement (e.g. leverage stays under 3.5x); headroom is how much room there is before it is breached.
- **Bridge facility** — a large short-term loan used to fund a deal until permanent financing replaces it.
- **Basis points (bps)** — hundredths of a percent; 63bps = 0.63%.
- **Free cash flow (FCF / FCFF)** — cash the business generates after spending to run and grow itself; FCFF is that cash before financing choices.
- **Merger arbitrage** — buying a target's stock below the agreed deal price to capture the gap if the deal closes.

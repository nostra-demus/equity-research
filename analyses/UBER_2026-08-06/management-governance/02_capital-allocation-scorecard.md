**Serial-acquirer + opportunity-cost test (CLAUDE.md §24, Filter 4).** Uber closed or has pending at least seven acquisitions in roughly 18 months, layered under one transformative, debt-funded deal — Delivery Hero at $14.8bn (≈10.6% of Uber's own $139.3bn market cap as of 2026-08-05), financed by a fresh ~€14bn bridge facility. Management's own words on the Q2 FY2026 call — "we tactically pivoted quite heavily towards M&A" and pulled $4bn of capital from the buyback program to build the stake before the deal was even announced — are the opportunity-cost evidence this test asks for, not an inference: capital explicitly promised to buybacks (see §3) was redirected to build a pre-announcement equity stake, and a further $1.6bn of existing overlap businesses must be sold to SSW Partners to clear antitrust. Bare "synergies / strategic fit" language ($1.2bn run-rate, communicated but not yet underwritten by any closed transaction) is not evidence of value creation — it is a target. This is a serial-acquirer pattern per §24 Filter 4, consistent with `business-model/11_capital-allocation-governance.md`'s finding (acquisition-pattern severity 78/100, above the ≥70 threshold). **Red Flag RF-CAP-004 triggered — Critical for the M&A-discipline component; caps this scorecard's Capital Allocation total at 50/100 regardless of the underlying component sum (§4).**

Uber's capital-return framework substitutes buybacks for dividends entirely; given the capital already committed to the AV investment program (~$10bn multi-year, per `01_management-and-track-record.md`) and the pending Delivery Hero financing, a policy of retaining/buying back rather than paying a dividend is a defensible structural choice for this stage of the business — not itself a governance negative — but it also means shareholders have no cash floor if the M&A and AV bets underperform.

## 5. Capital-Allocation Read

Judged on what management actually did with cash, not what it said it would do: the **organic** business is a genuine per-share value creator — Uber turned EBIT from a $(1,832)mm loss (FY2022) to $5,565mm of profit (FY2025) on capex that never exceeded $336mm/year, an incremental ROIC in the 25–35% range by this module's approximation, comfortably clearing its cost of capital. Buybacks reduced net share count modestly (−2.2% since Dec-2024) and ran ahead of stock-based-comp dilution in dollar terms (≈3.9x), which clears the minimum bar of "not merely offsetting SBC" — but the heaviest buying quarters were priced well above where the stock trades today, so this was not price-disciplined buying by the observable trading-range test. The single biggest capital-allocation signal, and the worst use of capital on the evidence available, is the M&A program: at least seven bolt-on deals in eighteen months culminating in a $14.8bn, debt-funded acquisition of Delivery Hero — financed by a fresh ~€14bn bridge facility, requiring a forced $1.6bn divestiture of existing overlap businesses to clear antitrust, and funded in part by diverting $4bn away from the buyback program the company had promised shareholders. The synergies management cites ($1.2bn run-rate) are a target communicated weeks before this report, not a delivered result, and the deal will not even close until H2 2027. **Verdict: Mixed** — organic capital allocation is value-creative and buybacks are (barely) per-share accretive on a share-count basis, but the M&A pattern is the dominant, least-proven, and highest-risk capital decision management has made, and it is debt-funded at a moment the balance sheet had only just rebuilt itself toward net cash.

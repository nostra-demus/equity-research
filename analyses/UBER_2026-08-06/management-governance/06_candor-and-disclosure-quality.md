# Candor & Disclosure Quality — UBER

**Data-pool caveat.** No DEF 14A, 10-K, 10-Q, 8-K, or shareholder letter is in `data/UBER/`. What IS present, and used here: one **verbatim** earnings-call transcript (Q2 FY2026, Aug-05-2026, S&P Global Market Intelligence transcript of the live call — full trust applies to the words themselves), a multi-year Capital IQ guidance-vs-actual history (2019–2026), and the cross-module `earnings/06_earnings-quality.md` (non-GAAP and accrual read, available — no cross-check gap) and `earnings/04_guidance-consensus.md` (beat/miss pattern). **Limitation that matters for this specific agent:** the one verbatim quarter in the pool happens to be a strong quarter (Adjusted EBITDA beat, record free cash flow) — it is NOT the worst recent quarter. FQ3 2025 and FQ4 2025 (Adjusted EPS misses of −25.4% and −9.6% vs. consensus) are the actual weak prints in this window, and no transcript for either exists in the pool. Section 4 below is therefore built on how management handled the negative sub-plots embedded WITHIN an otherwise good quarter (a soft revenue print, a margin-optics reclassification, a broken capital-return framework, competitive softness in Brazil) — a genuine but imperfect proxy for "tone in bad times," not a direct read of the actual worst quarter. Confidence on every transcript-sourced row is held at 3, not 5, for this reason.

## 3. Disclosure Transparency

Segment-level economics that matter ARE disclosed with real numbers when management chooses to volunteer them — the U.K. take-rate reclassification (Section 1) came with an exact basis-point breakdown and a redirect to the true operating-margin number (7.6%), and the CFO named the specific mechanics of the $10bn AV investment program (equity stakes vs. offtake/vehicle-purchase commitments, "$2.50 of external capital raised for every $1 Uber invests"). But two things got LESS transparent over time, not more: Uber stopped guiding Revenue in 2020 — the exact line now missing Street consensus — and the pool contains no compensation-metric or related-party-transaction disclosure at all (a proxy-statement gap, not addressed on any call). Neither of these is a call-tone problem; both are structural disclosure gaps that predate this quarter and were not remedied by it.

## 4. Tone in Bad Times

The only verbatim quarter available is a good one (Adjusted EBITDA and EPS both beat guidance, record trailing FCF), so this section reads tone against the negative sub-plots inside a strong print, not against an actual weak quarter — a real limitation, stated up front. Within that constraint, the tone read is candid, not evasive: every negative or complicating item raised by an analyst (the buyback pause, the U.K. margin optics, Brazil competition) got a direct, numbers-first answer rather than a deflection to a different topic, and the CFO used the phrase "optical impact" about Uber's own reported take-rate rather than letting the improved-looking number stand unexplained. The one soft spot is the CEO's Delivery Hero framing — "we believe this deal will create significant long-term shareholder value" — asserted without a quantified return metric (no stated IRR, payback period, or ROIC target anywhere in the call), which is the closest this transcript comes to boilerplate.

## 5. Candor Read

Management answers direct questions about bad news with real numbers rather than talking around them — the $4bn buyback diversion, the U.K. take-rate's "optical" 400bps, and Brazil's competitive dynamics were all owned with specifics, not spin — and the company's own Normalized Net Income runs more conservative than GAAP, the opposite of the usual non-GAAP inflation pattern. Set against that: Uber stopped guiding the one line item now missing consensus, roughly a quarter of its own Adjusted EBITDA addback is never itemized, and a non-cash deferred-tax benefit has now inflated GAAP net income by a combined ~$10 billion over two straight years with no management commentary flagging the distortion to anyone not cross-checking the balance sheet — plus the evidence base here is thin, one good-quarter transcript, not a tested response to an actual bad print. No unresolved adverse integrity signal was routed from `business-model/01_disqualifier-scan` or `01_management-and-track-record` (RF-MGT-005 not triggered), so there is no compounding integrity concern weighing on this read. The single biggest candor signal is the U.K. take-rate call: management could have let an "improving" reported number stand uncorrected and chose instead to name it as optical and redirect to the harder metric — real evidence of trustworthiness — but it sits next to a genuinely opaque quarter of Adjusted EBITDA addbacks that management has never itemized on any call in this pool. Verdict: **mixed**.

## 5. Ownership / Insider Read

Skin in the game here is thin: officers and directors together hold just 0.18% of Uber's shares, the CEO's own stake (0.06%, $87.8mm) is stock-compensation-derived rather than bought with his own cash, and the only transaction in six months with a disclosed non-zero purchase value is an ambiguous $1.6mm exercise-or-purchase by the CFO — not a pattern of conviction buying. The net insider signal over the last 3 and 6 months is modest net selling (−3,609 and −33,973 shares respectively), but it is almost entirely mechanical RSU-vest-then-sell-to-cover activity on a recurring monthly schedule, not opportunistic dumping ahead of bad news — the one large CEO sale in the pool preceded a quarter that beat guidance, not missed it. The biggest structural point for minority holders is not control risk (Uber has no promoter, no dual-class stock, no pledging, and no controlling owner — the largest holder is BlackRock at 7.4%) but alignment risk: management runs the company with almost no personal capital at stake, which the master synthesizer should weigh alongside the separately-scored capital-allocation and incentive-compensation reads, not as a substitute for them.

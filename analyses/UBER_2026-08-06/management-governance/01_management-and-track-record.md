## 3. Stated Strategy & Targets

Management describes Uber as a three-segment (Mobility, Delivery, Freight) marketplace pursuing broad-based volume growth — penetrating "sparse markets" where under 10% of eligible U.S. consumers used Uber in the past year, versus over 50% in dense markets — while layering in a fourth, not-yet-monetized bet: positioning Uber as the "commercialization platform" for autonomous-vehicle (AV) makers, funded by roughly $10 billion of equity investments and vehicle-purchase commitments (120,000 vehicles) over a multi-year period, mostly through partner stakes (Waymo, Wayve, Zoox, Nuro, Pony, Baidu) rather than building Uber's own self-driving technology [Q2 FY2026 earnings call transcript, prepared remarks and Q&A, Aug-05-2026]. The explicit, checkable targets on the table: Adjusted EBITDA and Adjusted EPS guided quarter by quarter (Revenue has not been guided since FQ2 2020); a stated capital-return framework of "about 50% of free cash flow" to buybacks [Q2 FY2026 earnings call transcript, CFO remarks]; and a qualitative claim of "a track record of being disciplined on headcount addition over multiple years," offered as the basis for expecting AI-driven productivity gains to moderate future headcount growth [Q2 FY2026 earnings call transcript, CFO remarks, p.9] — this headcount claim has no supporting time series in the data pool and is **not verifiable from available data**.

## 5. Management Read

Dara Khosrowshahi has run Uber for nearly nine years and delivered a real, numbers-backed turnaround — operating income and operating cash flow both grew for three straight years (FY2023–FY2025) — which is proof, not a pitch [`business-model/01_disqualifier-scan.md`]. On the two metrics this management team actually guides, Adjusted EBITDA and Adjusted EPS, the record over the last several quarters is one of promises kept or beaten, though the company stopped guiding Revenue back in 2020 and that is exactly the line now missing Street estimates two quarters running. The single biggest management signal is the CFO chair: three different people have held it since 2018, with the middle tenure unusually short at about two years, right as the company is financing its largest-ever acquisition (Delivery Hero, $14.8bn) and a multi-year, ~$10bn AV investment program — a moment when finance-leadership continuity matters most, and the record shows the opposite.

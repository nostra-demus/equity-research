# Incentives & Compensation — UBER

**Data-pool caveat (carried from `00_governance-data-triage.md` and `01_management-and-track-record.md`):** no DEF 14A (proxy statement), 10-K, 10-Q, 8-K, or shareholder letter is present anywhere in `data/UBER/`. The pool is a Capital IQ vendor-export set (company profile, board/professionals bios, financial-statement workbooks, an estimates/consensus workbook) plus one Q2 FY2026 earnings-call transcript (Aug-05-2026). **None of these documents contains a Compensation Discussion & Analysis, a Summary Compensation Table, bonus/LTIP plan design, performance-metric weights, or a say-on-pay vote result for Uber.** Per this agent's partial-data rule, incentive alignment **cannot be assessed** from primary evidence. This report states that plainly, extracts the one high-level, pool-verifiable fact available (aggregate stock-based compensation, company-wide), and uses the web only for peer-comparator context (Lyft, DoorDash bonus/LTIP design), clearly labeled and dated — it does not use the web to fill in Uber's own metric weights, which would not be a pool-verified fact and is explicitly barred by this agent's operating rule.

On LTM revenue of $55,227mm (per `business-model/11_capital-allocation-governance.md`), that is roughly 3.3% of LTM revenue — directionally confirms a broadly equity-heavy pay culture at Uber, but this is an aggregate accounting line, not a CEO/CFO-specific compensation figure, and cannot be decomposed into base/bonus/LTIP by executive from the pool.

**Peer context (web-sourced, unverified, dated 2026-08-06 — for texture only, not a substitute for Uber's own proxy):** as one data point on rideshare/delivery-platform CEO pay design generally, Lyft's FY2025 DEF 14A (per third-party proxy summaries) describes CEO David Risher's base salary at $725,000 with an annual cash bonus tied 50%/50% to Gross Bookings and Adjusted EBITDA, both size/scale metrics rather than per-share or returns metrics [Web: Lyft FY2025 DEF 14A summary, accessed 2026-08-06, unverified]. DoorDash's CEO Tony Xu, by contrast, draws a nominal ~$300,000 salary with no cash bonus and effectively all upside from a 2020 "CEO Performance Award" that vests only if DoorDash's stock price clears a ladder of absolute price hurdles over seven years — a purely per-share/TSR-linked design [Web: multiple third-party compensation trackers (salary.com, AFL-CIO Paywatch) summarizing DoorDash's 2020 CEO Performance Award and FY2025 Summary Compensation Table, accessed 2026-08-06, unverified]. These two peers bracket the design space (size-metric annual bonus vs. pure stock-price LTIP) but say nothing about which design Uber itself uses.

## 4. Incentive Alignment Read

The honest answer is that incentive alignment **cannot be assessed** for Uber from the data available: there is no proxy statement, no Compensation Discussion & Analysis, no Summary Compensation Table, and no bonus/LTIP metric or weight disclosure anywhere in `data/UBER/`, so neither the size of CEO/CFO pay nor what it actually rewards can be verified. The only pool-anchored signal — aggregate, company-wide stock-based compensation running ~3.3% of LTM revenue [Uber Technologies Inc NYSE UBER Financials.xls, Cash Flow / Supplemental tabs] alongside a disclosed policy of directing roughly half of free cash flow to buybacks [Q2 FY2026 earnings-call transcript, CFO remarks] — points toward a broadly equity-funded, per-share-conscious capital posture at the company level, but it says nothing about how the CEO and CFO are personally scored or paid, and it must not be read as a substitute finding for the missing executive-level disclosure. Conclusion: **not assessable — insufficient data**, and the single highest-value next input is Uber's own DEF 14A (Compensation Discussion & Analysis, Summary Compensation Table, Grants of Plan-Based Awards, and the say-on-pay vote result).

**Verdict: Mixed earnings setup** — profit (margins) keeps improving, but the reported revenue number is slowing, and the gap between the two is unexplained.

Memo date: 2026-08-06. This memo condenses the earnings module synthesis; it adds no new numbers and does not change the synthesis's verdict, scores, or caps.

## What This Module Found

The picture is genuinely split, which is why the verdict is "Mixed" and not "improving" or "deteriorating." On the good side, profit margins have widened every single quarter for at least two years (adjusted EBITDA margin — a profit measure that adds back non-cash items like stock pay — went from 15.11% to 19.86%), and the company beat the top end of its own profit guidance in each of the last two quarters. The cash is real too: the business collected more cash from operations than its own headline profit measure in each of the last three years (115.7% in FY2025) [`01` §3; `06` §1–§2].

On the bad side, reported revenue is slowing. The last two quarters (+14.5%, then +12.2% year-on-year) were the two weakest of the past eight, and revenue came in below Wall Street's own estimate both times [`01` §3, §6].

The single most important driver is Mobility Gross Bookings — the total spend flowing through the app before Uber takes its cut — which grew 22% for a fourth straight quarter [`02` §4]. But here is the catch and the biggest risk: that 22% bookings growth sits about 10 percentage points above the 12.2% reported revenue growth for the same quarter, and this module cannot explain the gap from the data it has [`02` §6]. Management says the gap is mostly a UK accounting change it calls "optical," but no primary filing is in the pool to confirm that. If the gap is instead real price or take-rate erosion (Uber keeping a smaller cut of each ride), then the profit-beat streak is resting on a shrinking take rate, not durable cost control [`08` §1].

One structural weakness worth flagging: every number in this module comes from a Capital IQ vendor transcription — there is no primary SEC filing (10-K/10-Q) anywhere in the pool, so nothing here is checked against the audited source.

## The Specialists, Briefly

- **Data triage:** Data is sufficient (one-day-old transcript, current consensus, full financials) — but no primary SEC filing exists in the pool; every figure is a vendor transcription.
- **Historical financials:** Revenue growth is slowing while margins expand; GAAP EPS in FY2024/FY2025 is inflated by one-time tax benefits ($5,758mm, $4,346mm), so normalized EPS (0.73 → 1.70) is the cleaner trend.
- **Revenue drivers:** Mobility Gross Bookings is the biggest revenue driver and is still growing, but the ~10pp gap to reported revenue cannot be cleanly explained from this pool.
- **Margin drivers:** The insurance-cost / driver-incentive line is the single biggest margin lever — and its current benefit is being fully spent on pricing, leaving zero disclosed buffer if it reverses.
- **Guidance vs consensus:** The bar is fair — profit estimates now sit at the new guidance midpoints (closing the room that fed the last two beats), while revenue estimates keep falling.
- **Beat/miss setup:** Balanced — revenue missed twice while profit beat twice; watch for an in-line Q3 masking a soft, unsized Q4 guide.
- **Earnings quality (68):** Mostly clean and cash-backed, capped by GAAP profit being dominated by a non-cash tax benefit two years running.
- **Earnings sensitivity (45, Low confidence):** SG&A leverage is the largest quantified swing factor (±$780mm) and is already reversing in the latest data.
- **Red flags:** Material concerns — the most dangerous single flag is the unexplained ~10pp bookings-to-revenue gap.

**Most important disagreement:** The revenue team framed bookings as "improving/durable," while the historical-financials and guidance teams framed reported revenue as "decelerating." The synthesis resolved this by weighting the reported-revenue slowdown at least as heavily as the bookings growth — reported revenue is the audited-adjacent number this module ultimately measures, and Gross Bookings has no audited multi-period series here [`02` §6; `08` §1].

## What Would Change This Read

**Would upgrade it:** A primary filing (10-Q/10-K) confirming the bookings-to-revenue gap is genuinely an accounting effect (the UK reclassification) rather than real price erosion — combined with revenue re-accelerating above Wall Street's cut estimate and SG&A discipline resuming its earlier improvement.

**Would downgrade it:** A Q3 print or Q4 guide showing the SG&A reversal widening, the insurance-cost benefit reversing (with its zero disclosed buffer), or revenue missing Wall Street for a third straight quarter.

**Data that would settle it:** The FY2025 10-K or FQ3 2026 10-Q revenue bridge — the breakdown of bookings-to-revenue by take rate, mix, currency, and M&A. This is the single highest-value next request; it directly resolves the ~10pp gap.

---

## Bottom Line

- **Verdict: Mixed earnings setup.** Margins and cash are improving; reported revenue is slowing and has missed Wall Street twice. The two pull in opposite directions.
- **Why it could be better than it looks:** Cash generation is genuinely clean and improving (collected 115.7% of its headline profit measure as cash in FY2025), and profit margins have widened every quarter for two years.
- **Why it could be worse than it looks:** The unexplained ~10pp gap between bookings growth (22%) and reported revenue growth (12.2%). If that is real price erosion, the profit beats rest on a shrinking cut, not cost discipline — and the insurance-cost benefit propping up margins is fully spent, with no buffer if it reverses.
- **What evidence is missing:** No primary SEC filing anywhere in the pool — every figure is a vendor transcription, one step removed from the audited source.
- **One thing to watch next:** The Q4 2026 guide (Uber's seasonally strongest quarter, still unguided) — the first place Delivery Hero / self-driving investment costs could be sized. An in-line Q3 plus a weak, cost-laden Q4 guide would turn this setup negative without any Q3 miss.

## Plain-English Glossary

- **Adjusted EBITDA / EBITDA margin:** A profit measure that adds back non-cash and one-off items (like stock pay); the margin is that profit as a percent of revenue.
- **GAAP net income / EPS:** The official accounting profit (and per-share profit) investors see first — here distorted by a one-time tax credit.
- **Gross Bookings:** The total customer spend flowing through the app before Uber takes its cut.
- **Take rate:** The share of each booking Uber keeps as its own revenue.
- **Net debt:** Total debt minus cash — here 0.93x yearly adjusted profit.
- **CFO (cash from operations):** The actual cash the business generates from running its operations.
- **Consensus / the bar:** Wall Street analysts' average estimate that a print is judged "beat" or "miss" against.
- **SG&A leverage:** Whether sales, general and administrative overhead is falling as a share of revenue (leverage improving) or rising (reversing).

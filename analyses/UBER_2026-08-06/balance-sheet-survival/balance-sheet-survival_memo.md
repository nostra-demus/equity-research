## What This Module Found

Uber's balance sheet as reported at Jun-30-2026 is strong. Net debt (debt minus cash only — the strict basis) is $9,861mm, which is 1.32x this year's GAAP EBITDA (a rough measure of yearly operating profit) or 0.98x on Uber's own adjusted EBITDA measure — low either way [`01`].

The near-term maturity wall (debt coming due soon) is thin and self-funded: 14.8% of the debt is due within 12 months, covered more than twice over by cash alone; the next big bond cluster is FY2028 ($2,850mm), over two years out [`02`, `03`].

Liquidity has no finite end date. $10,059mm of committed cash, safe investments, and an undrawn credit line, plus about $10,116mm of yearly free cash flow (the spare cash the business throws off after spending), together cover everything owed over the next year several times over — enough to survive even a 100% wipeout in earnings [`03`, `06`].

The single most important driver of the verdict is forward-looking, not in any reported number yet: the near-certain Delivery Hero acquisition (signed 2026-07-16), funded by a roughly €14bn bridge loan (short-term financing) with no disclosed covenant terms. Once that loan is drawn, pro-forma leverage rises to 2.6x–3.5x current earnings (5.0x–6.6x on a mid-cycle earnings base) [`06`, Abstract].

The single most important risk: today an assumed 3.5x leverage covenant would not break until earnings fell 62–72%; once the Delivery Hero loan is drawn, that same assumed covenant would break on an earnings drop of just 0.9%–25.5% — so a normal 30–40% recession breaches it in three of the four modeled combinations [`06`]. Because the actual covenant terms are undisclosed, this break point rests on a labeled assumption, not a filed fact [`04`].

## The Specialists, Briefly

- **Solvency-data-triage:** partial sufficiency — the core financials are fully present and current, but no credit agreement, indenture, or covenant summary exists anywhere in the pool [`00`].
- **Capital-structure-and-leverage:** net leverage 1.32x GAAP / 0.98x adjusted; not net cash. Gross debt rose $2,429mm in H1 FY2026 to $14,731mm with no instrument-level breakout, and the €14bn Delivery Hero bridge is not yet in any balance-sheet figure [`01`].
- **Maturity-wall-and-refinancing:** low refinancing risk on the disclosed 24-month wall; the real forward risk is the unidentified $1,997mm of new short-term debt and the pending bridge, not the bond ladder [`02`].
- **Liquidity-runway:** no finite runway — $10,059mm committed liquidity plus $10,116mm free cash flow dwarfs $2,945mm of 12-month obligations; the pending bridge (1.6x the whole current liquidity pool) is not reflected here [`03`].
- **Coverage-and-covenants:** interest coverage strong (16.18x–21.74x); covenant headroom Not assessable — a full bridge draw would land pro-forma leverage almost exactly at an assumed 3.5x covenant (~0% headroom) [`04`].
- **Off-balance-sheet-and-contingencies:** contingent exposure of at least $6,414mm (22.6% of equity); `RF-OBS-001` fired on the $5,611mm tax benefit growing 13.7% a year, not broken out on the balance sheet [`05`].
- **Downside-stress-test:** a fortress today, but does not clearly survive a 30–40% earnings decline once the bridge is drawn — the covenant-breach point collapses from a 62–72% decline to 0.9–25.5% [`06`].

**Most important disagreement (Reconciliation):** the $1,997mm jump in current-portion debt is unresolved — it may be real new short-term debt, or a mechanical GAAP reclassification of the $1,725mm 2028 convertible notes (which would typically settle in equity, not cash). The synthesis adopts the conservative reading — counting the full $1,997mm as a real near-term obligation — and notes this does not change the survival conclusion, since the wall is covered several times over either way [`04`, `02`].

## What Would Change This Read

**Would strengthen it:** the Delivery Hero bridge's actual covenant package showing meaningful headroom after the draw; confirmation the $1,997mm current-debt jump is a non-cash convertible reclassification, not real short-term debt; a dedicated contingencies note showing the $5,611mm tax exposure is fully accrued and the other litigation immaterial.

**Would weaken it:** a full €14bn bridge draw plus a normal recession-scale earnings decline (which the pro-forma stress shows would breach an assumed covenant); the deal falling through and triggering the €700mm break fee at the same time as an operating downturn; a confirmed covenant breach or waiver requirement disclosed after close.

**Data needed (highest value):** the bridge facility's actual covenant terms and definitions; an updated, instrument-level Q2/Q3 FY2026 debt note explaining the $2,429mm gross-debt increase; a dedicated commitments-and-contingencies note.

## Bottom Line

- **Verdict: Adequate** — solid on the balance sheet as reported today, capped by undisclosed covenant terms and a $5.6bn contingent tax exposure.
- **Why it could be better than it looks:** committed liquidity ($10,059mm) plus cash-backed free cash flow ($10,116mm) never runs out under any earnings haircut modeled, including a full 100% wipeout; free cash flow has exceeded adjusted EBITDA every year since FY2023 [`03`, `06`].
- **Why it could be worse than it looks:** once the €14bn Delivery Hero loan is drawn, pro-forma leverage runs 5.0x–6.6x mid-cycle earnings and an assumed covenant breaks on as little as a 0.9% earnings decline — a normal recession would likely need lender accommodation [`06`].
- **What evidence is missing:** the actual loan-covenant terms — for both Uber's existing debt and the new Delivery Hero bridge. Without them the "how close to breaking" figure is a labeled assumption, not a filed fact [`00`, `04`].
- **One thing to watch next:** the Delivery Hero deal closing and the bridge being drawn — the point at which today's wide covenant cushion (+62–72%) collapses to near-zero. This is a leverage-covenant risk, not a liquidity risk; liquidity stays deep in every pro-forma scenario.

## Plain-English Glossary

- **Net debt (strict basis):** total debt minus cash only. Uber's is $9,861mm.
- **EBITDA:** a rough measure of yearly operating profit before interest, tax, and non-cash charges. "Adjusted EBITDA" is the company's own version, which here runs above the standard GAAP figure.
- **Net leverage:** net debt divided by EBITDA — how many years of profit it would take to repay the net debt.
- **Free cash flow:** the spare cash the business generates after its spending; Uber's is about $10,116mm a year.
- **Maturity wall:** debt coming due within a set window that has to be repaid or refinanced.
- **Covenant / covenant headroom:** a promise in a loan agreement (e.g. leverage must stay below a set level); headroom is the cushion before that limit is breached. Uber's actual covenants are not disclosed, so headroom is "Not assessable."
- **Bridge facility:** short-term financing used to fund a deal until longer-term money replaces it — here the roughly €14bn loan for the Delivery Hero acquisition.
- **Pro-forma:** what the numbers would look like after a transaction (the acquisition and its loan) is included.
- **Interest coverage:** how many times over EBITDA covers the interest bill — higher is safer.
- **Unrecognized tax benefit:** a disputed tax position not yet resolved; Uber's is $5,611mm and growing.

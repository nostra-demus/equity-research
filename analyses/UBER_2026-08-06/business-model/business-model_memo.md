## What This Module Found

Uber runs a two-sided marketplace app: it matches riders, eaters and shippers with drivers, couriers and freight carriers, and takes a commission (the "take rate," mid-20s% company-wide) on each transaction. It owns the app and payment rails, not the vehicles.

Mobility (ride-hailing) is the engine: 57.0% of FY2025 revenue but 90.5% of total EBITDA — earnings before interest, tax, and non-cash charges, a rough cash-profit proxy — at $7,899mm of $8,730mm [`03_segment-map.md`]. Delivery is a real, improving second segment (33.2% of revenue). Freight loses money at the EBITDA line and shrank 27% over three years.

The single most important positive: Mobility's segment EBITDA margin reached 26.6% in FY2025 (up from 19.2% in FY2020), well above Lyft's near-zero and DiDi's negative margins — a real, widening cost and scale edge over its two closest named rivals [`09_moat.md` §2–§4].

The single most important risk: driver/worker-classification regulation. A ruling or law reclassifying gig drivers as employees would strike the independent-contractor cost structure under both Mobility and Delivery at once. The U.K. already delivered a preview this quarter, cutting Mobility's reported take rate by roughly 500 basis points (5 percentage points) [`10_external-dependency.md` §5].

The decisive negative: through-cycle return on capital (ROIC) — the profit earned on each dollar invested — averaged 6.2% over FY2023–FY2025, below an estimated ~8.1% cost of capital (WACC, what that money costs to raise). Only FY2024 and FY2025 individually clear that bar, and that run is flagged as a peak, not a steady state [`09_moat.md` §3–§5].

## What Would Change This Read

The read improves toward "high-quality" if:
- Return on capital sustains above the ~8.1% WACC estimate for a full cycle without regulatory or competitive props; and
- The $14.8bn Delivery Hero deal closes on schedule near its committed terms without pushing leverage past management's stated target.

The read worsens toward "low-quality" if:
- A major-market (US or EU) driver-classification ruling lands, striking the independent-contractor cost base; or
- The Delivery Hero deal's leverage or integration outcome deteriorates materially from what is currently disclosed.

## Bottom Line

- **Verdict:** Average business — worth deeper work only if valuation is cheap enough to compensate for an unproven moat and a capital-allocation cap.
- **Could be better than it looks:** Return on capital has risen every year for five straight years (-9.40% FY2021 → 10.59% LTM), Mobility margin (26.6%) far outpaces Lyft and DiDi, and LTM free cash flow now exceeds $10bn — a widening structural edge, not a one-quarter blip.
- **Could be worse than it looks:** Over a full cycle Uber still earns less on capital (6.2% 3-yr avg) than its money costs (~8.1%), and FY2024–FY2025 net income is more than half explained by non-cash tax and mark-to-market items, not operating gains — so recent "clearing the bar" years are flattered.
- **What's missing:** The primary FY2025 10-K (or FQ2 2026 10-Q). Every upstream read was built from vendor exports, not the filing's own segment note, risk factors, MD&A, and related-party disclosure. English-language pool — this is a completeness gap, not a translation issue.
- **Watch next:** A driver/worker-classification ruling in the US or EU, and the Delivery Hero deal's closing terms and leverage (deal signed Jul-16-2026, offer expected H2 2027).

## Plain-English Glossary

- **EBITDA:** Earnings before interest, tax, and non-cash charges — a rough proxy for cash profit.
- **Take rate:** The percentage Uber keeps of each fare, order, or shipment that runs through the app.
- **Segment EBITDA margin:** That cash-profit measure as a percent of a single business line's revenue.
- **Return on capital (ROIC):** The profit earned on each dollar invested in the business.
- **Cost of capital (WACC):** What the money Uber uses costs to raise — the bar ROIC must beat to create value.
- **Basis points:** Hundredths of a percentage point; 500bps = 5 percentage points.
- **Free cash flow:** Operating cash left after spending to run and grow the business.
- **Net debt (strict basis):** Total debt minus cash.
- **Investment-grade:** A credit-quality rank signalling low default risk.

# Earnings Sensitivity — META

Reporting standard: US GAAP. Currency: USD, millions unless stated. Base impact metric used throughout this report: **GAAP Operating Income (EBIT — "Income from operations")**, on a trailing-twelve-month (TTM) basis ended Jun-30-2026 (Revenue $228,247m; Operating Income $89,327m) [`01_historical-financials.md` §2, sum of Q3'25–Q2'26 reported figures, sourced from the Capital IQ Financials_Quarterly export cross-checked to the FY2025 10-K and Q2 FY26 10-Q]. This report uses EBIT rather than the calculated EBITDA figure carried in `01_historical-financials` and `03_margin-drivers`, because `03_margin-drivers.md` §4 flags a ~$2.4bn unreconciled gap in the FY2026-quarter D&A add-back used to compute EBITDA, and its own §5 concludes EBIT is the more reliable, company-reported basis for this business. EPS is not used as the impact unit: a clean, filing-sourced diluted share count was not established from this data-pool session, so converting to EPS would require an unlabelled inference on share count; EBIT dollars keep every step traceable to a reported or cited figure. Where a revenue-side variable is converted to an EBIT effect, this report applies an explicit, labelled flow-through assumption (Section 2 methodology note) rather than assuming EPS math it cannot support.

**Sidecar note:** `analyses/META_2026-08-27/earnings/sensitivity_summary.json` was not emitted with this run. Neither `frameworks/sensitivity_summary.schema.json` nor `scripts/sensitivity_math.py` exist in this repository snapshot, and this run's own file-write instructions restrict output to this single report path. The coefficients that would populate that sidecar are shown, worked, and cited in Section 2 below so they can be transcribed once the sidecar tooling exists.

## 1. Variable Selection

Six variables were selected from the upstream driver tables, ranked by the magnitude rating each already carries in `02_revenue-drivers.md` and `03_margin-drivers.md`. From the revenue side: **ad impressions delivered** (Importance 85/100, the single biggest revenue driver at 49.1% of Q2 FY26's 27.96pp growth, per `02_revenue-drivers.md` §4/§7) and **average price per ad, ex-FX** (Importance 90/100, 42.1% of the same growth, per §6a/§7) — both rated High magnitude. From the margin side: **R&D / AI-infrastructure cost growth** (the single biggest margin driver, accounting for 69% of the -1,214bps Q2 FY26 operating-margin decline, per `03_margin-drivers.md` §7/§8) and **cost of revenue** (data-centre/cloud infrastructure costs, -76bps of the same decline, per §7). Two variables were added from cross-module and revenue-driver evidence to cover currency and macro exposure: **FX translation** (disclosed by the company itself as a $685m–$2,430m swing this year and flagged Mid dependency in `10_external-dependency.md` §1) and **advertiser budget / consumer-cycle shock** — the business-model module's own named "single biggest lever" (`10_external-dependency.md` §5, dependency level High, §4 score 55/100 inverted), which also matches revenue-drivers' own "end-market demand" driver rated Importance 85/100. Regulation (ad-data-signal access) was considered but excluded from the quantified table: both `02_revenue-drivers.md` §4 and `10_external-dependency.md` §2 confirm no dollar sensitivity is disclosed for it, and this report will not invent one; it is carried qualitatively into Section 6 instead.

## 2. Sensitivity Table

**Methodology note (read before the table):** For the two revenue-volume/price variables and the advertiser-budget-shock variable, the dollar move in the underlying metric (impressions growth, ex-FX price growth, or ad-spend shock) is first converted to a revenue dollar effect, then to an EBIT effect using an **assumed flow-through rate of 39.1%** — Meta's own current TTM EBIT margin ($89,327m ÷ $228,247m) — because no company-disclosed incremental margin exists for these levers. *Inference, not from filings.* True incremental margin could be higher (fixed R&D/G&A create operating leverage on upside volume) or lower (the AI cost base does not flex down on a downside shock — see Section 6), so this is a working assumption, not a company forecast. For the two cost-ratio variables (cost of revenue, R&D), the dollar effect is direct and dollar-for-dollar against TTM revenue — no flow-through assumption is needed, because a cost-ratio point is already an EBIT-dollar point. FX is carried as a direct dollar figure per the company's own disclosure that no offsetting FX cost-line effect exists (`03_margin-drivers.md` §7, FX row: "Not applicable on the cost side... no FX cost-line effect is disclosed").

## 4. The Single Highest-Sensitivity Variable

**Advertiser budget / consumer-cycle shock** moves earnings the most, at an average absolute swing of roughly $17.4bn of TTM operating income (about 19.5% of the $89,327m base), well ahead of the next-largest variable (R&D cost growth, ~$6.8bn). Its current direction is favourable — ad revenue is growing +27–28% YoY (Q2 FY26) with no evidence of stress — and it is explicitly external and not company-controlled: substantially all of Meta's revenue is advertising, and marketers generally carry no long-term spending commitments [`10_external-dependency.md` §5; FY25 Form 10-K, Risk Factors — Advertising Revenue, pp.15–16]. For this variable to swing to the adverse case would require a broad, macro-driven pullback in global online-ad budgets — a recession, a rate shock, or a trade-policy shock hitting the direct-response and SME advertiser base — consistent with the company's own disclosure that "inflation, high rates, economic policy and trade uncertainty have pressured budgets at times" [`10_external-dependency.md` §1, Consumer cycle row].

## 5. Interaction Effects

Several of these six variables are not independent. First, ad impressions and price-per-ad move together mechanically: `02_revenue-drivers.md` §6a quantifies a 1.65pp multiplicative cross-term between the two in Q2 FY26 alone, so a bull or bear case that moves one without the other understates or overstates the compound effect. Second, R&D/AI-infrastructure cost growth and cost-of-revenue growth share a common root cause — the AI compute build-out (data-centre capacity, third-party cloud, technical infrastructure, third-party AI token costs) — so a supply-side compute-cost shock (e.g., a power or GPU-price spike) would likely move both cost lines adversely at the same time, not independently; summing their two bear cases as if uncorrelated would understate how a single shock could hit both. Third, the advertiser-budget shock and FX have historically correlated: macro downturns that curb marketer spending often coincide with USD strength (flight-to-quality flows), which would turn a currently favourable FX tailwind into a headwind at the same time as ad demand weakens — a compounding "double hit" this table's separate rows do not capture. Regulatory risk (ad-data-signal access) is comparatively independent of the other five — it runs on its own policy timeline, not a macro or commodity cycle.

## 6. Non-Linear Or Asymmetric Risks

**Operating deleverage on the downside.** R&D/AI-infrastructure costs are now large (35.62% of revenue, Q2 FY26) and increasingly locked in — FY2026 total-expense guidance ($165bn–$169bn) has been RAISED, not cut, at every quarterly checkpoint this year [`03_margin-drivers.md` §9]. If advertiser demand decelerates from here, this cost base cannot fall proportionally in the same period, so a moderate revenue slowdown would translate into a disproportionately larger margin decline — `03_margin-drivers.md` §10 makes this point explicitly: "the now-larger, largely fixed AI-infrastructure cost base... would amplify — not cushion — any resulting margin decline, because operating leverage cuts in both directions."

**FX has no natural hedge on the cost side.** Revenue-side FX moves pass through to EBIT with no disclosed offsetting FX cost effect (`03_margin-drivers.md` §7 FX row: "not applicable on the cost side... no FX cost-line effect is disclosed"). This is not a typical multinational manufacturer's natural hedge (foreign costs offsetting foreign revenue); a downside FX move hits EBIT largely undiluted.

**Regulatory risk is a step function, not a smooth slope.** The DMA gatekeeper designation has already changed discretely once (Facebook Marketplace undesignated, April 2025, per `10_external-dependency.md` §1A) rather than eroding gradually. A further binary ruling — e.g., an adverse outcome on Meta's EU consent-model engagement — could remove monetisable ad-data signal in a single step, a risk this table's continuous bull/bear framing does not represent well.

**Sunk-cost asymmetry in the AI build-out.** Heavy capex is already landing in current-period D&A and cost of revenue, not deferred to a future period (`03_margin-drivers.md` §9: PP&E depreciation +40.2% YoY, Q2 FY26). If the advertiser-demand bull case does not materialise, this spend cannot be un-spent — the downside case inherits a cost base sized for a demand level that may not arrive, while the upside case (demand keeps accelerating) simply uses capacity already committed.

## 7. Earnings Volatility Score

**65 / 100 — INVERTED: higher = WORSE (more volatile / sensitive to small input changes).**

Reason: two variables carry EBIT swings above $5bn on an $89,327m TTM operating-income base (advertiser-budget shock ≈$17.4bn, ≈19.5% of the base; R&D cost growth ≈$6.8bn, ≈7.7% of the base), the single biggest margin driver (R&D/AI infrastructure) is currently moving in the adverse direction with guidance raised rather than cut, and the largest revenue driver's downside case is a macro variable entirely outside company control. This places META above the "material sensitivity" band (41–60) but short of "extremely volatile" (81–100), because the core advertising engine itself is not currently showing stress — the volatility risk is concentrated in the interaction between an unusually large, semi-fixed AI cost base and a demand variable the company does not control, rather than in the base business being erratic today.

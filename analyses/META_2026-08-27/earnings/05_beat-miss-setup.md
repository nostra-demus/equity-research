# Beat / Miss Setup — META

## 1. Next Reporting Period Context

Meta's next filing is the standalone three months ended Sep-30-2026 ("Q3 FY26"), a discrete quarter under US GAAP (Meta does not file cumulative half-year/nine-month statements), with the earnings release expected ~Oct-28-2026 and the Form 10-Q shortly after [`04_guidance-consensus.md` §1A]. Q3 is not a seasonally extreme quarter — it has averaged 25.2% of annual revenue over FY2023–FY2025, above the Q1 trough (~21.5%) but below the Q4 peak (~29.6%) [`01_historical-financials.md` §5].

**Bar (carried verbatim from `04` §1A, standalone-quarter basis, no restatement needed):** Revenue consensus $63,314.92M (guidance range $61,000M–$64,000M, midpoint $62,500M) / EPS Normalized consensus $6.97 (Meta gives no EPS guidance) — both on the FQ3 2026 standalone-quarter basis, since Meta's vendor period label already matches its filing basis [`04_guidance-consensus.md` §1A, §3]. That $6.97 implies −3.9% YoY versus the Q3 FY25 actual Normalized EPS of $7.25, even as consensus revenue of $63,314.92M implies +23.6% YoY versus Q3 FY25's $51,242M [`04_guidance-consensus.md` §1A].

## 6. Seasonality Read

Q3 is a middle-of-the-pack quarter for Meta, averaging 25.2% of annual revenue over FY2023–FY2025 — above the Q1 trough (~21.5%) but well below the Q4 peak (~29.6%, right at the 30% flag threshold) [`01_historical-financials.md` §5]. Seasonality is therefore roughly neutral to the Q3 FY26 setup itself: there is no seasonal tailwind or headwind of the size that would move the beat/miss call on its own. The seasonally strong quarter (Q4, holiday advertising demand) falls in the period AFTER next, which matters more for §9 below than for the immediate print.

## 7. Historical Pattern

Meta has beaten consensus revenue in each of the last four quarters, but the beat magnitude has shrunk every single quarter — +3.71% (Q3 FY25) → +2.43% (Q4 FY25) → +1.36% (Q1 FY26) → +0.85% (Q2 FY26) — while the most recent quarter (Q2 FY26) posted an outright EPS miss on both the GAAP basis (−14.40%) and the Normalized basis (−16.49%) [`04_guidance-consensus.md` §6]. This is a four-observation sample — below the roughly eight-observation threshold for a measured empirical frequency — so it is read here as **judgment informed by a small, directionally consistent trend**, not a statistically robust base rate. It should be weighted moderately by the synthesizer: it supports treating the historical "automatic revenue beat" cushion as largely priced out, and it corroborates (rather than independently proves) the margin/EPS concern already visible in the cost-guidance and revision data.

## 8. Setup Verdict

**Setup favors miss.**

The single most important factor is that consensus already sits above management's own guidance on every quantifiable Q3/FY2026 line — revenue (+1.3% above the guidance midpoint), EBIT (+6.0% above the qualitative floor), and capex (+1.5% above the midpoint) — leaving no cushion, while the R&D-driven cost structure that took 838bps (69%) off Q2 FY26's operating margin is management's own description of a continuing, not one-off, investment [`04_guidance-consensus.md` §3, §7; `03_margin-drivers.md` §7a, §8]. This is reinforced by the sharpest analyst-behavior signal in the dataset: 44 of 52 analysts cut their FY2026 GAAP EPS estimate and 45 of 58 cut their price target (zero raised it) in the last month [`04_guidance-consensus.md` §5]. The single biggest risk that could flip this verdict is a continuation of the ad-impression/price momentum seen in Q2 FY26 (+14%/+12% YoY) at a pace that outruns the guided deceleration enough to offset the margin drag at the headline level, as happened (on a shrinking scale) in each of the last four quarters.

## 9. Second-Quarter Look-Ahead

The quarter after next (Q4 FY26, filed ~Jan-2027) looks somewhat different: Q4 is Meta's seasonally strongest quarter, averaging 29.6% of annual revenue over the last three years on holiday advertising demand [`01_historical-financials.md` §5], which could provide a revenue tailwind independent of the margin story. However, FY2026 total-expense and capex guidance both run through year-end, and management's own tax-rate guidance (15–17%) is stated for "remaining FY2026 quarters" (Q3–Q4), so the structural cost and tax pressure identified above is expected to persist into Q4 rather than ease [`04_guidance-consensus.md` §2].

## 10. Pre-Mortem

If this setup fails, the most likely reason is that Meta's ad-impression and price-per-ad growth continued to reaccelerate (as it has in each of the last four quarters) fast enough to offset the R&D-driven margin compression at the headline level — i.e., the revenue side kept doing exactly what §2's beat scenarios describe, even as the miss scenarios in §3 also played out on the cost line, producing a "beat on revenue, in-line-to-soft on EPS" print that does not cleanly fit either a beat or a miss framing.

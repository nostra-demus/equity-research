## 5. Earnings Setup Summary

### Revenue Setup

The current revenue trajectory is not a one-time effect — it is broad-based across geography (US & Canada +32%, Europe +24%, Asia-Pacific +19%, Rest of World +36% in Q2 FY26) and across both volume (+14% YoY impressions) and price (+12% YoY per-ad), with no acquisition, divestiture, or accounting change identified as a contributor [`02_revenue-drivers` §3, §6]. It is, however, not fully organic in the "pure demand" sense: about 1.5 percentage points of the 11.76-point price contribution is currency, not ad-tech or demand improvement, and management's own words attribute part of the price gain to "improvements in macro conditions relative to Q2 of last year" — a comparison-base effect that rolls off as the prior-year comp normalizes [`02_revenue-drivers` §3, §6a]. The single factor that would flip revenue direction is a broad advertiser-budget pullback (the business-model module's own largest external lever); short of that, management's own Q3 FY26 guidance ($61–64bn, implying +19.0% to +24.9% YoY) already signals a deceleration in pace, not a reversal in direction, from Q2's +28% print [`02_revenue-drivers` §7]. Reported and underlying revenue tell a broadly consistent story here — the gap is small (≈1.5pp of FX embedded in price) and already isolated, not hidden.

### Margin Setup

Current margins are not sustainable at their FY2024 peak level through the next cycle without a change in cost trajectory: operating margin has now compressed for two consecutive comparison periods (−80bps for all of FY2025, then −1,214bps year over year in the single most recent quarter, Q2 FY26), and management's own FY2026 total-expense guidance ($165bn–$169bn) has been raised, not narrowed downward, at every quarterly checkpoint this year [`01_historical-financials` §1, §3; `03_margin-drivers` §4, §9]. The segment or driver that would take the largest bite if it worsened another 10–20% is R&D / AI-infrastructure cost (currently 35.62% of revenue, +838bps YoY) — a further 10–20% relative rise in that ratio would cost roughly another 350–700bps of operating margin on this report's own cost-ratio coefficient (~$2,282m per point on a TTM revenue base) [`03_margin-drivers` §3, §7; `07_earnings-sensitivity` §2]. Management has no contractual or structural margin-protection mechanism disclosed for this cost line — no pass-through clause, hedge, or take-or-pay structure is cited anywhere in the upstream reports; Meta is effectively a price-taker on data-center, cloud, and AI-compute input costs, and the disclosed October 2025 "Venture" joint venture (which shifts ≈$27bn of committed infrastructure spend off Meta's own consolidated capex line, with disclosed maximum exposure to $45.95bn) is a financing-structure choice, not a cost hedge [`06_earnings-quality` §5, §10].

### Quality Check

The single largest gap between reported and economic earnings is not a cash-flow problem — CFO has exceeded EBITDA (calc) every year for five straight years, and the gap between reported net income and cash generation actually favors cash over the accrual figure [`06_earnings-quality` §2]. The real gap is between reported earnings and the *underlying capital reality*: a January 2025 useful-life extension on servers and network assets is genuinely a one-time accounting-estimate change (audited, disclosed) but it is widening in effect precisely because it lowers depreciation on an asset base that is now growing fastest — it added ≈$1.00/share (≈4.3%) to FY2025 diluted EPS and will keep doing so as more AI-era assets are added at the extended useful life [`06_earnings-quality` §8, §10]. If modeling normalized earnings for next year, start from GAAP, not a company-defined non-GAAP figure — Meta discloses no adjusted EBITDA, EBIT, or EPS at all, so there is no alternative non-GAAP base to start from; the two adjustments that do exist (FX-neutral revenue, free cash flow) are both narrow, fully reconciled, and not earnings-inflating [`06_earnings-quality` §4].

### Consensus Bar

For Meta to beat the current Q3 FY26 bar by a material margin, ad-impression and price-per-ad growth would need to continue at or above Q2 FY26's pace (+14% / +12% YoY) rather than decelerate into the guided +19.0%–+24.9% range implied by the $61–64bn guidance — essentially a repeat of the pattern seen in each of the last four quarters, but at a moment when that pattern's magnitude has shrunk every single quarter (+3.71% → +0.85%) [`04_guidance-consensus` §6; `05_beat-miss-setup` §2]. The bar looks most likely set too low on revenue (consensus sits only 1.3% above the guidance midpoint, and revenue-estimate revision breadth is genuinely positive, +21 net) but set too high, or at least too optimistic, on GAAP earnings, where 44 of 52 analysts cut estimates in the last month against a raised tax-rate guide (15–17%, up from 13–16%) and a raised-and-narrowed capex range [`04_guidance-consensus` §5, §7]. None of the current consensus appears anchored to a one-off macro tailwind that could cleanly reverse — the comparison-base/FX effects inside the price line are already small and disclosed, and the bigger overhang is the structural, guided-to-continue AI-cost buildout, not a one-time event.

## 9. Simple Summary

- Revenue is speeding up: +28% year-over-year growth in the latest quarter, the fourth straight quarter of faster growth, mostly from more ads shown (+14% YoY) and higher prices per ad (+12% YoY).
- Margins are shrinking, and shrinking faster: operating margin fell 1,214 basis points (hundredths of a percentage point) in one quarter, mostly because AI/R&D spending (+67% year over year) is growing much faster than sales.

- The setup favors a miss next quarter, specifically on the cost/profit line, not the revenue line.

Do not compare FY2021–2022 FCF to FY2023–2025 FCF as like-for-like — the later years' figure is already net of an extra deduction the earlier years' figure is not.

**Net Debt computation, shown (per CLAUDE.md §15):** Total debt (long-term debt only, per Capital IQ's own "Long-Term Debt" line, which is distinct from its separately-reported lease lines) minus cash & cash equivalents only (strict cash basis, no short-term investments netted): FY2021 0 − 16,601 = (16,601); FY2022 9,923 − 14,681 = (4,758); FY2023 18,385 − 41,862 = (23,477); FY2024 28,826 − 43,889 = (15,063); FY2025 58,744 − 35,873 = 22,871 [2][6]. The FY2024 ($28,826m) and FY2025 ($58,744m) long-term-debt figures are cross-checked directly against the FY25 10-K's own Consolidated Balance Sheets, which show "Long-term debt" as a line item separate from "Operating lease liabilities, current" ($2,213m FY25) and "Operating lease liabilities, non-current" ($22,940m FY25) [2]. This composition is therefore **confirmed clean of lease liabilities** for FY2024–FY2025, so the "strict" label is used unqualified for those two years; FY2021–FY2023 use the same Capital IQ line item (not independently cross-checked against those years' own 10-Ks, which are not in this data pool) and are presented on the same basis by extension.

**Footnote — a vendor "Total Debt" aggregate would show a bigger, dirtier number.** Capital IQ's own "Total Debt" field (a different row from the "Long-Term Debt" line used above) sums Long-term Debt + Long-term Leases + Current Portion of Leases — i.e., it folds operating and finance lease liabilities into "debt," which the company's own balance sheet does not do (leases are reported as separate "Operating lease liabilities" lines). Net debt built off that vendor aggregate would read: FY2021 (2,728); FY2022 11,910; FY2023 (4,628); FY2024 5,171; FY2025 49,208 — all higher (more debt-like) than the filing-clean figures above by the amount of lease liabilities folded in (~$14bn–$31bn depending on year) [6]. This module reports the filing-clean figure as primary; the balance-sheet-survival module owns the canonical net-debt build from the debt note and its figure may differ further.

## 6. Key Trend Summary

Revenue growth is **accelerating**: after Meta's only-ever full-year decline in FY2022 (-1.1%), YoY growth climbed to +15.7% (FY2023), +21.9% (FY2024), +22.2% (FY2025), and quarterly YoY prints have kept climbing into 2026 (+26.2% Q3'25 → +33.1% Q1'26 → +28.0% Q2'26). Margins are **inflecting toward compression**: EBITDA margin troughed in FY2022 (36.2%) on cost-cutting and Reality Labs losses, expanded sharply through FY2024 (51.6%), then slipped in FY2025 (50.7%) and fell further in the most recent quarter (Q2'26 45.3%, -690bps YoY) as R&D and infrastructure D&A ramp with the AI buildout — this is the single most important emerging signal in this dataset and should be treated as a live margin question for the next 2–3 quarters, not yet a confirmed reversal. There is material, structural seasonality: **Q4 is consistently the strongest quarter** (~29.6% of annual revenue on average, right at the flag threshold) on holiday ad demand, and Q1 is consistently the weakest (~21.5%). The clearest inflection point in the balance sheet is the shift from a multi-year net-cash position (net debt strict-basis ranged from -$4.8bn to -$23.5bn every year FY2021–FY2024) to net debt of +$22.9bn at FY2025 year-end and +$68.2bn by the latest TTM period-end (Jun-30-2026) — driven by capex that grew +87% in FY2025 alone and is running +71% YoY on a TTM basis, which has also pushed company-disclosed free cash flow down -20.4% TTM YoY (and to just $784m in the single quarter Q2'26) even as cash from operations grew +27%. Two one-off tax items (a $15.93bn non-cash charge in Q3'25, an $8.03bn benefit in Q1'26) distort reported EPS in those two quarters and must not be read as operating trend.

## 6a. Decomposition Attribution and Residual (MODULE_RULES "Driver Attribution" / §15)

**Advertising revenue's 26.94pp is sub-decomposed here into volume and price**, using the two ratios Meta itself discloses for Family of Apps, Q2 FY26 vs Q2 FY25 (same segment, same quarter, nominal/FX-inclusive basis — both ratios are measured on this identical basis, so combining them is valid):

```
Volume:  Ad impressions delivered +14% YoY (FoA, Q2 FY26 vs Q2 FY25) × prior-period ad revenue $46,563m
  = $6,519m modelled dollar contribution
  = 13.72pp of the 27.96pp total revenue growth observed
  → basis matches (same segment, same quarter, nominal) — applied cleanly

Price:  Average price per ad +12% YoY (FoA, Q2 FY26 vs Q2 FY25, nominal — INCLUDES FX and macro effects per management's own commentary) × prior-period ad revenue $46,563m
  = $5,588m modelled dollar contribution
  = 11.76pp of the 27.96pp total revenue growth observed
  → basis matches (same segment, same quarter) — applied cleanly, but this figure is NOT constant-currency; see the FX memo below before treating it as "pure" pricing power

Volume × Price interaction (mathematical cross-term, since (1+v)(1+p)-1 = v + p + vp):
  14% × 12% × prior-period ad revenue $46,563m = $783m
  = 1.65pp of the 27.96pp total revenue growth observed
  → this is an arithmetic necessity of combining two multiplicative growth rates, not a separate business driver
```

**Reconciliation:** Volume (13.72pp) + Price (11.76pp) + interaction (1.65pp) = 27.13pp modelled dollar effect on advertising revenue = $12,889m, against the advertising line's actual reported increase of $12,800m [Q2 2026 Form 10-Q, Note 2]. Residual = $12,800m − $12,889m = **−$89m, or −0.19pp of total revenue** — the modelled volume/price combination very slightly *overshoots* the actual advertising-revenue increase. This is consistent with the two headline growth rates (14%, 12%) being disclosed as rounded whole percentages rather than exact figures; the gap is immaterial (0.7% of the modelled ad-revenue increase) and is shown, not rounded away.

Full component build against the Section 6 Total: Volume 13.72pp + Price 11.76pp + interaction 1.65pp + residual −0.19pp (= Advertising's 26.94pp) + FoA Other 0.89pp + Reality Labs 0.13pp = **27.96pp reconciled, ~0pp unexplained** (the only unreconciled amount is the −0.19pp residual inside the volume/price ratio itself, already stated above).

**FX basis flag — refused as a separate additive line.** Meta's own constant-currency disclosure shows FX added $693m to advertising revenue and $685m to total revenue in Q2 FY26 [Q2 2026 Form 10-Q, MD&A p.42]. That $693m is **already inside** the Price line above (management's own words: the 12% price-per-ad increase was driven in part by "currency tailwinds" [Earnings Call transcript, Jul-29-2026]). Adding a further FX line to the table would double-count roughly $693m (≈1.46pp of total revenue) that the Price ratio was measured on. This report therefore treats FX as a memo item on the basis it was actually measured (a constant-currency comparison against as-reported revenue), not as a component to sum: on that basis, price-per-ad growth **excluding** the disclosed FX effect is approximately 11.76pp − 1.46pp ≈ **10.30pp** of total revenue, versus the as-reported 11.76pp. The gap between those two figures (≈1.46pp) is the part of "Price" that is currency, not ad-tech or demand improvement — a distinction the next quarter's guidance makes concrete, since management now assumes FX flips to an ~1% headwind for Q3 FY26 [Earnings Call transcript, Jul-29-2026].

**What this supports for §7:** Volume (13.72pp) explains just under half — 49.1% — of the 27.96pp total revenue growth on its own; Price (11.76pp) explains 42.1%. Both clear "roughly half" loosely, but Volume is the larger and the one whose contribution requires no FX caveat to interpret.

## 7. The Single Biggest Revenue Driver

**Ad impressions delivered (the volume side of the advertising formula)** is the single biggest driver of Meta's revenue over the next 3–12 months. It contributed 13.72pp of the 27.96pp Q2 FY26 revenue growth (49.1% on its own, per §6a) — the largest single modelled component — and its current direction is improving and accelerating: +14% YoY in Q2 FY26 and +16% YoY for H1 FY26, both faster than the +12% posted for full-year FY2025 and the +11% for FY2024 [Q2 2026 Form 10-Q, MD&A p.41; FY2025 Form 10-K, MD&A]. A 10–20% swing in impressions growth would move total revenue by a comparable order of magnitude given advertising is ~98% of consolidated revenue, making this the clearest High-magnitude lever in §4. Average price per ad is a close second (42.1% of the same growth) and cannot be dismissed — but its recent strength is partly currency (≈1.46pp of the 11.76pp, per §6a's FX memo) and it is the direct transmission channel for the one identified structural headwind in this report: management's own words that ad-targeting regulation (GDPR/evolving CJEU interpretation, DSA, DMA, and browser/OS data restrictions) "has adversely affected revenue" and is expected to have "further impact" [Q2 2026 Form 10-Q, MD&A pp.32–33]. Cycle read (Cycle-Position Rule): based on the data available in this pool (FY2021 onward), Meta's revenue growth has accelerated in every year since the FY2022 trough (−1.1% → +15.7% → +21.9% → +22.2%, then +26–33% on a quarterly YoY basis through H1 FY26) with no evidence yet of deceleration — this reads as an **extended upcycle, not (on present evidence) a peak or a trough**; the pool contains no pre-FY2021 baseline, so a true multi-year "record high" cannot be confirmed, only that the run has not yet turned. No one-time policy, tax, or subsidy tailwind is identified as inflating current-period *revenue* (the two one-off tax items disclosed for Q3 FY25 and Q1 FY26 hit EPS through the tax line only, not revenue or operating income — see `01_historical-financials.md` §4) — the growth reads as broad-based (both impressions and price up, all four geographic regions growing), not a base-effect artifact. Management's own Q3 FY26 guidance ($61bn–$64bn total revenue, implying +19.0% to +24.9% YoY against Q3 FY25's $51,242m, and assuming an ~1% FX headwind) points to a deceleration from Q2's +28% print, which is the first forward-looking signal in this data that the upcycle's pace, if not its direction, may be moderating [Earnings Call transcript, Jul-29-2026, CFO Susan Li prepared remarks].

## 8. The Single Biggest Margin Driver

**R&D cost growth is the single biggest margin driver**, and it is currently a headwind. ## 10. Cycle Position (external-dependency cross-check)

`10_external-dependency.md` flags "Consumer cycle" as a High dependency (advertiser budgets are the largest external lever on this business) [`10_external-dependency.md` §1]. The current period is **not** at a cyclical trough: advertising revenue YoY growth has accelerated for four consecutive quarters (Q3 FY24 +18.9% → Q4 FY24 +20.6% → Q1 FY25 +16.1% → Q2 FY25 +21.6% → Q3 FY25 +26.2% → Q4 FY25 +23.8% → Q1 FY26 +33.1% → Q2 FY26 +28.0%) [`01_historical-financials` §3]. The margin implication: Meta is compressing operating margin now, in a period of accelerating ad demand and a favourable macro comparison; if advertiser demand turns down from here (the stated High external dependency), the now-larger, largely fixed AI-infrastructure cost base (D&A, technical compensation) would amplify — not cushion — any resulting margin decline, because operating leverage cuts in both directions.

**Bar is high** — driven principally by the earnings/cost line, with revenue closer to fair-but-thinning.

Consensus already sits above management's own guidance on every quantifiable line in Section 3: revenue consensus is 1.3% above the Q3 2026 guidance midpoint (sitting ~77% up the $3bn range), EBIT consensus is 6.0% above the qualitative "above 2025" floor management gave, and capex consensus (1.5% above the guided midpoint) sits toward the top of a range management itself has raised twice in the last two quarters. Revenue-estimate revision breadth is genuinely positive (+21 net in the last month, Section 5) and does not, on its own, argue for a high bar — but it is outweighed by the GAAP-EPS revision breadth of −39 net (44 of 52 analysts cutting) and the accompanying wave of price-target cuts (45 of 58 analysts, zero raising), which reflect the market digesting a raised tax-rate guide (15–17%, up from 13–16%) and a raised-and-narrowed capex range ($130–145bn) that push cost and depreciation assumptions higher just as GAAP earnings absorbed a one-off charge in the print just reported. Section 1A's YoY math on the Q3 2026 bar itself makes the same point directly: consensus implies +23.6% revenue growth against roughly flat-to-down (−3.9%) normalized EPS growth for the same quarter a year ago — a bar that is comfortably beatable on the top line but demanding on the bottom line.

## 7. Historical Pattern

Meta has beaten consensus revenue in each of the last four quarters, but the beat magnitude has shrunk every single quarter — +3.71% (Q3 FY25) → +2.43% (Q4 FY25) → +1.36% (Q1 FY26) → +0.85% (Q2 FY26) — while the most recent quarter (Q2 FY26) posted an outright EPS miss on both the GAAP basis (−14.40%) and the Normalized basis (−16.49%) [`04_guidance-consensus.md` §6]. The single most important factor is that consensus already sits above management's own guidance on every quantifiable Q3/FY2026 line — revenue (+1.3% above the guidance midpoint), EBIT (+6.0% above the qualitative floor), and capex (+1.5% above the midpoint) — leaving no cushion, while the R&D-driven cost structure that took 838bps (69%) off Q2 FY26's operating margin is management's own description of a continuing, not one-off, investment [`04_guidance-consensus.md` §3, §7; `03_margin-drivers.md` §7a, §8]. The single biggest risk that could flip this verdict is a continuation of the ad-impression/price momentum seen in Q2 FY26 (+14%/+12% YoY) at a pace that outruns the guided deceleration enough to offset the margin drag at the headline level, as happened (on a shrinking scale) in each of the last four quarters.

## 9. Second-Quarter Look-Ahead

The quarter after next (Q4 FY26, filed ~Jan-2027) looks somewhat different: Q4 is Meta's seasonally strongest quarter, averaging 29.6% of annual revenue over the last three years on holiday advertising demand [`01_historical-financials.md` §5], which could provide a revenue tailwind independent of the margin story. However, FY2026 total-expense and capex guidance both run through year-end, and management's own tax-rate guidance (15–17%) is stated for "remaining FY2026 quarters" (Q3–Q4), so the structural cost and tax pressure identified above is expected to persist into Q4 rather than ease [`04_guidance-consensus.md` §2].

## 10. Pre-Mortem

If this setup fails, the most likely reason is that Meta's ad-impression and price-per-ad growth continued to reaccelerate (as it has in each of the last four quarters) fast enough to offset the R&D-driven margin compression at the headline level — i.e., the revenue side kept doing exactly what §2's beat scenarios describe, even as the miss scenarios in §3 also played out on the cost line, producing a "beat on revenue, in-line-to-soft on EPS" print that does not cleanly fit either a beat or a miss framing.

## 4. The Single Highest-Sensitivity Variable

**Advertiser budget / consumer-cycle shock** moves earnings the most, at an average absolute swing of roughly $17.4bn of TTM operating income (about 19.5% of the $89,327m base), well ahead of the next-largest variable (R&D cost growth, ~$6.8bn). Its current direction is favourable — ad revenue is growing +27–28% YoY (Q2 FY26) with no evidence of stress — and it is explicitly external and not company-controlled: substantially all of Meta's revenue is advertising, and marketers generally carry no long-term spending commitments [`10_external-dependency.md` §5; FY25 Form 10-K, Risk Factors — Advertising Revenue, pp.15–16]. For this variable to swing to the adverse case would require a broad, macro-driven pullback in global online-ad budgets — a recession, a rate shock, or a trade-policy shock hitting the direct-response and SME advertiser base — consistent with the company's own disclosure that "inflation, high rates, economic policy and trade uncertainty have pressured budgets at times" [`10_external-dependency.md` §1, Consumer cycle row].

## 5. Interaction Effects

Several of these six variables are not independent. First, ad impressions and price-per-ad move together mechanically: `02_revenue-drivers.md` §6a quantifies a 1.65pp multiplicative cross-term between the two in Q2 FY26 alone, so a bull or bear case that moves one without the other understates or overstates the compound effect. Second, R&D/AI-infrastructure cost growth and cost-of-revenue growth share a common root cause — the AI compute build-out (data-centre capacity, third-party cloud, technical infrastructure, third-party AI token costs) — so a supply-side compute-cost shock (e.g., a power or GPU-price spike) would likely move both cost lines adversely at the same time, not independently; summing their two bear cases as if uncorrelated would understate how a single shock could hit both. Third, the advertiser-budget shock and FX have historically correlated: macro downturns that curb marketer spending often coincide with USD strength (flight-to-quality flows), which would turn a currently favourable FX tailwind into a headwind at the same time as ad demand weakens — a compounding "double hit" this table's separate rows do not capture. Regulatory risk (ad-data-signal access) is comparatively independent of the other five — it runs on its own policy timeline, not a macro or commodity cycle.

## 6. Non-Linear Or Asymmetric Risks

**Operating deleverage on the downside.** R&D/AI-infrastructure costs are now large (35.62% of revenue, Q2 FY26) and increasingly locked in — FY2026 total-expense guidance ($165bn–$169bn) has been RAISED, not cut, at every quarterly checkpoint this year [`03_margin-drivers.md` §9]. If advertiser demand decelerates from here, this cost base cannot fall proportionally in the same period, so a moderate revenue slowdown would translate into a disproportionately larger margin decline — `03_margin-drivers.md` §10 makes this point explicitly: "the now-larger, largely fixed AI-infrastructure cost base... would amplify — not cushion — any resulting margin decline, because operating leverage cuts in both directions."

**FX has no natural hedge on the cost side.** Revenue-side FX moves pass through to EBIT with no disclosed offsetting FX cost effect (`03_margin-drivers.md` §7 FX row: "not applicable on the cost side... no FX cost-line effect is disclosed"). This is not a typical multinational manufacturer's natural hedge (foreign costs offsetting foreign revenue); a downside FX move hits EBIT largely undiluted.

**Regulatory risk is a step function, not a smooth slope.** The DMA gatekeeper designation has already changed discretely once (Facebook Marketplace undesignated, April 2025, per `10_external-dependency.md` §1A) rather than eroding gradually. A further binary ruling — e.g., an adverse outcome on Meta's EU consent-model engagement — could remove monetisable ad-data signal in a single step, a risk this table's continuous bull/bear framing does not represent well.

**Sunk-cost asymmetry in the AI build-out.** Heavy capex is already landing in current-period D&A and cost of revenue, not deferred to a future period (`03_margin-drivers.md` §9: PP&E depreciation +40.2% YoY, Q2 FY26). If the advertiser-demand bull case does not materialise, this spend cannot be un-spent — the downside case inherits a cost base sized for a demand level that may not arrive, while the upside case (demand keeps accelerating) simply uses capacity already committed.

## 7. Earnings Volatility Score

**65 / 100 — INVERTED: higher = WORSE (more volatile / sensitive to small input changes).**

Reason: two variables carry EBIT swings above $5bn on an $89,327m TTM operating-income base (advertiser-budget shock ≈$17.4bn, ≈19.5% of the base; R&D cost growth ≈$6.8bn, ≈7.7% of the base), the single biggest margin driver (R&D/AI infrastructure) is currently moving in the adverse direction with guidance raised rather than cut, and the largest revenue driver's downside case is a macro variable entirely outside company control. This places META above the "material sensitivity" band (41–60) but short of "extremely volatile" (81–100), because the core advertising engine itself is not currently showing stress — the volatility risk is concentrated in the interaction between an unusually large, semi-fixed AI cost base and a demand variable the company does not control, rather than in the base business being erratic today.

## 5. Red-Flag Severity Verdict

**Material concerns** — high-severity flags present; earnings setup may be overstated or fragile.

No critical flags (no accounting fraud, going-concern, or governance disqualifier) were found, and data completeness is strong (00's own sufficiency verdict was "Sufficient," with no partial-data caps binding). But eight High-severity flags cluster around one story: revenue is genuinely accelerating, while operating margin is compressing at an accelerating rate (-1,214bps in the latest quarter) on a cost base (R&D, 70% unquantified) management has guided to keep growing, consensus already sits above guidance with no cushion, and the single biggest earnings sensitivity (advertiser-budget shock, ~$17.4bn) is entirely external and would be amplified — not cushioned — by that same fixed cost base if it turned adverse. The single most dangerous red flag is the R&D margin residual (§2.4, Row 1): 70% of the biggest driver of the entire earnings setup cannot be broken into its named components (technical hiring, severance, infrastructure, AI token costs), so neither the bull case (margin stabilises as severance rolls off) nor the bear case (infrastructure/token costs prove stickier and demand slows) can be confirmed from what Meta discloses. This would be resolved by two things: Meta quantifying the four named R&D sub-drivers individually in a future filing, or two further quarters of data showing whether margin re-stabilises as ad-impression/price growth meets or beats the guided deceleration.

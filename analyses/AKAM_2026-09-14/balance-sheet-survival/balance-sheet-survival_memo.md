## What This Module Found

Strict net leverage—debt minus cash divided by EBITDA, or operating profit before interest, tax, depreciation and amortization—rose from **2.49x at FY2025 to 5.14x at Q2 FY2026**; gross leverage—debt before cash divided by EBITDA—was **6.39x**. [FY2025 Form 10-K, pp.53–56; Q2 FY2026 Form 10-Q, pp.3–5] The later known-component LayerX pro forma—reported figures adjusted only for the disclosed transaction—raises strict net leverage to **5.31x** by adding the **$205.0m** cash purchase price, without assuming undisclosed target debt, cash, or EBITDA. [Q2 FY2026 Form 10-Q, Note 6]

The scheduled 24-month maturity wall—the debt due in that period—is **$1,150.0m**, or **15.1%** of principal, due 1 September 2027. Cash alone covers it, while cash plus current marketable securities cover it **2.92x**. [Q2 FY2026 Form 10-Q, p.3; Note 7, pp.17–20] Reported committed liquidity—cash, saleable current securities, and an available committed credit line—is **$4,355.4m = $1,480.3m cash + $1,875.1m current marketable securities + $1,000.0m undrawn revolver**. That produces a mechanical **100.5-month** liquidity runway, but it assumes **$629.9m** of last-twelve-month free cash flow—cash from operations minus total capital spending—continues and does not capture an undisclosed peak working-capital need. [Q2 FY2026 Form 10-Q, pp.3, 7–8, 19–20]

The main near-term cash risk is separate from the scheduled wall: holders of **$1,725.0m** of 2033 Notes had a conversion right that could pull payment forward, and the post-filing exercise status is not proven. [Q2 FY2026 Form 10-Q, Note 7, pp.17–20] Interest service is covered **35.73x** by EBITDA, but fixed-charge coverage—cash generation compared with interest, capital spending, leases, and debt repayment—was **0.23x** including a past **$1,150.0m** note repayment, or **0.85x** excluding that one-time debt repayment. This means low coupon cost is not the same as internally funding every fixed cash use. [FY2025 Form 10-K, pp.54–56; Q2 FY2026 Form 10-Q, pp.5, 7–8]

In the zero-mitigation bound, modeled cash survives EBITDA declines of **30%, 40%, and 60%**, and is not exhausted at a **100%** decline on known components. At a **60%** decline, the 12-month committed-liquidity surplus is **$3,097.4m**, but strict net leverage reaches **13.28x**. The first modeled warning is strict net leverage above the analyst’s **6.0x** refinancing threshold after an **11.5%** EBITDA decline; **6.0x is not a disclosed covenant**. [Q2 FY2026 Form 10-Q, Notes 6–7; downside-stress calculation from the stated filing inputs] The biggest risk is therefore not modeled cash exhaustion but an undisclosed lender test failing first, particularly if EBITDA falls while the conversion right is exercised. [Q2 FY2026 Form 10-Q, Note 7, pp.17–20]

## Plain-English Glossary

- **Strict net leverage:** Debt minus cash, divided by EBITDA.
- **EBITDA:** Operating profit before interest, tax, depreciation, and amortization.
- **Gross leverage:** Total debt divided by EBITDA, before subtracting cash.
- **Pro forma:** A reported figure adjusted for a known later transaction.
- **Maturity wall:** Debt principal due within a stated period.
- **Marketable securities:** Investments that are expected to be saleable and available as liquidity.
- **Committed liquidity:** Cash, current saleable securities, and contractually available borrowing capacity.
- **Revolver:** A reusable bank credit line.
- **Free cash flow:** Cash from operations minus total capital spending.
- **Fixed-charge coverage:** Cash earnings compared with fixed uses such as interest, capital spending, leases, and debt repayment.
- **Covenant headroom:** The distance between the company’s current lender-test result and the level that would breach the loan terms.
- **Refinancing threshold:** The analyst’s warning level for difficulty replacing debt; it is not a lender covenant.

## What the Company Does

Akamai sells cloud security, cloud infrastructure, and internet-delivery services. It uses a global network of servers and data centers to protect customers' applications, run computing workloads closer to users, and move online content.

The simplest revenue formula is:

**Revenue = Security + Cloud Infrastructure Services (CIS) + Delivery and other cloud applications.**

Customers pay through contracted and usage-based arrangements. Security was 55% of Q2 revenue and grew 10% to $604.436m. CIS grew 39% to $99.319m. Delivery and other cloud applications fell 6%. The company reports as one segment, however, and does not disclose category-level profit, capital employed, retention, or cash conversion. That means category revenue growth cannot by itself prove that the mix shift creates value. [Q2 FY2026 Form 10-Q, pp.23, 30; business-model/99_business-model-synthesis.md, §§1–3]

## The Variant Perception

### What everyone already knows

Security and CIS are growing while legacy Delivery is shrinking. In Q2 FY2026, total revenue grew 5.4%; Security grew 10%, CIS grew 39%, and Delivery and other cloud applications fell 6%. Management has also described more than $2.8bn of signed 2026 multi-year CIS commitments, with larger revenue conversion expected in Q4 2026 and Q1 2027. Capital IQ's recommendation is Outperform, and 23 target-price estimates average $153.31 with a $155 median. Those targets show consensus optimism, but they are opinions rather than valuation proof. [Q2 FY2026 Form 10-Q, p.30; Q2 FY2026 earnings call, prepared remarks and Q&A; Capital IQ Estimates Report, Consensus sheet, data as of 2026-09-14]

### What is probably priced in

The $106.79 share price can be explained in either of two non-exclusive ways. At the model's 6.22% weighted-average cost of capital (WACC, the return demanded by lenders and shareholders), strict free cash flow must grow 16.35% a year from $629.9m to about $1.245bn by FY2030. Alternatively, the DCF cash path supports the current price if the market keeps the required return near 5.05%. [valuation/05_reverse-dcf.md, §§2–5]

### What the engine thinks the market may be missing

The shift in revenue mix has not yet produced better reported economics. Q2 revenue rose 5.4%, but GAAP-derived EBITDA fell 18.7%, GAAP EPS fell 26.8%, and gross margin fell 331 basis points. Security and CIS profit, capital needs, and cash conversion remain undisclosed. Strict net leverage is 5.14x. The company also spent $2.066bn on acquisitions in FY2021–FY2025, plus $205m for LayerX, without a filed deal-level return scorecard. [Q2 FY2026 Form 10-Q, pp.5, 30, 32–34; management-governance/99_management-governance-synthesis.md, §§3–6]

The claim is deliberately narrower than “the moat is gone.” Gross margin fell from 63.3% in FY2021 to 57.5% on the latest trailing basis, EBIT margin fell from 23.3% to 11.9%, and vendor ROIC fell from 7.0% to 2.7%. But cash from operations divided by GAAP-derived EBITDA improved from 117.7% to about 134%. That contrary cash-conversion series stops the memo from calling deterioration confirmed across every measure. [business-model/99_business-model-synthesis.md, §§1–3]

### What would prove the edge is real

By Q2 FY2027, trailing strict free cash flow should be moving through roughly $733m on the same basis: cash from operations minus cash purchases of property and equipment and capitalized internal-use software. That is the first one-year step from $629.9m along the price-implied 16.35% growth path. A result below $733m with no improvement in like-for-like GAAP margins supports the negative expectations gap. A result at or above $733m, with improving margins and lower strict leverage, weakens or disproves it. This is a judgment test, not a measured base rate. [valuation/05_reverse-dcf.md, §§2–5; earnings/01_historical-financials.md]

**Edge score: 45/100.** The expectations gap is measurable and falsifiable, but it is not strong enough for high conviction because the valuation rests on one terminal-heavy model and the true upside case needs both cash growth and a lower required return.

## Why It Could Work — Bull Case

1. **Security and CIS can replace Delivery.** Security added 5.03 percentage points to Q2 revenue growth and CIS added 2.67 points, while Delivery subtracted 2.32 points. Security reached $604.436m and CIS $99.319m in the quarter. If those categories fill existing capacity and carry better economics, the current group-margin pressure could reverse. The missing category profit and capital data mean that last step is still unproven. [earnings/99_earnings-synthesis.md, §§2–3; Q2 FY2026 Form 10-Q, pp.23, 30]

2. **Signed CIS business may create a delayed revenue step.** Management reported more than $2.8bn of signed 2026 multi-year CIS commitments and described two large deployments expected to contribute roughly $15m and $20m in Q4, followed by faster conversion in Q1 FY2027. If that revenue arrives without another margin setback or cash-capex overrun, the latest cost build may prove to be a timing issue. These amounts remain management expectations, not reported results or matched-period thresholds. [Q2 FY2026 earnings call, prepared remarks and CFO Q&A; Goldman Sachs Communacopia + Technology Conference, 2026-09-09, CFO discussion]

3. **A low required return can support today's price.** The reverse-DCF solves to a 5.0466% market-implied WACC on the explicit DCF cash path, 1.17 percentage points below the model's 6.22% WACC. If cash conversion improves and investors keep requiring roughly that lower return, the explicit good-outcome case reaches $139.68, or 30.80% above the current price. That case has a 10% judgment probability because both conditions must occur together. [valuation/05_reverse-dcf.md, §2A; final_thesis.md, §8]

4. **Liquidity reduces near-term ruin risk.** Committed liquidity is $4.355bn: $1.480bn of cash, $1.875bn of current securities, and a $1.000bn undrawn revolving facility. Cash plus current securities cover the $1.150bn September 2027 maturity 2.92x. This supports the Adequate survival verdict, although it does not make leverage low or establish covenant headroom. [balance-sheet-survival/99_balance-sheet-survival-synthesis.md, §§1–5]

The best bull argument is therefore simple: Akamai may be paying for capacity one or two quarters before contracted CIS revenue arrives, while Security keeps growing. If that revenue becomes cash, margins stabilize, and the market's required return stays near 5%, the $68.78 base DCF can be too low.

## Why It Could Fail — Bear Case & the Killer Risk

1. **Reported profit is already falling.** Q2 revenue rose 5.4%, yet GAAP-derived EBITDA fell 18.7%, GAAP EPS fell 26.8%, gross margin fell 331 basis points, and GAAP EBIT margin fell 721 basis points. The revenue and margin bridges each have zero residual, so the core problem is not unexplained arithmetic: costs grew faster than revenue. [Q2 FY2026 Form 10-Q, pp.5, 30, 32–34; earnings/99_earnings-synthesis.md, §§2–3]

2. **The mix story lacks solution economics.** Delivery remains under pricing pressure, while capacity, co-location, network, payroll, and stock-compensation costs can arrive before CIS revenue. Without category profit, deployed capital, retention, and cash-conversion disclosure, 10% Security growth and 39% CIS growth cannot show whether returns exceed the cost of capital. [business-model/99_business-model-synthesis.md, §§1–3; earnings/99_earnings-synthesis.md, §§2–3]

3. **Leverage limits room for error.** Strict net debt is $6.083bn, or 5.14x GAAP-derived trailing EBITDA, up from 2.49x at FY2025. An analyst-only 6.0x warning level is crossed after an 11.5% EBITDA decline on the known LayerX basis. The company reports covenant compliance, but the executed credit agreement and the numerical covenant formula are absent, so actual headroom is Not assessable. A separate $1.725bn conversion right on the 2033 notes is a cash contingency, not a scheduled maturity. [balance-sheet-survival/99_balance-sheet-survival-synthesis.md, §§1–5]

4. **Capital allocation and reporting raise High flags.** FY2021–FY2025 cash acquisitions total $2.066bn, plus $205m for LayerX, with no deal-level return evidence. FY2025 stock-based compensation of $459.4m exceeded GAAP net income of $452.0m. Known gross lease-related commitments total $2.331bn, built from $1.8659bn of scheduled payments, $278.0m of uncommenced co-location leases, and a $187.1m final data-center commitment. These are not proof of fraud, but the distinct acquisition, adjusted-reporting, capitalization, and obligation signals cap any long and make the downside less forgiving. [management-governance/99_management-governance-synthesis.md, §§3–6; balance-sheet-survival/05_off-balance-sheet-and-contingencies.md, §§1–5]

**Killer risk to the Avoid:** the price-implied 16.35% free-cash-flow path and the 5.0466% market-implied WACC occur together; that conjunction produces $139.68 per share and would make the Avoid materially wrong.

## Plain-English Glossary

- **Long position** — owning the shares because you expect their value to rise.
- **Short position** — borrowing and selling shares in the hope of buying them back more cheaply; losses can grow if the price rises.
- **Expected return** — the probability-weighted gain or loss across the modeled outcomes.
- **Downside risk** — the modeled loss from the current price to the selected bear case.
- **Risk/reward** — expected gain or loss compared with the amount at risk to the bear case.
- **Free cash flow (FCF)** — cash from operations after total cash capital expenditure; this memo uses cash from operations minus property/equipment and capitalized-software spending.
- **Weighted-average cost of capital (WACC)** — the return demanded by the company's lenders and shareholders; it is the discount rate used here.
- **Discounted-cash-flow model (DCF)** — a valuation that converts expected future cash into a value today.
- **Reverse-DCF** — a DCF solved backward to show what cash growth or discount rate the current share price requires.
- **GAAP** — the U.S. accounting rules used in Akamai's filed financial statements.
- **EBITDA** — earnings before interest, tax, depreciation, and amortization; a rough operating-profit measure before financing and non-cash asset charges.
- **EPS** — earnings per diluted share, or profit allocated to each share after allowing for potential dilution.
- **Gross margin** — revenue left after the direct cost of providing services, shown as a percentage of revenue.
- **Basis point** — one-hundredth of a percentage point; 100 basis points equal one percentage point.
- **Net leverage** — debt minus cash, divided by EBITDA; “strict” here means marketable securities are not subtracted from debt.
- **EBIT** — earnings before interest and tax, after depreciation and amortization.
- **ROIC** — return on invested capital, or the operating profit earned on the money invested in the business.
- **Liquidity** — cash, readily usable securities, and committed borrowing capacity available to meet obligations.
- **Covenant headroom** — the gap between a lender's financial limit and the company's current tested level.
- **Non-GAAP or normalized EPS** — company- or vendor-defined earnings per share after selected exclusions; it is not interchangeable with filed GAAP EPS.
- **Stock-based compensation** — pay delivered in shares or share-linked awards, which is an economic cost and can dilute owners.
- **Fair value** — the estimated value from a stated method and assumptions, not a guaranteed trading price.
- **Margin of safety** — the cushion between estimated value and the price paid; a negative figure means price is above estimated value.
- **Free cash flow to the firm (FCFF)** — cash available to both debt and equity investors before financing claims.
- **Enterprise value** — the value of the operating business attributable to debt and equity holders before subtracting net debt to reach equity value.
- **Diluted shares** — the share count after allowing for options and other awards that could become shares.
- **Terminal value** — the model's estimate of all cash flows after the explicit forecast period.
- **Capital expenditure** — cash spent on long-lived operating assets and capitalized software.
- **Bull, base, and bear cases** — respectively the favorable, central, and adverse modeled outcomes.
- **Refinancing** — replacing a debt maturity with new funding rather than paying it solely from cash.
- **LTM and NTM** — last twelve months and next twelve months, used to keep historical and forward peer measures on comparable periods.

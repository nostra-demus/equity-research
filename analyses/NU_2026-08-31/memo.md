## What the Company Does

Nu is a digital retail bank and lender for consumers and small businesses in Brazil, Mexico, and Colombia. It offers cards, loans, accounts, cash-management products, and investments through one IFRS reportable segment. IFRS is the accounting standard used in its filed financial statements.

The simplest revenue formula is: **customer scale × product use × interest, credit, investment and fee income**. Interest income and gains on financial instruments supplied 85.2% of FY2025 revenue, so this is mainly a lending and financial-spread business, not a software-subscription business. [data/NU/Filings/Nu_Holdings_Ltd_-_Form_20-F(Apr-08-2026).pdf, FY2025, Item 5, pp.157–158].

The operating attraction is reach and use. Nu reported 139 million customers, 83.5% activity, and US$17 of monthly ARPAC—average revenue per active customer—in Q2 2026. [data/NU/Filings/Nu_Holdings_Ltd_-_Form_Preliminary_Interim_Report(Aug-13-2026).pdf, Q2 2026, slide 6]. The limitation is that country- and product-level profit is not disclosed. The available US$17.1 ARPAC less US$1 cost to serve leaves US$16.1 before funding cost, expected credit loss, customer-acquisition cost, and required capital. It is therefore not a complete measure of customer profit.

## The Variant Perception

### What everyone already knows

Nu is a fast-growing Latin American digital bank. Q2 2026 revenue grew 50.3%, diluted EPS—earnings per share after potential dilution—grew 66.3%, and the company reported 139 million customers with 83.5% activity. [data/NU/Filings/Nu_Holdings_Ltd_-_Form_Preliminary_Interim_Report(Aug-13-2026).pdf, Q2 2026, slides 6 and 15–16]; [data/NU/Filings/Nu_Holdings_Ltd_-_Form_Interim_Report(Aug-14-2026).pdf, Q2/H1 2026, p.5]. FY2025 ROE—return on equity, or profit earned on shareholders' capital—was 30.3%. The sell side remains constructive: its mean target was US$18.78, with a US$10–US$23 range. [data/NU/NuHoldingsLtdNYSENUEstimatesReport.xls, Capital IQ Estimates, Target Price, export 2026-08-29].

### What is probably priced in

The US$14.30 share price already gives Nu credit for high growth and returns. At a 16.51% cost of equity—the return shareholders require—the reverse-DCF, which works backward from the share price to the assumptions it needs, requires either 47.36% terminal ROE or 80.89% FY2027–30 profit growth. Yet the same earnings path reconciles to the market price at a 9.78% cost of equity. [analyses/NU_2026-08-31/valuation/99_valuation-synthesis.md, §§1–7, 2026-08-31]. This rate choice is the central valuation dispute.

### What the engine thinks the market may be missing

Current spread and revenue gains may arrive before the final credit losses are visible. Of the 294 basis-point sequential rise in Q2 risk-adjusted NIM—the lending spread after credit costs—178bp came from credit income and 115bp from lower credit cost, with 5bp from float, minus 5bp from funding, and a 1bp residual. The move was real and fully reconciled. But the implied credit-cost rate was 10.5%, against 8.9% a year earlier, so the evidence does not prove that the improvement will last. [data/NU/Filings/Nu_Holdings_Ltd_-_Form_Preliminary_Interim_Report(Aug-13-2026).pdf, Q2 2026, slides 15–16 and 22].

H1 net income also included a US$991.046 million deferred-tax benefit, equal to 51.3% of H1 net income. That makes the period less backed by current cash than the headline profit suggests. It does **not** prove improper accounting. [data/NU/Filings/Nu_Holdings_Ltd_-_Form_Interim_Report(Aug-14-2026).pdf, H1 2026, pp.5, 12 and 36–37].

### What would prove the edge is real

The skeptical edge is proven only if a sourced group-wide, post-tax USD cost of equity is materially above 9.78% **and** Q3 misses its raised revenue/EPS bar or shows worse credit quality. It is falsified by a defensible group rate at or below 9.78% and a material Q3 dual beat with stable or better credit metrics.

**Edge score: 45/100. There is no proven variant perception yet.** The filing evidence supports caution on earnings quality, but the central group discount rate is unresolved and the sell-side target evidence points the other way.

## Why It Could Work — Bull Case

The strongest case against the Avoid has three parts.

1. **The correct group cost of equity may be much lower than the engine's 16.51% input.** That 16.51% figure belongs to a Brazil Investments cash-generating unit, not to Nu Group. A 9.78% market-implied group rate reconciles the same modeled earnings path to US$14.30. A sourced group rate at or below 9.78% would show that much of the residual-income downside came from the wrong rate, not from weak operations. [analyses/NU_2026-08-31/valuation/99_valuation-synthesis.md, §§1–7, 2026-08-31].

2. **Growth and returns may justify a premium that mature-bank peers cannot capture.** FY2025 ROE was 30.3%. Diluted EPS grew at a 66.0% compound annual rate from FY2023 to FY2025, albeit from a low base. Vendor-basis last-twelve-month revenue growth was 44.33%, against a 6.63% direct-peer median. A material Q3 dual beat with stable or better delinquency, credit cost, and loss coverage would support this argument. [analyses/NU_2026-08-31/valuation/99_valuation-synthesis.md, §§3–6, 2026-08-31].

3. **Scale can support more use per customer.** A base of 139 million customers, 83.5% activity, and US$17 monthly ARPAC gives Nu room to sell more products through the same digital relationship. [data/NU/Filings/Nu_Holdings_Ltd_-_Form_Preliminary_Interim_Report(Aug-13-2026).pdf, Q2 2026, slide 6]. The proof still missing is country- and product-level customer profit after funding, credit losses, acquisition cost, and capital.

The scenario model gives this positive reconciliation path a 10% judgment weight at US$18.78, a 31.328671% return. That is not the central case, but it is a real outcome that prevents the Avoid from becoming a short call.

## Why It Could Fail — Bear Case & the Killer Risk

The Avoid rests on five main risks to a long position.

1. **Price exceeds every engine fair-value case.** The 12-month bull/base/bear values are US$8.43, US$6.47, and US$4.40. Even the bull value is 41.048951% below US$14.30. [analyses/NU_2026-08-31/valuation/99_valuation-synthesis.md, §§1–7, 2026-08-31].

2. **The Q3 bar is already high.** Frozen Q3 consensus is US$5.93674 billion of revenue and US$0.22164 of GAAP EPS, implying 42.3% and 39.0% growth from standalone Q3 2025. Revenue estimates rose 7.0% over 90 days. The four-quarter surprise history is too small to produce a measured frequency, so the 55% miss probabilities for revenue and EPS are judgment. [data/NU/NuHoldingsLtdNYSENUEstimatesReport.xls, Capital IQ Estimates, Consensus/Surprise/Trends/Revisions, export 2026-08-29].

3. **Credit gains may not be durable.** Credit income supplied 60.5% of the Q2 risk-adjusted-NIM increase. The implied credit-cost rate nevertheless rose to 10.5% from 8.9% a year earlier. Intentional risk expansion added 24bp to early delinquency and US$170 million to allowance. A weaker credit cycle can reduce income, tangible book, and the justified valuation multiple while also raising the required return. [analyses/NU_2026-08-31/earnings/99_earnings-synthesis.md, §§3–8, 2026-08-30].

4. **Headline profit is not fully backed by current cash.** H1 cash flow from operations was negative US$1.242 billion, while the US$991.046 million deferred-tax benefit was 51.3% of net income. Filing-built last-twelve-month cash flow from operations was negative US$1.382 billion, versus negative US$10.305 billion from Capital IQ; the US$8.923 billion definition gap is unresolved. Bank cash flow includes lending, securities, and deposit movements, so this is a lender-specific warning, not an industrial cash-conversion test or proof of false profit. [data/NU/Filings/Nu_Holdings_Ltd_-_Form_Interim_Report(Aug-14-2026).pdf, H1 2026, p.12]; [data/NU/Filings/Nu_Holdings_Ltd_-_Form_20-F(Apr-08-2026).pdf, FY2025, pp.F-14–F-15]; [data/NU/ciq_facts.json, ltm_ocf_m, read 2026-08-30].

5. **Important evidence is absent.** There is no completed bank-specific review of regulatory capital, liquidity, asset quality, and provisioning. Peer-call coverage was 0%. Founder David Vélez held 18.6% of the economics but 74.4% of the votes while serving as CEO and chair. That proves entrenched control, not misconduct. [data/NU/Filings/Nu_Holdings_Ltd_-_Form_20-F(Apr-08-2026).pdf, FY2025, Items 6A, 7A and 10B].

**Killer risk to the Avoid:** the modeled group discount rate is wrong—16.51% is a Brazil Investments unit rate, while 9.78% reconciles the same earnings path to the market price.

## Avoid-Big-Risks Check

**No §24 Avoid-Big-Risks filter was proven to have tripped.** There was no proven fraud or integrity failure, turnaround, serial-acquirer pattern, fast-changing-industry cap, or proven value diversion by the controller. Founder control remains a shareholder-rights risk, but the evidence does not prove an unaligned-owner filter trip.

The high-debt/survival filter is **unresolved, not tripped**. A mechanical strict net-cash build is US$7,744.6 million: US$13,551.6 million cash minus US$4,682.3 million borrowings, US$1,058.3 million repos, and US$66.4 million leases. This is not a bank-survival verdict because deposits, regulatory restrictions, and liquidity requirements are not reconciled. The corporate debt/earnings framework was invalid for this lender, so the module issued no Distress, Stretched, or Fortress verdict. Missing bank prudential evidence creates a 65 conviction ceiling. [data/NU/ciq_facts.json, debt_maturity_wall, read 2026-08-30].

Three recorded red flags remain and must not be softened:

- **High, earnings:** five findings converge on one credit-cycle risk—spread durability, the raised Q3 bar, lender-specific cash/accrual divergence, the US$991.046 million deferred-tax benefit, and a US$504.700 million downside-macro allowance bound. These are not proof of misstatement.
- **High, non-gate audit-firm risk:** KPMG Brazil was censured and fined US$700,000 by the PCAOB for another issuer. Nu was not named; its FY2023–FY2025 audit opinions were clean and FY2025 internal control was effective. [PCAOB Release 105-2025-008, 2025-03-11]; [data/NU/Filings/Nu_Holdings_Ltd_-_Form_20-F(Apr-08-2026).pdf, FY2025, Auditor Report and Item 15].
- **Medium, non-gate disclosure risk:** recurring share-based compensation is excluded from adjusted net income. The FY2025 US$359.0 million addback produced a US$203.7 million after-tax, or 7.1%, uplift. This is a candor concern, not evidence of misstatement. [analyses/NU_2026-08-31/management-governance/99_management-governance-synthesis.md, §§1–6, 2026-08-31].

## Valuation & Fair Value

The 12-month engine range is **US$4.40 bear, US$6.47 base, and US$8.43 bull**, against US$14.30. Forward P/TBV—price paid for each dollar of forecast tangible equity—sets these three levels. The US$6.47 base equally blends a US$7.38 peer next-twelve-month P/E value and a US$5.55 residual-income value. Residual income values the profit left after charging shareholders' required return. [analyses/NU_2026-08-31/valuation/99_valuation-synthesis.md, §§1–7, 2026-08-31].

There is no margin of safety—a price cushion below estimated value. On the thesis's base-value formula, margin of safety is **-121.0201%**. The separate loss from US$14.30 to the US$4.40 bear value is **69.2308%**. The base value implies a **-54.755245%** return, while the bull value still implies **-41.048951%**.

The methods disagree materially. The two weighted primary methods are 33% apart, and the full US$2.95–US$7.92 valuation field spans 168%. They are not independent confirmations because the peer methods share a bank peer set. The short 17-month own-history series gets no weight because seven observations do not establish a cycle-neutral normal value.

The reverse-DCF is the key disconfirmation test. At 16.51%, the price requires 47.36% terminal ROE or 80.89% FY2027–30 profit growth. But that rate is not group-wide. A 9.78% group-implied rate reconciles the same earnings path to the current price. The valuation gap therefore supports Avoid, while the rate gap caps confidence.

## What Would Change Our Mind

These are the five observable events that would make the Avoid wrong or require a fresh decision:

1. **A defensible NU Group cost of equity is 9.78% or lower** on a matched group-wide, post-tax USD basis. No such group-wide rate was proven in FY2025 or H1 2026.
2. **Q3 2026 revenue exceeds US$6.11484bn and GAAP EPS exceeds US$0.23272**, while early delinquency does not worsen year over year. The comparable is standalone Q3 2025 revenue of US$4.173bn and EPS of US$0.1595 on the same company-defined delinquency basis. There is no reported stub. This exact conjunction did not pass in Q1 or Q2 2026.
3. **Two consecutive standalone quarters materially beat both frozen revenue and EPS consensus**, while delinquency, credit cost, and ECL—expected credit loss—coverage remain stable or improve year over year. The prior Q1/Q2 pair did not pass.
4. **A matched direct-peer set justifies at least 3.90x FY2027 P/TBV** on comparable forward ROE, growth, and credit risk. Current peer work supports no more than 2.30x.
5. **NU closes at or below US$6.47** while the 12-month base value remains US$6.47 and bank prudential evidence has not worsened. That would require a new valuation; it is not an automatic buy.

The open forecast ledger expects both Q3 revenue and GAAP EPS to fall below their frozen bars with 55% judgment probability, settling from the Q3 filing between 5 and 20 November 2026. Revenue at or above US$5.93674bn or EPS at or above US$0.22164 falsifies the respective forecast.

## Bottom Line

- Nu is a digital bank and lender whose revenue mainly comes from interest and financial instruments; Q2 scale and growth were real.
- The stock may rise if Nu deserves a group cost of equity near 9.78% and credit performance remains durable; the model includes a US$18.78 positive tail.
- It may fall because US$14.30 is above the US$8.43 engine bull value and Q3 already requires 42.3% revenue and 39.0% EPS growth.
- Filing evidence supports caution: 60.5% of the Q2 risk-adjusted-NIM increase came from credit income, and a US$991.046 million deferred-tax benefit equaled 51.3% of H1 net income. Neither proves improper accounting.
- Bank-specific survival evidence, matched peer calls, complete customer economics, and the cash-flow reconciliation are missing.
- **Action: wait; no long and no short.** Avoid does not authorize a short without borrow and options evidence.
- Watch next: the group-wide cost of equity, then the vendor-scheduled Q3 result on 12 November 2026, whose date is not issuer-confirmed.

## Plain-English Glossary

- **Fair value** — a model-based estimate of what a share may be worth; it is a range, not a known fact.
- **Expected return** — the probability-weighted gain or loss across the stated scenarios.
- **Downside risk** — the modeled loss from the current price to the bear-case value.
- **Risk/reward** — the expected gain or loss compared with the modeled loss to the bear case.
- **Cost of equity / required return / discount rate** — the annual return shareholders require for taking the risk of owning the shares.
- **Cash-generating unit** — a business or asset group whose value is tested together for accounting purposes.
- **Sell side / consensus** — analysts who publish company forecasts; consensus is the combined estimate from those analysts.
- **IFRS** — the accounting rules Nu uses in its filed financial statements.
- **ARPAC** — average revenue per active customer.
- **Diluted EPS** — profit per share after allowing for shares that options or awards could create.
- **ROE** — return on equity: profit earned on shareholders' capital.
- **Compound annual rate** — the steady yearly rate that links a starting value to an ending value over several years.
- **Basis point (bp)** — one hundredth of one percentage point; 100bp equals one percentage point.
- **NIM** — net interest margin: the spread a lender earns between financial income and its funding and credit costs, on the company's stated basis.
- **Credit cost / allowance** — the period's charge for expected loan losses / the balance reserved for those expected losses.
- **GAAP EPS** — earnings per share under the applicable accounting rules, without a management-defined adjusted-profit substitution.
- **Deferred-tax benefit** — accounting income created by expected future tax effects; it is not the same as current-period cash received.
- **Cash flow from operations** — cash moving through operating activities; for a bank it includes lending, securities, and deposit flows and is not directly comparable with an industrial company's measure.
- **Tangible book / P/TBV** — tangible book is shareholder equity excluding intangible assets; P/TBV is the share price paid for each dollar of that equity.
- **P/E** — share price divided by earnings per share.
- **Residual income** — profit left after charging the business for shareholders' required return.
- **Reverse-DCF** — a model that starts with the market price and works backward to show the growth, returns, or discount rate needed to justify it.
- **Margin of safety** — the price cushion below estimated fair value; a negative figure means the market price is above that value.
- **ECL coverage** — allowance held for expected credit losses relative to the relevant loan exposure.
- **Net cash** — cash minus the specified debt-like items; for a regulated bank it is not by itself a measure of usable liquidity or survival.
- **Prudential evidence** — regulator-defined bank capital, liquidity, asset-quality, and loss-provisioning information.
- **Risk-weighted assets** — bank assets adjusted for regulatory risk; regulators use them to judge capital strength.
- **Short / borrow / options** — a short seeks to profit from a falling share price and requires borrowed shares; options are contracts whose value depends on the share price.
- **Buyback / repurchase** — a company buying its own shares.
- **Beta** — an estimate of how much a share tends to move with the wider market, used in some required-return models.
- **FX** — foreign-exchange movements between currencies.

## What This Module Found

NU is a digital retail bank and lender for consumers and small businesses in Brazil, Mexico, and Colombia; interest income from lending and interest-bearing assets, plus financial-instrument gains, supplied 85.2% of FY2025 revenue. [FY2025 Form 20-F, Item 4.B, pp.13–15; Item 5, pp.157–158]

Its clearest positive is reach and repeat use: Q2 2026 had 139 million customers, 83.5% activity, and US$17 monthly ARPAC—average revenue per active customer. [Q2 2026 earnings presentation, slide 6]

Its clearest weakness is concentration: Brazil supplied 91.3% of FY2025 geographic revenue, so one country's credit, rate, policy, regulatory, and currency conditions affect most of that measured base. [FY2025 Form 20-F, Note 34, F-97; Item 3.D, country-risk factors]

Customer economics remain incomplete because US$17.1 monthly ARPAC less US$1.0 cost to serve gives a US$16.1 partial spread, not contribution margin—the amount left after all customer-level variable costs—because funding costs and expected credit loss, the amount NU expects not to collect from borrowers, are excluded. [Q2 2026 earnings presentation, slide 28; FY2025 Form 20-F, p.171]

The moat is narrow and only provisionally widening: low-cost digital distribution and scale help, but pricing power and market-share widening are not proven. [FY2025 Form 20-F, Item 3.D; Item 5.A, pp.157–159; Moat — NU, Moat Trajectory, 2026-08-30]

The single killer risk is a weaker Brazilian credit cycle that raises expected credit loss while funding costs remain high; the filing does not quantify that combined scenario. [FY2025 Form 20-F, pp.131–132, 166–171]

The most severe new red flag is estimate dependence: a US$991.046m deferred-tax benefit supplied 51.3% of H1 2026 net income, while the US$3.649bn net deferred-tax asset—a potential future tax benefit dependent on enough future taxable profit—equalled 27.5% of equity, though the disclosure is not evidence of improper accounting. [Q2 2026 interim financial statements, pp.7–8; Note 30, pp.36–37]

Recent evidence argues against an immediate deterioration call because Q2 2026 net interest margin—net interest income relative to interest-earning assets—rose to 22.9% from 18.8% a year earlier, risk-adjusted net interest margin rose to 12.4% from 9.9%, and both delinquency measures improved sequentially; those readings do not establish through-cycle economics. [Q2 2026 earnings presentation, slides 15, 17–19]

## Plain-English Glossary

- **Valuation:** An assessment of what the business or its shares are worth.
- **Moat:** An advantage that can protect a company's economics from competitors.
- **Capital allocation:** How a company uses its money and financing.
- **Capital-structure transaction:** A change involving a company's shares or debt.
- **Interest income:** Income earned by lending or holding interest-bearing assets.
- **Financial-instrument gains:** Gains from financial assets or liabilities.
- **ARPAC:** Average revenue per active customer.
- **Contribution margin:** Revenue left after the customer-level variable costs needed to earn it.
- **Expected credit loss:** The estimated amount a lender expects not to collect from borrowers.
- **Deferred-tax benefit or asset:** A potential tax benefit whose value depends on enough future taxable profit.
- **Net interest margin:** Net interest income relative to interest-earning assets.
- **Operating cash flow:** Cash generated or used by normal business operations.
- **LTM:** Last twelve months.
- **Reportable segment:** A business unit shown separately in formal financial reporting.
- **Unit economics:** Revenue and costs measured for one customer or unit.
- **Interchange cap:** A regulatory limit on a card-payment fee.
- **IFRS:** The accounting rules used for NU's financial reporting.
- **ROE:** Return on equity, or net income earned on average shareholder capital.
- **Equity hurdle or cost of equity:** The return shareholders require for supplying capital.
- **Basis points:** A unit used to describe changes in percentage rates.
- **Gross margin:** Revenue left after direct costs.
- **Hedge:** A position used to offset a specified financial exposure.

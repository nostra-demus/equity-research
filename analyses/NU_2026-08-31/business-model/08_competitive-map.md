# Competitive Map — NU

## 1. Dominant Segment

Nu Group is one reportable segment: digital retail financial services, led by consumer credit, deposits, cards and related fees. It represents 100% of disclosed segment revenue and profit; Brazil produced 91% of the FY2025 geographic-revenue subtotal, so the Brazilian retail-credit market is the relevant competitive arena. [FY2025 Form 20-F, Note 34, F-97–F-98]

## 2. Named Competitors

The selected peers are the three listed Brazilian consumer-credit competitors Nu itself names. The LTM revenue and margin figures below are Capital IQ vendor figures in U.S. dollars, presented on that export's financial-institution revenue definitions; they are not interchangeable with Nu's audited IFRS revenue. The return measures are each bank's own 2Q26 managerial or recurring measure.

### Competitor A — Itaú Unibanco Holding S.A.

- **Ticker / listing:** BOVESPA: ITUB4 (also NYSE: ITUB ADR).
- **Where they compete:** Brazilian consumer credit, deposits, credit cards, payments, investments and insurance. Nu names Itaú among its main Brazilian consumer-credit competitors. [FY2025 Form 20-F, Item 3.D—“Substantial and increasingly intense competition”]
- **Scale:** US$27.594bn LTM total revenue, based on a 5 August 2026 income-statement filing; 3.3 times Nu's US$8.442bn on the same Capital IQ comparable-analysis basis. [Capital IQ Company Comparable Analysis, Financial Data, as of 2026-08-29 — vendor export]
- **Profitability / return on capital:** 32.58% LTM net-income margin on the Capital IQ comparable-analysis basis. Its 2Q26 annualized recurring managerial return on average equity (ROE — quarterly earnings relative to shareholders’ average capital) was 24.3%, and recurring managerial profit was R$12.407bn. [Capital IQ Company Comparable Analysis, Operating Statistics, as of 2026-08-29 — vendor export; Itaú Unibanco Form 6-K, 2Q26 earnings release, 2026-08-04]
- **Source named in:** Nu’s FY2025 Form 20-F names Itaú as a main Brazilian consumer-credit competitor. [FY2025 Form 20-F, Item 3.D—“Substantial and increasingly intense competition”]
- **One-line read:** The largest of the three selected listed bank peers, with broad retail products and a R$1.522tn credit portfolio at June 2026. [Itaú Unibanco Form 6-K, 2Q26 earnings release, 2026-08-04]

### Competitor B — Banco Bradesco S.A.

- **Ticker / listing:** BOVESPA: BBDC4 (also NYSE: BBD ADR).
- **Where they compete:** Brazilian consumer credit, cards, deposits, payments, investments and insurance. Nu names Bradesco among its main Brazilian consumer-credit competitors. [FY2025 Form 20-F, Item 3.D—“Substantial and increasingly intense competition”]
- **Scale:** US$17.910bn LTM total revenue, based on a 5 August 2026 income-statement filing; 2.1 times Nu's US$8.442bn on the same Capital IQ comparable-analysis basis. [Capital IQ Company Comparable Analysis, Financial Data, as of 2026-08-29 — vendor export]
- **Profitability / return on capital:** 26.07% LTM net-income margin on the Capital IQ comparable-analysis basis. Its consolidated 2Q26 ROAE was 16.2% and recurring net income was R$7.050bn. [Capital IQ Company Comparable Analysis, Operating Statistics, as of 2026-08-29 — vendor export; Banco Bradesco Form 6-K, 2Q26 Economic and Financial Analysis, p.7]
- **Source named in:** Nu’s FY2025 Form 20-F names Bradesco as a main Brazilian consumer-credit competitor. [FY2025 Form 20-F, Item 3.D—“Substantial and increasingly intense competition”]
- **One-line read:** A full-service incumbent competing for the same retail borrower and depositor while also using insurance and fee income; its 2Q26 expanded loan portfolio was R$1.137tn. [Banco Bradesco Form 6-K, 2Q26 Economic and Financial Analysis, p.7]

### Competitor C — Banco Santander (Brasil) S.A.

- **Ticker / listing:** BOVESPA: SANB11 (also NYSE: BSBR ADR).
- **Where they compete:** Brazilian consumer credit, cards, deposits, payments, investments and insurance. Nu names Santander Brasil among its main Brazilian consumer-credit competitors. [FY2025 Form 20-F, Item 3.D—“Substantial and increasingly intense competition”]
- **Scale:** US$9.271bn LTM total revenue, based on a 29 July 2026 income-statement filing; 1.1 times Nu's US$8.442bn on the same Capital IQ comparable-analysis basis. [Capital IQ Company Comparable Analysis, Financial Data, as of 2026-08-29 — vendor export]
- **Profitability / return on capital:** 28.66% LTM net-income margin on the Capital IQ comparable-analysis basis. Its management-defined recurring ROAE was 12.5% in 2Q26, alongside R$3.0bn recurring managerial net profit. [Capital IQ Company Comparable Analysis, Operating Statistics, as of 2026-08-29 — vendor export; Banco Santander (Brasil) Form 6-K, 2Q26 Earnings Release (BR GAAP), p.3]
- **Source named in:** Nu’s FY2025 Form 20-F names Santander Brasil as a main Brazilian consumer-credit competitor. [FY2025 Form 20-F, Item 3.D—“Substantial and increasingly intense competition”]
- **One-line read:** A scaled incumbent whose 2Q26 consumer-finance, corporate and SME books drove loan growth; its 2Q26 expanded loan portfolio was R$714.769bn. [Banco Santander (Brasil) Form 6-K, 2Q26 Earnings Release (BR GAAP), pp.3, 15]

## 3. Competitive Position

**Position vs peers: Not disclosed.** Nu reports approximately 5% of its Brazil–Mexico–Colombia serviceable market (SAM) for FY2025, but it does not disclose a prior comparable share or a Brazil-only share series. [FY2025 Form 20-F, Item 4.B, p.20] Its FY2025 revenue rose 37% while the company-estimated regional SAM rose 7% (11% FX-neutral), but the measures do not match: Nu's consolidated revenue is not the SAM's stated interest-and-fee revenue net of funding costs. That is not a clean share-gain calculation. [FY2025 Form 20-F, Item 4.B, pp.12, 20] The form names direct competitors but supplies no matched customer, loan-balance or card-volume share series by competitor; a directional share verdict would therefore be inference, not established fact.

## 4. Competitive Shape

Brazilian retail finance has powerful incumbent banks but is not a three-to-five-bank market controlling more than 70% of credit. The Banco Central do Brasil (BCB) classified the 2025 system-wide aggregates as unconcentrated: the four largest institutions held 57.1% of credit operations, and the four leading lenders were Caixa (19.2%), Banco do Brasil (15.4%), Bradesco (11.3%) and Itaú (11.2%). [Banco Central do Brasil, Financial Stability Report, May 2026, pp.66–67] The relevant credit-card market was moderately concentrated rather than an oligopoly: CR4 was 58.1% and the BCB counted 33 effective institutions. [Banco Central do Brasil, Financial Stability Report, May 2026, p.68] Nu's filing calls Brazil concentrated and quotes an HHI of 889 from 2024–25 sources, but that is not the same measure as the BCB's normalized HHI and market-specific CR4 data; the official BCB series is the better basis for the narrower read above. [FY2025 Form 20-F, Item 4.B—Industry Background; Banco Central do Brasil, Financial Stability Report, May 2026, pp.66–68]

## 5. Caveat

The three selected banks are directly named, public and within roughly 3.3 times Nu's LTM revenue on the same Capital IQ export. This does not exhaust competition: Nu also names Banco Inter, BTG, C6 and XP in investments, and Mercado Pago, PicPay, PagSeguro and StoneCo in payments. [FY2025 Form 20-F, Item 3.D—“Substantial and increasingly intense competition”] Their product-specific loan, card-spend, active-customer and pricing shares are not disclosed in the material reviewed. A matched Brazil consumer-credit and card-volume share time series for Nu and the named peers would resolve the position-versus-peers gap.

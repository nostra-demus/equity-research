## 3. Stated Strategy & Targets

Management describes an earnings model built on more active customers, higher ARPAC (average monthly revenue per active customer), and a scalable operating platform. It is investing in Brazil, Mexico and Colombia while treating U.S. entry as a bounded option rather than committing to unrestricted expansion. [Q2 2026 earnings presentation, slides 6 and 28; Q1 2026 earnings-call transcript, pp. 8–9]

The checkable operating targets are cost to serve at or below US$1 per active customer, FY2026 efficiency ratio of about 20%, and a U.S. investment drag below 100 basis points of the consolidated efficiency ratio in each of 2026 and 2027. Management gives no formal revenue, IFRS EPS, EBIT or capex target; that limits forecast-accuracy testing. [Q4 2023 earnings-call transcript, p. 8; Q1 2026 earnings-call transcript, pp. 8–9; Q2 2026 earnings-call transcript, pp. 8–11]

## 5. Management Read

Vélez's 13-year founder tenure and an eight-year median company tenure across the five-person operating core support a stable control center; the CFO replacement was planned and supported by the former CFO. The evidence supports promises mostly kept only for two concrete operating commitments, not a broad claim about earnings guidance, because management supplies no formal revenue, EPS, EBIT or capex targets. [FY2025 Form 20-F, Item 6A, pp. 187–192; Q2 2026 earnings-call transcript, pp. 6–8]

The biggest management signal is the combination of an orderly CFO handover with unclassified previous role changes: the FY2024 President/COO and CTO no longer appeared in the FY2025 officer table, and the available pool does not explain those changes. This is an Amber disclosure and succession-monitoring item, not a finding of improper conduct. [FY2024 Form 20-F, Item 6A, p. 272; FY2025 Form 20-F, Item 6A, pp. 190–192]

## 6. Seasonality Read

Seasonality cannot be confirmed with the available data — only one clean fiscal year (FY2025) of quarterly splits exists in the pool, and FY2024's quarterly data cannot be reconciled to the post-restatement annual total (`01_historical-financials.md` §5). As directional colour only, FY2025's Q2 revenue share was 25.59% of the full year, essentially in line with Q1 (26.17%) and Q3 (25.65%) — no evidence of Q2 being a structurally strong or weak quarter for Haier. This means seasonality is roughly neutral to the setup: it neither helps nor hurts the beat/miss case, and the module cannot lean on a seasonal tailwind or headwind argument for this specific print.

## 7. Historical Pattern

HAIER does not show a clean, systematic beat or miss pattern across the last four reported quarters — it is mixed (Q-4: revenue miss/EPS beat; Q-3: both beat; Q-2: both miss; Q-1: both miss) — but the pattern has trended negative into the most recent two quarters, both of which missed on revenue AND EPS against an already-lowered consensus, and the FY2025 full-year print itself missed EPS by −7.1% [`04_guidance-consensus.md` §6]. This should be weighted moderately-to-heavily by the synthesizer: two consecutive misses against a falling bar, combined with revision breadth that remains net negative into this print (EPS 1 up/11 down; gross margin 3 up/20 down over the trailing ~3 months, `04_guidance-consensus.md` §5), is stronger evidence than the four-quarter mixed record alone would suggest — recency should dominate per CLAUDE.md §4's conservative-default rule.

## 8. Setup Verdict

**Setup favors miss.**

The single most important factor is that the Street's estimate-cutting has not been validated by results: consensus has already been cut sharply (FY2026 EPS down ~14% over six months) yet the two most recently reported quarters both missed the already-lowered bar, and revision breadth remains net negative heading into this print (EPS 1 up/11 down, gross margin 3 up/20 down over the trailing ~3 months) — the Street has not finished cutting. The single biggest risk that could flip this verdict is North America genuinely normalizing post-weather-shock: management's own (unverified) claim that ex-North-America operating profit grew over 10% in Q1 2026 implies the consolidated miss was concentrated in a dated, one-time regional event that should not repeat in Q2.

## 9. Second-Quarter Look-Ahead

Visibility past this print is limited — no formal guidance exists for any period beyond qualitative FY2026 statements. The quarter after next (Q3 2026, reporting ~Oct-2026) likely carries a different mix of risk: if management's claim that tariff impact will "gradually ease in 2026" proves true, North America could turn from a headwind to neutral, but the China "high base" effect from the fading trade-in subsidy is a full-year 2026 phenomenon (not a one-quarter event) and should still be pressuring the China segment in Q3. Net, the setup does not clearly improve or worsen for Q3 versus Q2 based on available evidence — it is simply a different balance of the same two named forces (North America improving, China still fading).

## 10. Pre-Mortem

If this setup call fails, the most likely reason is that the "North America is a one-time weather-and-tariff shock" narrative was wrong — either the blizzard-driven demand drop masked a more structural North American softening, or the tariff costs did not ease as management (unverified) claimed, and the segment continued to drag results below the "ex-North-America op profit grew over 10%" claim that this report leaned on as the main upside case. A secondary failure mode is that the China subsidy-fade effect proved milder than the company's own cautious language implied, letting the largest single revenue driver come in better than the deteriorating direction assumed here.

## What This Module Found

Haier's earnings are slowing on every measure. Revenue growth cooled from +12.57% in FY2023 to +5.71% in FY2025, then went outright negative at -6.86% year-on-year in Q1 2026 [01_historical-financials §1, §3]. Gross, EBITDA (earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortization — a rough measure of operating cash profit), and EBIT margins all fell together off a FY2024 peak; EBITDA margin dropped to 8.78% in FY2025 from 9.80%, down 102 basis points (1.02 percentage points) [01_historical-financials §1].

The single most important driver is raw-material cost inflation — steel, aluminium, copper, and plastics make up 84.0% of appliance-segment cost of sales, the highest share in the disclosed record [03_margin-drivers §2, §8]. This alone would have cut gross margin by 178 basis points (1.78 percentage points) before mitigation. Haier cannot pass this cost through: there is no contract clause that lets it raise prices with costs, so it has absorbed the hit in two straight periods [Margin Setup, synthesis §5]. The cost-reduction program offsetting about 72% of that hit is a discretionary management lever the company itself labels "not guaranteed to repeat" — so it should be read as one combined exposure with the commodity cost, not two independent cushions.

The single most important risk is the consensus setup into the Aug-27-2026 print. Analysts have already cut FY2026 EPS (earnings per share — profit divided by shares outstanding) by about 14% over six months, yet the last two reported quarters *both* missed that already-lowered bar, and the FY2025 full-year print missed EPS by -7.1% [04_guidance-consensus §6]. Revision breadth is still net negative (EPS 1 up / 11 down) — meaning the Street has not finished cutting [04_guidance-consensus §5]. The module's one reason for not extrapolating the misses forward is a single unverified management claim: that excluding North America, Q1 2026 operating profit grew over 10% — a claim never reconciled to a segment-level profit-and-loss statement [05_beat-miss-setup §10].

## Bottom Line

- **Verdict: Earnings decelerating**, red-flag severity **Material concerns** — deceleration is already reported, not modelled: revenue +12.57% (FY2023) → +5.71% (FY2025) → -6.86% (Q1 2026).
- **Why it could be better than it looks:** earnings are cash-backed (CFO/EBITDA never below 93.9% in five years), the company's own adjustments run conservative, and China is still gaining share in a shrinking market — so if management's ex-North-America +10% claim holds, the miss was a regional shock, not broad decay.
- **Why it could be worse:** the whole "not a broad deterioration" case rests on one unverified management claim, while China (48.5% of revenue) is deteriorating and Haier has no price lever to pass through raw-material cost inflation.
- **What evidence is missing:** a company-wide commodity/EBITDA sensitivity disclosure (the top swing factor is agent-derived at only Medium confidence), a segment P&L to check the North America claim, and any post-2019 management commentary (the reason earnings clarity is capped at 70).
- **The one thing to watch next:** the Aug-27-2026 H1 2026 print — it resolves the verdict one way or the other within two weeks. A third straight miss confirms the deceleration; a clean beat with a corroborated North America recovery is the only thing that upgrades it.

## Plain-English Glossary

- **EBITDA** — earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortization; a rough measure of the cash profit the operations throw off.
- **EBIT** — earnings before interest and tax; operating profit after depreciation.
- **EPS** — earnings per share; profit divided by the number of shares.
- **Basis points (bps)** — hundredths of a percentage point; 100bps = 1 percentage point.
- **Consensus / the bar** — the average of analysts' published forecasts the company is measured against.
- **Cash-conversion cycle** — the number of days between paying suppliers and collecting from customers; a negative number means the company collects before it pays.
- **CFO/EBITDA** — operating cash flow divided by operating profit; how much of the reported profit actually shows up as cash.
- **Receivable factoring / supplier finance** — arrangements that sell off customer bills for cash early or stretch out supplier payments; they flatter the cash cycle without reflecting genuine faster collection.
- **Net debt / net-cash** — total debt minus cash; Haier holds more cash than debt (net-cash) on a strict basis.

# Earnings Module — HAIER (Synthesis)

## Abstract

Haier's earnings are decelerating: revenue growth cooled from +12.57% in FY2023 to +5.71% in FY2025 and turned outright negative (-6.86% year-on-year) in Q1 2026, while gross, EBITDA, and EBIT margins all fell together off a FY2024 peak. The biggest driver is raw-material cost inflation — 84% of appliance-segment cost of sales, worth -178bps of revenue before mitigation — compounded by the fading China trade-in subsidy, the single largest revenue lever at 48.5% of sales. Consensus has already been cut sharply (FY2026 EPS down roughly 14% in six months) yet the last two reported quarters still missed that lowered bar, with revision breadth still net negative. The biggest risk is that roughly RMB25 billion (about 8% of revenue) of receivable factoring and supplier finance underpins a cash-conversion cycle that has narrowed from -10.8 to -1.2 days. Red-flag severity: Material concerns. The setup favors a miss into the Aug-27-2026 print.

This is not a modelled risk; it is a company-stated headwind already visible in the most recent quarter.
- **Strongest bull point (steelman):** Management's own Q1 2026 claim that, excluding North America, operating profit grew over 10% [`2026 First Quarter Report, p.2`] — if true, the consolidated miss was concentrated in a dated, weather-and-tariff-driven regional shock rather than a broad-based deterioration, and China's share gains (refrigerator 47.7% offline, +3.6pp; washer 47.4%; water heater 32.5%, all gained in a shrinking market) are cushioning, not merely masking, the domestic decline [`02_revenue-drivers.md` §3-4]. Cash conversion has also never broken down in five years (CFO/EBITDA never below 93.9%) and the company's own non-GAAP adjustments run conservative, not inflationary [`06_earnings-quality.md` §2, §7].

- **Disconfirming evidence already visible:** Two consecutive reported quarters (FQ4 2025 and FQ1 2026) missed an ALREADY-lowered consensus on both revenue and EPS, and the FY2025 full-year print itself missed EPS by -7.1% [`04_guidance-consensus.md` §6]; revision breadth remains net negative into the next print (EPS 1 up/11 down; gross margin 3 up/20 down) [`04_guidance-consensus.md` §5]. This is standing, already-printed evidence against the bull case, not a hypothetical.

## 3. Reconciliation

Two genuine internal tensions were surfaced by upstream agents themselves, not papered over, and are reconciled here conservatively per CLAUDE.md §4:

1. **D&A basis conflict (`03_margin-drivers.md` §2-3):** COGS-embedded depreciation rose +12.66% YoY (linked to the Air Solutions segment's asset base nearly doubling on the CCR/Kwikot acquisitions) while CapIQ's broader total-D&A measure fell -7.3% YoY. The two bases are not reconcilable from this pool. This synthesis treats the narrower, filing-sourced, segment-linked COGS-embedded D&A trend (rising) as the more conservative read per CLAUDE.md §4, since it ties directly to a named, verifiable acquisition-driven asset build, while the CapIQ aggregate is an unexplained residual improvement. This affects only the EBITDA-margin-vs-EBIT-margin decline gap (-102bps vs -76bps), not the headline gross-margin or revenue findings.

2. **Cash strength vs. counterparty concentration (`06_earnings-quality.md` vs. `business-model/11_capital-allocation-governance.md`, flagged at `08_earnings-red-flags.md` §2.9/#32):** `06_earnings-quality` concludes cash conversion is strong on an accounting basis (CFO/EBITDA never below 93.9% in five years) — this is correct as far as it goes, but it does not examine that roughly 57% of group cash (RMB33,988mn) sits with the controlling shareholder's own affiliated finance company, and related-party borrowing from that entity rose roughly 19x YoY (RMB196mn → RMB3,767mn). This is a coverage gap between modules, not a factual disagreement — the earnings-quality score (66/100) should be read as "the accrual-to-cash bridge is genuinely strong" alongside, not instead of, "a material share of that cash sits with a related party," a point this earnings module cannot itself score but must not let disappear.

Beyond these two self-flagged tensions, *no material disagreements exist between specialists on the direction of the earnings setup* — 01 through 07 converge on deceleration, margin pressure, and elevated miss risk (`08_earnings-red-flags.md` §1, "Contradictions Between Agents").

## 5. Earnings Setup Summary

### Revenue Setup

The current revenue trajectory is not sustainable at its recent FY2023-2024 pace, and a meaningful share of it was never organic to begin with: FY2025's fastest-growing segments (Air Solutions +9.6%, Water Solutions +10.8%) both carry an unquantified inorganic overlay from the 2024-vintage CCR and Kwikot acquisitions, and the single fastest-growing revenue bucket in the whole company (Other Business, +18.5-19.9%) is a near-zero-margin channel/distribution line, not core appliance demand. The single factor that would flip the direction is China domestic demand: at 48.5% of revenue and already falling roughly 6% YoY on an industry basis in Q1 2026, it is the one lever big enough and correlated enough to move group revenue by several points on its own, and management's own words ("high base" pressure into 2026) point the same way it is already moving. There is a real gap between the reported FY2025 growth story and the underlying organic picture: two of six named growth contributors (Air Solutions +1.65pp, Water Solutions +0.60pp of the 5.71pp total) embed an unquantified M&A boost, and the largest single contributor by geography (Europe, +2.24pp) and by segment (Other Business, +2.14pp) are not the deteriorating China bucket — meaning FY2025's headline growth was already leaning on drivers other than the core China franchise before Q1 2026's outright decline arrived.

### Margin Setup

Current margins are not at a sustainable run-rate — they sit past a FY2024 peak (EBITDA margin 9.80% → 8.78%, EBIT margin 7.66% → 6.90%) that was itself inflated by the now-fading China trade-in subsidy cycle, so FY2024 should not be used as a normalization anchor. If raw-material costs move adversely by another 10-20% at the current pace (Q4 2025's copper/bulk-material rise already cost -178bps of revenue before mitigation), it takes the largest single bite out of consolidated margins by a wide margin — larger than the entire observed -110bps gross-margin decline — because the offsetting cost-reduction program is a discretionary management lever explicitly labelled "not guaranteed to repeat," not a structural cost advantage. Management has no contractual or structural margin-protection mechanism: business-model `06_value-chain.md` confirms there is no escalator, indexed-pricing, or cost-plus clause with any customer, so Haier is a price-taker on raw materials and has already absorbed, not passed through, cost inflation in two consecutive periods (FY2025 and Q1 2026).

### Quality Check

The single largest gap between reported and economic earnings is not a headline non-GAAP adjustment — the company's own adjustments run conservative (deducting non-recurring gains, not adding back real costs) — it is the working-capital financing scaffolding underneath an already-thinning cash-conversion cycle: roughly RMB25bn (~8% of revenue) of receivable factoring, bill discounting, and supplier-finance arrangements sit beneath a cash conversion cycle that narrowed from -10.8 days (FY2023) to -1.2 days (FY2025), and this gap is widening, not narrowing, as the buffer between customer collections and supplier payments keeps shrinking. The recurring, ~5-7%-of-net-profit government-subsidy stream that management excludes from "adjusted" earnings every year for three straight years is genuinely one-time by the CSRC's own definition but behaves economically like recurring income given its stability across three years — a real gray area, not a clean "one-off." To model normalized earnings for next year, start from GAAP reported net profit, not the company's adjusted figure — the adjusted number moves in the conservative direction and both figures track closely (a ~4.9% gap), but the accrual-quality flag (RF-EQ-001: revenue outgrowing CFO for two years, receivables outgrowing revenue in two of three years) means neither figure should be extrapolated without the cash-conversion-cycle caveat above.

### Consensus Bar

For Haier to beat the current bar by a material margin, North America would need to genuinely normalize post-weather-shock AND the cost-reduction program would need to sustain its FY2025 offset rate against continued raw-material inflation — a conjunction of two things going right, not one, since the two named beat scenarios (North America normalization, cost-program repeat) are largely independent of each other and neither alone has been validated by a reported quarter yet. The bar is most likely set too high relative to where results have actually landed: consensus has been cut sharply (FY2026 EPS down ~14% over six months) but the last two reported prints still missed that already-lowered number, meaning the Street's cuts have consistently lagged the deterioration rather than overshooting it. A meaningful share of the current consensus revenue base (FY2026 revenue consensus implies only +2.24% growth, itself already a sharp deceleration from FY2025's own +5.71%) is anchored to the assumption that China's subsidy-driven demand pull-forward from 2024-2025 does not reverse further — exactly the mechanism management itself has flagged as a headwind for 2026.

## 5b. Leverage & Capital Structure

Leverage is within normal range and did not change materially during the period — no dedicated treatment required. Haier remained net-cash on a strict basis throughout FY2021-FY2025 (net debt/EBITDA ranging from -1.24x to -0.19x), and the year-over-year move in that ratio (FY2024 -0.57x to FY2025 -0.19x, a +0.38x shift) falls well short of the 1.0x trigger for material leverage change; no evidence in this pool indicates total debt rose more than 50% YoY. The one related trend worth flagging for the record, though it does not meet either leverage trigger: the net-cash cushion has thinned steadily since FY2022 on both the strict basis and CapIQ's broader cash-inclusive basis, and separately (a capital-allocation/governance finding, not a leverage one) roughly 57% of group cash sits with the controlling shareholder's own finance company, with related-party borrowing from that entity up roughly 19x YoY [`business-model/11_capital-allocation-governance.md` §1, surfaced at `08_earnings-red-flags.md` §2.9].

## 9. Simple Summary

- Revenue: growing more slowly every year since FY2023, and turned negative (-6.86% YoY) in the most recent quarter — the main driver is China's fading trade-in subsidy and a soft property market.
- Margins: peaked in FY2024, fell in FY2025 — the main driver is rising raw-material costs (steel, copper, aluminium) that the company cannot pass through to customers.
- Earnings are mostly clean — cash backing them has never broken down — but a big chunk of that clean look (~8% of revenue) rests on receivable factoring and supplier-finance deals, and that support cushion is shrinking fast.
- The consensus bar is hard to beat: analysts already cut their numbers a lot, but the company still missed the lowered numbers the last two times it reported.
- Next quarter (due Aug-27-2026) is set up to favor a miss — the main hope for a better result rests on one unverified management claim about North America.
- Biggest single swing factor: raw-material costs, and it moves against Haier with no price-lever to offset it.
- Earnings are meaningfully sensitive to a small number of inputs (volatility score 62/100, where higher is worse) — not extreme, but real.
- This module is useful: it gives the master synthesizer a clear, evidence-backed "decelerating, miss-favored, cash-mostly-clean-but-thinning" read with a two-week-away catalyst to test it against.

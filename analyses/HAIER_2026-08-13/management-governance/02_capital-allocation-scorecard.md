**Serial-acquirer + opportunity-cost test (CLAUDE.md §24, Filter 4).** Counting third-party deals plus the one related-party injection over roughly 12–14 years (Fisher & Paykel 2012/2018, GE Appliances 2016, Candy 2018, Haier Electronics Group 2020, CCR 2023/2024, Kwikot 2024), Haier has done a material capital-allocation event roughly every 2 years — a materially *more active* cadence than `business-model/11`'s "one every 3–4 years" characterization, because that quick-read did not have the CCR deal (this agent found it via the FY2025 goodwill note and a web search; it is not separately itemized as a deal in the CapIQ Direct-Investments export). Despite the higher-than-previously-stated frequency, this does **not** clear the Filter-4 bar for a serial-acquirer cap: no single deal in the current 3–5-year scorecard window is large relative to the company's own market value (CCR + Kwikot combined ≈US$900mm against a ≈US$34bn market cap, under 3%), none were financed with debt approaching or exceeding company value, no forced divestitures of existing businesses were found in this pool, and the goodwill note shows no impairment on any acquired CGU. The one deal that genuinely deserves an opportunity-cost flag is the **undisclosed price on the 2018 Fisher & Paykel related-party injection** — not because it is large, but because a minority shareholder cannot verify whether the listco paid its own controlling shareholder a fair price for an asset the Group had already been operating for three years. **RF-CAP-004 is not triggered** at this scorecard's severity threshold, but the M&A-cadence finding and the FPA pricing gap are both routed as follow-ups to `04_ownership-and-insider-behavior` for the related-party-transaction lens.

## 5. Capital-Allocation Read

Capital has been allocated toward genuinely conservative, disclosed uses — a covered, fast-growing dividend, low and stable leverage, and no impaired goodwill on any of five acquisitions in twelve years — but the per-share value-creation case is weaker than the headline dividend growth suggests once the most recent data is weighted properly: incremental returns on newly invested capital turned negative in FY2025 (−8.3%, the freshest and most decision-relevant number this module can compute), diluted EPS fell on both a trailing-twelve-month basis (−4.3%) and in the standalone Q1 2026 quarter (−15.3% YoY), and dividend coverage by free cash flow has compressed from 3.93x to 1.24x in five years even as management commits to push the payout ratio higher still (to a 60% floor by FY2028). The best use of capital over the period was the dividend — disclosed, covered, growing, and formally guided to keep growing — and the worst-monitored gap is the undisclosed price on the 2018 Fisher & Paykel related-party asset injection from the controlling shareholder, a fact minority shareholders cannot currently verify. The single biggest capital-allocation signal is the divergence between the backward-looking narrative (five straight years of dividend growth, no goodwill impairments, a shrinking share count) and the forward-looking arithmetic (negative marginal ROIC and falling EPS in the most recent period, plus a CNY 6,000mn "buyback" that is structurally an SBC offset, not a capital return). On balance this reads as **"mixed"** — competent, low-risk stewardship of the capital that is easy to observe (dividends, leverage, no impairments), sitting on top of a genuine and recent deterioration in the return on the capital actually being reinvested.

Company: Haier Smart Home Co., Ltd. — dual-primary listed A-share (SSE 600690) and H-share (HKEX 6690), plus a small Frankfurt D-share tranche and a US Level-I ADR. Total share capital at 2025-12-31 was 9,377,629,650 shares (6,253,028,411 A shares = 66.68%, 271,013,973 D shares = 2.89%, 2,853,587,266 H shares = 30.43%) [FY2025 Annual Report (HKEX, IFRS, Traditional Chinese) — translated, Directors' Report "董事及行政總裁於股份及相關股份之權益及淡倉", p.121-122]. **Reconciliation note on the CapIQ figure.** CapIQ's Public Ownership Summary reports a single "Haier Group Corporation" line of 37.45% (3,466,550,782 sh, position date 2026-03-27) — about 2.96 percentage points (≈233mn shares) above the filing's 34.49% aggregate. Investigating the CapIQ "Top Buyers" table (below) shows the entire apparent +538,560,000-share "increase" attributed to "Haier Group Corporation" matches, share-for-share, the existing stake of HCH (HK) Investment Management Co., Limited (5.74%, a disclosed Haier Group concert party) as shown in the FY2025 annual report's own shareholder table. This is very likely a CapIQ reclassification of an existing concert-party holding into the aggregated "Haier Group Corporation" beneficial-ownership line, not fresh open-market accumulation by the parent — see Section 2 for the full read. The residual ~2.96pp gap between CapIQ's 37.45% and the filing's 34.49% is not explained by any single line item found in the pool and is flagged as an unresolved reconciliation item, not a confirmed additional stake. The filing figure (34.49%) is used as the authoritative total insider/control-group ownership in this report.

**Individual insider ownership is negligible.** Outside the controlling Haier Group bloc, named directors and officers collectively hold 0.066% of total share capital (A-share table) — and every disclosed increase in FY2025 is attributed to Employee Share Ownership Plan vesting, not to a cash purchase. There is no evidence in the pool of any director or officer buying stock in the open market with personal cash.

## 5. Ownership / Insider Read

Skin in the game sits almost entirely at the corporate-controller level, not the individual-executive level: Haier Group Corporation and its four disclosed concert-party vehicles hold 34.49% of the company (per the filing's own reconciled figure — the CapIQ-reported 37.45% is a vendor aggregate that does not fully reconcile and is treated as unconfirmed context), while every named director and officer combined holds just 0.066% of shares, virtually all of it received through Employee Share Ownership Plan vesting rather than bought with personal cash. The insider-transaction signal is a mild net sale (about 0.07% of shares outstanding, unnamed, low confidence) once the apparent 538.56mn-share "Haier Group buy" is corrected for what looks like a CapIQ reclassification of an existing concert-party stake rather than genuine new accumulation — so there is no real evidence of insider buying conviction here, and no evidence of urgent insider selling either. The biggest control-related risk to minority holders is not pledging (0%, cleanly disclosed) or a disqualifying unaligned-owner structure (Filter 6 does not trip on current evidence) — it is that the controlling bloc's economic interests are increasingly channeled through affiliated entities (the Haier Group Finance Co. cash/lending relationship already flagged at the business-model level) rather than through direct, cash-funded personal share ownership that would tie individual executives' wealth to the stock price the way a founder-buyer's stake would.

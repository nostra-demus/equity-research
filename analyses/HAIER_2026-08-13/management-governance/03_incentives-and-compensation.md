## 2. Performance Metrics

The **annual cash bonus** for executive directors has **no disclosed formula or weighted metrics** — the Remuneration & Assessment Committee ("薪酬與考核委員會") states only that pay "will also make reference to the Company's performance, current market conditions, and each executive director's performance or contribution," and that discretionary bonuses are set with reference to peer-company pay and each individual's contribution to Group objectives, without a published scorecard [FY2025 Annual Report (HKEX), Corporate Governance Report §"薪酬與考核委員會之主要職責", p.94]. This is a genuine disclosure gap on the smaller, cash portion of pay.

The **long-term incentive** — which is ~70–77% of CEO/CFO total pay — has an unusually well-disclosed, explicit metric structure, set out per plan vintage:

| Plan | Metric | Weight | Threshold(s) | Per-Share/Returns or Size-Based? | Source |
|---|---|---:|---|---|---|
| Annual cash bonus (all vintages) | Discretionary — no published metric/weight; "company performance, market conditions, individual contribution" | Not disclosed | Not disclosed | **Cannot classify — undisclosed formula** | [FY2025 Annual Report (HKEX), p.94] |
| A/H-Share Core Employee Stockholding Plan, 2023 & 2024 vintages (covers CEO, President, Supervisors, "platform" staff) | (a) 扣非歸母淨利潤複合增長率 — net profit attributable to parent, excluding non-recurring items, compound annual growth rate vs. base year | 50% | ≥15% CAGR for full-weight credit; completion ratio = actual CAGR ÷ 15% | **Returns/growth-based** (earnings growth, not a pure per-share metric, but not revenue/EBITDA/size either) | [FY2025 Annual Report (HKEX), pp.129–133, Notes 1–2 to Share Award Plan] |
| Same plan, same vintages | (b) 加權平均淨資產收益率 (ROE) — weighted-average return on equity, excluding capital-raising and M&A distortions | 50% | ≥16.8% for full-weight credit; completion ratio = actual ROE ÷ 16.8% | **Returns-based** | [same source] |
| A/H-Share Core Employee Stockholding Plan, 2025 vintage (directors/President/platform staff only) | ROE (weighted-average, ex-refinancing/M&A/disposal effects) — sole metric | 100% | FY2025: ROE ≥17.7% → full (40%) vesting of first tranche; FY2026: 16.8%–17.7% → partial vesting at Committee discretion; <16.8% → forfeiture | **Returns-based** | [FY2025 Annual Report (HKEX), pp.134–135] |
| A-Share Stock Option Plan (2021 vintage, 5 annual tranches 2021–2025) | 歸母淨利潤增長率/複合增長率 — net profit attributable to parent, growth vs. base year (2020, adjusted) | 100% | Yr1: ≥30% growth; Yrs2–5: ≥15% CAGR vs. base year, each tranche 20% of grant | **Growth-based** (not per-share, not size/absolute; a % hurdle on parent-attributable profit) | [FY2025 Annual Report (HKEX), pp.144–145] |
| A-Share Stock Option Plan (2022 vintage, 4 annual tranches 2022–2025) | Same metric family, ≥15% CAGR each year vs. 2021 base, each tranche 25% of grant | 100% | ≥15% CAGR | **Growth-based** | [same source] |

**No revenue, absolute EBITDA, deal-count, or headcount metric appears anywhere in the disclosed LTI structure.** Every quantitative long-term hurdle is either ROE (a returns metric) or a % growth rate on net profit attributable to parent shareholders (an earnings-growth metric, not an absolute-size metric) — this is a meaningfully different, more shareholder-aligned design than a typical revenue- or EBITDA-anchored plan. The weak point is the annual cash bonus, which carries no published formula at all.

## 4. Incentive Alignment Read

Haier Smart Home's long-term incentive plans — which make up roughly three-quarters of CEO and CFO total pay — are anchored entirely to ROE (a returns metric) and net-profit-growth hurdles (an earnings-growth metric), with zero exposure to revenue, absolute EBITDA, deal count, or headcount anywhere in the disclosed structure; the FY2025 results show the top ROE hurdle (17.7%) was missed (actual 16.98%) and reported profit growth (+4.4%) fell well short of the 15% CAGR bar embedded in two option/stockholding-plan vintages, evidence these targets bite rather than rubber-stamp vesting. The weak spot is the annual cash bonus — roughly a fifth to a third of executive pay — which carries no published metric or weight at all, just board/remuneration-committee discretion referencing "company performance and individual contribution," and the fact that the single highest-paid individual in the whole table (GE Appliances CEO Kevin Nolan, CNY 23.2m) is paid more than the Haier Smart Home CEO under an incentive structure this disclosure does not detail. The single biggest incentive signal is that Haier's equity incentive design has no growth-for-its-own-sake metric in it at all — it pays out on ROE and profit growth, and in FY2025 those hurdles were genuinely missed rather than gamed. **Conclusion: well-aligned (returns/per-share)**, with two flagged gaps (undisclosed cash-bonus formula; undisclosed metrics for the highest-paid individual) that keep this from a stronger rating.

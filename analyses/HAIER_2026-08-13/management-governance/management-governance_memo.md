## What This Module Found

Management has kept the specific promises it controls: the March-2025 buyback (company buying back its own shares) closed on schedule (CNY1,201mn), and no goodwill — the premium paid over book value in an acquisition — has been written off across five deals in over a decade [`02`, 02-002]. But the business has missed Wall Street's own already-lowered forecasts in each of the last three reported periods (FY2025 EPS surprise −7.1%) [`01`, 01-011].

The single most important driver is that pay actually has teeth. Roughly 70–77% of CEO and CFO pay is multi-year equity tied only to return on equity (ROE — profit earned per CNY100 of shareholder money) and profit-growth targets — no sales-growth or deal-count metrics [`03`, 03-004]. In FY2025 those targets were missed, not waved through: achieved ROE of 16.98% fell short of the 17.7% full-vesting bar [`03`, 03-006]. That is checkable evidence the pay plan is not rigged to reward growth for its own sake.

The single most important risk is the deepening relationship with Haier Group Finance Co., the controlling shareholder's in-house bank. 57% of group cash (RMB33,988mn) sits there, and related-party borrowing jumped roughly 19-fold in one year (RMB196mn to RMB3,767mn, against a RMB10,000mn cap) [`05`, 05-011, 05-012]. It is fully disclosed and independent-director-reviewed today, but it is the fastest-deteriorating governance metric in the module.

Two other cautions: the newest capital invested in FY2025 lost value at the margin — incremental ROIC (the return on each fresh CNY of investment) turned −8.3%, reversing four straight positive years [`02`, 02-011] — and individual executives own almost nothing (0.066% of shares, nearly all free grants, not bought with cash), sitting beneath the 34.49% Haier Group controlling bloc [`04`, 04-003].

## What Would Change This Read

**Would strengthen it:** a named director or officer buying stock with cash (not free grants); Finance Co. borrowing utilization stabilizing or falling from 37.7%; a segment-level P&L that reconciles the Q1 2026 North America claim; incremental ROIC turning positive again in FY2026; a majority-independent board at the next election.

**Would weaken it:** borrowing utilization climbing further toward the RMB10,000mn ceiling; another related-party asset injection with an undisclosed price (as in the 2018 Fisher & Paykel deal); a second straight year of negative incremental ROIC; any pledging appearing on a previously-clean holder; a combined Chair/CEO entrenchment episode.

**Data that would settle open questions:** an FY2025/Q1 2026-era earnings-call transcript (the single highest-value missing document); the FY2026 annual report's actual vesting outcome; the June-2026 AGM vote tallies; the original 2018 Fisher & Paykel transaction price.

## Bottom Line

- **Verdict: Standard / mixed, Watchlist rating.** No dealbreaker — no fraud signal, no pledged shares, no failed audit, no related-party ratio near the disqualifying level.
- **Why it could be better than it looks:** pay is tied only to ROE and profit growth, and those targets were missed rather than gamed in FY2025 — real, checkable alignment, not narrative.
- **Why it could be worse than it looks:** the controlling shareholder's in-house bank is becoming a larger, faster-growing counterparty to the listed company's own cash (57% parked there) and credit (borrowing up ~19x in one year).
- **What evidence is missing:** no earnings-call transcript newer than 2019, so management's tone and Q&A in the worst recent quarter cannot be tested; this is what caps the candor score.
- **One thing to watch next:** Haier Group Finance Co. borrowing cap utilization (37.7% today) at the next annual report — plus the FQ2 2026 print due 2026-08-27, for whether the consensus-miss streak continues.

## Plain-English Glossary

- **Buyback:** a company buying back its own shares, which reduces the share count.
- **Goodwill:** the premium a buyer pays over an acquired company's book value; if the deal sours, it gets written off.
- **Return on equity (ROE):** profit earned for every CNY100 of shareholders' money.
- **Vesting / full-vesting bar:** the performance level executives must hit for their equity awards to actually pay out.
- **Incremental ROIC:** the return earned on each fresh unit of capital newly invested (as opposed to the whole business's average return).
- **Related-party transaction:** a deal between the company and an entity connected to its controlling owner — worth watching because pricing may not be at arm's length.
- **EPS (earnings per share):** company profit divided by the number of shares.
- **Pledging:** using shares as collateral for a loan; zero pledging here is a positive sign.
- **Payout ratio:** the share of profit paid out as dividends.
- **Net worth / net cash:** shareholders' equity; net cash means cash exceeds total debt.

## 5. Minority-Shareholder Protection Read

The anti-takeover and voting-mechanics profile is genuinely shareholder-friendly — no poison pill, no dual-class stock, no golden parachutes, cumulative voting for board seats, and a 10%-threshold right to call a special meeting — but this profile mainly protects against a different risk (defensive entrenchment against an outside bidder) than the risk that actually applies here, which is self-dealing by an already-controlling shareholder. On that axis the picture is weaker: the board clears China's one-third independence minimum (45.5%) but falls short of a majority-independent norm, the Chair/President/CEO roles sit in one person with no separation, the Audit Committee is only 60% independent (two non-independent members, one with almost no public biographical disclosure), and the Nominating Committee includes a sitting executive. The single biggest risk to minority holders is the deepening financial relationship with Haier Group Finance Co. — 57% of Group cash parked with an affiliated, non-independent finance company and related-party borrowings up roughly 19x year-on-year (RMB196m to RMB3,767m against a RMB10,000m cap) — a fully disclosed and independent-director-reviewed arrangement today, but one that concentrates counterparty and liquidity risk inside the controlling shareholder's own financial arm rather than diversified independent banks. No hard disqualifier is triggered (`business-model/01_disqualifier-scan.md` found nothing) and `04_ownership-and-insider-behavior` has not yet produced a finding on RF-OWN-004 in this run — this agent's own read of the ownership data finds no structurally unaligned owner (Haier Group Corporation is a collectively-owned enterprise, not a central-government SOE, not a listed subsidiary of an unrelated value-maximizing parent, and not a sprawling unrelated conglomerate), so no RF-OWN-004 floor applies here. Verdict: **adequate**, weighted down from "strong protection" specifically by the combined Chair/CEO role, the sub-majority-independent audit committee, and the growing related-party cash/lending concentration — none of which is disqualifying today, but any further increase in the Haier Group Finance Co. cap utilization or a weakening of the arm's-length pricing claim would be the trigger that moves this read to "weak / entrenched."

## 3. Disclosure Transparency

Disclosure is genuinely two-tiered. On related-party and financing-arrangement detail — the areas with the most direct governance-risk relevance — Haier discloses in granular, quantified, auditor-reviewed detail: the RMB33,988mn parked with the affiliated Haier Group Finance Co., the RMB3,767mn related-party borrowing increase, and roughly RMB25bn of receivable-factoring and supplier-finance arrangements are all itemized in the notes with independent-director review [`business-model/11_capital-allocation-governance.md`, §1; `earnings/06_earnings-quality.md`, §8, §10]. But on operating KPIs that would let an outsider verify the story — unit volumes, average selling prices by segment, quarterly EBITDA, or a company-wide commodity-cost sensitivity — nothing is disclosed at any point in the filing history, a structural feature of the CAS/HKEX quarterly-filing regime (revenue, net profit, EPS, and CFO only) rather than a deliberate concealment choice [`earnings/06_earnings-quality.md`, §1; `earnings/08_earnings-red-flags.md`, entries on quarterly-EBITDA and unit-volume gaps]. There is no evidence in this pool of disclosure getting LESS transparent over time — the related-party and factoring notes read as consistently detailed year over year, and the FY2025 filing's naming of specific macro drags (trade-in subsidy fade, property weakness, tariffs) is at least as candid as the sparse commodity/FX sensitivity disclosure that has been a standing gap across the whole earnings module.

## 4. Tone in Bad Times

**Not assessable from primary evidence.** No verbatim transcript exists for any period after Q3 2019, so tone in the worst recent quarter (which was FQ4 2025 or FQ1 2026 on the evidence above) cannot be judged, and whether Q&A questions were answered directly or deflected cannot be tested — there is no Q&A to read. The only tone-adjacent evidence available is the written MD&A narrative in the FY2025 Annual Report, which reads as matter-of-fact rather than promotional (it names specific drags without softening language), but written filing narrative is not a substitute for management's live, unscripted answers under analyst questioning, and this module does not treat it as one.

## 5. Candor Read

Management's written disclosure is more candid than promotional on the two axes that can actually be checked from filings: the FY2025 MD&A names real, specific drags (trade-in subsidy fade, property-market weakness, US tariffs) instead of vague "headwinds" language, and the company's own non-GAAP adjustment runs conservative — it strips gains out of reported profit rather than adding costs back in, the opposite of the earnings-inflation pattern this check exists to catch. Set against that: a recurring ~RMB1.1–1.3bn/year government-subsidy stream has been labelled "non-recurring" for three straight years, a −59% sequential profit drop in the CapIQ-derived FQ4 2025 quarter went unexplained, and the Q1 2026 filing highlighted a specific favorable statistic (">10% ex-North-America operating profit growth") for the part of the business doing well without reconciling it to a segment P&L, while the consolidated print missed consensus. The single biggest candor signal is the absence of any verbatim transcript or Q&A record for the entire FY2025/2026 reporting cycle — this is the one gap that would most change this read, because it is precisely where evasiveness or spin would show up, and it cannot be filled from filings alone. No unresolved adverse integrity signal was routed from `business-model/01_disqualifier-scan` or noted in `01_management-and-track-record` [`01_management-and-track-record.md`, §4A: "no adverse signal was routed"], so there is no compounding integrity overhang here. Verdict: **mixed** — candid on the written record, but with a real, unverified selective-framing instance and a structural gap (no recent Q&A) that keeps this from being scored "candid / high-trust."

# Off-Balance-Sheet & Contingencies — HAIER

**Company:** Haier Smart Home Co., Ltd. (SHSE:600690 A-shares / SEHK:6690 H-shares). Dual filer: A-share Annual Report under China Accounting Standards for Business Enterprises (CAS/ASBE), H-share Annual Report restated to IFRS. **Reporting currency: RMB (CNY).** Figures below are quoted in RMB yuan as stated in the source note, then converted to CNY millions for comparability with `01`'s debt stack; the guarantee table in the A-share filing is denominated in 万元 (RMB 10,000s) — converted explicitly below. No FX conversion to another currency is applied anywhere in this report.

Both the CAS (A-share) and IFRS (H-share) filings carry the equivalent of a Contingent Liabilities & Commitments note (CAS: "十五、或有事项"; IFRS: "或然負債" in the Financial Review). Both are read and cited directly — the H-share note is in Traditional Chinese and is translated here, not treated as a data gap (CLAUDE.md §27).

Cross-module input used: `business-model/11_capital-allocation-governance.md` (capital-commitment and shareholder-guarantee figures, cited there from the same IFRS filing) — cross-checked directly against the primary source below, not taken on faith.

**Note on the bank-acceptance-bill exposure:** the CNY 9,969.0mn figure is the single largest number in this report. It is included here in full because PRC negotiable-instruments law creates a real, if low-probability, contingent liability — not because the company flags it as a live risk.

## 5. Contingency Read

The largest number in this report — CNY 9,969.0mn of bank-acceptance bills endorsed/discounted but not yet matured — is routine trade-finance activity where the credit risk sits mainly with the accepting bank, not with Haier's own customers or balance sheet; the company discloses no history of a drawee default triggering recourse, and it is not the kind of exposure the company's own probability language (used for litigation and provisions) applies to. The CNY 4,757mn capital-commitment figure is ordinary, declining, factory-construction capex, not a loss contingency. Both the CAS and IFRS filings make an explicit, audited negative representation — no material contingent liabilities, no material litigation or arbitration this year — and the one recognized litigation provision (CNY 41.0mn) is small and falling year-on-year (from CNY 52.9mn).

Guarantees are essentially internal: CNY 7,213.5mn of guarantees run from the listed parent to its own subsidiaries, whose debt is already consolidated in `01`'s CNY 42,669.5mn gross-debt figure, so crediting this back on top would double-count. External guarantees to third parties, shareholders, or the controller are CNY 0. If the balance sheet is under stress, the item most likely to actually move is the bill-endorsement contingent liability (CNY 9.97bn) — but only in the tail case of a Chinese bank defaulting on an acceptance it has already guaranteed, which is a banking-system event, not a Haier-specific one.

The ratio in §4 crosses both the 3x-of-recognized and 15%-of-equity rule-of-thumb thresholds on paper, but neither condition in the trigger rule for a live contingent-liability spike is met on substance: the company's own probability language marks litigation as immaterial/non-material for the year, and the largest off-balance-sheet items (capital commitments, non-recourse factoring, bank-backed bill endorsement) are not "reasonably possible" or "probable" loss contingencies at all — they are disclosed financing and capex mechanics. No `RF-OBS-001` tag is emitted.

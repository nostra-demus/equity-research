## What This Module Found

Haier holds more cash than debt. Total gross debt is CNY 42,669.5mn (about CNY 42.7bn), but cash beats it by CNY 4,952.2mn on the strict basis (net debt = total debt minus cash) — so it is net cash, not net debt, and has been at every period from FY2021 through March 2026 [`01_capital-structure-and-leverage.md` §4/§5]. Leverage (gross debt ÷ EBITDA, i.e. earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortization — a rough proxy for operating cash earnings) is a modest 1.61x.

The single most important driver of the "fortress" read is that the near-term debt does not depend on refinancing markets. More than half the debt — 55.0%, CNY 23,452.2mn — is due within about a year, bunched into one fiscal-year-end date, but FY2025 cash on hand alone (CNY 47,621.7mn) covers it 2.0x over [`02_maturity-wall-and-refinancing.md` §1/§2]. The stress test confirms the resilience: cash never runs out at any earnings drop tested, even a full 100% EBITDA wipeout for a year, which still leaves a CNY 10.7bn liquidity surplus [`06_downside-stress-test.md` §3].

The single most important risk is not leverage or liquidity — it is **disclosure**. No committed/undrawn bank facility, no formal covenant package (the lender rules that can trigger a default), and no credit rating exist anywhere in the data pool. So the fortress numbers cannot be cross-checked against any real lender's own terms [`04_coverage-and-covenants.md` §2/§3]. Worth watching alongside this: the net-cash cushion has narrowed CNY 19.4bn since FY2022, driven by the dividend payout ratio rising from ~35% to ~71% plus resumed buybacks — leverage rising off a very light base, not operating weakness [`01_capital-structure-and-leverage.md` §6].

## Bottom Line

- **Verdict: Fortress balance sheet** — net cash on every basis, gross leverage 1.61x, interest cover 9.91x, and the near-term debt wall self-funded by cash 2.0x over.
- **Why it could be better than it looks:** a full 100% EBITDA wipeout for a year still leaves CNY 10.7bn of spare liquidity; two 2025 bond placements at 1.66%–1.99% coupons prove current market access without any refinancing dependence.
- **Why it could be worse than it looks:** if a real, undisclosed bank covenant is tighter than the assumed 3.5x proxy, or if the CNY 15,717.3mn of undated long-term bank facilities were callable rather than rolling, the true wall and headroom could be materially worse than shown.
- **What's missing:** no committed/undrawn bank facility, no covenant package, and no credit rating anywhere in the data — so the fortress numbers cannot be independently confirmed against a lender's own terms.
- **One thing to watch next:** the dividend payout ratio (~35%→~71%) and gross debt both rising — the direction of travel is toward less cushion, not more.
- **Highest-value next data request:** the bank credit-facility agreements, or total committed/undrawn credit-line capacity — this one document would remove both the liquidity-confidence cap and the "no revolver identified" gap.

## Plain-English Glossary

- **Net cash / net debt (strict basis):** total debt minus cash. Negative net debt means the company holds more cash than debt (net cash).
- **EBITDA:** earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortization — a rough proxy for operating cash earnings.
- **Gross leverage (gross debt/EBITDA):** total debt measured against a year of those earnings; 1.61x means debt is about 1.6 years of EBITDA.
- **Interest cover (EBITDA/interest):** how many times over the year's earnings could pay the interest bill; 9.91x here.
- **Liquidity runway:** how many months the cash and liquid investments would last against near-term bills and dividends.
- **Covenant / covenant headroom:** a covenant is a lender rule that can trigger a default if broken; headroom is the distance to breaking it. "Not assessable" means no covenant terms were disclosed, so the distance can only be estimated against an assumed rule of thumb.
- **Maturity wall:** the share of debt coming due within a set window (here, within about a year).

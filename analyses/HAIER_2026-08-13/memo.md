## 2. What the Company Does

Haier makes and sells major home appliances worldwide — refrigerators, washing machines, air conditioners, kitchen appliances, and water heaters — under several owned brands: Haier, Casarte, GE Appliances, Candy, Fisher & Paykel, and AQUA. It built this footprint mostly by **buying** established regional appliance makers rather than growing them from scratch: GE Appliances (2016), Fisher & Paykel (2018), and Candy (2019).

The revenue model, in plain terms:

> **Revenue ≈ units sold × price per unit**, across brands and regions — and profit is whatever is left after the cost of the raw materials and the parts (steel, copper, aluminium and components), the factories, and selling costs.

The catch sits in that cost line: raw materials are **84% of the cost of what the appliance business sells**, and Haier has **no disclosed way to pass rising material costs straight through to customers** [FY2025 Annual Report, MD&A; `business-model/06_value-chain.md`]. China is its single biggest market at 48.5% of revenue, and that demand is discretionary — customers can delay a new fridge. The largest product segment is Household Food Storage & Cooking Solutions (41.7% of revenue, 43.4% of segment profit).

## 3. The Variant Perception

This is the honest test of whether we actually see something the market doesn't. The edge score is **25/100** — real but modest, not a strong proprietary insight.

- **What everyone already knows:** Haier trades at a 25–38% discount to its peers Midea and Gree on every reported multiple (a multiple is how many years of profit the market pays for the stock). Its margins trail both — operating profit is 6.9% of sales (EBIT margin, i.e. profit from the core business before interest and tax) versus Midea's 9.8% and Gree's 17.4%. China's appliance trade-in subsidy is fading and raw-material costs are rising. The last two reported quarters both missed forecasts.

- **What is probably priced in:** Most of it. The peer-relative method — the dossier's most trusted valuation approach because it is anchored in real, dated market prices — finds that once you adjust for Haier's lower margins and shrinking moat, the leftover gap to peers is only about **+1.5%** (fair value CNY 22.08 vs. CNY 21.75 price). The market is not obviously mispricing the known facts.

- **What the engine thinks the market may be missing** (two things, both modest, neither decisive):
  1. The common "China state-linked stock trades cheap because of a bad controlling owner" story **does not hold here**. The governance module tested specifically for a controlling owner whose interests clash with minority shareholders (§24 Filter 6) and did not find one: Haier Group Corporation is a collectively-owned enterprise, with no share pledging, no dual-class stock, and cumulative voting. So the discount is an **earnings-trajectory discount, not an ownership discount** — anyone assuming otherwise is wrong.
  2. A genuine **cross-module accounting-signal cluster**: three separate, individually small integrity signals (rising accruals, an unreconciled favorable regional claim, and a recurring subsidy repeatedly labelled "non-recurring") span two different modules. No single module flagged this as material on its own, but stacked together they are a real, monitorable caution a surface read of "cheap fortress-balance-sheet appliance maker" would miss.

- **What evidence would prove the edge is real:** If the FY2026 annual report (~March 2027) shows the Haier Group Finance Co. related-party borrowing continuing to climb past its current 37.7% of cap **and** the accruals pattern (receivables and inventory growing faster than sales and cost of sales) persisting for a third straight year, the caution is confirmed. If borrowing utilization stabilizes or falls and the accruals pattern reverses, the caution is falsified and should be dropped.

Because the edge score is under 50, conviction is capped at 60 no matter what else is in the dossier; the computed conviction of 42.0 sits well inside that cap.

## 4. Why It Could Work — Bull Case

The drivers below are the ones that most move the thesis up. Each is cited; none of them, on today's evidence, is yet confirmed.

1. **The North America weakness may genuinely be a one-off shock.** Management claims that outside North America, Q1 2026 operating profit grew more than 10% — which, if true, would mean the group-level miss was a dated regional (weather/tariff) event, not broad decline [2026 First Quarter Report, p.2]. This is the single most credible bull argument. **It is unverified** — never reconciled to a segment-level profit-and-loss statement — so it counts as a claim, not evidence.

2. **The core business still earns more than its money costs.** Through-cycle return on capital (ROIC — the profit earned on each CNY 100 put into the business) averaged 8.32% over five years, clearing the cost of that money (WACC, roughly 3.9–4.8%) by 350–440 basis points (a basis point is one-hundredth of a percent, so ~3.5–4.4 percentage points) [`business-model/09_moat.md`]. If China's price war eases as the subsidy laps its worst comparisons, gross margin (sales left after the direct cost of making the goods) could stop falling.

3. **Own-history and peer valuation anchors agree.** Haier's own eight-year average multiple (7.38x) and the quality-adjusted peer multiple (7.35x) land in almost the same place independently — a genuine anchor, not one method's artefact [`valuation/02`, `valuation/03`]. A clean beat-and-raise quarter that breaks the consensus-cutting cycle would re-rate the stock toward that ~7.4x anchor (the bull case, CNY 31.78).

4. **Net cash funds counter-cyclical action.** Haier holds more cash than debt on every measure and has since FY2021. That is a strategic asset — it funds dividends, buybacks, or opportunistic deals if the cycle worsens — not lazy capital [`balance-sheet-survival/99`].

5. **A real, dated buyback.** The CNY 6,000mn buyback (a company buying back its own shares) is proven-dated and self-limiting, 24.8% complete as of June 2026, with unused shares cancelled after three years [Key Developments, 2026-03-26]. An accelerating pace would be a positive signal — though it is small next to the earnings print.

Rough sensitivity: the whole gap between the base case (+8.8%) and the bull case (+46.1%) rests on one thing — raw-material excess-cost growth reverting to zero **and** China demand recovering — with the multiple expanding to the 7.4x ceiling.

## 5. Why It Could Fail — Bear Case & The Killer Risk

The risks below are the ones that most move the thesis down.

1. **Rising raw-material costs with no pass-through.** Materials are 84% of appliance cost of sales, and Q4 2025 copper and bulk-material inflation already cut gross margin by about 178 basis points (~1.8 percentage points) on its own [`earnings/03_margin-drivers.md`]. There is no disclosed mechanism to pass this to customers.

2. **A fading China subsidy propping up demand.** China's trade-in subsidy is winding down, and China retail (excluding phones/computers) ran at −6.2% year-on-year in Q1 2026. This is a realized headwind, not a feared one [`earnings/02_revenue-drivers.md`].

3. **The earnings trend is already worsening.** Revenue growth fell from +12.6% (FY2023) to +5.7% (FY2025) to **−6.9%** in the latest quarter (Q1 2026). Two straight quarters missed an already-cut forecast, and revision breadth is net negative (1 estimate up, 11 down on EPS) [`earnings/04`, `earnings/05`].

4. **The related-party financing relationship is deepening fast.** Borrowing from the affiliated Haier Group Finance Co. rose ~19x year-on-year (from RMB196mn to RMB3,767mn, now 37.7% of a RMB10,000mn cap), and 57% of group cash is parked with the same affiliate [FY2025 Annual Report (HKEX, IFRS), Note 13, pp.274–278]. Rated a High red flag.

5. **A concentrated goodwill exposure sitting behind the weak segment.** Goodwill and indefinite-life intangibles (the accounting value paid above net assets for acquisitions) are 25.3% of parent-company equity, 76% of it tied to the 2016 GE Appliances deal — the exact North America segment now absorbing tariff pressure. An impairment there would confirm the pressure is structural [`business-model/12_red-flags-sweep.md`].

**The single killer risk, in one sentence:** the confirmed moat-erosion trend — a 460 basis point (~4.6 percentage point) gross-margin decline over five years and two consecutive declines in return on capital — continues rather than stabilizes, and the H1 2026 print two weeks away is the first live test.

## 6. Avoid-Big-Risks Check (§24)

**No §24 filter tripped to a hard cap.** All six were tested explicitly:

- **Crooks / integrity** — no disqualifier; clean unqualified 13-year audit tenure. Not tripped.
- **Turnaround** — this is not pitched as a turnaround; not tripped.
- **High debt / survival** — the opposite: net cash on every basis since FY2021. Not tripped.
- **Serial acquirer** — Haier is acquisition-built, but the acquisition-pattern check scored 35/100, well under the 70 trigger, with no pending deal. Not tripped.
- **Fast-changing industry** — appliances are not a fast-disruption industry. Not tripped.
- **Unaligned owner** — tested specifically (Filter 6) and **not found**: collectively-owned parent, no pledging, no dual-class stock, cumulative voting.

**Survival read:** genuinely strong. Net cash of CNY (4,952.2)mn on the strict basis (total debt minus cash) and CNY (16,975.1)mn on the broad basis (also netting liquid investments); gross debt/EBITDA (debt against annual operating cash earnings) of 1.61x; CNY 47,621.7mn cash on hand. 55% of gross debt falls due within 12 months, but cash alone covers that 2.0x. In a stress test, liquidity is never exhausted even through a full 100% one-year wipeout of EBITDA. **Caveat:** the fortress is uncorroborated — no committed bank facility, no covenant package, and no credit rating exist anywhere in the data, which caps the liquidity-runway confidence at 60/100 and makes covenant headroom "Not assessable."

The important distinction: **net cash protects solvency, not the equity multiple.** A strong balance sheet limits how far this can fall in a genuine crisis; it does not protect shareholders from a re-rating if the moat keeps eroding.

## 14. Plain-English Glossary

- **Multiple** — how many years of a company's profit the market is willing to pay for its stock; a lower multiple means the market pays less for the same profit.
- **EV/EBITDA** — enterprise value (the whole company's value, including debt, minus cash) divided by EBITDA; a common way to compare how expensive two companies are.
- **EBITDA** — earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation; a rough measure of the operating cash the business throws off in a year.
- **EBIT / EBIT margin** — profit from the core business before interest and tax; the margin expresses it as a percent of sales.
- **Gross margin** — the share of sales left after the direct cost of making the goods.
- **Basis point** — one-hundredth of one percent; 100 basis points = 1 percentage point.
- **ROIC (return on capital)** — the profit the business earns on each CNY 100 put into it; worth more than its cost when it clears the cost of capital.
- **WACC / cost of capital** — what the company's money costs to raise, blending debt and equity; capital earns its keep only above this line.
- **Net cash / net debt** — total debt minus cash; net cash means more cash than debt. "Strict" nets only cash; "broad" also nets liquid investments.
- **Margin of safety** — the cushion between the price you pay and your estimate of fair value.
- **Reverse-DCF** — working backwards from today's price to figure out what growth the market must be assuming.
- **DCF (discounted cash flow)** — valuing a business on its expected future cash, discounted back to today.
- **Consensus** — the average of analysts' forecasts for revenue or earnings.
- **Accruals** — the gap between reported profit and actual cash; rising accruals can be an early sign profit quality is slipping.
- **Goodwill** — the accounting value paid above net assets when buying a company; written down (impaired) if the deal underperforms.
- **Related-party transaction** — business done with an affiliated company (here, the parent's finance arm) rather than an arm's-length outsider.
- **Covenant** — a condition in a loan agreement the borrower must keep to; "headroom" is how much slack exists before breaching it.
- **Buyback** — a company buying back its own shares, which can return cash to holders or offset dilution.
- **Risk/reward** — the size of the potential gain measured against the potential loss; below 1.0 means more at risk than to gain on these numbers.

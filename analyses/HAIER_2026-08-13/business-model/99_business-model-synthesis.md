# Business Model Reality Check — HAIER (Synthesis)

## Abstract

Haier Smart Home makes and sells refrigerators, washing machines, air conditioners, and water heaters worldwide under owned brands including Haier, Casarte, GE Appliances, and Candy, built largely through regional acquisitions since 1984. Household Food Storage & Cooking Solutions is the biggest segment, generating 41.7% of FY2025 revenue and 43.4% of segment profit before tax. The clearest positive is a five-year through-cycle return on capital (ROIC) of 8.32%, comfortably above an estimated 3.9%–4.8% cost of capital (WACC). The clearest negative is a five-year, 460-basis-point gross-margin slide (30.9% to 26.3%), with an EBIT margin (6.9%) that trails both named Chinese peers, Midea (9.8%) and Gree (17.4%). No disqualifier triggered; this is an average, commodity-exposed manufacturer with a narrow, eroding moat, worth deeper work only if the price is cheap.

## 4. Note To The Final Synthesizer

- **Strongest business-model positive:** Through-cycle ROIC (8.32%, five-year average) sits 350–440bp above an estimated cost of capital (≈3.9%–4.8%, CAPM inference) — the company clears the absolute economic-moat test even though it trails named peers on margin [`09_moat.md` §3-4].
- **Strongest business-model negative:** Gross margin fell 460bp over five years (30.9% FY2021 → 26.3% FY2025), and the company's own MD&A attributes the FY2025 leg of that decline directly to Q4 copper/bulk-material price rises and intensifying domestic competition overwhelming its cost-reduction program — pricing power is trending the wrong way, not the right one [`06_value-chain.md` §3; `07_business-quality.md` Pricing power row].
- **Most important segment:** Household Food Storage & Cooking Solutions (refrigerators/freezers + kitchen appliances) — 41.7% of FY2025 revenue, 43.4% of segment profit before tax, with no revenue-vs-profit conflict to resolve [`03_segment-map.md` §2].
- **Cleanest unit-economics read:** Cannot be built to a true per-unit level — Haier discloses no unit-volume or ASP figure for any product line, so "does each new appliance sold create value" cannot be separated from "is the aggregate segment profitable" (the latter is answered: yes, at a thin 7.24% margin for refrigerators, slightly improving; the kitchen-appliance line is deteriorating) [`04_unit-economics.md` §3].
- **Where the company sits vs named peers:** Bottom of the three profiled peers on margin — LTM EBIT margin 6.9% vs Midea 9.8% and Gree 17.4%, ahead only of Whirlpool (5.4% GAAP operating margin); gross margin (26.3%) roughly ties Midea (26.3%) and trails Gree (29.8%) [`08_competitive-map.md` §2; `09_moat.md` §4].
- **Main external dependency:** Four simultaneously High-rated variables — commodity prices (steel/aluminium/copper/plastics at 84.0% of appliance COGS), the fading China trade-in subsidy, US/EU tariffs and geopolitics, and the China/US consumer-property cycle — with commodities and tariffs already shown moving FY2025 gross margin [`10_external-dependency.md` §1, §4].
- **Most important capital allocation or governance signal:** Related-party financial-services concentration is the single item that would move the classification if it worsened — up to RMB33,988m (~57% of group cash & short-term investments) is parked with Haier Group's own affiliated finance company, and related-party borrowing from that entity jumped ~19x year on year (RMB196m → RMB3,767m against a RMB10,000m cap); this is fully disclosed and independent-director-reviewed today, not a red flag on its own, but it is the signal to watch (severity 45/100, inverted) [`11_capital-allocation-governance.md` §1, §3].
- **Severity ≥40 red flag requiring propagation:** Goodwill + indefinite-life intangibles are 25.3% of parent-company book equity, with 76% of that goodwill (RMB20,883.0m of RMB27,300.2m) concentrated in the single 2016 GE Appliances acquisition, tested over an unusually long 5–15 year discounted-cash-flow budget period (versus the standard 5-year explicit horizon), and sitting directly behind the North America segment — the one segment the company itself says US tariffs are currently hurting. No impairment has been taken and the audit opinion is unqualified; this is a monitoring item for the North America/GE Appliances thesis, not a standalone disqualifier (severity 50/100) [`12_red-flags-sweep.md` §2-3].
- **Lower-severity flag (below 40, summarized per discretion):** A modest defined-benefit pension/post-retirement-medical net liability (RMB1,293.5m, ~6.6% of FY2025 net profit attributable to owners) sits inside two foreign subsidiaries tied to the same GE Appliances / CCR acquisitions — real but small, not requiring separate escalation (severity 25/100) [`12_red-flags-sweep.md` §2].
- **Whether any automatic disqualifier triggered:** No. All eight tests came back N with zero near-misses in band [`01_disqualifier-scan.md`].
- **Which rejector filters tripped:** None of the three this module owns (crooks/integrity, serial acquirers, fast-changing industry) tripped — see the REJECTOR-FILTER CAPS block in Section 1 for the evidence and thresholds on each.
- **Biggest missing data point:** Unit-volume and average-selling-price figures for any product segment — without them, revenue and margin moves can be split by segment and geography but not by volume vs. price/mix within a segment, and true per-unit value creation cannot be tested [`02_business-identity.md` §3a; `04_unit-economics.md` §1].
- **Whether the business deserves deeper work, and what would change the answer:** Worth deeper work only if valuation is cheap relative to a business earning modestly above its cost of capital but trailing peers on margin and showing a real, multi-year erosion in gross margin and ROIC. What would upgrade the answer: gross margin and ROIC stabilizing once the China trade-in subsidy fully laps and commodity costs normalize, and/or the margin gap to Midea/Gree closing. What would downgrade it further: a further leg down in China property-linked demand, an unmitigated commodity-cost shock given the segment's thin ~7% margin cushion, a GE Appliances goodwill impairment if North America tariff pressure persists, or a deepening of the related-party finance-company concentration.

## 5. Simple Summary

- **What it does:** Makes and sells refrigerators, washing machines, air conditioners, kitchen appliances, and water heaters worldwide under Haier, Casarte, Leader, GE Appliances, Candy, Fisher & Paykel, and AQUA — built largely by buying established regional appliance makers rather than growing organically.
- **How it makes money:** Revenue = units sold × average selling price, summed across five product segments and nine geographic regions; the household refrigeration/kitchen segment alone supplies 41.7% of revenue and 43.4% of profit.
- **Whether each new unit creates value:** Not provable to a true per-unit level — no unit-volume or price-per-unit figure is disclosed for any product line — but the aggregate refrigerator segment is profitable at a thin, slightly improving 7.24% margin, while kitchen appliances are deteriorating.
- **Which segment matters most:** Household Food Storage & Cooking Solutions (refrigerators/freezers + kitchen appliances) — the clear leader on both revenue and profit share, with no close second.
- **Whether it has a moat, and against whom:** A narrow, eroding moat. It clears its own cost of capital (ROIC 8.32% vs WACC ≈3.9%–4.8%) but earns less on every disclosed margin line than two of its three named peers, Midea and Gree, and the gap has been widening, not closing.
- **What external variables it depends on:** Commodity input prices (84% of appliance COGS), the now-fading China appliance trade-in subsidy, US/EU tariffs, and the China/US consumer-property cycle — all four rated High dependency, with two already shown moving reported margin.
- **Whether capital is allocated well:** Mostly — net cash balance sheet, low leverage, a covered and growing dividend, a flat-to-shrinking share count, no promoter pledging, clean unqualified audits — but a growing share of group cash and borrowing runs through the controlling shareholder's own affiliated finance company, the one signal to watch.
- **Whether it deserves deeper work:** Only if the valuation is cheap enough to compensate for weak business quality (42/100), a narrow and eroding moat, and material external dependency (52/100) — this is not a durable-compounder story on the business-model evidence alone.

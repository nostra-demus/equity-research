## What This Module Found

Haier Smart Home makes and sells refrigerators, washing machines, air conditioners, kitchen appliances, and water heaters worldwide under owned brands including Haier, Casarte, GE Appliances, and Candy, built largely by buying regional appliance makers rather than growing on its own [`02_business-identity.md`]. Its biggest segment, Household Food Storage & Cooking Solutions (refrigerators/freezers plus kitchen appliances), supplies 41.7% of FY2025 revenue and 43.4% of segment profit before tax, with no close second [`03_segment-map.md`].

The clearest positive: over five years, the return on capital (ROIC — the profit it earns on each RMB100 of money put into the business) averaged 8.32%, which clears an estimated 3.9%–4.8% cost of capital (WACC — what that money costs to raise) by 350–440 basis points (3.5–4.4 percentage points) [`09_moat.md`]. So the business does pass the basic economic test of earning more than its money costs.

The clearest negative: gross margin (revenue left after the direct cost of making the goods) fell 460 basis points (4.6 percentage points) over five years, from 30.9% in FY2021 to 26.3% in FY2025, and the company's own management commentary blames the FY2025 leg on Q4 copper and bulk-material price rises plus a domestic price war overwhelming its cost cuts [`06_value-chain.md`, `07_business-quality.md`]. Its EBIT margin (operating profit as a share of sales) of 6.9% trails both named Chinese peers, Midea at 9.8% and Gree at 17.4% [`08_competitive-map.md`].

The single killer risk specific to the business model: a sustained jump in raw-material costs (steel, aluminium, copper, plastics are 84.0% of appliance-segment cost of sales) landing on a China sales channel that is 48.5% of revenue and places orders whenever and in whatever quantity it chooses, with no long-term contract. The unit-economics module's own arithmetic shows a 20% unmitigated rise in the refrigerator line's costs would flip the whole main segment from about RMB6.1bn profit to roughly a RMB5.6bn loss [`04_unit-economics.md` §4 — inference on a zero-pass-through assumption]. Bottom line: an average, commodity-exposed manufacturer with a narrow, eroding moat.

## What Would Change This Read

- **Upgrade:** gross margin and ROIC stabilizing once the China trade-in subsidy fully laps and commodity costs normalize, and/or the margin gap to Midea and Gree closing.
- **Downgrade:** a further leg down in China property-linked demand; an unmitigated raw-material cost shock given the segment's thin ~7% margin cushion; a GE Appliances goodwill write-down if North America tariff pressure persists; or a deepening of the related-party finance-company concentration.

---

## Bottom Line

- **Verdict:** Average business — worth deeper work only if valuation is cheap. Not a durable-compounder story on the business-model evidence alone.
- **Biggest reason it could be better than it looks:** despite the margin slide, five-year ROIC (8.32%) still clears its estimated cost of capital by 350–440bp, the balance sheet is net cash with debt under 1.5x a year's earnings, the disqualifier scan was clean, and disclosure quality is high for a dual-standard filer.
- **Biggest reason it could be worse:** gross margin down 460bp over five years and ROIC down for two straight readings from its FY2024 peak, even as China share rose — consistent with defending share through price cuts rather than pricing power (the opposite of a widening moat).
- **What evidence is missing:** unit-volume and average-selling-price figures for any segment — without them, true per-unit value creation cannot be tested.
- **The one signal to watch next:** related-party financial concentration — up to RMB33,988m (~57% of group cash and short-term investments) parked with the controlling shareholder's own finance company, and related-party borrowing from it up ~19x year on year (RMB196m → RMB3,767m, against a RMB10,000m cap). Fully disclosed and reviewed today; not a red flag on its own, but the item that would move the classification if it worsened.

## Plain-English Glossary

- **ROIC (return on capital):** the profit the business earns on each RMB100 of money invested in it.
- **WACC (cost of capital):** what the company's money costs to raise, blending debt and equity.
- **Basis points:** hundredths of a percentage point; 460bp = 4.6 percentage points.
- **Gross margin:** revenue left after the direct cost of making the goods.
- **EBIT margin:** operating profit as a share of sales.
- **Net cash:** cash on hand exceeds total debt.
- **Goodwill / indefinite-life intangibles:** the accounting value of brands and premium paid in past acquisitions, carried on the balance sheet and tested for write-down.

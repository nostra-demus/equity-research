## 4. Read

This is a conservatively financed, cyclical global manufacturer of a commodity-input, price-competed durable good — not a durable compounder and not a fast-changing technology bet either. The industry rate-of-change row scored 72 (above the 40 threshold), so the §24 Filter-5 "sector / technology-cycle bet" flag does **not** apply here; the risk in this name is old-economy cyclicality and cost-pass-through, not disruption. The single quality factor a buyer should watch over the next 24 months is **gross margin**: it fell from 30.9% (FY2021) to 26.3% (FY2025) even as net income margin held near a five-year high (6.5%, FY2024/FY2025) — the gap was closed by SG&A leverage (20.7%→15.8% of revenue) and by a China trade-in subsidy that management's own FY2026 outlook says is now past its "high base," with growth "expected to face pressure" [FY2025 Annual Report (China ASBE, SSE 600690), p.3, p.20-21]. Reconciling the two readings: net margin and EBITDA margin (FY24 peak 9.8%) look near-cycle-peak because they were flattered by SG&A cuts and subsidy-driven volume, while gross margin — the cleaner read on underlying pricing power versus commodity cost — is at a trough, not a peak. Return on capital tells the same story: it peaked at 9.1% in FY2024 and pulled back to 8.0% in FY2025 and 7.6% on an LTM basis (Mar-2026) [Financials.xls, tab: Ratios, Return on Capital %], consistent with a business coming off a subsidy-assisted high rather than one compounding returns structurally upward. A buyer should treat FY2024's margin and ROIC prints as the cyclical high-water mark, not the sustainable run-rate, and should expect further gross-margin and ROIC pressure if commodity costs stay elevated and the subsidy tailwind keeps fading as management itself now expects.

# External Dependency Check — HAIER

Note on scope: Haier Smart Home Co., Ltd. (SSE: 600690 / HKEX: 6690) is a Chinese-incorporated, globally-manufacturing home-appliance group (refrigerators, washers, air conditioners, kitchen and water appliances, plus commercial refrigeration/HVAC via the acquired Carrier Commercial Refrigeration — "CCR" — business and GE Appliances in North America). Figures below are read from the FY2025 A-share annual report (China ASBE standard, filed to the Shanghai Stock Exchange) and the FY2025 H-share annual report (IFRS, filed to HKEX) — same fiscal year (ended 2025-12-31), reconciled rather than averaged per CLAUDE.md §15/§27.

None of the FX or rate sensitivities disclosed above exceed roughly 1.5% of FY2025 net profit attributable to owners (RMB19,553m — FY2025 Annual Report (China ASBE, SSE 600690), p.3). No commodity-price sensitivity table (e.g., "a 10% copper price move changes gross margin by X bps") is disclosed anywhere in either annual report — the 84%-of-COGS raw-material weight (Section 1 above) and the FY2025 margin-decline commentary are the only quantified windows into that exposure, and they are a cost breakdown and a narrative attribution, not a formal sensitivity table.

## 3. Classification

**Partly externally driven.** Haier controls real, demonstrated levers — brand and channel execution (China refrigerator/washer/water-heater share leadership at 47.7% / 47.4% / 32.5%, FY2025 Annual Report (China ASBE, SSE 600690), p.4; GE Appliances #1 in the US for four straight years and high-end revenue +7% despite tariffs, p.4), disciplined FX/rate/commodity hedging programs (forwards, NDFs, swaps, commodity futures hedging under a dedicated internal policy, p.35-37), and active supply-chain relocation (Europe production shifted toward Turkey/Southeast Asia/China re-export routes to blunt tariff exposure, p.16). But four variables it does not control — raw-material commodity prices, the Chinese national trade-in subsidy cliff, US/global tariff policy, and the broader consumer/property cycle in both China and the US — are each independently named by the company itself as principal risks, and at least two of them (commodities, tariffs) are shown in the FY2025 numbers actually moving the reported gross margin. This is not a business whose outcomes are a pure pass-through to any single external variable, but it is also not a business where external variables are background noise.

## 4. External Dependency Risk Score

**52/100** (inverted scale — higher = worse / more externally dependent).

This sits in the 41-60 "material external exposure, mixed mitigation" band. It is pushed toward the middle-upper part of that band because four separate variables (commodities, government subsidy policy, tariffs/geopolitics, consumer cycle) are simultaneously High, and two of them (commodities, tariffs) are shown by the company's own FY2025 numbers to have actually dented gross margin (-1.1pp) despite active mitigation — not merely a disclosed risk that never bites. It is kept out of the 61-80 band because the financially hedgeable variables (FX, interest rates) are demonstrably well-managed (disclosed sensitivities under 1.5% of net profit), and the company shows real, executed management levers — supply-chain relocation, share gains in both the US and China, brand-driven price/mix — that are moving results in the opposite direction from the external headwinds.

## 5. The Single Biggest Lever

Raw-material commodity prices (steel, aluminium, copper, plastics/foam) — at 84% of the appliance segment's cost of sales (FY2025 Annual Report (China ASBE, SSE 600690), p.23), a 20% adverse move in input costs, if only partially passed through, would do more group-wide margin damage than any single tariff or FX shock, because it hits both the domestic and overseas businesses simultaneously and is already shown (Q4 2025 copper) to overwhelm the company's own cost-reduction program.

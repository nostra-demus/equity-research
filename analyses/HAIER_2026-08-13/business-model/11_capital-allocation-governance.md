## 2. Classification

**Standard professional management.** The balance sheet is run conservatively (net cash on a strict basis, Total Debt/EBITDA under 1.5x), the dividend is generous and covered, buybacks have started and the share count is drifting down, the auditor relationship is clean and unqualified with no recent change, and the controlling shareholder's shares carry no pledge. None of this reaches "owner-operator discipline," though, because a material and growing slice of the balance sheet's cash management runs through an affiliated party — up to RMB33,988m, or roughly 57% of the Group's cash & short-term investments, sits with the Haier Group's own finance company rather than an independent bank — and roughly 5% of revenue-equivalent volume flows through other related-party goods/services agreements every year. None of these signals individually reaches disqualifier scale (the ~5% goods/services related-party-transaction ratio sits well under the 25% disqualifier-scan threshold, and no promoter pledging, audit qualification, or going-concern flag was found), so this is a monitoring item, not a governance red flag.

## 3. Most Material Signal

The related-party transaction row is the one signal that would change this classification if it deteriorated. The Group already parks the majority of its cash with an affiliated, non-independent finance company and has sharply increased its borrowing from that same entity (RMB196m in 2024 to RMB3,767m in 2025, against a RMB10,000m cap) — a pattern that is fully disclosed, independent-director-reviewed, and auditor-confirmed under HKEX Rule 14A.56 today, but which concentrates counterparty and liquidity risk inside the controlling shareholder's own financial arm rather than diversified independent banks. If the cap utilization kept climbing, or if pricing terms stopped tracking independent-bank benchmarks as management currently states they do, this would move the classification from "standard professional management" to "governance red flags."

## 4. Capital Allocation Score /100

**66/100.**

This reflects genuinely disciplined signals — net cash balance sheet, low and stable leverage, a covered and growing dividend, a share count that is flat-to-down, no promoter pledging, and a clean three-year unqualified audit history on both the CAS and IFRS books — offset by the related-party financial-services dependency (the largest weight in this score), a working-capital trend that is drifting the wrong way (cash conversion cycle less negative, DSO up ~8 days over two years), and a large, debt-assisted 2016 acquisition (GE Appliances, $5.6bn) that — while apparently well-integrated given its CEO's subsequent elevation to the main board — was material relative to the company's size at the time.

**Rejector-filter cap:** not applied. The acquisition-pattern row scored 35/100, well under the 70-severity trigger for a clear serial-acquirer pattern — the observed cadence (roughly one material third-party deal every 3-4 years, confined to the core appliance industry, with no evidence of forced divestitures or distracted core-franchise focus) does not meet the Filter 4 bar, so the 50/100 cap does not apply here.

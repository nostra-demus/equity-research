**The economic moat test.**

> Return on capital **above** cost of capital: **8.32% ROIC** (FY2021–FY2025 through-cycle average, Capital IQ "Return on Capital %") vs **≈3.9%–4.8% WACC** (CAPM estimate — *Inference, not from filings*) — a gap of roughly **350–440 basis points**.

- **Metric match.** Haier is a generic operating manufacturer (business-identity found no matching row in `frameworks/SECTOR_OVERLAYS.md` — not a bank, insurer, or REIT [`07_business-quality.md` §"Sector overlay"]), so ROIC vs WACC is the correct pair, not ROE vs cost of equity.
- **Return-on-capital source and cross-check.** The primary figure is Capital IQ's own "Return on Capital %" series: 7.88% (FY2021), 8.12% (FY2022), 8.51% (FY2023), 9.12% (FY2024, the cyclical peak), 7.97% (FY2025), 7.56% (LTM Mar-2026) [`Haier Smart Home Co Ltd SHSE 600690 Financials (2).xls`, tab: Ratios]. As a cross-check against the "standard base" (NOPAT ÷ average invested capital), a manual FY2025 build gives: EBIT RMB20,866.8m × (1 − 14.1% effective tax rate) = NOPAT RMB17,921.4m, divided by average Total Capital (Total Debt + Total Equity, including minority interest) of RMB163,548.1m (FY2024 RMB158,449.7m / FY2025 RMB168,646.5m average) [`Haier Smart Home Co Ltd SHSE 600690 Financials (2).xls`, tabs: Income Statement, Balance Sheet] = **10.96%** — materially *higher* (less conservative) than Capital IQ's reported 7.97% for the same year. The two cannot be fully reconciled from the pool exports (Capital IQ's exact averaging window and capital-base definition are not disclosed), so per CLAUDE.md §4's conservative-default rule, **this report uses the lower, vendor-computed 7.97% (FY2025) / 8.32% (5-yr average) figure**, not the higher manual estimate, for the verdict below. Haier does not disclose its own headline ROIC/ROCE metric anywhere in the pool (only a company-disclosed weighted-average ROE of 16.98% for FY2025, close to Capital IQ's own ROE of 16.47% [FY2025 Annual Report (SSE, China ASBE), 主要财务指标 table, p.12] — not the metric this test uses, but shown for completeness).
- **Tax-rate normalization.** The 14.1% FY2025 effective tax rate used above is structural, not a distorted single-year figure: the effective rate declined smoothly from 16.97% (FY2021) to 13.54% (LTM Mar-2026) with no visible one-off spike or dip, and no FVTPL fair-value loss, one-off deferred-tax charge, or settlement item was found in the pool for Haier [`Haier Smart Home Co Ltd SHSE 600690 Financials (2).xls`, tab: Ratios, Effective Tax Rate % row]. No `valuation/04_intrinsic-dcf` output exists yet in this pool to reconcile against — this rate is the anchor for that module when it runs.
- **Through-cycle basis.** FY2024's 9.12% print is the cyclical high-water mark, not the run rate — business-quality independently found the same pattern (ROIC "peaked at 9.1% in FY2024 and pulled back to 8.0% in FY2025 and 7.6% on an LTM basis," a subsidy-assisted high, not a structurally rising return) [`07_business-quality.md` §4]. The verdict below uses the 5-year (FY2021–FY2025) average of 8.32%, not the FY2024 peak.
- **Net-cash basis check.** Haier held net cash of −RMB16,975.1m (strict basis, total debt minus cash & short-term investments) at Dec-2025 [`11_capital-allocation-governance.md`, Debt level row] — about 10% of the RMB168,646.5m FY2025 Total Capital base. Capital IQ's own "Total Capital" figure already does **not** net out cash (it sums Total Debt + Common Equity + Minority Interest with no cash subtraction) [`Haier Smart Home Co Ltd SHSE 600690 Financials.xls`, tab: Key Stats, "Total Capital includes Common Equity and Total Liability"], so the reported ROIC is already a gross-capital figure — no further net-cash inflation adjustment is needed, and the modest net-cash position does not distort the through-cycle read.
- **Cost-of-capital source.** No company-disclosed WACC, cost-of-equity, or hurdle rate was found anywhere in the pool. Per the §3 source-priority (company-disclosed → CAPM estimate, labelled → not determinable), a CAPM estimate is used, **labelled Inference, not from filings**: risk-free rate 1.70% (China 10-year government bond yield, Aug-2026 [Web: tradingeconomics.com, 2026-08-07, unverified]); beta 0.46 (5-year, Capital IQ) [`Company Comparable Analysis Haier Smart Home Co Ltd .xls`, tab: Operating Statistics; corroborated by `Haier Smart Home Co Ltd SHSE 600690 Public Company Profile.rtf`, "Beta 5Y: 0.46"]; equity-risk-premium assumption 6.5% (mid-point inference for China, not sourced from a filing) → cost of equity ≈ 4.7%. Pre-tax cost of debt 1.699% is the current yield-to-worst on Haier's own Jun-2028 senior unsecured RMB note [`Haier Smart Home Co Ltd SHSE 600690 Fixed Income Securities Summary.rtf`] → after-tax cost of debt ≈ 1.46% (at the 14.1% normalized tax rate). ## 4. Where The Company Sits

1. **Relative to peers.** Company sits toward the **bottom** of named peers on margin: Haier's LTM EBIT margin (6.9%) and net margin (6.3%) rank 3rd of the four names in this set — below Gree (17.4% EBIT / 17.0% net) and Midea (9.8% EBIT / 9.6% net), ahead only of Whirlpool (5.4% GAAP operating margin / 2.2% GAAP net margin) [`08_competitive-map.md` §2; `Company Comparable Analysis Haier Smart Home Co Ltd .xls`, tab: Operating Statistics]. On gross margin Haier (26.3%) roughly ties Midea (26.3%) and trails Gree (29.8%).
2. **Absolute (the economic moat test).** The company earns a return on capital **above** its estimated cost of capital: **8.32% ROIC** (5-yr through-cycle average) vs **≈3.9%–4.8% WACC** (CAPM inference) — a gap of roughly 350–440 basis points that survives the ERP sensitivity check.

## 5. Moat Verdict

**Narrow moat.**

The company clears the absolute economic test — through-cycle ROIC (8.32%) sits above even a conservatively-sensitized cost-of-capital estimate (≈3.9%–4.8%) — so this is not the "moat in structure, not economics" failure case. But it cannot be called Strong: none of the ten possible sources scored above 48/100, the two least-weak (Brand 48, Scale 42) are both real but do not convert into peer-superior economics, and Haier's own profitability sits at the bottom of its three named peers rather than the top — a company earning below two of three named rivals on every disclosed margin line is not exercising pricing power over them, even if it beats its own cost of capital in absolute terms. The clearest evidence a moat exists at all is that ROIC has stayed above a very low China-linked WACC through a full five-year window without dropping to breakeven; the clearest evidence it is not strong is that Gree, with a fraction of Haier's scale, earns roughly 2.5x Haier's EBIT margin in the same country and adjacent product categories.

**Moat trajectory: eroding.** Gross margin fell 460bp over five years (30.9% FY2021 → 26.3% FY2025), a real multi-year step-down the company itself attributes partly to Q4 copper/commodity price rises overwhelming its cost-reduction program [`07_business-quality.md`, Pricing power row]. ROIC has now declined for two consecutive readings from its FY2024 peak — 9.12% (FY2024) → 7.97% (FY2025) → 7.56% (LTM Mar-2026) [`Haier Smart Home Co Ltd SHSE 600690 Financials (2).xls`, tab: Ratios]. This is happening even as Haier gains nominal China market share (offline refrigerator share +3.6pp to 47.7% in FY2025 [`08_competitive-map.md` §3]) — the combination (share up, margin and return on capital down) is consistent with share being defended or gained through price/promotion rather than through pricing power, the opposite of a widening moat. Business-quality's competitive-intensity row (24/100, "one of the most crowded consumer-durables categories globally") and the segment map's own flagged "price-war risk in a fragmented, oversupplied sub-industry" for Air Solutions corroborate the same direction [`07_business-quality.md`, Competitive intensity row]. Because business-quality's industry rate-of-change score (72/100) rules out a fast-changing-industry discount, this erosion reads as competitive/cost-pass-through pressure in a stable-technology category, not disruption — a slower-moving but still real threat to durability. Over the next five years the test this narrow moat would need to pass is whether gross margin and ROIC stabilize once the China trade-in subsidy fully laps and commodity costs normalize, or whether the gap to Gree and Midea keeps widening.

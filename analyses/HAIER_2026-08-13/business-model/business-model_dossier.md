# Business Model Reality Check — HAIER (Synthesis)

## Abstract

Haier Smart Home makes and sells refrigerators, washing machines, air conditioners, and water heaters worldwide under owned brands including Haier, Casarte, GE Appliances, and Candy, built largely through regional acquisitions since 1984. Household Food Storage & Cooking Solutions is the biggest segment, generating 41.7% of FY2025 revenue and 43.4% of segment profit before tax. The clearest positive is a five-year through-cycle return on capital (ROIC) of 8.32%, comfortably above an estimated 3.9%–4.8% cost of capital (WACC). The clearest negative is a five-year, 460-basis-point gross-margin slide (30.9% to 26.3%), with an EBIT margin (6.9%) that trails both named Chinese peers, Midea (9.8%) and Gree (17.4%). No disqualifier triggered; this is an average, commodity-exposed manufacturer with a narrow, eroding moat, worth deeper work only if the price is cheap.

## 5. Simple Summary

- **What it does:** Makes and sells refrigerators, washing machines, air conditioners, kitchen appliances, and water heaters worldwide under Haier, Casarte, Leader, GE Appliances, Candy, Fisher & Paykel, and AQUA — built largely by buying established regional appliance makers rather than growing organically.
- **How it makes money:** Revenue = units sold × average selling price, summed across five product segments and nine geographic regions; the household refrigeration/kitchen segment alone supplies 41.7% of revenue and 43.4% of profit.
- **Whether each new unit creates value:** Not provable to a true per-unit level — no unit-volume or price-per-unit figure is disclosed for any product line — but the aggregate refrigerator segment is profitable at a thin, slightly improving 7.24% margin, while kitchen appliances are deteriorating.
- **Which segment matters most:** Household Food Storage & Cooking Solutions (refrigerators/freezers + kitchen appliances) — the clear leader on both revenue and profit share, with no close second.
- **Whether it has a moat, and against whom:** A narrow, eroding moat. It clears its own cost of capital (ROIC 8.32% vs WACC ≈3.9%–4.8%) but earns less on every disclosed margin line than two of its three named peers, Midea and Gree, and the gap has been widening, not closing.
- **What external variables it depends on:** Commodity input prices (84% of appliance COGS), the now-fading China appliance trade-in subsidy, US/EU tariffs, and the China/US consumer-property cycle — all four rated High dependency, with two already shown moving reported margin.
- **Whether capital is allocated well:** Mostly — net cash balance sheet, low leverage, a covered and growing dividend, a flat-to-shrinking share count, no promoter pledging, clean unqualified audits — but a growing share of group cash and borrowing runs through the controlling shareholder's own affiliated finance company, the one signal to watch.
- **Whether it deserves deeper work:** Only if the valuation is cheap enough to compensate for weak business quality (42/100), a narrow and eroding moat, and material external dependency (52/100) — this is not a durable-compounder story on the business-model evidence alone.

# Business Identity — HAIER

## 1. What The Company Actually Does

Haier Smart Home makes and sells large home appliances — refrigerators, washing machines, air conditioners, water heaters, and kitchen appliances — under a family of owned brands: Haier (the mass-market flagship), Casarte (the premium brand), Leader (value-tier), GE Appliances (United States), Candy (Europe), Fisher & Paykel, and AQUA [FY2025 Annual Report (HKEX, IFRS), MD&A / Business Review, p.27–28]. Its customers are households, reached mainly through retail chains and third-party distributors (for example New Zealand's The Warehouse Group), plus the company's own e-commerce and brand stores; no single customer accounted for 10% or more of FY2025 revenue [Haier Smart Home Co Ltd SHSE 600690 Customers.xls, current disclosed-customer list; FY2025 Annual Report (HKEX, IFRS), Note 4]. The company is headquartered in Qingdao, China, and its revenue splits roughly evenly between mainland China (RMB146,537m of FY2025 revenue) and everywhere else — chiefly North America (RMB79,871m) and Europe (RMB38,491m), with smaller amounts from South Asia, Southeast Asia, Australia/New Zealand, Japan, and the Middle East/Africa [FY2025 Annual Report (HKEX, IFRS), Note 4 (Segment Information)]. It grew from a single Qingdao refrigerator factory founded in 1984 into its current footprint largely by buying established appliance makers in each region it wanted to enter — the former GE Appliances business in the US (2016), Fisher & Paykel (2018), Candy in Europe (2019), and two smaller 2024 deals (CCR, a commercial-refrigeration business, and Kwikot, a South African water-heater brand) [Preliminary Annual Report (HKEX Results Announcement), Mar-26-2026, Note 1 (General Information); FY2025 Annual Report (HKEX, IFRS), MD&A, p.28]. The problem it solves for a customer is a basic replacement or new-household need — a fridge, a washer, an air conditioner, or a water heater for the home — sold at a price and brand tier that matches the buyer's budget, from the value-tier Leader brand up to the premium Casarte brand [FY2025 Annual Report (HKEX, IFRS), MD&A, p.27].

## 2. How The Company Makes Money

`Revenue = units sold × average selling price`, summed across five reported product segments and across geography.

FY2025 external revenue by IFRS reportable segment [FY2025 Annual Report (HKEX, IFRS), Note 4 (Segment Information); consistent with Preliminary Annual Report, Mar-26-2026, p.10–11]:
- Refrigerators / freezers: RMB84,487m
- Kitchen appliances: RMB41,488m
- Air conditioners ("Air Energy Solutions"): RMB54,021m
- Washing machines / laundry care ("Household Laundry Care Solutions"): RMB65,386m
- Water heaters / water purifiers ("Whole-House Water Solutions"): RMB17,736m
- Other businesses (small appliances, channel distribution for third-party brands, logistics services): RMB39,211m
- Total: RMB302,329m, up 5.7% year on year [Preliminary Annual Report (HKEX Results Announcement), Mar-26-2026, p.1]

**What drives volume:** appliance replacement cycles and new-household formation, plus government demand-support programs — the FY2025 shareholder letter and MD&A both flag China's appliance trade-in subsidy program as a volume driver whose effect faded through the second half of 2025, and a weak China property market as a further drag on new-appliance demand [Preliminary Annual Report (HKEX Results Announcement), Mar-26-2026, "致股東的函件" (Letter to Shareholders); FY2025 Annual Report (HKEX, IFRS), MD&A, p.30].

**What drives price/mix:** the blended selling price moves with brand-tier mix (premiumization toward Casarte versus the value-tier Leader) and with the "other businesses" channel-distribution and small-appliance lines, which carry a different price structure from the core five appliance categories [FY2025 Annual Report (HKEX, IFRS), MD&A, p.27–29].

**What drives margin:** raw-material costs (steel, copper, plastics/resins are the main appliance input costs), the RMB translation of the roughly half of revenue earned outside China, and trade policy — the FY2025 shareholder letter explicitly names 2025 US tariffs on China-linked appliance imports as a factor that hurt the North America segment's operating results, on top of high US interest rates depressing appliance demand there [Preliminary Annual Report (HKEX Results Announcement), Mar-26-2026, "致股東的函件"].

## 3. Business Type Classification

Multi-brand, acquisition-built global manufacturer of major home appliances, selling through third-party retail/distribution channels and owned e-commerce, with a local-for-local manufacturing footprint (R&D, production and marketing in 30+ countries) built mainly through regional brand acquisitions rather than organic entry [FY2025 Annual Report (HKEX, IFRS), Corporate Profile p.2 and MD&A p.28].

## 3. Value Creation Read

**Unclear — the core per-unit math (contribution margin × unit lifetime vs. acquisition cost) cannot be built because Haier discloses no unit volume, no ASP, and no per-unit build cost for either product line.** What the filings do support: the refrigerators/freezers segment is unambiguously profitable at the aggregate level (segment margin 7.24% of external revenue, up 0.07pp YoY) even as its gross margin compressed 0.62pp on rising input costs (cost growth of 2.02% outpaced revenue growth of 1.11% for the 电冰箱 product line, `FY2025 Annual Report (SSE, CAS), p.42`) — opex efficiency, not pricing power, is doing the offsetting work this year. The segment also runs with segment liabilities (RMB81,067mn) exceeding segment assets (RMB55,796mn) by RMB25,271mn `[FY2025 Annual Report (SSE, CAS), Note 11(1)]` — consistent with a negative-working-capital manufacturing model where suppliers effectively fund a slice of operations, a structural positive for cash generation, though this is a segment-level balance-sheet observation, not itself a per-unit value-creation proof, and it is not close to a "returns" calculation given segment liabilities mix trade payables with other items the filing does not decompose (Inference, not from filings, on the exact driver of the gap). The single most valuable missing disclosure is unit volume (or ASP) by product line — without it, "does each new refrigerator sold create value" cannot be separated from "is the aggregate refrigerator business profitable," which the filings do answer (yes, at a thin and slightly improving margin) but which is not the same question.

Kitchen appliances, the segment's secondary unit type, reads differently: gross margin (−0.76pp) and segment margin (−0.76pp) moved down in lockstep, with no offsetting opex efficiency this year — a cleaner (if still aggregate, not per-unit) deteriorating read.

## 4. Sensitivity

Given a 7.2% segment margin for refrigerators/freezers, the business is running on thin operating leverage, so either a price move or a cost move swings profit by a large multiple of the margin itself. Raw-material cost is the more historically volatile of the two: the filing itself attributes the FY2025 gross-margin compression to input-cost pressure (steel/aluminum/plastic, flagged as the segment's "Main Risk" in `03_segment-map.md`), and cost already grew faster than revenue this year (+2.02% vs +1.11%). Doing the arithmetic: a 20% rise in the 电冰箱 product line's COGS (RMB58,788mn), if not passed through in price, adds RMB11,758mn of cost — nearly double the entire 冰箱/冷柜 segment's RMB6,115mn FY2025 profit, which would flip the segment from a ~RMB6.1bn profit to roughly a RMB5.6bn loss on this single input alone `[calculated from FY2025 Annual Report (SSE, CAS), p.42 and Note 11(1); Inference, not from filings, on the assumption of zero price pass-through]`. A 20% ASP increase, by the same logic, would add roughly RMB16,897mn to segment revenue (20% of RMB84,487mn external revenue) — a swing of similar or larger magnitude in the other direction if costs held flat. Both are Inference, not from filings (the filing provides no elasticity or pass-through data), but the arithmetic shows the segment's thin ~7% margin leaves almost no room to absorb an unmitigated input-cost shock of this size, which is the more evidence-grounded tail risk given the filing's own attribution of this year's margin move to cost, not price.

## 2. Input Cost Pass-Through

Haier does not pass input-cost increases through to customers on any disclosed lag or contractual formula — no escalator, indexed-pricing or cost-plus clause appears anywhere in the pool. Instead it manages commodity risk financially, through hedging (a dedicated internal policy governing bulk-commodity futures hedging) and negotiated "volume-price betting" arrangements with suppliers, and operationally, through a cost-reduction program ("极致成本战略," global co-ordinated procurement, material-code standardization) [FY2025 Annual Report (SSE, China ASBE), p.22, p.58 item 3]. The evidence that this financial/operational buffer is incomplete is direct: FY2025 group gross margin fell 1.1 percentage points to 26.7% because Q4 copper and other bulk-material price rises, combined with intensifying domestic competition, drove the industry average selling price down faster than the company's cost cuts could offset [FY2025 Annual Report (SSE, China ASBE), MD&A, p.22]. The same pattern repeated in Q1 2026: group gross margin was 25.3%, down 0.1 percentage points year-on-year, as "the positive impact from global supply-chain layout and extreme cost optimization was offset by high tariffs and rising bulk material prices" in overseas markets [Q1 2026 Report (HKEX), p.4]. In China specifically, hedging and cost programs did lift margin year-on-year in Q1 2026 [Q1 2026 Report (HKEX), p.4] — so the mitigation tools work at the margin, but they are cost-absorption tools, not price pass-through to the end customer.

**Supplier / input concentration (quantify).** Top 5 suppliers combined took RMB34,914.80m of FY2025 purchases, 16.4% of the year's total procurement spend; of that, RMB10,534.53m (5.0% of total purchases) went to related parties inside the Haier Group [FY2025 Annual Report (SSE, China ASBE), Section 3(7)A, p.43]. The company explicitly checked "not applicable" to both "purchases from a single supplier exceed 50% of the total" and "severe dependence on a small number of suppliers" [FY2025 Annual Report (SSE, China ASBE), Section 3(7)B, p.44]. No single-source or sole-source input or component is separately named in the pool. The real dependency is not on any one vendor but on a concentrated set of global commodity inputs — steel, aluminium, copper, and plastics/foam are named as the specific raw materials whose price volatility is a disclosed principal risk, and together raw materials are 84.0% of the appliance segment's cost of sales (up from 82.0% in FY2024) [FY2025 Annual Report (SSE, China ASBE), p.23, p.58 item 3]. This is a market-wide commodity-price exposure, not a bargaining-power problem with a specific supplier — the filing does not quantify a commodity-price sensitivity (e.g., "a 10% copper move changes gross margin by X bps"), so the size of that exposure beyond the 84%-of-COGS weight is not proven from available data.

## 3. Customer Pricing Power

Haier cannot reliably raise prices without losing volume in its largest market. The company's own FY2025 MD&A attributes the year's 1.1-point gross-margin decline directly to an accelerating industry-wide fall in average selling price in China, driven by hyper-competitive conditions on top of rising input costs — the opposite of pricing power [FY2025 Annual Report (SSE, China ASBE), MD&A, p.22]. The one lever that has worked is mix, not price: Casarte's premium Refinement/Radiance product lines grew from 30% to 43% of Casarte's own retail sales in a single year (Q1 2025 to Q1 2026), and the mass-market Haier-brand "bundle" package rose from 17% to 25% of Haier-brand retail sales over the same period [Q1 2026 Report (HKEX), p.3] — but this is premiumization within the portfolio, not evidence the company can raise list prices on a given product without volume loss. Overseas, the North America segment cites "dynamic pricing" as one of several efficiency levers used to offset tariff and demand pressure [Q1 2026 Report (HKEX), p.4], but no explicit price increase, its date, or its volume effect is disclosed anywhere in the pool — this claim is Not proven from available data beyond the general "dynamic pricing" language. Tariffs on China-linked appliance exports to the US are explicitly said to have "weighed on the Company's performance in the North American market" [Q1 2026 Report (HKEX), p.2] rather than being shown as fully recovered through higher US shelf prices.

## 4. Economic Control Verdict

**Mixed.** Upstream, Haier has real (if not dominant) leverage: no single supplier or supplier group is concentrated, it hedges commodity exposure with a dedicated futures policy, and it negotiates volume-price terms with suppliers — none of which stopped Q4-2025 copper and bulk-material inflation from compressing margin, but the supplier relationship itself is not the constraint (§2). Downstream, the company is structurally weaker: its own filings show it could not raise blended prices fast enough to offset input-cost and tariff inflation in FY2025 and Q1 2026, because both its China retail/distribution channel and its overseas retail channel order "at such time and in such quantities as they deem appropriate" with no binding volume commitment [FY2025 Annual Report (HKEX, IFRS), "Relationship with Customers," p.80], and China's appliance market is explicitly described as intensely price-competitive.

## 5. The Single Biggest Bargaining Risk

The China domestic retail/distribution channel — supplying roughly half of group revenue on discretionary, non-contracted order terms inside an industry-wide price war that already erased Haier's cost-reduction gains and cut FY2025 gross margin by 1.1 points [FY2025 Annual Report (SSE, China ASBE), MD&A, p.22; "Relationship with Customers," p.80] — is the relationship most likely to hurt the company further if it deteriorates.

## 4. Read

This is a conservatively financed, cyclical global manufacturer of a commodity-input, price-competed durable good — not a durable compounder and not a fast-changing technology bet either. The industry rate-of-change row scored 72 (above the 40 threshold), so the §24 Filter-5 "sector / technology-cycle bet" flag does **not** apply here; the risk in this name is old-economy cyclicality and cost-pass-through, not disruption. The single quality factor a buyer should watch over the next 24 months is **gross margin**: it fell from 30.9% (FY2021) to 26.3% (FY2025) even as net income margin held near a five-year high (6.5%, FY2024/FY2025) — the gap was closed by SG&A leverage (20.7%→15.8% of revenue) and by a China trade-in subsidy that management's own FY2026 outlook says is now past its "high base," with growth "expected to face pressure" [FY2025 Annual Report (China ASBE, SSE 600690), p.3, p.20-21]. Reconciling the two readings: net margin and EBITDA margin (FY24 peak 9.8%) look near-cycle-peak because they were flattered by SG&A cuts and subsidy-driven volume, while gross margin — the cleaner read on underlying pricing power versus commodity cost — is at a trough, not a peak. Return on capital tells the same story: it peaked at 9.1% in FY2024 and pulled back to 8.0% in FY2025 and 7.6% on an LTM basis (Mar-2026) [Financials.xls, tab: Ratios, Return on Capital %], consistent with a business coming off a subsidy-assisted high rather than one compounding returns structurally upward. A buyer should treat FY2024's margin and ROIC prints as the cyclical high-water mark, not the sustainable run-rate, and should expect further gross-margin and ROIC pressure if commodity costs stay elevated and the subsidy tailwind keeps fading as management itself now expects.

## 3. Competitive Position

**Position: Gaining share** in the dominant segment. Haier states its China offline refrigerator retail-value share rose to 47.7% in FY2025, up 3.6 percentage points year on year, and that Haier's refrigerator and freezer retail volumes have ranked #1 globally for 18 and 15 consecutive years respectively (Euromonitor-sourced company claim) [FY2025 Annual Report (SSE, CAS), Business Review, "（四）产业经营分析 1、智慧大厨房 (1) 制冷产业", p.27]. No independent (non-company) share-tracking source in the pool corroborates these Euromonitor-attributed figures, so this read rests on the company's own citation of a third-party data provider, not on the provider's report directly — a Level-3/4 claim, not Level-5. ## 4. Competitive Shape

The market is best read as fragmented globally but concentrated domestically. Globally, dozens of national and regional manufacturers compete in large appliances — the Capital IQ competitor export for Haier lists more than 50 distinct rival relationships spanning Whirlpool, Electrolux, Midea, Gree, LG Electronics, Samsung Electronics, BSH Hausgeräte (Bosch/Siemens, privately held), Panasonic, Arçelik, Hisense, Sharp, Daikin, and many India/Bangladesh-focused regional players [`Haier Smart Home Co Ltd SHSE 600690 Competitors.rtf`] — consistent with a fragmented global structure with no single dominant player (Haier's own claimed global refrigerator volume-share rank is #1, but no absolute global share percentage is disclosed in the pool). Within China specifically, the structure is concentrated among a small number of brands: Haier holds 47.7% offline refrigerator share, 47.4% washing-machine share, and 32.5% water-heater share domestically [FY2025 Annual Report (SSE, CAS), Chairman's Letter, p.3], with Midea and Gree as the next-largest named domestic rivals — consistent with an oligopoly-leaning domestic market led by these three groups, though the pool does not disclose Midea's or Gree's category-specific China refrigerator share needed to state a precise top-3 concentration ratio.

## 5. Caveat

Haier's own annual report (Chinese-language, SSE/CAS filing) discusses competitive rank in relative terms ("行业第二", "位居行业前二") without naming the specific rival company in the text reviewed, so none of the three profiled competitors above is "named by company" in the strict sense — all three are sourced from the Capital IQ third-party competitor-relationship export, cross-checked against Haier's own Capital IQ comparable-company set for two of the three (Midea, Gree). BSH Hausgeräte (Bosch/Siemens) is a comparably large global rival named in the same Capital IQ export (LTM revenue $13.1bn as of a stale Dec-2014 figure) but is privately held as a subsidiary of Robert Bosch GmbH, so no current audited margin or return figure could be sourced for it — it was excluded from the three profiles for this reason, not because it is not credible. AB Electrolux (publ) (OM:ELUX B) is another highly credible, comparably scaled competitor (LTM revenue $13,463.41m per the Capital IQ export) that was considered but not profiled in the top 3 to keep the list to the most segment-relevant and best-evidenced peers; its FY2025 operating margin (2.8%, on SEK 131,282m net sales and SEK 3,657m operating income excluding non-recurring items) is available from its own year-end report [AB Electrolux/Electrolux Group, Year-end Report Q4 2025, 2026-01-30 — Web, unverified, not in HAIER data pool] and downstream `moat` may want it as a fourth peer data point.

**Narrow moat.**

The clearest evidence a moat exists at all is that ROIC has stayed above a very low China-linked WACC through a full five-year window without dropping to breakeven; the clearest evidence it is not strong is that Gree, with a fraction of Haier's scale, earns roughly 2.5x Haier's EBIT margin in the same country and adjacent product categories.

Because business-quality's industry rate-of-change score (72/100) rules out a fast-changing-industry discount, this erosion reads as competitive/cost-pass-through pressure in a stable-technology category, not disruption — a slower-moving but still real threat to durability. Over the next five years the test this narrow moat would need to pass is whether gross margin and ROIC stabilize once the China trade-in subsidy fully laps and commodity costs normalize, or whether the gap to Gree and Midea keeps widening.

## 2. Classification

**Standard professional management.** The balance sheet is run conservatively (net cash on a strict basis, Total Debt/EBITDA under 1.5x), the dividend is generous and covered, buybacks have started and the share count is drifting down, the auditor relationship is clean and unqualified with no recent change, and the controlling shareholder's shares carry no pledge. None of this reaches "owner-operator discipline," though, because a material and growing slice of the balance sheet's cash management runs through an affiliated party — up to RMB33,988m, or roughly 57% of the Group's cash & short-term investments, sits with the Haier Group's own finance company rather than an independent bank — and roughly 5% of revenue-equivalent volume flows through other related-party goods/services agreements every year. None of these signals individually reaches disqualifier scale (the ~5% goods/services related-party-transaction ratio sits well under the 25% disqualifier-scan threshold, and no promoter pledging, audit qualification, or going-concern flag was found), so this is a monitoring item, not a governance red flag.

## 3. Most Material Signal

The related-party transaction row is the one signal that would change this classification if it deteriorated. The Group already parks the majority of its cash with an affiliated, non-independent finance company and has sharply increased its borrowing from that same entity (RMB196m in 2024 to RMB3,767m in 2025, against a RMB10,000m cap) — a pattern that is fully disclosed, independent-director-reviewed, and auditor-confirmed under HKEX Rule 14A.56 today, but which concentrates counterparty and liquidity risk inside the controlling shareholder's own financial arm rather than diversified independent banks. If the cap utilization kept climbing, or if pricing terms stopped tracking independent-bank benchmarks as management currently states they do, this would move the classification from "standard professional management" to "governance red flags."

## 4. Capital Allocation Score /100

**66/100.**

This reflects genuinely disciplined signals — net cash balance sheet, low and stable leverage, a covered and growing dividend, a share count that is flat-to-down, no promoter pledging, and a clean three-year unqualified audit history on both the CAS and IFRS books — offset by the related-party financial-services dependency (the largest weight in this score), a working-capital trend that is drifting the wrong way (cash conversion cycle less negative, DSO up ~8 days over two years), and a large, debt-assisted 2016 acquisition (GE Appliances, $5.6bn) that — while apparently well-integrated given its CEO's subsequent elevation to the main board — was material relative to the company's size at the time.

**Rejector-filter cap:** not applied. The acquisition-pattern row scored 35/100, well under the 70-severity trigger for a clear serial-acquirer pattern — the observed cadence (roughly one material third-party deal every 3-4 years, confined to the core appliance industry, with no evidence of forced divestitures or distracted core-franchise focus) does not meet the Filter 4 bar, so the 50/100 cap does not apply here.

## 3. Most Severe New Flag

The goodwill/intangible concentration is the more material of the two new flags. A quarter of Haier Smart Home's parent-company book equity (25.3%) is intangible-asset judgment rather than hard, verifiable value, with three-quarters of that goodwill (RMB20,883.0m of RMB27,300.2m, 76%) sitting behind a single decade-old acquisition — GE Appliances — in the one region (North America) the company itself says tariffs are currently hurting. This is not evidence of impropriety: the audit opinion is unqualified, the impairment test is disclosed as a Key Audit Matter with the underlying assumptions shown, and no impairment charge would necessarily be warranted today. But the combination of a large intangible base, a 5–15 year discounted-cash-flow runway (longer than the 5-year explicit-period norm), and a currently-pressured segment sitting directly behind the largest CGU is exactly the setup where a future write-down — if tariff or consumer-cycle pressure in North America persists — would land as a sudden, non-cash but headline-grabbing hit to reported equity and earnings. The synthesizer should treat this as a monitoring item for the North America / GE Appliances thesis specifically, not as a standalone disqualifier.

## 4. Cross-Cutting Patterns

Both new flags in Section 2 trace back to the same event: the 2016 GE Appliances acquisition, already named upstream (`11_capital-allocation-governance.md`) as the largest deal in Haier's acquisition history. That single transaction is now the source of (a) the largest CGU by far in a goodwill balance that is a quarter of parent equity, and (b) part of the pension/OPEB obligation newly identified here (Haier U.S. Appliance Solutions is the GE Appliances entity). Combined with the external-dependency and customer-geography reports' independent finding that North America (26.4% of revenue, GE Appliances-led) is the segment currently absorbing US tariff costs, the pattern is that one nine-year-old acquisition still concentrates several distinct, only-loosely-connected risk types — goodwill-impairment judgment, actuarial/pension exposure, and tariff-driven margin pressure — inside a single operating entity. No individual piece rises to disqualifier scale, but a synthesizer weighing North America execution risk should know all three sit on the same asset.

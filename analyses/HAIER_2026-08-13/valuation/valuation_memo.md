## What This Module Found

Haier is fairly valued: the base fair value of CNY 23.67 is only 8.8% above the CNY 21.75 price (A-shares, last close 2026-08-12), inside the fairly-valued band [§1]. The fair-value range runs from a bull case of CNY 31.78 down to a structural bear case of CNY 15.90 — a 2.0x spread [§1].

The single most important driver is the peer-relative method (the comparison to real, dated prices of named rivals Midea, Gree, and Whirlpool), which carries 68% of the weight. Haier trades at a 25–38% discount to those peers, and this method finds most — not all — of that discount warranted by Haier's below-peer margins and a confirmed eroding competitive moat. Once quality-adjusted, the residual gap is a modest +1.5% (CNY 22.08 vs CNY 21.75) [§2, §5].

The single most important risk is the moat's already-firing erosion: gross margin (the profit left after direct production costs) is down 460 basis points — 4.6 percentage points — over five years, and return on invested capital (ROIC, the profit earned on each unit of capital put in) has fallen for two straight readings from a subsidy-assisted FY2024 peak [§1]. If that erosion keeps going rather than stabilizing, the structural bear case of CNY 15.90 (−26.9%) becomes the realistic path, not a tail risk [§1, §5].

The central number is the asymmetry: a modest 8.1% cushion if the base case is right, against a 26.9% drop if the moat keeps eroding [§1, §5].

## Plain-English Glossary

- **Basis points (bp):** one-hundredth of a percentage point; 460bp = 4.6 percentage points.
- **Gross margin:** the profit left after the direct cost of making the product, as a share of sales.
- **ROIC (return on invested capital):** the profit earned on each unit of capital put into the business.
- **Enterprise value (EV):** the price of the whole business including its debt, minus its cash.
- **Net cash (strict / broad):** cash minus debt; the strict basis nets only cash, the broad basis also nets in liquid investments.
- **WACC (weighted-average cost of capital / discount rate):** the blended cost of the company's debt and equity funding, used to value future cash flows.
- **DCF / reverse-DCF:** a discounted-cash-flow model values a company from its projected future cash; a reverse-DCF works backwards to find what growth the current price already assumes.
- **Margin of safety:** the cushion between the price and the estimated fair value — here +8.1%.
- **Sum-of-the-parts (SOTP):** valuing each business segment separately, then adding them up.

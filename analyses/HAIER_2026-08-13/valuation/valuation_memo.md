## What This Module Found

Haier is fairly valued, not cheap: the base fair value of CNY 23.67 is only 8.8% above the CNY 21.75 price, inside the fairly-valued band [99_valuation-synthesis, §1].

The read is driven mainly by comparing Haier to named rivals (68% of the weight). Haier trades at a 25–38% discount to Midea, Gree, and Whirlpool on price multiples, and the synthesis judges most — not all — of that discount to be deserved, because Haier's profit margin is about 24% below peers and its competitive edge (its "moat") is confirmed to be shrinking [99_valuation-synthesis, §2, relative-valuation-peers]. Once you haircut the multiple for that weaker margin, fair value lands at CNY 22.08, just 1.5% above today's price.

The single most important driver is that peer-comparison read, and it is unusually well-corroborated: the quality-adjusted multiple (7.35x EV/EBITDA — the price paid for each unit of earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation) lands almost exactly where Haier's own last-8-quarters trading average sits (7.38x), despite being reached by a completely different route [99_valuation-synthesis, §1A, §3].

The single most important risk is that the moat erosion is already happening, not hypothetical: gross margin is down 460 basis points (4.6 percentage points) over five years, and return on invested capital (ROIC — the profit earned on each unit of money put into the business) has fallen for two straight readings from a subsidy-assisted FY2024 peak [99_valuation-synthesis, §1A]. If that trend keeps going instead of stabilising, the structural bear case of CNY 15.90 (−26.9%) becomes the realistic path, not a tail risk. That is the central finding: a thin 8.1% cushion if the base case is right, against a much larger 26.9% drop if erosion continues.

One method disagreement is flagged and settled: the intrinsic DCF (a model of future cash flows) says Haier is worth CNY 29.84, +37.2% above price — but its own author warns the discount rate behind it (WACC 4.10% — the cost of the money used, here pulled down by China's very low bond yields) is far below what the market actually pays for this stock. The synthesis discounts that DCF headline and caps its weight at 17%.

## Bottom Line

- **Verdict:** Fairly valued — base fair value CNY 23.67 is 8.8% above the CNY 21.75 price. Not cheap, not obviously expensive.
- **Biggest reason it could be better than it looks:** two independent methods (own 8-quarter trading mean 7.38x and the quality-adjusted peer multiple 7.35x) converge almost exactly, so the "cheap because deserved" read is not one method's artefact; and a net-cash balance sheet (−0.95x EBITDA) limits the depth of the bear case.
- **Biggest reason it could be worse:** the moat is confirmed to be eroding right now (margin −460bp over five years, ROIC down two readings). If it keeps going, the CNY 15.90 structural bear (−26.9%) is the realistic path, not a tail. The cushion (8.1%) is far smaller than the potential drop (26.9%).
- **What evidence is missing:** FQ2 2026 segment-level results (due 2026-08-27) — needed to tell whether margin compression is stabilising or continuing; and a pure-play laundry comp to firm up the sum-of-the-parts value.
- **The one thing to watch next:** the 2026-08-27 print — a beat-and-raise argues cheapness; another miss confirms the value-trap risk. This is an earnings-trajectory value-trap risk, not an ownership one — no misaligned controlling owner was found.

## Plain-English Glossary

- **Fair value:** an estimate of what a share is worth, against which the market price is judged.
- **Multiple (EV/EBITDA, 7.35x):** how many times a year's earnings-before-interest-tax-depreciation-amortisation the whole business is priced at; a way to compare firms of different sizes.
- **Moat:** a company's durable competitive advantage; "eroding" means it is weakening.
- **Basis point (bp):** one hundredth of a percentage point; 460bp = 4.6 percentage points.
- **ROIC (return on invested capital):** the profit earned on each unit of money put into the business.
- **Enterprise value (EV):** the price of the whole business — market value of shares plus debt, minus cash.
- **Net cash / net debt (strict vs broad):** cash minus debt; "strict" nets only cash, "broad" also nets in short-term investments.
- **DCF (discounted cash flow):** a model that values a company from its expected future cash, discounted back to today.
- **WACC (4.10%):** the cost of the money a company uses (debt and equity blended); the rate future cash is discounted at. A lower WACC pushes DCF values up.
- **Reverse DCF:** works backwards from today's price to reveal what growth or margin the market is assuming.
- **Terminal margin / terminal multiple:** the profit margin or valuation multiple assumed for the far-future, steady-state years of a model.
- **Margin of safety (8.1%):** the cushion between price and fair value — how much room there is if the base case is right.
- **Sum-of-the-parts (SOTP):** valuing each business segment separately, then adding them up.
- **Consensus / beat-and-raise:** analysts' shared forecast; a "beat-and-raise" is when a company tops the forecast and lifts its own guidance.

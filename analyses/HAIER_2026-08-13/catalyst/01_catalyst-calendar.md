## 2. The Nearest Dated Catalyst

The technically nearest proven-dated event is the **final FY2025 dividend payment on 2026-08-21** (RMB 8.9151 per 10 shares) — about one week from today. But its price-moving power is close to zero: the amount was fixed at the 2026-06-24 AGM and the ex-dividend date (2026-08-03) has already passed, so the market has already priced this in. The nearest catalyst that can actually move the stock in either direction is the **H1 2026 interim results, due 2026-08-27/28** — about two weeks away. The setup favors a miss: the last two reported quarters (FQ4 2025 and FQ1 2026) both missed an already-cut consensus, and revision breadth remains net negative into this print. A beat would need North America's post-weather-shock recovery (management's own, unverified claim) to prove durable; a miss would most likely come from China's subsidy-fade demand drag deepening or raw-material (copper) costs continuing to outrun the cost-reduction program.

## 3. The Single Most Important Catalyst

The single biggest swing factor for this stock is not one dated event — it is **whether Haier's margin erosion stabilizes or keeps going**, which is what separates the valuation module's bull case (CNY 31.78/share) from its headline structural-reset bear case (CNY 15.90/share), a 2.0x spread. That trajectory is driven by the same two forces named above (raw-material commodity costs and China's subsidy-fade demand), and it cannot be resolved by a single print — it will be tested incrementally: first at the 2026-08-27/28 H1 results (does gross margin hold near FY2025's 26.3%?), then at the inferred ~late-October 2026 Q3 results, then at the inferred ~March 2027 FY2026 annual results, where the moat module's "eroding" verdict (gross margin down 460bp over five years, ROIC falling for two straight readings) would either be confirmed or broken. A buyer cannot time this to one date — they can only watch it print-by-print, and the reverse-DCF's own honest caveat applies: today's price could be pricing in unprecedented (and possibly excessive) pessimism about this trajectory, or it could simply reflect a market discount rate materially higher than the DCF module's own low, mechanically-derived 4.10% WACC. Neither this module nor the valuation module can resolve that ambiguity from the data available.

## 4. Negative / Bearish Catalysts

- **China trade-in subsidy fade / high-base effect (ongoing through FY2026)** — management's own #1-named 2026 demand risk, already visible in Q1 2026's −6.2% YoY China retail print, with no confirmed replacement policy in the pool.
- **US / EU tariff persistence (ongoing, undated)** — overseas gross-margin gains were "offset by high tariffs" in FY2025; management's "tariff impact will gradually ease in 2026" claim is an unverified forecast, not a scheduled policy decision.
- **Raw-material cost inflation (ongoing, undated)** — 84.0% of appliance-segment cost of sales; Q4 2025 copper increases already overwhelmed the cost-reduction program and cut FY2025 gross margin by 1.1 percentage points.
- **H1 2026 print (2026-08-27/28)** — setup itself skews negative: two consecutive misses against an already-lowered consensus, and revision breadth remains net negative into the print (EPS 1 up / 11 down over the trailing ~3 months).
- **Related-party financial-services dependency on Haier Group Finance Co. (ongoing, undated)** — 57% of Group cash parked with the controlling shareholder's own finance arm, and related-party borrowings up ~19x year-on-year against a RMB 10,000mn cap; a further climb toward that cap, without an independent-bank pricing benchmark, is the one deterioration path management-governance flags as capable of moving Haier from "standard professional management" to a governance red flag.
- **2028 bond maturities (2028-02-25/26 and 2028-06-17/18)** — currently assessed as low risk (self-funded, reprice lower not higher), but included here because any deterioration in China's benchmark-rate trend or the company's own cash position between now and 2028 would change that read; buyers should not treat "low risk today" as "zero risk regardless of what happens between now and then."

## 5. Calendar Read

Of the eleven catalysts identified, five carry a proven, exact, or exact-window date (the dividend payment, the H1 2026 print, the buyback program's 12-month completion deadline, and the two 2028 bond maturities); the remaining six — two inferred future filing dates and four thematic/policy drivers (subsidy fade, tariffs, commodity costs, related-party dependency) — are real but genuinely undated, and are labelled Vague rather than dressed up as scheduled events. The one hard-dated, near-term event that can actually move the stock — the H1 2026 print on 2026-08-27/28 — skews bearish on setup: two straight misses against an already-cut consensus and net-negative revision breadth going in. The single most important timing caveat is that this calendar cannot pin down the variable that matters most (whether margin erosion stabilizes or continues): it will only be confirmed or denied incrementally, print by print, over the next two to three reporting cycles — not resolved by any one date on this calendar.

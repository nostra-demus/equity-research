## 5. Ownership / Insider Read

**Skin in the game is large in dollars and small in share, and none of it was bought.** The CEO and his firm ZMC hold 1,342,568 shares — about $291m at $216.61, roughly 15× his own ownership requirement — but that is 0.72% of the company, every share of it arrived as a grant under a related-party management agreement, and the only cash conviction purchase in the whole Form 4 record is a director's $10.0m buy in August 2018. Aggregate insider ownership is 0.95% and fell 11.7% in nine months.

**The insider signal is one-directional selling into the biggest catalyst the company has ever had.** Thirteen open-market sales for $85.2m in twelve months, **zero** purchases, with the controller side taking $72.4m of it — and the pool's export excludes automatic and derivative transactions, so that is a floor. But the inference people will reach for is **not proven**: the last insider sale was 2026-08-17, there was none in the 13 weeks into today's launch or before the weak 2026-11-09 print, and the two largest CEO sales came *after* the audited 10-K had already disclosed the November date. The one sale that cannot be cleared is 60,000 shares on 2026-03-02, inside the undated window of the second delay — and the reason it cannot be cleared is that this pool contains no 8-K and the material-event export is another company's.

**The biggest risk to minority holders is not control or pledging — it is that the people running the company own almost none of it and are contracted from outside it.** One share one vote, 99.6% float, no dual class, no controlling bloc, no pledge anywhere in the record and an anti-pledging policy in the proxy; Filter 6 has no subject because there is no controlling owner. What replaces that risk is structural rather than abusive: executive leadership sits in a private firm on an agreement running to 2029 that shareholders do not vote on annually, which collected $16.6m of fees plus $62.9m of stock compensation in FY2026 and is also the largest insider holder. That channel's economics belong to `09`; what belongs here is the consequence — the controller's upside comes from grants and fees, not from shares he bought, and in the last twelve months he has been converting those grants to cash rather than adding to them.

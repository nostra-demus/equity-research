**Verdict: Amber, not Green, and not Red — and the reasons are specific.**
- **Not Red.** The quantum is **1.19% of revenue**, inside A5-01's Green band (<1–5%) and inside the Materiality Thresholds' **Medium** band (1–5% of revenue/assets), nowhere near the 5–15%+ High/Critical line. I state that reading explicitly because the Materiality Thresholds table's RPT row lists "promoter-linked" among its Critical qualifiers; read literally and alone, that would make every disclosed controller-pay arrangement Critical on existence, which the module's own ban on flagging Red for existence alone, and the Regime & structure nuance rule, both forbid. - **Not Green.** A5-01's Green band requires "all at documented arm's length". "Recurring" and "promoter-linked" are two of the Red band's own qualitative disjuncts; what keeps the item off Red is the quantum plus the documented approval, not the absence of those features.

**The >25% hard lock: reported, not re-adjudicated.** At **1.19% of revenue** and **1.18% of total expenses**, the ratio is an order of magnitude below the 25% line. `business-model/01_disqualifier-scan.md` §1 row 3 recorded disqualifier 3 as **N**, with the bars computed at **$1,664.1** of related-party sales (25% of revenue) and **$1,690.2** of related-party purchases/expenses (25% of total expenses). **This agent defers to `business-model/01_disqualifier-scan` (hard lock) and does not re-decide it.** One refinement is handed back, because that agent recorded the amount as "not quantified anywhere in this pool": it **is** quantified, at Note 3, and the correct figure to set against the $1,690.2 expense bar is **$79.5** — i.e. **4.7% of the bar**. ## 14. Read

**About $79.5m a year leaves Take-Two for the controller's own firm, and that is the entire related-party ledger.** The single largest channel is the ZMC Advisors management agreement — $16.6m of cash consulting expense plus $62.9m of non-cash stock compensation on units granted to ZMC, **$79.5m in FY2026, $205.2m cumulatively over three years, 1.19% of revenue and 9.09% of total G&A**, with the equity leg alone taking **20.6% of every dollar of stock compensation the company booked**. It is not a trading relationship and not a margin skim: it is how the Executive Chairman/CEO and the President are paid, routed through a private-equity firm they founded and run rather than onto the payroll, with a severance multiple of **3× (fee + target bonus) = $29.7m** and 1.3m unvested units (0.70% of shares) still to settle. Because every GAAP year since FY2024 was a net loss, the charge cannot be expressed as a share of profit at all — $8.52bn of cumulative GAAP losses were posted while $205.2m went to the controller's vehicle, which says more about goodwill impairment than about leakage but is the honest framing of what minority holders paid for.

**Would the structure let leakage hide? Partly — and the weakness is structural, not transactional.** The group runs **145 entities across 31 jurisdiction labels with no published ownership chain**, three serially numbered holding companies implying at least four layers, roughly 20 subsidiaries whose business purpose cannot be identified from anything disclosed, and **82.2% of cash sitting in foreign subsidiaries at 2026-09-30 while all $2,500m of senior notes sit at the parent** and the repatriation tax is "not practicable to estimate". Against that: there are no VIEs or structured-finance entities, no loans or guarantees to any related party, no inter-corporate deposits, no promoter-linked customer anywhere on a $6.66bn revenue line, no royalty or brand fee (the core marks are owned inside the group), no related-party M&A including nothing aborted, every related-party counterparty named, a written $120,000 policy with the **independent directors** doing the approving, a Lead Independent Director with the ZMC agreement explicitly in her remit, and **4.66% say-on-pay dissent**. So the honest answer is that this group has the *capacity* for opacity and no *evidence* of using it — and the disclosure of the one thing that does flow is better than the pool suggested, not worse.

**The one disclosure that would settle the biggest open question: name the counterparty to the $62.1m non-cash acquisition of non-voting shares in a privately held entity, and say what was given for it.** That transaction quintupled the group's unconsolidated investment balance in six months, is structured in the one share class that keeps a stake of that size below significant influence, and sits in a fiscal year no proxy has yet covered — so it is the only material movement in this entire report whose counterparty nobody outside the company can identify. Everything else is named.

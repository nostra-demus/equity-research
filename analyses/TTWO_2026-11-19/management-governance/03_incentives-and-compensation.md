### 1A. Reconciling the three different numbers for the same arrangement (handoff item)

Three figures for the ZMC arrangement are in circulation across this module. They are not in conflict; they measure different things, and here is the arithmetic.

| Figure | What it actually measures | Basis | Source |
|---:|---|---|---|
| **$275,000 per month = $3.3m/yr** | The **fixed base management fee only** — one component | Contractual rate | `Web: FY2026 DEF 14A, Certain Relationships and Related Person Transactions`, via `07_people-integrity-dossiers.md` — and independently corroborated by the audited note's "annual management fee of $3.3" [`FY26 10-K, Note 3`] |
| **$16.6m (FY2026)** | The **cash-route consulting expense recorded in General and administrative expenses** — the base fee plus the performance bonus actually earned and any other cash components | US GAAP expense recognised | `FY26 10-K, Note 3 — Management Agreement` (the identical figure agent `07` reported from the proxy — same number, both documents) |
| **$79.5m (FY2026)** | **Total accounting cost of the arrangement** = $16.6m cash-route expense **+ $62.9m stock-based compensation expense** on units granted to ZMC. Arithmetic: 16.6 + 62.9 = 79.5 | US GAAP expense recognised | `FY26 10-K, Note 3`; `FY26 10-K, Note 16 — ZMC Non-Employee Awards`; computed by agent `01` (finding 01-021) and `business-model/11` |
| **$66.818m (FY2026)** | The proxy's own **"total compensation paid to ZMC in fiscal 2026"** figure, from CD&A page 37 ("Detailed ZMC"). Almost certainly a *cash-plus-grant-date-fair-value* basis rather than an expense basis — my own target-basis reconstruction from Note 16 lands at **≈$64.8m** (cash $16.6m + time-based 74k units × $226.28 + target market-based 112k × $225.09 + target performance-based 37.5k × $168.76 = $16.6m + $16.7m + $25.2m + $6.3m), which is within 3% of the proxy's figure | Cash + grant-date fair value (inferred) | `Web: search-engine restatement of FY2026 DEF 14A, CD&A p.37 "Detailed ZMC", retrieved 2026-11-19 — unverified; I could not read p.37 directly`. **Inference, not from filings** for the basis attribution |

**Verdict on the reconciliation:** the $3.3m fee is *inside* the $16.6m, which is *inside* the $79.5m. The $66.818m is the same economics measured on a grant-value rather than an expense basis. Nothing is double-counted and nothing contradicts; the only residual uncertainty is the exact composition of the proxy's $66.818m, which I could not read at source. The gap between the Summary Compensation Table's **$403,019** for the CEO and the **$66.8m–$79.5m** his firm received is not an error either — it is what happens when an executive is supplied under a contract rather than employed, and it is the single most important thing to know about reading this company's pay tables.

**Note the trend in the cash leg.** The consulting expense ran $6.5m (FY2024) → $10.2m (FY2025) → $16.6m (FY2026): **+155% in two years**, against net revenue up 24% over the same two years ($5,349.6m → $6,656.4m) [`FY26 10-K, Note 3`; `FY26 10-K, Consolidated Statements of Operations`]. The maximum cash available is $3.3m fee + $13.2m bonus = $16.5m, so FY2026's $16.6m means the performance bonus was earned at or very near its cap.

**The mix, with its basis stated (§15).** Note 16's ZMC table prints FY2026 grants of 74 thousand time-based, **224** market-based and **75** performance-based units, and footnotes that the market- and performance-based figures are the **maximum** number eligible to vest, with target = 50% of maximum. So the two bases are:

- **At target:** 74 + 112 + 37.5 = **223.5k units** → time 33.1%, relative TSR 50.1%, RCS 16.8%. **66.9% of the CEO vehicle's equity is performance- or market-conditioned.**
- **At maximum:** 74 + 224 + 75 = **373k units** → time 19.8%, relative TSR 60.1%, RCS 20.1%. **80.2% conditioned.**

Both are quoted wherever the mix appears below. Never mix them.

**Two things worth naming.** First, the CEO vehicle's equity is *more* TSR-weighted than the broad employee pool's. Company-wide FY2026 grants (employees and ZMC together, at 100% of target) were 1.2m time-based, 0.6m market-based and 1.0m performance-based — so for employees the dominant performance metric is RCS, while for ZMC it is relative TSR [`FY26 10-K, Note 16`, the three non-vested-activity tables]. Second, **no metric anywhere in this structure pays on return on capital, earnings per share, free cash flow per share, or cash conversion.** Return on invested capital (ROIC — the profit earned on each dollar of capital put into the business) has been **negative for four consecutive fiscal years**: −4.3% (FY2023), −3.1% (FY2024), −4.2% (FY2025), −0.8% (FY2026) [`CIQ Financials→Ratios, annual columns Mar-31-2023 → Mar-31-2026`, vendor data]. Nobody's pay is affected by that.

### 3C. Equity dilution and who pays for the pay (A6-04, and the minority-holder lens)

Shown separately because it is where compensation touches the minority holder's per-share claim directly.

- **Burn rate.** FY2026 grants, all three types at 100% of target: 1.2m + 0.6m + 1.0m = **2.8m units**, against **185,666,663 shares outstanding** at 2026-05-11 → **1.51% annual burn**. Inside the <2% green band [`FY26 10-K, Note 16`; `FY26 10-K, cover page`].
- **Overhang.** Unvested units at 2026-03-31, at 100% of target: 2.9m + 1.1m + 4.1m = **8.1m**, plus **11.2m** still available under the 2017 Plan = **19.3m = 10.4%** of shares outstanding — just over the <10% green line, hence Amber. Including the ESPP's 7.2m available shares the figure is **26.5m = 14.3%**; that basis is labelled because an employee purchase plan at 85% of market is not the same instrument as a free grant [`FY26 10-K, Note 16`].
- **No offset.** The board has authorised buybacks of up to 21.7m shares with 10.0m still available, and the company **repurchased nothing in FY2024, FY2025 or FY2026** [`FY26 10-K, Item 5 — Issuer Purchases of Equity Securities`]. So three straight years of roughly 1.5%-a-year grant dilution landed on existing holders with no buyback to absorb it.
- **Share count.** Weighted-average basic shares **170.1m (FY2024) → 175.1m (FY2025) → 183.9m (FY2026)**, +8.1% over two years. *Attribution, honestly:* management states the FY2026 jump of 8.8m shares was "**primarily due to our May 2025 underwritten public offering** of common stock, as well as normal stock compensation activity", so most of the latest year's dilution is a capital raise, not pay, and I do not charge it to compensation. The FY2025 step (+5.0m, no offering) is the cleaner compensation-driven read [`FY26 10-K, Item 7 — Net loss and Loss per share`; `FY26 10-K, Note 12 — Loss Per Share`].
- **Pricing.** No discount grants and no options: awards are restricted stock units priced at the grant-date closing price (time-based grants valued at $227.49 per share for employees and $226.28 for ZMC in FY2026), and market-based awards are Monte-Carlo valued. The only discounted instrument is the ESPP at 85%, a standard broad-based employee feature [`FY26 10-K, Note 16`].
- **Director pay.** Non-employee directors receive **restricted stock and stock grants, not options** — the plan's director forms are a "Form of Non-Employee Director Restricted Stock Agreement" (Ex. 10.10) and a "Form of Non-Employee Director Stock Grant Agreement" (Ex. 10.11), with **no director option form in existence**. The fee amounts live in the unreadable director-compensation table [`FY26 10-K, Exhibit Index, Ex. 10.10–10.11`].

## 4. Incentive Alignment Read

The pay rewards getting bigger and getting the share price to beat an index — not earning a return on the capital it takes to do either. The cash bonus (up to $13.2m a year) pays on **absolute Adjusted EBITDA**; the second equity metric pays on **Recurrent Consumer Spending**, a slice of revenue, on whichever of two grids pays more; and nothing in the structure touches return on capital, earnings per share, free cash flow per share or cash conversion — while ROIC has been negative for four straight years and GAAP earnings per share was missed in three of the last four. The genuinely good part is real and should be said plainly: **relative Total Shareholder Return against the NASDAQ-100 over three years, paying zero below the 40th percentile, carries the single largest weight in the CEO vehicle's equity (50.1% at target, 60.1% at maximum)** — that is a per-share test with a long window and a real floor, and it is better than most.

The single biggest incentive signal is structural, not metric: **the CEO is a contractor, not an employee.** His firm, ZMC Advisors, received $66.8m (proxy basis) or cost $79.5m (audited accounting basis) in FY2026 for supplying him and the President — 20.6% of the company's entire stock-compensation expense for two of its 12,909 people — while the Summary Compensation Table reports his pay as **$403,019**. Anyone who reads this company's pay through the standard table will understate the CEO's economics by roughly two orders of magnitude, and the $3.3m base fee is contractually fixed to 2029-03-31 regardless of anything. Set against five-year total shareholder return of **+11.8% versus +69.1% for the NASDAQ Composite** and four consecutive years of GAAP losses, with the cash leg of that fee up **155% in two years**, the magnitude is not matched by the delivery. The counterweights are genuine and are not averaged away: say-on-pay support of **95%**, a clawback policy that expressly reaches ZMC, double-trigger-only change-of-control vesting, no options and no repricing, 1.51% annual burn, and median employee pay rising **18.7%** — within a point of the executive rise, so the classic divergence flag does not fire.

**Conclusion: mixed.** One per-share metric doing most of the equity work, sitting on top of a size-based cash bonus, a revenue-based second equity metric, a magnitude at the top of the thin peer evidence available, and a delivery record the pay did not follow down.

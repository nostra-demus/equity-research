### Serial-acquirer + opportunity-cost test (CLAUDE.md §24, Filter 4)

The upstream quick-read (`business-model/11_capital-allocation-governance`) scored this signal 75 and carried RF-CAP-004 into the triage. This module is the dedicated deep-dive and **supersedes** that quick-read. I rebuilt the test from the filings rather than inheriting it. **It trips, and it trips on arithmetic, not on style.**

**Frequency and size.** Two material deals in four fiscal years. Zynga's ≈$9,168m build compares with the company's own **total capitalization of $4,059.9m** (total debt $250.2m + book equity $3,809.7m) at March 31, 2022 — **about 2.3x the entire pre-deal capital base** [CIQ Financials_Annual → Historical Capitalization — vendor export]. The 50.0 shares issued were **43.2% of the 115.8 shares outstanding before the deal**.

**Debt-funded.** $3,248.9m of long-term debt was issued in FY2023, taking total debt from **$250.2m (Mar-31-2022) to $3,682.1m (Mar-31-2023)** and later to a peak of **$4,122.3m (Jun-30-2024)** [CIQ Financials_Annual → Historical Capitalization and Cash Flow — vendor export; FY25 10-K, Consolidated Statements of Cash Flows]. A debt-funded deal at roughly twice the company's own pre-deal capital base is precisely the condition Filter 4 names.

**Outcome, in the company's own numbers.** $6,665.1m of acquisition-related write-downs — **84.7% of every dollar of goodwill the company has ever recorded**. The company itself ties the charge to the deal: "potential impairment charges incurred to write down the carrying amount of intangible assets generated as a result of an acquisition, such as the Goodwill impairment charge of approximately $3,500 we recognized during the fiscal year ended March 31, 2025" [FY26 10-K, Item 1A]. **One qualifier must travel with that attribution:** the FY2026 10-K attributes the goodwill impairments to "one of our reporting units" **without naming it**; the FY2025 10-K separately attributes the *intangible* impairments to the Zynga acquisition. So the Zynga attribution is explicit for the intangibles and strongly implied but **not named in the filing** for the $5,887.3m of goodwill.

**Opportunity cost, charged (not just the deal's own loss).** Three charges, each cited above and each a real cost of the deal rather than a footnote to it: (a) a **label sold** — Private Division and its title rights, at an $11.4m loss, for $32.7m; (b) **focus and people removed** — studios shut, two titles' development terminated, several rounds of layoffs, all under the cost programme that followed; (c) **$516.1m of cash interest over four years, equal to 91.5% of the CFO those four years produced** — money that existed as an obligation only because the deal was part-financed with debt. Bare "synergies / strategic fit" language was credited nowhere above.

**The moderating fact, stated plainly.** The deal-making has stopped. Business acquisitions were $(2.6)m in FY2026 and **nil** in the six months to September 30, 2026 (against $(2.0)m a year earlier) [FY26 10-K and Q2 FY27 10-Q, Consolidated Statements of Cash Flows]. No new material transaction is disclosed in either FY2027 10-Q or either FY2027 earnings release. Goodwill was **not** impaired in FY2026 and the FY2026 test passed: "our qualitative and quantitative assessments indicated that it is more likely than not that the fair value of our reporting units exceeds their carrying amounts" [FY26 10-K, Note 9]. The pattern has been dormant for roughly two and a half years.

**Verdict: RF-CAP-004 confirmed on the historical record, dormant on current conduct.** The M&A discipline component is capped per the MODULE_RULES rejector table, and the serial-acquirer pattern is **named as the most material capital-allocation signal** — not because deals are still happening, but because the write-down it produced is still the largest single fact in this company's capital history and still sits on the balance sheet as $1,061.9m of residual goodwill and $1,631.2m of acquired intangibles.

**Four facts that settle this section.**

1. **The program exists and has been left idle.** "Our Board has authorized the repurchase of up to 21.7 shares… As of March 31, 2026, we had repurchased a total of 11.7 shares… and 10.0 shares of our common stock remained available" [FY26 10-K, Item 5]. 2. **Average price paid — stated, with its basis.** No year-by-year average price is disclosed and none is computable for FY2023–FY2026 because the numerator and denominator are both zero. The only computable figure is the **cumulative implied cost of all treasury shares ever bought: $1,020.6m ÷ 23.7 shares = $43.06 per share** [FY26 10-K, Consolidated Balance Sheets — undated, multi-programme cumulative basis, not attributable to any single year]. Against today's $216.61 [`ciq_facts.json` `current_price` — CIQ Comps→Financial Data, as-of 2026-11-19], historic repurchases were made far below the current price — but that tells you about a decade-old programme, not about conduct in the window scored here. For FY2022, **the average price is not determinable.**
3. **A vendor-mapping error that must not travel (§5).** The CIQ cash-flow export shows "Repurchase of Common Stock" of $108.1m (FY23), $94.1m (FY24) and $2.9m (FY26). They are shares withheld to pay employees' taxes when restricted stock vests, **not buybacks**, and the filings say in terms that no shares were repurchased in those years. Anyone reading the vendor line as a buyback would record $205.1m of returns to shareholders that did not happen.
4. **Dilution was not offset — it was compounded.** Shares outstanding went **115.8 (Mar-31-2022) → 185.7 (Mar-31-2026) → 187.2 (Sep-30-2026), +61.7%** [CIQ Financials_Annual → Historical Capitalization — vendor export; `ciq_facts.json` `shares_outstanding_m`]. **The one genuinely favourable price-conduct fact, and it belongs on the other side of the ledger.** On **May 22, 2025 the company issued 5.5 shares at a price to the public of $225.00, raising $1,192.8m net** [FY26 10-K, Note 12], and used it to retire $1,150.0m of notes. Issuing stock high is the mirror image of buying it low, and it is price discipline on the issuance side — it is credited. But it also means the plain description of this company over the window is that it was a **net seller of its own equity** to continuing holders, not a buyer.

**Leverage and insider context.** The share-count increase did not coincide with rising leverage — total debt fell from the $4,122.3m peak to $2,911.4m (vendor basis, incl. ~$0.4bn of leases) at Sep-30-2026, and total debt / EBITDA from 8.77x (FY2025) to 3.64x (LTM Sep-26) [CIQ Financials_Annual → Ratios and Historical Capitalization — vendor export; `ciq_facts.json`]. ## 5. Capital-Allocation Read

**Capital allocation has destroyed per-share value, and the arithmetic is not close.** Since March 2022 the company has put roughly $9,856.7m of acquisition consideration and $2,476.7m of capitalised game development to work, funded almost entirely by $5,196.7m of new debt and $1,449.3m of new equity rather than by the business — which produced **minus $14.6m of cumulative free cash flow over five years**. The results, per share: **book value per share fell 42.5%** ($32.90 at Mar-31-2022 to $18.91 at Mar-31-2026), **EBITDA per share fell 26.7%** ($5.79 to $4.24) even as *absolute* EBITDA rose 17.4%, **basic EPS went from $3.62 to $(1.62)** with four straight loss years, and **revenue grew 89.9% while revenue per share grew 18.5%** — dilution ate roughly four-fifths of the growth. Tangible book value per share is now **negative $6.55**, against a peer-set median of **+$3.20**, and **8 of the 10 peers carry net cash while TTWO carries net debt** [CIQ Comps → Financial Data, as-of 2026-11-19 — vendor export]. The market's own scorecard agrees: $100 invested on March 31, 2021 was worth **$111.77** at March 31, 2026, against **$169.11** in the NASDAQ Composite and **$176.74** in the S&P 500 — **57.3 and 65.0 percentage points behind** [FY26 10-K, Item 5 — Stock Performance Graph].

**Best use of capital: debt reduction, with one qualification.** $3,864.9m repaid over five years took total debt from a $4,122.3m peak down to $2,911.4m and leverage from 8.77x to 3.64x total debt / EBITDA, with interest cover of 5.3x, all-fixed-rate maturities and the nearest wall at $600m in 2027 [CIQ Financials_Annual → Ratios and Historical Capitalization; `ciq_facts.json` `debt_maturity_wall`, `interest_coverage_x`]. The qualification is that the FY2026 $1,150.0m repayment was funded by **selling equity at $225.00**, not by operating cash — undoing a financing mistake by issuing stock, which was at least done at a good price (3.9% above today's $216.61).

**Worst use of capital: the Zynga acquisition.** $9,168m committed, 43.2% dilution of the pre-deal share base, $3,248.9m of debt taken on, and **$6,665.1m written off — 84.7% of all the goodwill the company has ever recorded** — plus the opportunity cost charged above: a label sold at a loss, studios closed, two titles' development terminated, and $516.1m of cash interest equal to 91.5% of four years' operating cash flow.

**The single biggest capital-allocation signal is the serial-acquirer pattern (RF-CAP-004, confirmed on independently rebuilt numbers).** Two material deals in four years, the larger at about 2.3x the company's own pre-deal capital base and part debt-funded, followed by write-downs of 84.7% of all goodwill ever recorded. CLAUDE.md §24 calls this close to a disqualifier, and the evidence here supports the filter rather than merely inheriting the quick-read's conclusion. Two facts pull the other way and must travel with it: the deal-making has been **dormant for roughly two and a half years** (business acquisitions $(2.6)m in FY2026, nil in H1 FY2027, no new material transaction disclosed), and FY2026 brought no goodwill impairment with the recoverability test passed. What is *not* yet evidence either way is *Grand Theft Auto VI*, launched today, carrying $2,149.7m of unreleased-title cost — a bet whose outcome is unquantified in this pool and which cannot be scored as an allocation result until it reports.

**Conclusion: value-destructive / size-driven.**

Two honest limits on it: the filing does not state coupon payment dates, so the accrual is inferred from each maturity date; and the fair values are **as of 2026-09-30**, about seven weeks stale at this report's date. That staleness is why basis (b) is carried alongside rather than discarded.

**The basis mismatch in (b), named rather than buried (§15 matched basis).** The 116 bp figure is an **index-level option-adjusted spread across all BBB US corporate bonds of every duration**, not a matching-tenor spread on TTWO's own paper. Adding it to a single-point Treasury yield is an approximation, and the two web inputs are from different dates (2026-11-19 and 2026-11-18). The internal consistency check is reassuring but not proof: the own-bond read (a) at ~1.5 years was 5.27% on 2026-09-30, against the ~5.95% two-year indicative figure now, so the direction of travel is a wider cost, consistent with the 2-year Treasury having firmed.

**What the floating share means if rates move — the answer is "nothing on the drawn debt, and the exposure runs the other way."** With 0% of drawn borrowings floating and no swaps, a **+200 bp** rate shock changes drawn interest cost by **$0.0**. Two real exposures remain, and neither is a cost: (i) **if** the revolver were fully drawn at its $997.9 availability, +200 bp would add **$20.0 a year** — a contingent figure, not a current one; (ii) the company's rate exposure is predominantly on the **asset** side — "$524.2 of short-term investments… also had $1,149.4 of cash and cash equivalents that are comprised primarily of money market funds and bank-time deposits," on which it concluded "there was no material interest rate risk exposure… or liquidity as of September 30, 2026" [`Q2 FY27 10-Q, Item 3`]. Interest **income** was $85.1 in FY2026 [`01` §5a, from `FY26 10-K, Note 17 (Interest and Other, Net)`], so **falling** rates, not rising ones, are the adverse direction for TTWO's net interest line. That is the opposite of the usual floating-rate worry and must not be restated as "no rate risk".

**One rating-linked term to carry forward.** The revolver's spread is "determined by the Company's credit rating" [`Q2 FY27 10-Q, Note 10`], so a downgrade raises the cost of the one facility the company would lean on in a closed market. The only rating in the pool is a vendor indication — S&P Foreign Currency LT **BBB** [`CIQ Credit Health Panel → Summary`, LTM period ending 2026-09-30, financials updated 2026-11-10 — tier-5 vendor export]. **No rating-agency rationale or outlook is in the pool**, so no rating trajectory may be inferred, and the size of any pricing step is not disclosed. No commitment fee is disclosed anywhere in the pool either.

### The cover, arithmetic shown

**Is the near-term wall covered by cash and FCF, or does it need market access?** The next 12 months' $629.4 is covered **1.83x by cash & equivalents alone** ($1,149.4), before any FCF, before the $524.2 of short-term investments and before the revolver [`Q2 FY27 10-Q, Condensed Consolidated Balance Sheets`; `Note 10 (Debt)`]. The next 24 months' $1,429.4 is covered **1.17x by cash plus liquid short-term investments** ($1,673.6) with no market access at all, and **1.87x** if the $997.9 of committed, non-borrowing-base revolver availability is included — so on the balance sheet as filed, the wall does **not** require an open bond market. What it does require is that the cash be replenished: paying the whole 24-month wall out of liquidity would leave about **$244.2** on the balance sheet, against realised **LTM FCF of only $62.6** and a **−$336.8** FCF half-year just reported, so the practical funding of the 2028 Notes rests on the company's guided **FY2027 CFO of over $1,000.0 and FCF of more than ~$710** landing — which in turn rests on one product cycle, Grand Theft Auto VI, released **2026-11-19** and with **no reported period in this pool**. On rating posture and recent refi activity, the only rating available is a vendor indication of **S&P BBB** with no agency rationale or outlook in the pool, and the vendor's relative credit-health scores put TTWO in the **bottom quartile on all four of overall, operational, solvency and liquidity against 25 interactive-entertainment peers** [`CIQ Credit Health Panel → Summary`, LTM ending 2026-09-30 — tier-5 vendor export]; recent activity has been **repayment, not refinancing** — $600.0 of 2025 Notes repaid 2025-04-14 and $550.0 of 2026 Notes repaid 2026-03-28, both "with proceeds from our May 2025 equity issuance", and the revolver was amended on 2025-05-19 to lift the commitment from $750.0 to $1,000.0 and extend it to 2030-05-19, which is a completed, committed refinancing of the facility itself [`Q2 FY27 10-Q, Note 10 (Debt)`; `FY26 10-K, Note 11 (Debt)`]. Because **0% of drawn borrowings float and no swaps are disclosed**, none of the interest cost on existing debt reprices if rates move; the repricing happens only when a note is refinanced, at roughly **+90 to +145 bps** (§3), and the asset side — $1,673.6 of cash and deposits earning $85.1 of interest income in FY2026 — reprices in the *opposite* direction.

## 5. Refinancing Read

The wall is near and concentrated but pre-funded: **$629.4 (24.9%) comes due within 12 months and $1,429.4 (56.5%) within 24**, on a weighted-average maturity of just **2.93 years**, with the single largest bucket being **$1,400.0 (55.3%) inside fiscal 2028** — though that is two payments, **$600.0 on 2027-04-14 and $800.0 on 2028-03-28**, eleven and a half months apart, not one cliff; against that sits **$1,149.4 of cash, $524.2 of liquid short-term investments and $997.9 of committed undrawn revolver availability**, all at 2026-09-30 [`Q2 FY27 10-Q, Note 10 (Debt)`, `Condensed Consolidated Balance Sheets`]. Refinancing rather than repaying would cost roughly **+90 to +145 bps** over the **4.59%** weighted-average coupon — about **$12.5–20.3 a year** on the $1,400.0 fiscal-2028 wall, or **$22.5–36.7** if the whole $2,529.4 were refinanced at today's indicative level, which is 8–14% of FY2026's $151.4 interest expense and small beside guided FY2027 EBITDA of $1,024–1,082 (a company forecast, non-GAAP, on the company's own definition). The single biggest refinancing risk is not the market: it is that **the cash paying this wall is replenished almost entirely by one product cycle** — realised LTM free cash flow (CFO minus total capex) is **$62.6** and the latest half-year was **−$336.8**, so if the Grand Theft Auto VI cycle launched 2026-11-19 does not convert into the guided **over $1,000.0 of operating cash flow**, TTWO either drains liquidity to roughly **$244.2** by 2028 or goes to market from the **bottom credit-health quartile of its 25-peer group** on a vendor panel, with a revolver whose spread is set by a credit rating that has **no agency rationale or outlook in this pool**.

**Does it survive the next 12 months under market closure (no new unsecured issuance)?

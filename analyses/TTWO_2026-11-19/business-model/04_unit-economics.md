# Unit Economics — TTWO

**Regime:** US / SEC domestic filer, US GAAP, USD, fiscal year ends March 31. FY2026 = year ended March 31, 2026; FY2027 is in progress. All dollar figures in millions unless stated. Sources used: `FY26 10-K` (year ended March 31, 2026), `FY25 10-K` (year ended March 31, 2025), `Q2 FY27 10-Q` (quarter and six months ended September 30, 2026), `Q2 FY27 investor deck` and `Q2 FY27 earnings call` (both November 9, 2026), `Q1 FY27 earnings call` (August 7, 2026), and the tier-5 `CIQ Financials_Annual` export.

**Upstream used:** `03_segment-map.md` was present and read. It finds ONE reportable segment — "sale of software titles", 100% of $6,656.4 FY26 revenue, with segment profit equal to the consolidated operating loss of $(104.2) [`FY26 10-K, Note 21, p.102`]. There is therefore no segment-level margin split to work with, and the unit below is chosen for the whole company.

**Hard disclosure limit, carried from `02_business-identity.md` and confirmed independently here:** the pool contains almost no engagement data. The FY26 10-K discloses no monthly or daily active users, no paying-player count, no revenue per player, and no quantified player-acquisition cost. So the volume half of `players × spend per player` **cannot be read** — it is "Not proven from available data", not an estimation problem. Two transcript fragments are the only exceptions and are used below: a 15% rise in average daily active users for NBA 2K26 [`Q1 FY27 earnings call, Aug 7 2026, prepared remarks`] and 11 million registered users for one China mobile title [`Q2 FY27 earnings call, Nov 9 2026, prepared remarks`].

**Pool notes honoured:** the DEF 14A export (July 27, 2026) is an empty "no tabular data" shell and was not used; `Alphabet-Inc-NasdaqGS-GOOGL-Key-Developments` is a misfiled export for a different issuer and was not read as TTWO evidence.

## 1. Natural Unit

**The natural economic unit for the "sale of software titles" segment is one released game title — the franchise release that Take-Two funds with development capital up front, then earns back over that title's life from full-game sales plus continuing in-game spending.**

This is the unit the company itself capitalises and amortises: development costs are capitalised "on a product-by-product basis" and amortisation "is recorded on a **title-by-title basis** in cost of revenue" [`FY26 10-K, Note 1 — Software Development Costs and Licenses`]. So the title-level profit-and-loss exists inside the company; it is simply not published.

Three secondary unit types sit inside the same segment, and each is named here rather than modelled: **the individual paying player** (mobile in-app purchases, where 50.1% of FY26 revenue sits — unreadable, no player counts or spend-per-player disclosed) [`FY26 10-K, MD&A, p.44`]; **the sold-in copy** (lifetime franchise unit counts are disclosed, per-period units and price per unit are not); and **the advertising impression** (FY26 advertising revenue $494.3 of $6,656.4, with no impression count and no price per thousand impressions disclosed) [`FY26 10-K, Consolidated Statements of Operations`].

## 3. Value Creation Read

**Unclear at the unit level — the per-unit math cannot be done from what Take-Two discloses — and the only hard aggregate evidence available points the other way.** The value test (contribution margin × unit lifetime > cost to build the unit) needs per-title revenue and per-title cost; the company publishes neither, so no title can be tested. What can be tested is the sum of all titles, and over the three audited years FY24–FY26 the sum does not clear: total content spend was $5,011.9 ($1,983.9 capitalised cash plus $3,028.0 of expensed research and development) against gross profit less selling and marketing of $4,109.0 — a shortfall of **$902.9** over three years [`FY26 10-K, MD&A, p.43`; `FY26 10-K, Consolidated Statements of Cash Flows`]. The audited bottom line says the same: operating losses in each of the last three years — $(104.2) FY26, $(4,391.1) FY25, $(3,590.6) FY24 — and $5,887.3 of accumulated goodwill impairment against gross goodwill of $6,949.2, i.e. roughly 85% of all goodwill ever recorded written off, caused by "a reduction in the forecasted performance of the reporting unit" [`FY26 10-K, MD&A, p.43`; `FY26 10-K, Note 9, p.83`; `FY26 10-K, Critical Accounting Policies — Goodwill`]. A write-off of that size is the audited statement that one large block of acquired game units did not earn back what was paid for it.

**Ring-fence on the cycle, in both directions.** FY26 is **not** a peak-cycle year and must not be read as one: it is the last full year before the largest launch in the company's history, with Grand Theft Auto VI released November 19, 2026 [`Q2 FY27 10-Q`; `Q2 FY27 investor deck, p.13`]. Equally, FY27 will not be a structural year. The through-cycle return series shows both ends: return on invested capital (the profit earned on each dollar of capital put into the business) ran 6.0% → 6.9% → 7.4% → 11.2% → 13.4% → 9.0% in FY2017–FY2022, then turned negative in every year since — $(4.3)\%$ FY2023, $(3.1)\%$ FY2024, $(4.2)\%$ FY2025, $(0.8)\%$ FY2026, $(1.0)\%$ for the twelve months to Sep 30, 2026 [`CIQ Financials_Annual → Ratios`, retrieved 2026-11-19 — **tier-5 vendor export, not a filing**]. So the honest through-cycle read is: single-digit-to-low-teens positive returns before FY2023, negative for four straight years since, with the best year in a decade still only 13.4%.

**Why the three-year shortfall is not yet proof of destruction.** $2,149.7 of the $2,346.3 capitalised content balance — 91.6% — relates to titles **not yet released** [`FY26 10-K, Note 7, p.83`], so FY24–FY26 spending was substantially building revenue for FY27 and later while FY24–FY26 gross profit carried only older vintages. That makes the three-year comparison conservative-biased against the company by a size the filings do not let an outsider measure. The test resolves in FY27, and management's own guidance is the first resolution point: GAAP net revenue $7,980–$8,180 with GAAP net income of **$109–$142**, i.e. a net margin of roughly **1.3%–1.8%** in the year carrying the biggest release the company has ever had [`Q2 FY27 investor deck, p.7`]. That is thin for a cycle peak, and it is guidance, not a result.

**Single most valuable missing disclosure:** a title-level vintage statement — for each released title, cumulative net revenue to date against the capitalised development cost of that title. The company already computes both halves (amortisation is "recorded on a title-by-title basis"), and revenue is partly triangulable from the concentration percentages; the cost side is not triangulable at all, which is why one title-level vintage table would do more than any other single disclosure.

## 4. Sensitivity

**Revenue per unit — the hit outcome — dominates, by roughly two to one over the next-largest lever.** A 20% move in top-five-franchise revenue is **$722.9** (20% of $3,614.4), against an FY26 operating loss of $(104.2) — a swing about seven times the size of the loss, and on its own enough to decide whether the year is profitable. The next-largest levers are smaller and close to each other: a 20% relative move in the sales-linked cost ratio (26.2% of revenue → 21.0% or 31.4%) is **$348.9**, and a 20% move in total content spend ($1,763.5) is **$352.7**. Price per unit is the smallest lever of the three that can be sized — a 20% move in all full-game-and-other revenue is $292.0 (20% of $1,459.8) — and the company routinely gives price away anyway, cutting wholesale prices "typically three to nine months after a product's initial launch" [`FY26 10-K, Item 1 — Marketing`]. Retention cannot be sized: a 20% shortening of the disclosed five-to-fifteen-month player life would move both the revenue-recognition period and the amortisation schedule, but with no player count or spend-per-player disclosed, the dollar effect is **not assessable** from this pool — the same gap applies to the platform fee, whose rate is nowhere in the pool even though Apple and Google "generally have the discretion to set the amounts of their platform fees… at their sole discretion" [`FY26 10-K, MD&A, p.44`].

**Which has historically been most volatile: per-unit revenue, clearly.** Group revenue rose 18.2% in FY26 on five franchise moves, then fell 7.5% in the six months to September 30, 2026 [`FY26 10-K, MD&A, p.44`; `Q2 FY27 10-Q, MD&A, p.29`]. Q2 FY27 net bookings came in at $1,601 against guidance of $1,620–$1,670, with recurrent consumer spending down 9% against guidance of down 5% [`Q2 FY27 investor deck, p.4`]. Vendor EBITDA swung $380 (FY25) → $788 (FY26) → $725 (twelve months to Sep 30, 2026) [`CIQ Financials_Annual → Income Statement` — tier-5 vendor export]. And the timing of a single unit has slipped repeatedly: the FY25 10-K stated Grand Theft Auto VI was planned for May 26, 2026; the FY26 10-K states November 19, 2026 [`FY25 10-K, MD&A`; `FY26 10-K, MD&A, p.44`]. By contrast the cost ratios moved only 205bp (sales-linked costs) and 363bp (content spend as a share of revenue) between FY25 and FY26. The volatility is in what each unit earns and when it ships, not in what it costs to make or in its price.

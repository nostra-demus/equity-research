# Segment Map — TTWO

**Regime:** US / SEC domestic filer, US GAAP, USD, fiscal year ends March 31. FY2026 = year ended March 31, 2026. All dollar figures in millions unless stated. Source basis: `FY26 10-K` (year ended March 31, 2026), `Q2 FY27 10-Q` (quarter ended September 30, 2026), `Q2 FY27 investor deck` (November 9, 2026), and the tier-5 `CIQ Financials_Annual → Segments` export.

**Headline:** Take-Two is a **single-segment** company by its own audited disclosure — one operating and reportable segment, "sale of software titles", 100% of revenue and 100% of profit (a loss, in each of the last three audited years) [`FY26 10-K, Note 21 (Segment Reporting and Geographic Information), p.102`]. There is no second segment to compare it against, and no segment-level profit split exists anywhere in the pool. What the company *does* disclose instead are four ways of cutting the same single segment's revenue (content type, platform, distribution channel, geography) — with no profit attached to any of them.

## 2. Dominant Segment

**Take-Two is a single-segment business and says so in its own audited segment note: one operating and reportable segment, "sale of software titles", 100% of revenue and 100% of the result.** No sub-segments are manufactured here. The CODM — the Chief Executive Officer — "manages our operations on a consolidated basis" and evaluates performance on consolidated net income (loss), supplemented only by sales information by product category, major product title, and platform [`FY26 10-K, Note 21, p.102`]. The single-segment presentation is confirmed by the vendor export, which carries one business segment ("Sale of Software Titles") equal to total revenue in every one of the ten annual columns from FY2017 to FY2026 [`CIQ Financials_Annual → Segments` — tier-5 vendor export].

Because there is only one segment, the profit-first dominance test cannot discriminate: the dominant segment by profit is the same single segment, and it reported an operating loss of $(104.2) and a net loss of $(298.2) in FY26 [`FY26 10-K, MD&A, p.43`]. **Limitation stated plainly: there is no segment-level profit disclosure below the consolidated total anywhere in this pool**, so any statement about where the money is actually made inside Take-Two rests on revenue cuts and on one directional margin comment from management, not on disclosed segment profit.

What the revenue cuts do tell you about where the value sits: **continuing in-game spending, not game launches, is the larger half of the business** — recurrent consumer spending was 78.1% of FY26 revenue ($5,196.6) and rose to 82.5% of revenue in the six months to September 30, 2026 ($2,500.0 of $3,030.9) [`FY26 10-K, MD&A, p.44`; `Q2 FY27 10-Q, Note 4 and MD&A, p.24`]. **Mobile is the largest single platform** at 50.1% of FY26 revenue, slightly ahead of console at 39.0% [`FY26 10-K, MD&A, p.44`] — and mobile is the platform management identifies as carrying the *lower* gross margin, because Apple and Google platform fees run through cost of revenue [`Q2 FY27 10-Q, MD&A, p.23`]. So the biggest revenue line is explicitly not the best-margin revenue line, and the filings do not quantify by how much.

That mix is in the middle of a large, dated shift. Grand Theft Auto VI launched **November 19, 2026** [`Q2 FY27 10-Q`; `Q2 FY27 investor deck, p.13`], and FY27 guidance of $8,000–$8,200 net bookings with recurrent consumer spending "flat YoY" implies RCS falling to roughly **65% of total net bookings** in FY27 from 78% in FY26 [`Q2 FY27 investor deck, pp.5 and 8`]. Guidance assumes the Grand Theft Auto series up, NBA 2K up high single digits, and **mobile down** [`Q2 FY27 investor deck, p.8`]. In the September 2026 quarter mobile revenue had already fallen $90.5 year on year to $731.1 [`Q2 FY27 10-Q, MD&A, p.24`]. The single segment's internal centre of gravity is therefore moving from mobile in-game spending toward console full-game sales — a change in margin mix and in cyclicality that no segment disclosure will let an outsider measure directly.

## 3. Segment Disclosure Quality

**Consistent year over year, and consistently thin.** The segment definition has not changed: the FY25 10-K and the FY26 10-K use nearly identical wording for one operating and reportable segment [`FY25 10-K, Note 22 (Segment Reporting and Geographic Information)`; `FY26 10-K, Note 21, p.102`], the Q2 FY27 10-Q repeats it [`Q2 FY27 10-Q, Note 14`], and the vendor export shows a single business segment in all ten annual columns FY2017–FY2026 [`CIQ Financials_Annual → Segments` — tier-5 vendor export]. The only visible change in the last three years is additive, not a redefinition: the FY26 note now itemises "other segment items" and states that all significant expense categories appear on the consolidated statements of operations and that segment assets equal consolidated total assets — the newer US GAAP segment-reporting disclosures. The FY25 note listed goodwill impairment and loss on fair-value adjustments among other segment items; the FY26 note does not, simply because those line items were nil or absent in FY26 [`FY25 10-K, Note 22`; `FY26 10-K, Note 21, p.102`]. **There is no "Other" or "Corporate" bucket at all** — with one segment there is nothing to bucket, so the >10% "Other" test is not applicable rather than passed. **Profit is disclosed only at the consolidated level**: the segment measure of profit *is* consolidated net income (loss), and segment assets *are* consolidated total assets, so the segment note adds no information beyond the face of the financial statements. Three real gaps follow, and downstream agents should treat them as hard limits, not as estimation problems: (1) **no profit, margin, EBITDA, or capital-employed figure exists for mobile vs console vs PC, for RCS vs full game, or for US vs International** — only a qualitative statement that mobile gross margin is lower than console or PC [`Q2 FY27 10-Q, MD&A, p.23`]; (2) **the three labels (Rockstar Games, 2K, Zynga) are described in the Business section but never given revenue or profit** [`FY26 10-K, Item 1 Business, pp.4–6`], so any label-level split would be invention; (3) **the two goodwill reporting units are unnamed**, even though one of them absorbed $5,887.3 of accumulated impairment and is down to $570.0 of goodwill — the company tells you an internal economic split exists and withholds its identity and its size [`FY26 10-K, Critical Accounting Policies — Goodwill`; `FY26 10-K, Note 9, pp.83–84`]. Two pool-level notes, carried from triage: the DEF 14A export (July 27, 2026) is an empty "no tabular data" shell, so proxy-derived segment or incentive detail is unavailable (it would matter for management-governance, not for the segment map — note, though, that the FY26 10-K's own equity-award exhibits tie performance units to **recurrent consumer spending** net bookings, which is the closest thing in the pool to management running the business by one of these revenue cuts [`FY26 10-K, Exhibit — form of performance-based RSU award agreement`]); and `Alphabet Inc NasdaqGS GOOGL Key Developments.xls` is a misfiled export for a different issuer and was not read as TTWO evidence.

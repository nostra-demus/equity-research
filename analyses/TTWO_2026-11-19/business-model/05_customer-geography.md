# Customer And Geography Map — TTWO

**Regime:** US / SEC domestic filer (NasdaqGS:TTWO, Delaware), US GAAP, reporting currency USD, fiscal year ends **March 31** — "FY2026" means the year to March 31, 2026 [`FY26 10-K, cover page`; `00_data-triage.md §2A`]. All dollar figures are USD millions as the filing states them.

**What "customer" means here.** Take-Two does not sell most of its games to players directly. It sells through the companies that own the storefronts and consoles — Apple's App Store, the Google Play Store, Sony's PlayStation network, Microsoft's Xbox network, Valve's Steam — plus physical retailers and distributors. Those platform owners are the company's accounting customers, and that is why four of them each exceed 10% of revenue. [`FY26 10-K, Item 1 — Sales, p.4`; `FY26 10-K, Note 1 (Concentration of Credit Risk and Accounts Receivable), p.68`]

## 4. Read

This is a concentrated business on both sides, and the customer side is the sharper of the two: five customers are **80.6% of FY26 net revenue** and two of them individually clear 22% [`FY26 10-K, Note 1, p.68`], while one country — the United States — is **59.2%** of revenue [`FY26 10-K, Note 21, p.102`]. The concentration is close to naked rather than contractually secured: the company states in its own risk factors that sales "are made primarily without long-term agreements or other commitments, and our customers may terminate their relationship with us at any time" [`FY26 10-K, Item 1A, p.23`], and two of the four >10% customers — Apple and Google — are governed only by platform terms of service the customer itself can rewrite [`FY26 10-K, Item 1A, p.11`]. What contracts do exist are thin cover: both the Sony and Microsoft console licences expire March 31, 2027 with one-year automatic renewals, and Sony holds a right to terminate "for any or no reason upon 30 days' notice" [`FY26 10-K, Item 1 — Arrangements with Platform Manufacturers, pp.3–4`].

**The single biggest dependency for the synthesizer:** Take-Two reaches its players almost entirely through four gatekeepers — Apple, Sony, Google and Microsoft, each individually above 10% of net revenue and together inside a top five that is 80.6% of it [`FY26 10-K, Item 1 — Sales, p.4`; `Note 1, p.68`] — on terms those gatekeepers set, can change, and in Sony's case can cancel on 30 days' notice. Every one of them also publishes or distributes competing games, which the filing itself flags: "because some of our customers are also publishers of games for their own hardware platforms… such customers may give priority to their own products or those of our competitors" [`FY26 10-K, Item 1A, p.23`].

**Not proven from available data:** which named customer corresponds to which disclosed percentage (the 10-K separates the names from the numbers); any country-level revenue split inside the 40.8% International bucket; and the size of any relationship named in the Capital IQ Customers export, which proves existence only.

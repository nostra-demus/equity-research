The pattern here trips the filter, and it trips it on hard numbers rather than on a judgment about style.

*Frequency and size.* Two material deals inside four fiscal years, plus a tail of small ones. Zynga closed May 23, 2022; the consideration build is 46.3m shares worth $5,377.7m, a further 3.7m shares worth $479.7m issued for Zynga's assumed convertible notes [FY25 10-K, Consolidated Statements of Stockholders' Equity, FY2023 rows], and $3,310.9m of cash acquisitions in FY2023 [CIQ Financials_Annual → Cash Flow — vendor export; this cash line is the vendor's full-year total and may include other small deals] — summing to roughly **$9,168m**, with the components shown so the reader can rebuild it (§15). Gearbox closed June 2024 for 2.8m shares worth $448.6m [FY26 10-K, Consolidated Statements of Stockholders' Equity]. Smaller items continue: $3.1m of goodwill from "immaterial acquisitions" in FY2025 and $27.4m, $20.3m and $27.4m of asset acquisitions in FY2026, FY2025 and FY2024 [FY26 10-K, Note 9; FY26 10-K, Consolidated Statements of Cash Flows].

*Size relative to the company, and how it was funded.* The Zynga build of roughly $9,168m compares with the company's own total capitalization of **$4,059.9m** (total debt plus book equity) at March 31, 2022 — the deal was about 2.3x the entire pre-deal capital base on that basis [CIQ Financials_Annual → Historical Capitalization — vendor export]. The 50.0m shares issued were 43% of the 115.8m shares outstanding before the deal. And it was part debt-funded: $3,248.9m of long-term debt was issued in FY2023, taking total debt from $250.2m to $3,682.1m [CIQ Financials_Annual → Cash Flow and Historical Capitalization — vendor export]. A debt-funded deal at roughly half the company's own value is the exact condition the filter names.

*Outcome.* $6,665.1m of acquisition-related write-downs, built from $5,887.3m of accumulated goodwill impairment ($3,545.2m in FY2025 and $2,342.1m in FY2024) plus $777.8m of acquisition-related intangible impairments ($577.4m FY24, $137.0m + $39.3m FY25, $24.1m FY26) [FY26 10-K, Note 9]. That is 84.7% of the $6,949.2m of gross goodwill the company has ever recorded, leaving $1,061.9m net. The company's own risk-factor language ties it to the deal: "potential impairment charges incurred to write down the carrying amount of intangible assets generated as a result of an acquisition, such as the Goodwill impairment charge of approximately $3,500 we recognized during the fiscal year ended March 31, 2025" [FY26 10-K, Item 1A]. One qualifier must travel with this: the FY2026 10-K attributes the goodwill impairments to "one of our reporting units" without naming it; the FY2025 10-K separately attributes the intangible impairments to "our Zynga acquisition" [FY25 10-K, MD&A — Gross profit discussion]. So the Zynga attribution is explicit for the intangibles and strongly implied but **not named in the filing** for the goodwill.

*Opportunity cost, not just the deal's own loss.* The Private Division label — and "our rights to substantially all of the label's titles" — was sold in October 2024 [FY25 10-K, Item 1], producing $32.7m of proceeds, removing $15.8m of goodwill and booking an $11.4m loss on divestitures [FY26 10-K, Note 9; FY25 10-K, Consolidated Statements of Cash Flows and segment disclosures]. The company describes a wider retreat: divestitures and "shutting down studios, that no longer fit our long-term strategies," and "several rounds of layoffs in the recent" period [FY25 10-K, Item 1A]. Title cancellations and restructuring write-downs ran $3,805.9m (FY25) and $3,038m (FY24) on the vendor's "Asset Writedown & Restructuring Costs" line [CIQ Financials_Annual → Cash Flow — vendor export]. Businesses sold under deal pressure and attention pulled off the existing franchises are part of the charge, not a footnote to it.

*What keeps it off a higher score.* The deal-making has stopped. Business acquisitions were $(2.6)m in FY2026 [FY26 10-K, Consolidated Statements of Cash Flows] and nil in the six months to September 30, 2026 against $(2.0)m a year earlier [Q2 FY27 10-Q, Condensed Consolidated Statements of Cash Flows]. No new material transaction is disclosed in either FY2027 10-Q or either FY2027 earnings release. That is why this row sits at 75 and not higher. Bare "synergies / strategic fit" language is not treated as evidence of value creation anywhere above, and none was credited.

## 2. Classification

**Capital allocation concerns.**

Specific, cited issues with how capital has been spent, against a governance structure that is otherwise largely clean. On the capital side: $6,665.1m of acquisition-related write-downs, 84.7% of all goodwill ever recorded [FY26 10-K, Note 9]; share count up 61.7% since March 2022 with zero buybacks in three years [CIQ Historical Capitalization — vendor export; FY26 10-K, Item 5]; $688.9m a year going into capitalized game development while return on capital is −0.8% [FY26 10-K, Consolidated Statements of Cash Flows; `ciq_facts.json`]; and $1,150m of debt repaid out of equity proceeds rather than operating cash, with operating cash flow negative in FY2024 and FY2025 [Q2 FY27 10-Q, Debt note; FY26 10-K, MD&A cash-flow table].

On the governance side the evidence does not support the harsher "Governance red flags" label: Ernst & Young has audited the company without interruption for 20 years and gave unqualified opinions on both the FY2026 accounts and internal control [FY26 10-K, Report of Independent Registered Public Accounting Firm]; no restatement is disclosed; nine of ten directors are independent with a named Lead Independent Director [CIQ Board Members export — vendor export]; the filing states there are no material unconsolidated entities or variable interest entities [FY26 10-K, MD&A — Off-Balance Sheet Arrangements]; and the vendor's supply-chain graph shows 0 intra-group rows out of 28 disclosed relationship rows, a 0.0% intra-group share — no sign that disclosed trading runs through the company's own entities [CIQ Suppliers/Customers export (TTWO), retrieved 2026-11-19 — vendor export; scope is the vendor's own two-year recently-disclosed window for current subsidiaries, not the full counterparty base, and it proves relationships exist, never their size]. The three counterparties the graph flags `likely_group` are Epic Games, Inc., Sony Interactive Entertainment Inc. and Prima Interactive (Pty) Ltd — plainly outside platform and distribution partners, so that flag reads as a vendor name-and-industry heuristic artefact rather than a related-party finding; no filing in this pool places them inside the group.

Nor is this "Owner-operator discipline": the Executive Chairman and CEO holds 216,403 shares, 0.12% of the 187.2m outstanding, down 28% in nine months, the President holds none directly, and open-market insider activity over the trailing twelve months was 0 buys against 16 sells [CIQ Public Ownership → History and Insider Trading — vendor export; `ciq_facts.json`]. Insider economic ownership is small and one-directional.

Two caveats carried forward rather than resolved. First, the ZMC Advisors management agreement is a real related-party channel — $79.5m of combined FY2026 cost, 1.2% of revenue, with the consulting fee up 156% in two years against revenue up 24% [FY26 10-K, Note 3] — well below the 25%-of-revenue disqualifier bar, but the proxy that would carry the audit committee's related-party policy and the full compensation picture is absent from the pool, so that side is **Not proven from available data**, not clean. Second, the $215.0m receivables-purchase facility was unused in FY2026 [FY26 10-K, Note 2]; if drawn, its proceeds land in operating cash flow by the company's own stated accounting, which would flatter a cash-conversion line that is already the weak point.

## 3. Most Material Signal

The acquisition pattern. One further large deal — particularly a debt-funded one — would move this from "capital allocation concerns" to "governance red flags," because the company would be repeating the exact transaction type whose outcome its own notes already quantify at $6,665.1m of write-downs, and it would be doing so from a weaker footing than in 2022: borrowings of $2,519.8m against $1,149.4m of cash [Q2 FY27 10-Q, Condensed Consolidated Balance Sheets], operating cash flow of $367.1m on the last twelve months [`ciq_facts.json`], and $600m of notes due in 2027 and $800m in 2028 [`ciq_facts.json` `debt_maturity_wall`]. The currency last time was 50.0m newly issued shares, 43% of the pre-deal count; the share base has already grown 61.7% since then, so a repeat would compound the dilution signal rather than sit beside it. The second route to the same downgrade is quieter: the $2,494.9m capitalized software development asset at September 30, 2026 [Q2 FY27 10-Q, Condensed Consolidated Balance Sheets], which the company has impaired in each of the last three fiscal years and re-tests for recoverability every quarter [FY26 10-K, Note 1 and Note 9]. A material impairment there would show the same pattern — capital committed ahead of proven returns — with the ROIC already negative at −1.0% on the latest twelve months. Conversely, the single piece of evidence that would most improve this read is a few years of operating cash flow comfortably funding both the $688.9m-a-year development spend and debt reduction without new equity.

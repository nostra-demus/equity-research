**Regime:** US / SEC domestic filer (NasdaqGS:TTWO, Delaware), US GAAP, reporting currency USD (millions as the filings state them), fiscal year ends **March 31** — "FY2026" = year ended March 31, 2026; FY2027 is in progress [`FY26 10-K, cover page`; `00_data-triage.md §2A`]. ## Abstract

Take-Two makes and publishes video games through its own studios — Grand Theft Auto and Red Dead under Rockstar, NBA 2K and Borderlands under 2K, and free mobile titles under Zynga. It runs as one reportable segment, inside which continuing in-game spending was 78.1% of FY26 revenue and mobile was the biggest platform at 50.1%. The strongest positive is a brand that just carried an $80 price for Grand Theft Auto VI against the industry's $70, on a series that has sold over 465 million units. The strongest negative is capital: return on capital averaged −0.7% across the five years to FY2026 against a roughly 8.2% estimated cost of capital. No automatic disqualifier triggered. This is a cyclical, hit-driven business that needs a timing edge, and today is the hinge.

Nine disagreements or supersessions were found. In each case the more conservative or later-filed reading is adopted, per CLAUDE.md §4.

**1. Console licence expiry — the live contradiction, reconciled on the filing date, not averaged.**
`05_customer-geography.md §3` (added flag), `06_value-chain.md §5` and `07_business-quality.md §4` all state that **both** console licences — Sony's and both Microsoft agreements — expire **March 31, 2027**, covering the 39.0% of FY26 revenue earned on console. That was correct as of the FY26 10-K, signed May 11, 2026 [`FY26 10-K, Item 1 — Arrangements with Platform Manufacturers, pp.3–4`]. `12_red-flags-sweep.md` read the newer document and found it superseded on the Microsoft limb. **The later filing wins (§4). The corrected position, stated precisely:**

- **Microsoft limb — superseded.** A **new XBOX Publisher License Agreement, effective September 17, 2026**, between Take-Two and Microsoft Corporation was filed as Exhibit 10.1 to the Q2 FY27 10-Q (filed November 9, 2026). Its merger clause states the agreement "merges, replaces, and supersedes all related oral understandings, representations, prior discussions, letters of intent, or any preliminary or earlier agreements, **including the XBOX Console Publisher License Agreement between the parties**" [`Q2 FY27 10-Q, Exhibit 10.1, §18.10`; `Q2 FY27 10-Q, Exhibit Index, item 10.1`]. So **the March 31, 2027 expiry no longer describes the Microsoft limb.** What replaced it is **not readable**: the Initial Term `[***]`, each Renewal Term `[***]`, the non-renewal notice window `[***]` and the **Royalty Percentage** `[***]` are all redacted [`Q2 FY27 10-Q, Exhibit 10.1, §17.1 and Digital Content Royalty Terms §1.3`]. Redacting confidential commercial terms is normal and permitted; the consequence is still that the fee and the term on two-fifths of revenue are now invisible to an outsider where the expiry date previously was not. And one term IS readable and is sharper than an expiry date: Microsoft may terminate **"without cost or penalty" on a "Game-by-Game or country-by-country basis" on notice** if it determines at any time before commercial release that a game does not materially comply with the Publisher Guide or any Law [`Q2 FY27 10-Q, Exhibit 10.1, §17.2`].
- **Sony limb — intact and unchanged.** The PlayStation Global Developer and Publisher Agreement (effective March 23, 2017; PS5 Amendment September 30, 2020) still **expires March 31, 2027** with automatic one-year renewals, and **"Sony may terminate the agreement for any or no reason upon 30 days' notice"** [`FY26 10-K, Item 1 — Arrangements with Platform Manufacturers, p.3`].
- **The qualifier that travels with it:** console revenue was **39.0% of FY26 net revenue and 41.4% of the six months to September 30, 2026** [`FY26 10-K, MD&A, p.44`; `Q2 FY27 10-Q, MD&A, p.23`] — but that share is split across the two limbs and the filings do not disclose a Sony-only or Microsoft-only share, so neither limb's exposure can be sized on its own. **Downstream agents must not restate "both console licences expire March 31, 2027" as a current fact.** The accurate short form is: *the Sony limb carries a March 31, 2027 expiry plus a 30-day at-will termination right; the Microsoft limb was replaced on September 17, 2026 with its term and royalty redacted and a per-title, per-country cancellation right added.* Net direction is genuinely mixed — one expiry cliff removed, one piece of disclosure lost, one new counterparty right gained.

## 5. Simple Summary

- **What it does:** writes and publishes video games in its own studios — Grand Theft Auto and Red Dead (Rockstar), NBA 2K and Borderlands (2K), free mobile games like Toon Blast and Color Block Jam (Zynga). 9,998 of its 12,909 staff are developers.
- **How it makes money:** mostly not from the first sale. **78.1% of FY26 revenue ($5,196.6m) was continuing in-game spending** — virtual currency, add-on content, in-game purchases and advertising bought after the game is already owned. 97.0% of revenue arrives digitally, and **80.6% of it is paid by just five customers**: Apple, Sony, Google, Microsoft and one other, each of the first four above 10% on their own.
- **Does each new unit create value? Cannot be answered from what the company publishes** — no per-title revenue, no per-title cost, no payback period, even though the company computes amortisation title by title internally. What can be checked is the total, and over FY24–FY26 content spending exceeded gross profit after marketing by **$902.9m** — a comparison biased against the company, because **$2,149.7m of capitalised content sits in titles nobody has bought yet**.
- **Which segment matters most:** there is only one, by the company's own audited note. Inside it, continuing in-game spending is the bigger half (78.1%) and mobile is the biggest platform (50.1%) — and mobile is the one management says carries the **lower** margin, by an amount it never discloses.
- **Does it have a moat, and against whom? No moat proven.** The brand is real — Grand Theft Auto has sold over 465 million units and just carried an $80 price against the industry's $70 — but it covers only 12.4% of revenue and does not lift returns: **through-cycle return on capital of −0.7% against a cost of capital of roughly 8.2%.** It is up against Electronic Arts (now private, levered), Roblox (growing ten times faster), Playtika, Nintendo, Konami, NEXON, Ubisoft and others — **and against three of its own biggest customers**, since Sony, Microsoft and Apple/Google all publish or distribute competing games.
- **What external variables it depends on:** four gatekeepers above all — Apple, Google, Sony and Microsoft — who set the fee, write the terms, and in Sony's case can walk away on 30 days' notice. Then regulation of in-game spending in six jurisdictions aimed at 78.1% of revenue, the third-party console cycle under 39–41% of revenue, discretionary consumer spending, and currency (a 10% stronger dollar cuts revenue 4.0%). No commodity exposure at all.
- **Is capital allocated well? No — and the evidence is audited, not inferred.** Roughly $9.6bn of acquisitions have produced **$6,665.1m of write-downs**, including $5,887.3m of goodwill impairment against the $6,949.2m ever recorded (84.7%). Share count is up 61.7% since March 2022 with zero buybacks in three years, $1,150m of debt was repaid out of a new equity issue rather than operating cash, and insiders made 0 open-market buys against 16 sells in the last twelve months. The one mitigating fact: the deal-making has stopped.
- **Does it deserve deeper work? Yes — as a cycle-timing call, not as a compounder.** No disqualifier triggered, the brand is genuine, and the decisive test is dated: Grand Theft Auto VI went on sale **today, November 19, 2026**, and the first full quarter with it on sale reports on or about **February 9, 2027** against FY27 guidance of $8,000–8,200m of net bookings and **$109–142m of net income**. Treat today's weak numbers as a trough and next year's as a peak — neither is the real level.

# Value Chain Position — TTWO

**Regime:** US SEC domestic filer (NasdaqGS:TTWO, Delaware), US GAAP, reporting currency USD (millions as the filing states them), fiscal year ends **March 31** — "FY2026" = year to March 31, 2026 [`FY26 10-K, cover page`; `00_data-triage.md §2A`]. Upstream inputs read: `02_business-identity.md`, `00_data-triage.md`, `03_segment-map.md`, `05_customer-geography.md`, `10_external-dependency.md`.

**Where the chain actually bites.** Take-Two owns the content and the brands but owns none of the doors to the player. It buys permission to publish from Sony and Microsoft, pays Apple and Google a fee they alone set to reach phones, and sells to a top five that is **80.6% of FY26 net revenue** [`FY26 10-K, Item 1 — Sales, p.4`]. So this report splits the question in two: the company's power over **price** (which it has, and has chosen not to use fully) versus its power over **terms** (which it does not have at all).

**One data-quality note before the numbers.** The vendor workbook reads gross margin as 58% for both FY25 and FY26, while the 10-K's own cost-of-revenue table reads 54.3% then 57.2%; the vendor flags FY25 as reclassified [`ciq_facts.json → margin_trend` (`CIQ Financials → Income Statement`) — tier-5 vendor export]. The gap on FY25 is 3.7 percentage points and is a basis difference, not a misread of either source. Every margin calculation below uses the 10-K's own table (CLAUDE.md §4, §5), and I flag the vendor difference rather than override it silently.

## 1. Stages Occupied

Take-Two does **not** occupy the raw-material or component-supply stages: there is no commodity input. Its real input is developer time, which arrives as payroll and capitalised software, not as a purchased material — R&D was $1,074.6m in FY26, and 9,998 of 12,909 full-time staff sit in development studios [`FY26 10-K, Item 7 (MD&A) — Operating Expenses`; `FY26 10-K, Item 1 — Human Capital Management`].

Bargaining power bands used: **Strong** = sets price, dictates terms, has alternatives. **Mid** = negotiated outcomes, no extreme leverage either way. **Weak** = price-taker, terms imposed, few alternatives. Higher is better; this is not an inverted scale.

## 2. Input Cost Pass-Through

| Axis | What it asks | Verdict | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Contractual pass-through** | Is there an escalator, indexed-pricing, or cost-plus clause that moves price automatically? | **No — 0% of revenue sits under a disclosed escalator, indexed-pricing or cost-plus clause.** And the contractual asymmetry runs *against* the company on both sides. | There is no contract on the output side to carry an escalator: "Our sales are made primarily without long-term agreements or other commitments, and our customers may terminate their relationship with us at any time" [`FY26 10-K, Item 1A — dependence on a limited number of customers`]. On the input side the counterparties hold the pricing pen: "Some of these platforms have retained the right to change the fee structures for online distribution of both paid content and free content… and their ability to set or influence commission rates and service fees may increase our costs, which could negatively affect our operating margins"; "Apple and Google generally have the discretion to set the amounts of their platform fees"; and on IP, "we are subject to guaranteed minimum payments, royalties or standards of performance and may not be able to terminate these agreements prior to their stated expiration" [`FY26 10-K, Item 1A — limited number of channel partners`; `FY26 10-K, Item 7 (MD&A) — Online Content and Digital Distribution`; `FY26 10-K, Item 1A — ability to acquire and maintain licenses`] |
| **Realised recovery** | Of a given input-cost increase, how much did the company actually recover — through price, mix, hedging, re-sourcing, or a cost programme? | **H1 FY27: 56% recovered as reported; ~106% (fully recovered) once a one-off stock-compensation swing is removed. FY26: ~171%, i.e. more than fully recovered — but the dominant FY26 offset was an accounting one, not an operating one.** Not zero on any basis. | Built below from the 10-K's and 10-Q's own cost-of-revenue tables [`FY26 10-K, Item 7 (MD&A) — Cost of revenue, FY26 vs FY25`; `Q2 FY27 10-Q, Item 2 (MD&A) — Six Months Ended September 30, 2026 Compared to September 30, 2025`] |

**The arithmetic, shown.** Two cost lines rose; the rest of the table and the margin tell you how much of the rise actually landed.

*Six months to September 30, 2026 (the most recent reported period):*
- Licences: 6.9% → **7.6%** of net revenue = **+70bps**
- Third-party software development costs and royalties: 6.2% → **8.0%** = **+180bps**
- Gross pre-mitigation input-cost increase = **+250bps of revenue**
- Gross margin in the event: 58.7% → **57.6%** = **−110bps**
- `realised recovery = 1 − (110 ÷ 250) = 0.56` → **56% recovered**

*Same period, stripping a distortion the filing itself flags.* The development-cost line carries $6.5m of stock-based compensation in FY27 H1 against a $(36.2)m credit in FY26 H1 — a $42.7m swing worth about 141bps on $3,030.9m of revenue, which is most of the apparent 180bps rise [`Q2 FY27 10-Q, Item 2 (MD&A), footnote (1)`]. Ex-stock-compensation, the development line rose only ~53bps and licences ~76bps, so the gross increase was **+129bps**, while gross margin ex-stock-compensation went 57.64% → 57.72%, i.e. **+8bps better**. `1 − (−8 ÷ 129) ≈ 1.06` → **~106% recovered**.

*FY26 full year:*
- Licences 6.5% → 7.0% = +50bps; third-party development costs and royalties 3.0% → 6.6% = +360bps → gross increase **+410bps**
- Gross margin in the event: 54.3% → **57.2%** = **+290bps better**
- `1 − (−290 ÷ 410) ≈ 1.71` → **~171% recovered**. **But read the honest qualifier:** the single largest offset was game-intangible amortisation falling 14.4% → 9.9% of revenue (−450bps), which the filing attributes to "lower amortization of intangible assets primarily due to higher impairments in the prior year" — a consequence of the FY25 $3,545.2m goodwill impairment cycle, not an operating recovery. The *operating* offsets were product costs −160bps and internal royalties −90bps [`FY26 10-K, Item 7 (MD&A) — Cost of revenue and gross profit discussion`].

**What the paragraph actually says.** Take-Two has **no contractual pass-through** — there is no escalator clause anywhere in the pool, and the platforms hold the unilateral right to change the fee. But it has recovered most or all of its rising input costs anyway, and it has done so through **cost absorption rather than price pass-through**. The absorbing levers the filings name, with what each has done: (i) **direct-to-consumer commerce**, which bypasses the Apple/Google fee entirely and which management calls "a significant driver of revenue and margins" with "a material effect positive on our margins in the Mobile business" — real, but its share of revenue is **not disclosed**, so I cannot size it [`Q2 FY27 earnings call, prepared remarks`; `Q1 FY27 earnings call, Q&A (Zelnick)`]; (ii) **platform mix**, where mobile fell from 52.2% to 50.1% of FY26 net revenue and console rose from 37.3% to 39.0%, and product costs (where the app-store fee sits) fell 160bps in FY26 and a further 110bps in H1 FY27 — mobile "generally generates a lower gross margin percentage than our Console or PC revenue" because of the platform fee, so moving mix away from mobile mechanically lifts margin [`FY26 10-K, Item 7 (MD&A) — net revenue by platform; Online Content and Digital Distribution`; `Q2 FY27 10-Q, Item 2 (MD&A)`]; (iii) **lower internal royalties**, 7.2% → 6.3% of FY26 revenue and 5.1% → 4.8% in H1 FY27; (iv) **currency forwards**, which are balance-sheet only — $349.2m of contracts to sell foreign currency and $140.3m to buy at March 31, 2026, not designated as hedges, producing a $5.5m FY26 loss, and the filing's own limit: "Our hedging programs are designed to reduce, but do not entirely eliminate, the effect of currency exchange rate movements" [`FY26 10-K, Item 7A — Balance Sheet Hedging Activities`]. The timing and lag: there is no lag to measure because there is no price mechanism — the recovery shows up in the same period the cost rises, because it comes from the cost side.

Two cautions attach. First, **cost absorption can run out in a way a price clause would not**: the FY26 recovery leaned on an impairment-driven amortisation drop that will not repeat, and the CFO has already guided FY27 gross margin slightly lower — "it's due to the mix of the business, and it's being offset by some changes in the operating expenses" [`Q2 FY27 earnings call, Nov 9 2026, Q&A (Goldstein)`]. Second, the company's one clear price action — the $80 GTA VI base price — is a **price rise on a new product, not a recovery of a cost increase**, and management explicitly disavows cost-based pricing: "Our goal is not to maximize price" [`Q1 FY27 earnings call, Aug 7 2026, Q&A (Zelnick)`]. Do not restate this section as "no pass-through". The accurate short form is: **no contractual pass-through; 56%–106% realised recovery in the latest half, achieved on the cost side.**

**Supplier / input concentration (quantify).** **No supplier's share of cost of revenue or of purchases is disclosed anywhere in the pool** — not for the largest supplier, not for the top three, and not for any platform. That is a real gap and I do not invent a percentage. What *is* quantified is the shape of the input bill: FY26 cost of revenue was $2,846.7m, 42.8% of net revenue, made of product costs $863.8m (13.0%), game intangibles $662.2m (9.9%), licences $463.5m (7.0%), third-party software development costs and royalties $439.8m (6.6%) and internal royalties $417.4m (6.3%) [`FY26 10-K, Item 7 (MD&A) — Cost of revenue table`]. Contractually locked minimums are small: $740.1m in total across all future years, of which $323.7m falls in FY27 — 4.9% of FY26 net revenue — split software development and licensing $175.8m, marketing $206.9m (which "primarily reflect our agreements with major sports leagues and players' associations") and purchase obligations $357.4m [`FY26 10-K, Note 14 (Commitments and Contingencies), p.92`].

The material supply-side dependency here is **permission, not materials**, and there are four single-source inputs with no alternative:

1. **The Sony PlayStation publisher licence** — sole source of the right to publish on PlayStation, expiring March 31, 2027 with one-year automatic renewals, terminable by Sony "for any or no reason upon 30 days' notice" [`FY26 10-K, Item 1 — Arrangements with Platform Manufacturers, p.3`]. **Material** — the console channel it is half of was 39.0% of FY26 net revenue.
2. **The two Microsoft Xbox publisher licences** — same function, both expiring March 31, 2027 [`FY26 10-K, Item 1 — Arrangements with Platform Manufacturers, p.3`]. **Material**, same channel.
3. **Apple's and Google's payment rails** — "most of the virtual currency we sell is purchased using these platform providers' payment processing systems," on terms of service the platform writes and reinterprets at its "sole discretion," covering 91.0% of mobile revenue where mobile is 50.1% of the group [`FY26 10-K, Item 1A — third-party mobile and web platforms`; `FY26 10-K, Item 7 (MD&A)`]. **Material** — roughly 46% of group revenue reaches the company through two app stores (*my arithmetic, 0.910 × 0.501; not a disclosed figure*).
4. **Sports-league and players'-association licences** for the 2K label, with the National Basketball Association named as a licensor in the vendor graph. No term, renewal date, or per-licence cost is disclosed in the pool. **Material at 7.0% of revenue and rising 26.7% year on year**, with the filing warning that losing or failing to renew them means "our revenue and profitability could decline significantly" [`FY26 10-K, Item 1A — ability to acquire and maintain licenses`].

One further supplier-side dependency the filing itself raises, routed on rather than judged here: the Executive Chairman/CEO and the President are **partners of ZMC Advisors, L.P.**, and the 10-K lists ZMC alongside its own executive officers as a loss that "could significantly harm our business," with a management agreement with ZelnickMedia Corporation in the exhibit index [`FY26 10-K, Item 1A — we depend on our key management and product development personnel`; `FY26 10-K, Exhibit Index`]. That is a related-party observation for `capital-allocation-governance` (CLAUDE.md §13), not a value-chain conclusion. Note also that the DEF 14A export in this pool is an empty shell, so the proxy-level detail on that arrangement is not readable here [`Take-Two_Interactive_Software_Inc_-_Form_DEF_14A-Jul-27-2026__No-Data` — empty export].

## 3. Customer Pricing Power

Take-Two can raise prices, and in the last 24 months it did so once, deliberately and above the industry norm — and then chose not to on the title where it could have. It set **Grand Theft Auto VI's base price at $80** against the long-standing $70 AAA price point (a ~14% rise), while leaving **NBA 2K27's base price at $70** [`Q1 FY27 earnings call, Aug 7 2026, Q&A (Pitz / Zelnick)`]. Management's stated reason is not weakness but choice: "the real cost of a AAA video game is a whole lot lower today than it was 20 years ago. Pricing has not kept pace with inflation… Our goal is not to maximize price" [same source]. It also raises *realised* price without touching the headline, through edition laddering — the premium above the $80 standard edition for GTA VI's Ultimate Edition is booked into recurrent consumer spending, as the CFO confirmed: "the add-on content can be also purchased separately, so it's allocated to RCS" [`Q2 FY27 earnings call, Nov 9 2026, Q&A (Handler / Goldstein)`]. **The volume reaction to the $80 price is not yet measurable** — GTA VI released today, November 19, 2026, and the only read available is a qualitative one management refuses to quantify: pre-orders are "unprecedented and extraordinary" and "tracking far in excess of our initial expectations," with Zelnick declining to put them into guidance because "we're days away from the launch… we tend to avoid claiming success before it occurs" [`Q2 FY27 earnings call, Nov 9 2026, prepared remarks and Q&A`]. Two facts cut the other way and belong in the same paragraph. First, the company's standing practice is to **cut** price, not hold it: it stimulates sales by "reducing the wholesale prices of our products to retailers, digital storefronts, and platform providers," with price protection "typically… three to nine months after a product's initial launch," plus "volume rebates" in certain international markets [`FY26 10-K, Item 1 — Marketing, p.4`]. Second, the in-game spending limb — 81% of Q2 FY27 Net Bookings — went backwards at unchanged prices: recurrent consumer spending Net Bookings fell 9% and GAAP recurrent consumer spending fell 5% in the September 2026 quarter [`Q2 FY27 earnings release, Nov 9 2026, p.1`]. Read together: **pricing power on the headline price of a new premium release is evidenced and partly unused; pricing power to hold up spend between releases is not evidenced.**

## 4. Economic Control Verdict

**Mixed.**

The company controls price and content but not terms or access. On the strong limb: it owns the IP behind 54.3% of FY26 revenue in five franchises and needs no licensor's consent to make it [`FY26 10-K, Item 1A — dependence on key products`; `FY26 10-K, Item 1 — Intellectual Property`]; it priced GTA VI at $80 above the industry's $70 and held NBA 2K27 at $70 by choice [`Q1 FY27 earnings call, Aug 7 2026, Q&A`]; and it recovered 56% as reported — ~106% ex-stock-compensation — of a 250bps input-cost rise in the latest reported half without any escalator clause [`Q2 FY27 10-Q, Item 2 (MD&A)`]. On the weak limb: four counterparties that are each above 10.0% of its revenue, together inside a top five that is 80.6% of it, also own the only doors to the player, set the fee at their "sole discretion," and in Sony's case can terminate "for any or no reason upon 30 days' notice" [`FY26 10-K, Item 1 — Sales, p.4`; `FY26 10-K, Item 1 — Arrangements with Platform Manufacturers, p.3`]; the company itself concedes those partners are gaining ground — "Increased competition for digital 'shelf space' has put channel partners in more favorable bargaining positions" [`FY26 10-K, Item 1A — limited number of channel partners`]. It is not **Squeezed**, because it is demonstrably not a price-taker on outputs and its measured recovery on inputs is well above zero. It does not **Control economics**, because the fee schedule on roughly 46% of its revenue and the licence on another 39.0% are written by someone else.

## 5. The Single Biggest Bargaining Risk

The **Sony PlayStation Global Developer and Publisher Agreement** — a single-source permission covering part of the 39.0% of FY26 revenue earned on consoles, held by a counterparty that is itself above 10.0% of net revenue, that publishes competing games, that expires March 31, 2027, and that may terminate "for any or no reason upon 30 days' notice" [`FY26 10-K, Item 1 — Arrangements with Platform Manufacturers, p.3`; `FY26 10-K, Item 1A, Item 7 (MD&A)`]. (The Apple/Google fee limb is the larger-dollar exposure at roughly 46% of revenue, but it degrades by degrees rather than by cancellation.)

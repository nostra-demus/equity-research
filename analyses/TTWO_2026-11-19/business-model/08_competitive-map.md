## 1. Dominant Segment

One operating and reportable segment — "sale of software titles", 100% of $6,656.4 of FY26 net revenue — so the segment competitors are profiled against is the whole consolidated interactive-entertainment publishing business: a hit-driven premium console/PC franchise publisher (Rockstar Games, 2K) bolted onto a free-to-play mobile microtransaction business (Zynga), with 78.1% of FY26 revenue from recurrent consumer spending and 50.1% of it from mobile [`FY26 10-K, Note 21 (Segment Reporting and Geographic Information), p.102`; `FY26 10-K, Item 7 — net revenue by content and by platform`; upstream `03_segment-map.md §2`, `02_business-identity.md §3`].

## 2. Named Competitors

Take-Two names its competitors twice in its own FY26 10-K, in the same list both times: **Electronic Arts, Embracer Group, Epic Games, Playrix, Playtika, Roblox, Savvy Games, Tencent, and Ubisoft** as publisher rivals, plus **Sony, Microsoft, and Nintendo** "for the sale of interactive entertainment software", plus **Apple, Google, and Microsoft** as "high-profile companies with significant online presences with new and expanded mobile gaming offerings" [`FY26 10-K, Item 1 — Competition`; `FY26 10-K, Item 1A — "The interactive entertainment software industry is highly competitive"`]. Nothing below is invented: every name is in that list, and the three profiled names are also in the Capital IQ comp set, so they carry comparable numbers.

### Competitor A — Electronic Arts Inc.

- **Ticker / listing:** **No longer listed.** The vendor comp export carries no ticker, no day-close price, no shares outstanding, no market capitalisation and no trading multiples for Electronic Arts — every one of those cells is blank, while all nine other peers are populated [`CIQ Comps → Financial Data` and `→ Trading Multiples`, As-Of 2026-11-19 — vendor export]. The same export shows an S&P issuer credit rating of **BB-** (below investment grade, versus TTWO's BBB) [`CIQ Comps → Credit Health Panel`, As-Of 2026-11-19 — vendor export]. Reason, from outside the pool: EA was taken private on **August 4, 2026** in a ~$55bn buyout by Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund, Silver Lake and Affinity Partners, including ~$20bn of debt financing, and delisted from Nasdaq [`EA Form 8-K Ex.99.1, filed 2026-08-04 (SEC EDGAR, CIK 0000712515)`; `Web: thewrap.com / esportsadvocate.net, Aug 2026 — dated, unverified`]. The pool itself does not state why EA is unlisted — the delisting fact is corroborated by the blank vendor cells, the cause is web-sourced.
- **Where they compete:** The same dominant segment, head-to-head, worldwide and on the same three platforms (console, PC, mobile). The overlap is specific, not categorical: EA's licensed-sports franchises — EA SPORTS FC, Madden NFL, College Football, NHL, UFC, F1, **PGA TOUR** — sit directly against Take-Two's NBA 2K, WWE 2K and **PGA TOUR 2K**, the same licensed-sports-simulation model with the same annual-release cadence and the same third-party licence cost [`CIQ Comps → Business Description` (EA and TTWO rows), As-Of 2026-11-19 — vendor export; `FY26 10-K, Item 1 — General / Our Businesses`]. EA also runs live services and mobile, the two things that generate 78.1% of Take-Two's FY26 revenue.
- **Scale:** LTM total revenue **$7,846** against Take-Two's **$6,409.7** — the only named rival in the comp set larger than Take-Two on revenue apart from Nintendo. EA's LTM window ends **June 30, 2026** (income-statement filing date 2026-08-03), Take-Two's ends September 30, 2026, so the two windows do not line up [`CIQ Comps → Financial Data`; `CIQ Comps → Credit Health Panel` — vendor export].
- **Profitability / return on capital:** LTM gross margin **79.9%**, EBITDA margin **22.0%**, EBIT margin **17.9%**, net income margin **13.8%**; LTM revenue growth +5.0%, EBITDA growth −3.3%, EBIT growth −5.2%; total debt/capital 20.9%, total debt/EBITDA 1.0x [`CIQ Comps → Operating Statistics` and `→ Financial Data`, LTM to Jun-30-2026, As-Of 2026-11-19 — vendor export]. **ROIC / ROE: not in this pool.** The comp export has no return-on-capital or return-on-equity column for any peer, and EA no longer files, so no post-buyout equity base is observable. Caution for the `moat` agent: these margins are measured **before** the ~$20bn of buyout debt landed on EA, so EA's forward interest burden is not in the 13.8% net margin.
- **Source named in:** `FY26 10-K, Item 1 — Competition` (first name in the list); `FY26 10-K, Item 1A — industry competition risk factor`; `CIQ Comps → Business Description`, As-Of 2026-11-19 — vendor export.
- **One-line read:** The closest structural twin — a licensed-sports-plus-owned-IP publisher across console, PC and mobile — and it currently converts revenue to profit far better than Take-Two does (EBIT margin +17.9% against Take-Two's −1.8%), but it has just swapped a public balance sheet for the largest leveraged buyout on record, so its future pricing and spending behaviour is less predictable than its history.

### Competitor B — Roblox Corporation

- **Ticker / listing:** **NYSE:RBLX**, listed. Day close $47.33, 715.7m shares, market capitalisation $33,874.7, total enterprise value $32,562.7 [`CIQ Comps → Financial Data`, As-Of 2026-11-19 — vendor export].
- **Where they compete:** For exactly the revenue line Take-Two depends on most. Roblox runs a user-generated immersive platform and earns from in-experience spending, not from selling games — which is the same economics as Take-Two's recurrent consumer spending, 78.1% of FY26 revenue and 82.5% of the six months to September 30, 2026 [`CIQ Comps → Business Description` (Roblox row) — vendor export; `FY26 10-K, Item 7`; `Q2 FY27 10-Q, MD&A, p.24`]. Geographic overlap is "the United States and internationally", and Take-Two's own filing identifies the threat precisely: competitors "with relatively limited start-up time or expertise" whose "proliferation of titles in these open developer channels makes it difficult for us to differentiate ourselves… and to compete for players without substantially increasing our marketing expenses and development costs" [`FY26 10-K, Item 1A — low barriers to entry / open developer channels`].
- **Scale:** LTM total revenue **$5,897.6** (window ending September 30, 2026 — the same window as Take-Two's), i.e. 92% of Take-Two's $6,409.7. The closest peer in the whole comp set by revenue size. Net **cash** of $1,286 against Take-Two's net **debt** of $1,237.8 [`CIQ Comps → Financial Data`; `CIQ Comps → Credit Health Panel`, LTM period ending 2026-09-30 — vendor export].
- **Profitability / return on capital:** LTM gross margin **27.3%**, EBITDA margin **−13.0%**, EBIT margin **−17.4%**, net income margin **−16.5%**; LTM revenue growth **+32.1%** — the fastest in the comp set; LTM diluted EPS −$1.36; total debt/capital 101.4%, debt/EBITDA "NM" (EBITDA is negative); S&P issuer credit rating BBB−, credit-health ranks Overall 3 / Operational 4 / Solvency 4 / Liquidity 2 (1 = best) [`CIQ Comps → Operating Statistics`, `→ Financial Data`, `→ Credit Health Panel`, As-Of 2026-11-19 — vendor export]. **ROIC / ROE: not in this pool** (no return column in the peer export); both would be negative on the above figures, but no figure is published here and none is invented.
- **Source named in:** `FY26 10-K, Item 1 — Competition`; `FY26 10-K, Item 1A — industry competition risk factor`; `CIQ Comps → Business Description`, As-Of 2026-11-19 — vendor export.
- **One-line read:** Take-Two's nearest rival by size, growing ten times faster, and losing money at every line — it is competing for the same player hours and the same in-game wallet while charging nothing for access, which is a pricing problem for Take-Two's premium model rather than a margin benchmark.

### Competitor C — Playtika Holding Corp.

- **Ticker / listing:** **NasdaqGS:PLTK**, listed. Day close $2.57, 382.7m shares, market capitalisation $983.5, total enterprise value $2,882. Note the vendor export records Playtika as operating "as a subsidiary of Playtika Holding UK II Limited" — it is a controlled listed company, not a free-floating one [`CIQ Comps → Financial Data` and `→ Business Description`, As-Of 2026-11-19 — vendor export].
- **Where they compete:** Directly against Zynga, in mobile free-to-play. Playtika "develops mobile games in the United States, Europe, the Middle East, Africa, the Asia Pacific, and internationally" with "a portfolio of casual and social casino-themed games" distributed "through various web and mobile platforms and direct-to-consumer platforms" [`CIQ Comps → Business Description` — vendor export]. That is the same genre set and the same channel as Take-Two's Zynga titles (Toon Blast, Toy Blast, Match Factory!, Color Block Jam, Zynga Poker, Wizard of Oz Slots Casino, Hit It Rich!) [`FY26 10-K, Item 1 — Our Businesses`; `CIQ Comps → Business Description` (TTWO row)]. This matters out of proportion to Playtika's size: mobile was **50.1% of Take-Two's FY26 revenue** and is the platform management says carries the lower gross margin [`FY26 10-K, Item 7 — net revenue by platform`; `Q2 FY27 10-Q, MD&A, p.23`].
- **Scale:** LTM total revenue **$2,828.4**, window ending September 30, 2026 — 44% of Take-Two's consolidated revenue, and 85% of Take-Two's $3,333.0 of FY26 mobile revenue. Smaller than Take-Two overall but well inside 5x, and roughly the same size as the part of Take-Two it actually competes with [`CIQ Comps → Financial Data`; `CIQ Comps → Credit Health Panel` — vendor export; `FY26 10-K, Item 7`].
- **Profitability / return on capital:** LTM gross margin **73.6%**, EBITDA margin **24.5%**, EBIT margin **18.9%**, net income margin **−9.8%**; LTM revenue growth +3.7%, EBITDA growth +13.8%, EBIT growth +30.4%; LTM diluted EPS −$0.74; total debt/EBITDA 3.5x and total debt/capital **116.7%** (shareholders' equity is negative, so the ratio exceeds 100%); S&P issuer credit rating BB−, credit-health ranks Overall 4 / Operational 2 / Solvency 4 / Liquidity 4 [`CIQ Comps → Operating Statistics`, `→ Financial Data`, `→ Credit Health Panel`, As-Of 2026-11-19 — vendor export]. **ROIC / ROE: not in this pool**, and in Playtika's case return on equity would be uninterpretable anyway because the equity base is negative — say so rather than print a number.
- **Source named in:** `FY26 10-K, Item 1 — Competition`; `FY26 10-K, Item 1A — industry competition risk factor`; `CIQ Comps → Business Description`, As-Of 2026-11-19 — vendor export.
- **One-line read:** A pure mobile free-to-play operator that turns 73.6% gross margin into 18.9% at the operating line on a smaller revenue base — evidence that the mobile model Take-Two bought with Zynga can earn an operating margin, which makes Take-Two's consolidated −1.8% EBIT margin a Take-Two problem rather than a mobile-industry problem.

## 3. Competitive Position

**Losing share — on the only measurable proxy in the pool, and the proxy is cycle-distorted.** No market-share figure exists anywhere in this pool: the FY26 10-K never states a share of market, and the only rank claim management makes is unquantified — "Our NBA 2K Online business is the top online PC sports game in China" [`FY26 10-K, Item 1 — Identify and Lead New Paradigms and Market Trends`]. The usable proxy is revenue growth against the same comp set over a broadly matched window: Take-Two grew LTM revenue **+3.1%** against a ten-peer **median of +14.6%**, with Roblox +32.1%, Nintendo +25.2%, Konami +24.8% and NEXON +24.1% all growing far faster [`CIQ Comps → Operating Statistics`, As-Of 2026-11-19 — vendor export]. Take-Two's own latest reported period points the same way: Q2 FY27 net bookings fell **18%** year on year to $1,601.3, recurrent consumer spending fell **9%** against guidance of −5%, and mobile revenue fell **$90.5** year on year to $731.1 [`Q2 FY27 earnings release (November 9, 2026), p.1`; `Q2 FY27 investor deck (November 9, 2026), p.4`; `Q2 FY27 10-Q, MD&A, p.24`].

**Now the numbers that disagree, named.** Two figures in this same engine's data point the other way and are not overturned, only explained. First, FY26 revenue grew **+18.2%** ($1,022.8 higher, to $6,656.4) — faster than the peer median growth above [`FY26 10-K, Item 7`]. Second, FY27 guidance of **$8,000–8,200** net bookings against FY26 net bookings of $6,721 implies **+19% to +22%** growth [`Q2 FY27 investor deck, pp.5 and 8`]. Both are consistent with the proxy rather than contradicting it, because the cause is release timing, not competitive loss: the LTM window to September 30, 2026 is the pre-launch trough between Borderlands 4 (September 2025) and Grand Theft Auto VI, which released **today, November 19, 2026** [`Q2 FY27 10-Q`; `Q2 FY27 investor deck, p.13`]. Two honest limits on the read: the peer LTM windows end on different dates (Take-Two and Roblox September 30, 2026; EA June 30, 2026; Ubisoft March 31, 2026), and revenue growth is a share proxy only if the market itself grew — which this pool does not measure. **Verdict: Losing share on the latest twelve-month window; the read is a one-metric proxy inside a stated launch trough, is not confirmed, and is not durable evidence of competitive decline.**

## 4. Competitive Shape

**Fragmented in development and publishing, gated by a four-firm distribution oligopoly — with no HHI or top-N share data in this pool to size either half.** The fragmented half is Take-Two's own description: it competes with firms "that range in size and cost structure from very small with limited resources to very large with greater financial, marketing, technical, and other resources than ours", names twelve of them, and expects more — "As there are relatively low barriers to entry to develop a mobile or online game, we expect new game competitors to enter the market", including "a vast number of small companies and individuals who are able to create and launch games… with relatively limited start-up time or expertise" [`FY26 10-K, Item 1 — Competition`; `FY26 10-K, Item 1A — low barriers to entry`]. The comp set itself spans $38.6 to $13,684.5 of LTM revenue across six countries, with no peer above 14% of the set's combined revenue — consistent with fragmentation, not proof of it [`CIQ Comps → Financial Data` — vendor export]. The concentrated half is where the money actually passes: Take-Two's **five largest customers were 80.6% of FY26 net revenue, with Apple, Sony, Google, and Microsoft each above 10.0% individually** — and that concentration has been stable for three years (80.6% FY26, 81.0% FY25, 79.8% FY24) [`FY26 10-K, Item 1 — Sales`; `FY26 10-K, Note 2 (Concentration of credit risk)`]. Three of those four gatekeepers are simultaneously named competitors, which the filing states as a risk in its own heading: "We cannot publish our titles without the approval of hardware licensors that are also our competitors", with title-by-title approval required and Sony and Microsoft licences that must be obtained from rivals [`FY26 10-K, Item 1A — hardware licensor risk factor`]. Inside each publisher the revenue is concentrated again — Take-Two's top five franchises were 54.3% of FY26 net revenue and Grand Theft Auto products 12.4% — and the filing generalises it to the industry: "a relatively small number of hit titles account for a large portion of total revenue in our industry" [`FY26 10-K, Item 1A — dependence on key products / industry competition`]. **So the honest structural label is a two-layer market: many competing content suppliers, four tollbooths.** One structural change is only months old and worth flagging: the single closest rival, Electronic Arts, left the public market on August 4, 2026 in a ~$55bn buyout carrying ~$20bn of debt, so the largest direct competitor is now a private, levered entity whose results will not be publicly observable in future periods [`EA Form 8-K Ex.99.1, filed 2026-08-04 (SEC EDGAR, CIK 0000712515)`; `Web: thewrap.com / esportsadvocate.net, Aug 2026 — dated, unverified`; corroborated by the blank price/multiple cells and BB− rating in `CIQ Comps → Financial Data` / `→ Credit Health Panel`, As-Of 2026-11-19 — vendor export].

**One unresolved ownership overlap, flagged and routed, not concluded.** A holder recorded as "Saudi Electronic Games Holding Co, Asset management arm" is a top-five institutional holder of Take-Two with **11,414,680 shares** — about 6.1% of the 187.2m shares outstanding — unchanged across every quarter from December 31, 2025 to September 30, 2026 [`CIQ Public Ownership → History`, periods Dec-31-2025 to Sep-30-2026, retrieved 2026-11-19 — vendor export; `ciq_facts.json → top_institutional_holders`; share count from `CIQ Comps → Financial Data`]. Take-Two's FY26 10-K separately names **"Savvy Games"** as a competitor [`FY26 10-K, Item 1 — Competition`]. Whether these are the same corporate group is **not established anywhere in this pool** — the pool contains no filing, no ownership note and no vendor row that links them, so no link is asserted here. Recorded as a question for `capital-allocation-governance` and `management-governance` to resolve, because a named competitor's affiliate holding ~6% of the company would be a related-party and alignment matter (CLAUDE.md §24 filter 6), not a competitive-map conclusion.

## 5. Caveat

Credible competitors **were** identified — three profiled, eight more tabulated, every one of them either named in Take-Two's own FY26 10-K competition disclosure or carried in the Capital IQ comp set — so the "cannot identify competitors" limitation does not apply. Four specific gaps do apply, and downstream agents should treat them as hard limits rather than estimation problems:

1. **No market-share data of any kind.** Neither the filings nor any vendor tab in this pool gives Take-Two's or any peer's share of console, PC, mobile or total interactive-entertainment spending, and there is no industry-size figure to divide by. The Section 3 read is therefore a revenue-growth proxy, not a share measurement. *What would resolve it:* a Newzoo, Circana/NPD, Sensor Tower, data.ai or IDC market-sizing report, or a peer's own disclosed share.
2. **No peer return-on-capital.** The comp export carries margins, growth, leverage and multiples but **no ROIC, ROCE or ROE column**, for any of the ten peers. Take-Two's own return on capital is available (−0.8% FY26, −1.0% LTM to September 30, 2026), so the gap is one-sided: the company can be measured, the peers cannot. The `moat` agent must not state or imply that Take-Two earns more (or less) than peers on capital from this pool. *What would resolve it:* a Capital IQ or Bloomberg peer-ratio export including ROIC/ROE, or each peer's own annual filing.
3. **Mismatched measurement windows, and vendor-not-filing basis.** Peer LTM periods end anywhere from March 31, 2026 (Ubisoft) to November 2026, and every peer figure is the vendor's normalised read rather than that peer's audited accounts. Any cross-peer comparison carries both qualifiers. *What would resolve it:* each peer's own latest annual and interim filing.
4. **No numbers at all for six named competitors** — Embracer Group, Epic Games, Playrix, Savvy Games, Tencent and the platform owners Sony, Microsoft, Apple and Google. Epic Games and Playrix are private; Savvy Games is state-backed and private; Tencent, Sony, Microsoft, Apple and Google are listed but outside this comp set, and for them "interactive entertainment" is a fraction of a much larger company, so a group-level margin would be the wrong comparable even if it were here. The gatekeeper trio's economics in particular — the platform fee they charge Take-Two — are the single most important competitive number in this business and are nowhere in the pool as a rate. *What would resolve it:* Tencent's and Sony's own segment disclosures for their games divisions, and the published platform-fee schedules or any Take-Two platform agreement exhibit stating the royalty rate.

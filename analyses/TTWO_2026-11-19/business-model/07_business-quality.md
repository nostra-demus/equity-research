**How the weighting works for this business, with the weights named.** For a hit-driven owner of entertainment IP, the four factors that decide whether a holder actually earns anything across a full release cycle dominate, and they carry **57%** of the weight between them: cyclicality **18%** (×22), industry rate-of-change **15%** (×35), competitive intensity **12%** (×25), margin stability **12%** (×30). ## 4. Read

**This is a cyclical, hit-driven owner of entertainment intellectual property — not a durable compounder on the evidence in this pool.** It owns one genuinely scarce asset (a 25-year franchise with >465m lifetime units and a live-service tail still running 13 years after launch) bolted onto a free-to-play mobile business that is 50.1% of revenue and sits in a market whose own filing describes barriers to entry as *"relatively low"* [`FY26 10-K, Item 1, p.1`; `Q1 FY27 transcript, Aug 7 2026`; `FY26 10-K, Item 1A, p.11`; `MD&A, p.44`]. The industry rate-of-change row scored **35 — Weak**, so, per `CLAUDE.md` §24 Filter 5: **this thesis is closer to a sector / technology-cycle bet than to a durable compounder, conviction is capped, and moat durability must be discounted downstream.** The strongest proof is in the company's own accounts, not in a forecast — it has written off **$5,887.3m of accumulated goodwill impairment against $6,949.2m of gross goodwill (~85% of all goodwill ever recorded)** because of *"industry conditions and changes in our strategies for games within the reporting unit"* [`FY26 10-K, Note 9, pp.83–84`; `Critical Accounting Policies — Goodwill`].

RF-BQ-005 (fast-changing industry: rate-of-change ≤40)

**Anchoring the headline returns, and reconciling them with the Cyclicality row.** The usual trap runs the other way round here: TTWO's reported returns are at a cyclical **trough**, not a peak. ROIC (what it earns on each $100 of capital invested) has been **negative five years running — −4.3%, −3.1%, −4.2%, −0.8% (FY2026), −1.0% LTM to Sep-30-2026** — against a FY2021 peak of +13.4%, and LTM EBITDA of $724.9m is *below* the FY26 figure of $788m [`ciq_facts.json → multi_year_trajectory, ltm_ebitda_m` (`CIQ Financials→Ratios` / `→Income Statement`) — tier-5 vendor export]. The through-cycle anchor, computed across the ten years FY2017–FY2026, is an EBITDA margin of roughly **12%** (average EBITDA $471.8m on average revenue $3,919.8m — *my arithmetic from the vendor series*), which is almost exactly where FY26 (12%) and the LTM (11%) sit. So the low Cyclicality score (22) and today's weak returns are the **same fact**, not a contradiction: the business is between launches, and the audited record is three straight operating losses on a through-cycle margin near 12%. The forward trap is therefore symmetric and must be flagged for the moat and DCF layers: Grand Theft Auto VI launched **November 19, 2026**, and FY27–FY28 will print this cycle's **peak**. A peak-year ROIC or margin off that launch is not the through-cycle level any more than today's −1.0% is; the vendor workbook already shows the distortion, with TEV/LTM EBITDA at **52.2x** on a trough denominator the day before the launch [`ciq_facts.json → ev_ebitda_current_x, ltm_ebitda_m` — vendor export].

**The single quality factor a buyer should keep their eye on over the next 24 months: whether recurrent consumer spending on the new Grand Theft Auto base builds a second durable live-service annuity, or whether the business reverts to the FY2024–FY2026 pattern of negative return on capital between launches.** That is the one factor that would move this scorecard materially, because it drives three rows at once (repeat revenue 55, margin stability 30, cyclicality 22). The measurable tests already exist in the company's own reporting: RCS as a share of net bookings against the guided fall to ~65% in FY27 from 78% in FY26, and RCS growth against the −9% it printed in Q2 FY27 versus guidance of −5% [`Q2 FY27 investor deck, Nov 9 2026, slides 4, 5 and 8`]. Two near-dated items sit alongside it and belong to other agents' scorecards, noted here only because they land inside the same window: both console publisher licences — Sony's and both Microsoft agreements, covering the 39.0% of FY26 revenue earned on console — **expire March 31, 2027**, with Sony able to terminate *"for any or no reason upon 30 days' notice"* [`FY26 10-K, Item 1 — Arrangements with Platform Manufacturers, pp.3–4`; `Item 1A, p.11`]; and the loot-box and in-game-currency rulemaking in six jurisdictions points at the 78.1% of revenue that is in-game monetisation [`FY26 10-K, Item 1A — loot boxes / virtual currencies`].

**Not proven from available data:** player retention, MAU/DAU, paying-user count and ARPDAU (zero occurrences anywhere in the pool) — so the durability of the 78.1% recurrent-spending base is asserted by the company and unverifiable by an outsider; player-acquisition cost and mobile payback; the direct-to-consumer share of mobile revenue; profit or margin for mobile vs console vs PC, for RCS vs full game, or for Rockstar vs 2K vs Zynga; and the identity and size of either of the two goodwill reporting units.

## 2. The Nearest Dated Catalyst

The nearest proven-date catalyst is the **second-quarter (FQ2) 2026 results release on 22-Jul-2026 — three days from this report's as-of date**, confirmed by the company's own published financial calendar [First Quarter Report 2026, "Financial calendar" box] and by the Capital IQ consensus workbook's release-date field. It matters because it is the first live test of the exact bet the reverse-DCF says the market is making: that Hydro's Adjusted EBITDA margin holds near its elevated FY2025 level (13.9%) rather than fading toward a more conservative mid-cycle level (13.0%). The setup itself is mixed — Street EBITDA/EPS estimates for this specific quarter were cut sharply in the final 30 days before the print (a lowered bar), while revenue estimates for the same quarter kept rising (no volume-side concern) — so a beat on the now-lower profit bar is plausible even without volume-side heroics, but a miss on the guided cost ranges or a harder-than-flagged reversal of the Metal Markets trading gain would cut the other way and start confirming the market's optimistic margin assumption is wrong.

## 3. The Single Most Important Catalyst

The single most important item on this calendar is not one dated event but a question the next two prints (22-Jul-2026 and 23-Oct-2026) will start to answer: **whether the current elevated aluminium price and Metal Markets trading gains — both explicitly tied to a Middle East supply shock management itself calls "not run-rate" — normalize toward Hydro's own mid-cycle margin (13.0%) or persist near the FY2025 actual (13.9%).** The valuation module's reverse-DCF finds the current price (NOK 84.96) already assumes the higher, persistent case — a "Stretch, tilting aggressive" bet, not a conservative one [`valuation/05_reverse-dcf.md` §5]. Because the company issues no formal group-level guidance (only capex), a buyer cannot time this with a single scheduled decision — it has to be read quarter by quarter through the segment bridges management discloses each print. Layered underneath this commodity-cycle question is the structural one: Extrusions' proposed plant closures (§1, undated) are Hydro's own attempt at fixing a segment whose EBIT margin has fallen from +4% to −2.2%, but with no 2–3-year delivered record yet, this is flagged §24 Filter 2 (unproven turnaround) and should not be read as bullish until the segment numbers actually turn — which, after 01-Jan-2027, will show up under a new reporting metric (Adjusted OPDAI), adding a genuine measurement discontinuity right when it matters most.

## 4. Negative / Bearish Catalysts

- **The 2026 debt-maturity bucket (NOK 8,250m, due through 31-Dec-2026)** is currently assessed as low-tension (covered 1.36x by cash alone), but it is the largest single-year maturity on the schedule and the one the buyer should re-check each quarter against cash balances actually reported [`balance-sheet-survival/02_maturity-wall-and-refinancing.md` §1, §4].
- **The next Alumetal impairment test (~Feb-2027, inferred)** sits on only 18% recoverable-value headroom today, with management's own words flagging continued weak demand is "likely to result in impairment in a future period" [`management-governance/02_capital-allocation-scorecard.md`, Finding 02-004].
- **Tomago's 2028 power-contract expiry** is the disclosed reason the smelter was already fully written down — a real forward risk to production volume, even though the P&L exposure is already at zero [`business-model/10_external-dependency.md` §1].
- **The five proposed European Extrusions plant closures (undated completion)** carry §24 Filter 2 risk: this is an unproven turnaround, not a delivered one, and FY2025 alone already saw four separate impairment events across three segments [`business-model/12_red-flags-sweep.md` §Impairment pattern].
- **EU ETS phase-out (2026–2034) and the Norway ICC scheme's 2030 expiry** are structural cost step-ups with no single date to react to — they accrue quietly rather than announce themselves [`business-model/10_external-dependency.md` §1].
- **A reversal of the Middle East LME supply shock** — de-escalation, resumed Gulf capacity (~9% of global output), or a China supply release — could pull the still-unbooked ~33% of Q2/Q3 primary aluminium volume back toward pre-shock spot levels inside either of the next two print windows, and is the most immediate way the "what's priced in" margin bet could start failing [`earnings/05_beat-miss-setup.md` §3].
- **The Norwegian State's ~34.3–34.5% blocking-minority stake** is not itself dated, but it caps how any of the above catalysts can be realized as a re-rating: the Board has explicitly declined to adopt takeover-bid-handling principles "due to the Norwegian state... ownership," and the valuation module's own RF-OWN-004 flag caps valuation attractiveness at 60 on this basis [`management-governance/05_board-and-shareholder-rights.md` §4; `valuation/07_scenario-and-fair-value.md` §5]. A buyer should not expect a takeover premium or an activist-driven re-rating to be an available exit path regardless of how the operating catalysts above resolve.

## 5. Calendar Read

Six of the thirteen catalysts on this calendar carry a proven, filing-confirmed date; the rest are either multi-year windows (EU carbon policy, the Norway ICC scheme, Tomago's contract renewal) or genuinely undisclosed dates inferred from past cadence (the FY2026 annual report, the 2027 AGM) or with no date at all (the Extrusions plant closures). The near-term calendar (next six months) is dominated by two hard-dated earnings prints (22-Jul-2026, 23-Oct-2026) rather than by capital-return, M&A, or regulatory-decision catalysts — there is no active buyback authorization, no scheduled regulatory ruling, and no index or market-structure event anywhere in the pool [`catalyst/00_catalyst-data-triage.md` §1]. Read together, the near-term calendar does not skew cleanly bullish or bearish on its own — it skews toward **evidence-revealing**: the two prints will show whether the market's already-aggressive margin assumption (13.9% held flat, per the reverse-DCF) is right, and the single biggest timing caveat is that Hydro gives no formal group guidance, so a buyer cannot pre-position for a scheduled "guidance day" the way one could at a company that issues quarterly outlooks — the segment bridges disclosed at each print are the only forward-looking signal available, and even those cover only one quarter ahead.

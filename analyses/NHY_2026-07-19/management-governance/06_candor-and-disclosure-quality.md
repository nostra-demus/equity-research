# Candor & Disclosure Quality — NHY

Norsk Hydro ASA reports under IFRS Accounting Standards as adopted by the EU, in Norwegian Krone (NOK), fiscal year ending 31 December [Integrated Annual Report 2025, p.140]. This agent uses two **verbatim** earnings-call transcripts in the data pool — the FQ4 2025 call (2026-02-13) and the FQ1 2026 call (2026-04-29), both S&P Global Market Intelligence-transcribed records of management's own words and analyst Q&A — so the partial-data cap for "no transcripts" does NOT apply here. `earnings/06_earnings-quality.md` is present and used as the non-GAAP cross-check, so that cap also does not apply.

## 2. Non-GAAP / Adjustment Aggressiveness

| Signal | Detail | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Recurring "one-off" addbacks | "Significant rationalization and closure costs" have been added back to EBITDA/EBIT every year for three straight years and the amount is growing, not shrinking: NOK 265m (FY2023) → NOK 407m (FY2024) → NOK 1,795m (FY2025). A goodwill/PP&E impairment has also been booked every year (NOK 4,424m / 22m / 1,069m). The module's own earnings-quality rule treats a charge that recurs every period as not genuinely one-off | earnings/06, §4–§5, §8 (Accounting Trap Checklist, severity 60/100 for recurring restructuring) |
| GAAP-to-adjusted gap (magnitude / trend) | The gap is modest and sign-flipping at the EBITDA line (−4.4% FY2023, −0.8% FY2024, +12.4% FY2025) but large and **directionally consistent every year** at net income/EPS: reported net income sat below the company's own adjusted figure by +34% (FY2025), +84% (FY2024), and +180% (FY2023). This is the single largest reported-vs-adjusted divergence earnings/06 identifies for Hydro | earnings/06, §7 (Reported vs Adjusted Reconciliation), §10 |
| Does adjusted framing flatter reality? | Partially. At EBITDA, the adjusted figure is a fair operating read (the swings are structural, non-cash derivative-timing effects). At net income and EPS, the adjusted figure is persistently and materially higher than reported, driven by impairments and the recurring rationalization add-back — a reader who anchors on adjusted EPS alone (e.g. NOK 4.26 adjusted vs NOK 1.77 reported in FY2023) gets a rosier read of that year than the audited bottom line supports. The company discloses the full reconciliation and it is audited — this is a disclosed-but-persistent framing bias, not concealment, and earnings/06 scored overall earnings quality 55/100 ("mixed... material concerns from recurring one-off charges") on this exact pattern | earnings/06, §7, §9, §10 |

## 3. Disclosure Transparency

Segment-level disclosure is genuinely granular: each quarter management gives a five-segment (Bauxite & Alumina, Aluminium Metal, Metal Markets, Extrusions, Energy) forward bridge with quantified NOK/USD cost and price ranges — e.g. the FQ1 2026 call gave specific figures such as "67% of Q2 primary production booked at USD 3,000/tonne," "carbon costs expected to increase NOK 150-250 million," and "fixed costs NOK 300-400 million higher" — well beyond boilerplate segment commentary [Q1 2026 transcript, presentation section]. One disclosure gap has persisted rather than improved: Hydro still does not split maintenance ("sustaining") capex from growth capex in the data pool, despite qualitatively flagging "historically high sustaining capital expenditure," which forces this module's free-cash-flow read to use total capex as a blunter proxy [earnings/06, §1, footnote 3; integrated-annual-report-2025.pdf, p.51]. No evidence was found of any KPI or segment disclosure becoming *less* transparent over time — if anything the quarter-over-quarter cost/price bridges have become more numerically specific across the two transcripts reviewed.

## 4. Tone in Bad Times

The worst reported quarter in the pool is FQ4 2025 (reported net loss of NOK (2.2)bn, EPS consensus miss of −6.8%, negative Extrusions EBITDA, plant closures and a workforce reduction). The CEO's tone was matter-of-fact rather than promotional — he named the restructuring as "difficult but necessary" and did not use language claiming strength where the numbers showed weakness. In Q&A, analysts asked pointed, specific questions (extrusion order-book visibility for H2 2026, whether the strong B&A trading result was sustainable, the drivers of the fixed-cost increase) and received direct numeric answers each time (e.g., "around NOK 300 million in very strong commercial results in B&A in Q4") rather than deflection to "we'll follow up offline." The one genuinely evasive-sounding answer — on whether extrusion volumes would recover in H2 2026 — was an honest "too early to conclude" rather than a confident but unsupported forecast, which is itself a candor-consistent response to real forward uncertainty rather than spin.

## 5. Candor Read

Management owns its misses in plain language: it wrote "2025 EBITDA targets not reached; 2030 targets postponed" on its own investor-day slide, led its Q1 2026 highlights with a negative free-cash-flow number rather than hiding it behind an EBITDA beat, and opened a call with a fatal workplace incident before any financial content. Q&A across both verbatim transcripts shows analysts getting specific, numeric answers rather than deflection. The one real soft spot is non-GAAP discipline — a "rationalization and closure cost" that has been added back as non-recurring every year for three years, growing each time, and a net-income/EPS gap versus the adjusted figure that has run from +34% to +180% across FY2023–FY2025 — fully disclosed and audited, but a persistent pattern that flatters the adjusted bottom line versus the reported one. No unresolved adverse integrity signal was routed from `business-model/01_disqualifier-scan` (confirmed in `01_management-and-track-record.md`, §4A), so there is no compounding integrity concern to weigh against this read. On balance: **candid / high-trust**, with the qualifier that reported net income/EPS should never be read standalone given the scale and persistence of the adjusted-vs-reported gap.

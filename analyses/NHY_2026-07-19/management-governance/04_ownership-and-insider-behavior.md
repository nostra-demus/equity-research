| Controlled-company status | Not a formally "controlled company" under any single-majority test (no holder exceeds 50%), but the Ministry of Trade, Industry and Fisheries' 34.26–34.49% stake is a **blocking minority** under Norwegian company law (many special resolutions require a two-thirds majority of votes cast), and combined with Folketrygdfondet's 7.3% state-linked stake, state-affiliated holders control ~41.8% of the register — the single largest bloc by a wide margin over the next holder (BlackRock/Vanguard, each under 5%). | [integrated-annual-report-2025.pdf, Note 9.1, p.198; NUES Report, p.293]; [NorskHydroASAOBNHY_PublicCompany.pdf, Top Holders, 2026-07-18] |
| Shareholder bloc with board rights | The State has no charter-level board-nomination right disclosed; Board members are elected by the General Meeting via the Nomination Committee under ordinary process. However, the NUES Report explicitly carves out a Section-14 (Takeover) deviation "due to the Norwegian state['s]... ownership of 34.26 percent," meaning the Board has NOT adopted general takeover-defense principles because the State's blocking stake already functions as a de facto takeover deterrent — a real, if informal, control-rights effect of the stake's size. | [integrated-annual-report-2025.pdf, NUES Report §14 "Takeovers," p.293: "The Board has not established general principles for handling takeover bids due to the Norwegian state['s]... ownership of 34.26 percent"] |
| Controlling-owner objective alignment *(Filter 6)* | **Government control test trips.** The Ministry describes its stake in Hydro via Norway's "Active Ownership Report" (Report to the Storting no. 6, 2022-2023) as held for "a long-term ownership perspective... for the purpose of retaining a leading technology and industrial company with head office functions in Norway" — an explicit industrial-policy / jobs-and-headquarters objective, not a stated per-share-value-maximization mandate. This is the textbook Filter 6(a) government-control conflict: the controlling shareholder pursues national-strategic objectives (keeping HQ functions and technology in Norway) that can diverge from a minority holder's pure return objective. | [integrated-annual-report-2025.pdf, NUES Report §14, p.293: "the Active Ownership Report (Report to the Storting no. 6 (2022-2023))... expressed a long-term ownership perspective in the company for the purpose of retaining a leading technology and industrial company with head office functions in Norway... p.44"] |

**Unaligned-owner test (CLAUDE.md §24, Filter 6) — RF-OWN-004 triggered.** The Ministry of Trade, Industry and Fisheries' 34.26–34.49% stake is government control per Filter 6(a): the State's own disclosed rationale for holding the stake is industrial/employment policy (retaining head-office functions and technology in Norway), not per-share value creation. This is a structural cap on shareholder-friendliness, not a discount to arbitrage — a stock trading cheap under this ownership structure can stay cheap indefinitely because the controlling holder is not selling into strength, forcing capital discipline, or pushing for a re-rating; it is holding for national-strategic reasons. That said, two mitigants are disclosed and should not be discarded: (1) there is no preferential voting right and one share class, so the State cannot extract value through differential voting power; (2) the State's own buyback participation was capped to keep its percentage stake exactly flat (34.26%) rather than let its influence creep up via non-participation in a shrinking share count — a small positive signal that it is not using the buyback mechanism to increase control cheaply. Net: the government-control conflict is real and structural, but it is a "hold for strategic reasons" risk (patient, non-extractive) rather than a "hold to loot/self-deal" risk — Note 9.1's related-party transactions with the State-linked ecosystem (Statkraft/DNB/wind-JV power purchases) are individually small (~NOK 428m combined vs NOK 207,971m FY2025 revenue) and disclosed at arm's length. **Persistent cheapness under this ownership structure should be read by the valuation module as a possible value trap, not a margin of safety, until there is direct evidence the State would support or vote for value-accretive corporate actions (buybacks, M&A, structural change) against its stated jobs/HQ mandate.**

## 5. Ownership / Insider Read

Executives bought their stake through a Long-Term Incentive program that requires them to invest net proceeds in Hydro shares and hold for three years — a real purchase mechanism, not a pure grant — but the resulting personal holdings are tiny in percentage terms: the CEO's 111,605 shares and the CFO's 23,659 shares, plus the rest of the Board and Executive Leadership Team, add up to roughly 0.027% of the company, so there is essentially no insider ownership signal to read either way. There is no pool record of open-market insider buying or selling in the last 12 months (only a web-sourced, unverified, plan-based purchase in April 2026, which does not count as a red flag or a conviction signal under this module's rules), and no pledging by the State or any director is disclosed anywhere. The biggest structural fact for minority holders is not insider behavior — it is that the Norwegian state, through the Ministry of Trade, Industry and Fisheries, controls 34.26–34.49% of the company for an explicitly stated industrial-policy purpose (keeping head-office functions and technology in Norway), not for per-share value maximization; that single fact, not management's modest personal stake, is the dominant ownership risk this module can identify.

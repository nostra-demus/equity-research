| Long-term incentive (LTI) | 3-year Total Shareholder Return (TSR) relative to a named peer group (Nalco, Rusal, Alcoa, Century Aluminium, Hindalco, Chalco, Grupa Kety, Constellium, Kaiser, ProfilGruppen, Tredegar); payout band is Hydro's TSR minus peer TSR between −5pp and +10pp | 50% | **Returns-based** — TSR is by definition a per-share (price + dividend) return metric, and it is measured *relative to peers*, not on absolute scale | [integrated-annual-report-2025.pdf, "The Hydro share," p.64, footnote 4 (peer list); Remuneration Report 2025, "LTI performance goals," p.21-22] |

**No metric anywhere in the disclosed CEO/CFO plan is revenue, absolute EBITDA, deal count, or headcount.** The entire LTI (100% of weight) and over a third of the STI (35%) are pure capital-efficiency or shareholder-return metrics; the remaining STI weight (65%) is safety/ESG/cost-efficiency and an undisclosed individual component — not empire-building measures, but also not per-share value metrics. The LTI plan requires the earned amount to be invested in Hydro shares with a **3-year lock-in**, and **Hydro has no share-option scheme** — removing the asymmetric risk-taking incentive that options can create. The LTI is also conditional on the company posting a positive adjusted EBIT in the prior year (a loss-year payout blocker) [Remuneration Report 2025, "LTI performance goals," p.21; "Overview of remuneration elements," p.7].

**2025 LTI outcome shows real teeth, not a rubber stamp:** the RoaCE component paid 68.7% of its potential (3-year average market-value RoaCE of 6.9% — 5.46% in 2023, 6.9% in 2024, 8.3% in 2025 — against the 10% target), but the **TSR component paid zero**: Hydro's cumulative 2023–2025 TSR was +7.6% versus the named peer group's weighted average of +24.1%, a −16.5 percentage-point shortfall that falls below the −5pp minimum threshold for any payout. Blended, the 2025 LTI paid out at 34.3% of maximum potential — NOK 829k for the CEO, equal to 10.3% of his base salary against a 30%-of-base maximum [Remuneration Report 2025, "LTI results for 2025," p.22].

**Redesign in progress.** Following shareholder and proxy-advisor feedback on the FY2024 remuneration report (see §3), the Board is replacing the current backward-looking restricted-share LTI with a forward-looking performance-share plan from the 2027 grant, keeping the same 50%/50% RoaCE/TSR-vs-peer split but shifting the performance period to run *before* vesting rather than being locked in after a historical award — intended to tighten the link between pay and future, not past, shareholder value creation [Remuneration Report 2025, "Revised Long-Term Incentive (LTI) structure — Effective from 2027 grant," p.23].

**CEO's ESG/climate-linked pay is material and separately tracked.** Within the Performance-goal bucket, the report separately discloses that the CEO could earn up to 19.2% of his base salary on ESG-tagged sub-targets (environmental, nature, climate, social, governance, human-rights-in-supply-chain) in 2025 and actually earned 19.0% of base salary against them — a meaningful share of his roughly 44% of base salary in total STI, and distinct from the RoaCE/TSR financial metrics [Remuneration Report 2025, "CEO Compensation linked to ESG targets and achievements," p.21].

## 4. Incentive Alignment Read

Hydro's pay plan rewards **capital efficiency and relative shareholder return, not scale**: RoaCE (a return-on-capital metric, split between the STI's 35%-weighted financial goal and half of the LTI) and TSR-versus-a-named-peer-group (the other half of the LTI) are the only two financial metrics in the CEO's and CFO's compensation, and neither revenue, absolute EBITDA, acquisition count, nor headcount appears anywhere in the disclosed structure — and the mechanism has real teeth, having paid zero on the LTI's TSR leg for 2023-2025 despite a strong 2025 on its own. The remaining ~65% of the STI (safety/ESG/cost-efficiency scorecards, plus an undisclosed 25% individual component) is not size-chasing either, but it dilutes the purity of the per-share signal and leaves a real disclosure gap on a quarter of annual bonus; combined with heavy fixed-pay weighting (71% for the CEO) and below-peer-median total pay that drew a notable 26% say-on-pay opposition vote on the prior year's report, the picture is one of a deliberately moderated, mostly-returns-based plan rather than an aggressive pay-for-growth one. **Conclusion: well-aligned (returns/per-share)**, with the caveats that the individual-goal weighting is opaque and the plan's teeth were demonstrated on the downside (a zero LTI payout leg) more convincingly than they have yet been tested on unchecked upside.

# Capital-Allocation Scorecard — NHY

Reporting currency: Norwegian Krone (NOK million unless stated per-share, in NOK). Reporting standard: IFRS Accounting Standards as adopted by the EU, fiscal year ending 31 December [Integrated Annual Report 2025, p.140]. Window covered: FY2021–FY2025 (5 fiscal years) plus the LTM Mar-2026 quarter where disclosed. This deepens and supersedes `business-model/11_capital-allocation-governance.md` per MODULE_RULES.md.

**Data-quality flag carried forward from `earnings/01_historical-financials.md`:** the Capital IQ workbook's own EBITDA/EBIT/"Return on Capital %" fields for NHY do not reconcile to the company's audited figures (a ~NOK 27bn gap on EBITDA), and the CIQ Cash-Flow tab's "Cash Acquisitions" and "Divestitures" lines read NOK 0 in every year shown — inconsistent with the disclosed FY2023 Alumetal acquisition and the 2024–2025 battery-portfolio divestments. This looks like a vendor template/classification mismatch for Hydro's statement structure, not a real economic zero. Per CLAUDE.md §4/§5, this report uses the company's own reported figures (EBIT after tax, Capital Employed, RoaCE, buyback/dividend cash flows, share counts) wherever the annual report discloses them, and does not use the CIQ EBIT, EBITDA, "Return on Capital %," "Cash Acquisitions" or "Divestitures" fields. The gap is flagged, not silently overridden.

## 5. Capital-Allocation Read

Capital has been allocated to create per-share value in some respects and to grow for its own sake in at least one clear, admitted instance. The best use of capital was debt discipline and the buyback mechanic: net debt never breached the company's own 2x adjusted-EBITDA ceiling even in the two years (FY2023–FY2024) when dividends outran free cash flow, and buybacks genuinely shrank the share count by 4.2% over five years rather than merely mopping up stock-based-comp dilution. The worst use of capital was the battery-materials and green-hydrogen growth bet (Vianode, Hydrovolt, Lithium de France): capital and strategic attention were committed to an adjacent-growth area for roughly three years, then explicitly abandoned in November 2024, with the Vianode stake written to zero and finally sold "with no gain or loss" — a clean value write-off that management itself narrated candidly. The single biggest capital-allocation signal is that this abandoned bet sits right alongside the one disclosed M&A deal (Alumetal) that is *also* running thin — an 18% impairment-test headroom the company itself flags as fragile to "continued weak market conditions" — so two of Hydro's three material capital-allocation bets over the period (Alumetal, batteries/hydrogen) are either unproven or already reversed, while the third (buybacks/dividends/debt) has been genuinely disciplined. Overall verdict: **mixed** — disciplined on the balance sheet and on returning cash, unproven-to-value-destructive on the growth side.

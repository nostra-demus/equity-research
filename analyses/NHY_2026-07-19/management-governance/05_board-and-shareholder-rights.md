**One deviation worth flagging on its own:** the Board has explicitly declined to adopt the NUES-recommended practice of establishing "general principles for handling takeover bids," and states this is specifically **because of the Norwegian state's 34.26% ownership stake** — implying the Board itself judges a contested takeover to be impractical given the state's blocking position, rather than because Hydro has installed its own defenses [integrated-annual-report-2025.pdf, NUES Report §14, p.297: "The Board has not established general principles for handling takeover bids due to the Norwegian state... ownership of 34.26 percent of the Hydro shares"]. This means the practical takeover-defense mechanism at Hydro is not a poison pill or staggered board — it is the sheer size of the state's stake, which also functions as a blocking minority (above the roughly one-third threshold) against special resolutions requiring a two-thirds majority under Norwegian company law (e.g., mergers, capital-structure changes, Articles amendments). This is disclosed, not hidden, but it is a real structural constraint on any change-of-control outcome that the state's Ministry does not support.

## 5. Minority-Shareholder Protection Read

The architecture is genuinely shareholder-friendly on paper: one share class with equal voting rights, no poison pill, no staggered board, no preferential voting for the state, a fully split Chair/CEO structure, a majority-independent shareholder-elected board bench that has refreshed three of seven seats in the last two years, and related-party dealings that are small (about 0.27% of revenue), recurring, and explicitly attested as arm's length by the Board — none of the promoter-style leakage risk that shows up in controlled-company structures elsewhere applies here. The single biggest structural risk to minority holders is not board entrenchment in the usual sense — it is that the Norwegian state's ~34.3-34.5% stake is large enough to function as a blocking minority over major corporate-law resolutions and large enough that Hydro's own Board has declined to even draft takeover-bid-handling principles, citing the state's position as the reason a contested change of control is not realistically on the table; the state's stated rationale for holding the stake ("retaining a leading technology and industrial company with head office functions in Norway," per the Storting Active Ownership Report) is a national-strategic objective, not a per-share-value mandate, and that gap between the controlling shareholder's goal and a per-share-value goal is the item `04_ownership-and-insider-behavior` should test directly against CLAUDE.md §24 Filter 6 (structurally unaligned controlling owner) — this agent flags the fact pattern but does not itself apply that filter's score cap, since `04_ownership-and-insider-behavior` cross-module input is not available at time of this report (parallel layer, not yet run). A second, more contained gap is that the pool contains no AGM vote-tally data for either the 2025 or 2026 meeting, so opposition levels on pay, RPT, or director resolutions cannot be checked. On the evidence actually in hand, this reads as **adequate** protection: the mechanical rights (one share/one vote, no defenses, clean RPT, independent-majority board) are real and verified, but the concentration of a strategically-motivated 34%+ shareholder and the missing voting-transparency data mean the read stops short of "strong."

*Cross-module note for `99_management-governance-synthesis`:* if `04_ownership-and-insider-behavior` independently concludes the Norwegian state's stake and stated national-strategic mandate meet the RF-OWN-004 bar (structurally unaligned controlling owner, CLAUDE.md §24 Filter 6), the synthesis should apply the associated Shareholder friendliness cap (max 55) and Governance risk floor (55) per the Score Cap Rules, and treat this module's minority-protection read as no better than "weak / entrenched" on that basis, superseding the "adequate" conclusion reached here on the board-mechanics evidence alone.

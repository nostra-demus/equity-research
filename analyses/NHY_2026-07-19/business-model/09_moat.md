## 1. Named Competitors

- **United Company RUSAL (SEHK:486)** — Russia-based, integrated bauxite-alumina-aluminium producer; larger volume but structurally weaker margin, far more leveraged (Total Debt/EBITDA 9.3x vs Hydro's 0.7x), sanctions-exposed [`08_competitive-map.md`, Competitor A].
- **Aluminum Corporation of China Limited ("Chalco") (SEHK:2600)** — the largest-revenue peer (1.7x Hydro), state-linked, China-domestic-demand-driven, mid-pack margins between Hydro's and RUSAL's [`08_competitive-map.md`, Competitor B].
- **Hindalco Industries Limited (BSE:500440)** — larger (1.4x Hydro revenue), copper-diversified integrated aluminium producer whose reported ROCE may currently exceed Hydro's own cycle-adjusted return [`08_competitive-map.md`, Competitor C].

**The economic moat test.** Return on capital sits **above** cost of capital on Hydro's own 5-year through-cycle basis, but only **at / marginally above** it on the latest-year and unadjusted bases:

> Return on capital **above** cost of capital on the 5-year through-cycle basis: **13.3% 5-yr average Adjusted RoaCE (FY2021-2025)** vs **10% "over-the-cycle" target**, +330bps [integrated-annual-report-2025.pdf, Alternative Performance Measures, p.234: "Over the last 5 years, the average adjusted RoaCE is 13.3 percent... above the target of 10 percent over the cycle"]. On the latest single year and on an unadjusted basis, the gap nearly disappears: FY2025 Adjusted RoaCE 10.2% vs the 10% target (+20bps only), and FY2025 **Reported** (unadjusted) RoaCE is 7.2% — **below** the 10% target [integrated-annual-report-2025.pdf, p.234, "Reported / Adjusted" RoaCE table].

Notes on this test:
- **Cost-of-capital source.** Hydro does not disclose a single group WACC figure. The best available company-disclosed anchor is its own stated strategic target, "Adjusted RoaCE > 10 percent over-the-cycle," which management explicitly frames as calibrated to differentiated business-area cost of capital ("Cost of capital and ARoaCE targets are differentiated for each business area... due to industry cyclicality") [integrated-annual-report-2025.pdf, p.1006-1007]. As corroborating (not primary) context, Hydro separately discloses a pre-tax nominal WACC range of 9.25%-11.75% (FY2025) used for goodwill/asset impairment testing, differentiated by asset [integrated-annual-report-2025.pdf, Note on impairment testing, p.162]. Converting this pre-tax range to post-tax terms at Hydro's own 30% standardized adjustment tax rate implies roughly 6.5%-8.2% post-tax — *Inference, not from filings* — which brackets the 10% target from below and is broadly consistent with using 10% as the working cost-of-capital proxy.
- **Segment split matters more than the group number.** Hydro's own FY2025 disclosure states plainly: "Energy and the upstream business areas delivered returns well above their cost of capital... The downstream segments delivered ARoaCE below their cost of capital" [integrated-annual-report-2025.pdf, p.1559-1562]. FY2025 Adjusted RoaCE by segment: Bauxite & Alumina 18.6%, Energy 17.2%, Aluminium Metal 14.4%, Metal Markets 0.8%, Extrusions -2.2% [integrated-annual-report-2025.pdf, p.1552-1570 chart]. The group's above-cost-of-capital read is entirely carried by the upstream/Energy chain where the natural-resource-access and cost-advantage moat sources actually sit (§2); Metal Markets and Extrusions — the two segments with no evidenced moat source — sit below cost of capital.
- **Management-headline cross-check.** Hydro's own Reported (unadjusted, actual-tax) RoaCE (7.2%, FY2025) is materially lower than its Adjusted RoaCE (10.2%, FY2025) — the adjusted figure strips out items including a NOK 1,504m unrealized LME-related loss, NOK 1,795m rationalization/closure charges and NOK 1,069m impairment charges [integrated-annual-report-2025.pdf, p.1546-1551]. Per source hierarchy, the more conservative Reported figure is flagged here rather than silently accepting the higher Adjusted headline; the 5-year through-cycle Adjusted average (13.3%) is used for the primary verdict because it is the best available multi-year, cycle-spanning figure disclosed, but the single-year Reported figure (7.2%, below the 10% proxy) is a live caveat, not a rounding footnote.
- **Vendor figure not preferred.** Capital IQ's own standardized "Return on Capital %" for Hydro is higher still — 18.0% (FY2025), 5-yr range 16.1%-24.6% [Norsk Hydro ASA OB NHY Financials.xls, Ratios tab] — but this uses a different EBIT definition (CIQ's "Operating Income" of NOK 41,218m for FY2025 does not reconcile to Hydro's own segment-note reported EBIT of NOK 14,401m [`03_segment-map.md` §2], a NOK ~27bn gap this pool cannot fully explain from available detail) and a capital base not verified against Hydro's own Capital Employed figure. This vendor figure is **not preferred** for the economic-moat test; Hydro's own audited-report RoaCE figures are used instead, consistent with the source hierarchy (company filing over vendor export for the same metric).
- **Normalized tax rate.** Hydro's own Adjusted net income methodology tax-effects one-off adjusting items (unrealized LME losses, impairments, rationalization charges) at a standardized 30 percent rate rather than the distorted actual effective tax rate, which swung from 24.3% (2021) to 57.2% (2023) to 39.5% (2025) [Norsk Hydro ASA OB NHY Financials.xls, Income Statement tab; integrated-annual-report-2025.pdf, Note on adjusted net income, p.10317]. This report uses that same 30% standardized rate as the anchor for any normalized NOPAT read, for consistency with `valuation/04_intrinsic-dcf` where that module runs.
- **Net-cash check.** Not applicable — Hydro carries net debt, not net cash (Net Debt/EBITDA 0.24x-0.35x, FY2021-FY2025; cash NOK 16,085m vs total debt NOK 36,574m, FY2025) [Norsk-Hydro-ASA-OB-NHY-Financials, Ratios tab and Balance Sheet tab], so no gross-vs-net-of-cash capital-base adjustment is required.

## 4. Where The Company Sits

1. **Relative to peers:** Hydro sits at the **top** of the named peer set on margin — LTM EBITDA margin 23.7% vs Chalco 18.6%, Hindalco 12.9% and RUSAL 7.0% [`08_competitive-map.md` §3] — but the peer return-on-capital figures are dated, unverified web reads (not each peer's own primary filing), so a precise ranking on return on capital cannot be made with confidence; on the numbers as reported, Hindalco's cited ROCE (~14.6%) and Chalco's cited ROE (~18.6%) may exceed Hydro's own FY2025 Adjusted RoaCE (10.2%), though these are not apples-to-apples metrics (ROCE/ROE vs RoaCE) [`08_competitive-map.md`, Competitor C].
2. **Absolute (the economic moat test):** the company earns a return on capital **above** its cost-of-capital proxy on a 5-year through-cycle basis (13.3% vs 10%) but only **at/marginally above** it on the latest year (10.2% Adjusted vs 10%) and **below** it on the latest year's unadjusted/Reported basis (7.2% vs 10%) — see §3 for the full breakdown and the segment split that explains the gap.

## 5. Moat Verdict

**Narrow moat.** The strongest, best-evidenced advantage is **cost advantage / natural resource access**, combined score reflecting captive bauxite (Paragominas, ~100% of two smelter/refinery chains) and captive/long-contract hydropower, which place Hydro's primary smelting at the 16th percentile of the global cost curve — genuinely hard for RUSAL, Chalco or Hindalco to replicate without owning equivalent captive resources. But the hard rule applies here: the group's return on capital sits only modestly above its cost-of-capital proxy through the cycle (13.3% vs 10%, +330bps) and is at or below it on the latest year depending on which return figure (Adjusted vs Reported) is used — this is not the clear, durable margin the "Strong moat" bar requires, and it is entirely carried by the upstream/Energy segments while Metal Markets and Extrusions sit below cost of capital with no evidenced moat source of their own. The durability test over the next 5 years: can the 16th-percentile cost position and captive-resource base be defended as Chinese and Russian-linked producers (Chalco, Hongqiao, RUSAL) expand or as Norwegian hydropower concessions approach their ~2050 reversion point, while Extrusions either stabilizes or continues shedding capacity. Industry rate-of-change scored 68/100 in `07_business-quality.md` (above the ≤40 fast-changing threshold), so no additional disruption-based durability discount applies — this is a slow-moving, capital-cycle industry, not a technology-cycle bet.

**Moat trajectory: eroding.** At the whole-company level the trajectory points down, not sideways: Hydro Extrusions' EBIT margin fell from ~4% (2020-2023) to -2.2% (2025), triggering five proposed European plant closures [`03_segment-map.md`, Extrusions row] — a segment losing, not gaining, competitive ground. The upstream cost-curve position (16th percentile) has no disclosed multi-year trend in this pool to confirm it is widening, and `07_business-quality.md`'s competitive-intensity row (score 45/100) flags that "a mid-cost-curve competitor a few years from now is a live possibility, not a tail risk" given global overcapacity dynamics — a stable-to-slightly-eroding read for the segment that actually carries the moat. Combined, the group-level trajectory is judged eroding, driven by the downstream segment's real, evidenced decline and a caution flag (not yet a trend) on the upstream cost position.

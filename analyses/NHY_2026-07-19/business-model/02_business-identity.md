# Business Identity — NHY

## 1. What The Company Actually Does

Norsk Hydro digs up bauxite ore in Brazil, turns it into alumina powder, then uses electricity to turn that alumina into liquid aluminium metal, and finally shapes some of that metal into finished products such as window frames, car parts and cooling tubes — while also running a Nordic and Brazilian hydropower business that supplies much of the electricity the smelters need. It owns "one of the largest bauxite mines in the world" (Paragominas, Brazil) and "the largest alumina refinery outside China" (Alunorte, Brazil), five wholly-owned primary-aluminium smelters in Norway plus stakes in five more abroad (Qatar, Brazil, Canada, Australia, Slovakia), a network of 12 metal recyclers, and 69 extrusion plants in 20 countries [integrated-annual-report-2025.pdf, "About Hydro" / Business areas, p.10-18]. Its buyers are other industrial companies, not consumers: automakers, construction and building-systems firms, packaging and beverage-can makers, and electrical/renewable-energy manufacturers, reached through roughly 30,000 customers across more than 140 locations in 40 countries [integrated-annual-report-2025.pdf, "Our presence", p.12]. Customers pay Hydro to solve two problems: getting a steady, contracted supply of a metal that is priced daily on a public commodity exchange (the London Metal Exchange, LME), and — increasingly — getting that metal with a certified low carbon footprint, for which some customers pay a premium [integrated-annual-report-2025.pdf, p.16-17]. FY2025 total revenue was NOK 207,971 million and adjusted EBITDA — earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortization, a company-defined measure that strips out one-off items — was NOK 28,889 million [integrated-annual-report-2025.pdf, Consolidated income statement, p.135; Key figures, p.36].

## 2. How The Company Makes Money

Hydro is really five linked commodity businesses sharing one value chain, each with its own formula:

- **Hydro Bauxite & Alumina:** `Revenue = alumina tonnes sold × realized alumina price (USD/mt)`. FY2025: alumina production 6,086 thousand tonnes (kmt), realized alumina price USD 432/mt [integrated-annual-report-2025.pdf, Key figures, p.36].
- **Hydro Aluminium Metal (primary smelting):** `Revenue = primary aluminium tonnes produced × (LME aluminium price + product premium)`. FY2025: primary aluminium production 2,065 kmt, realized aluminium price (LME) USD 2,573/mt [integrated-annual-report-2025.pdf, Key figures, p.36].
- **Hydro Metal Markets (recycling + trading):** `Revenue = recycled/traded tonnes sold × (LME price + premium)`, plus trading and hedging margin [integrated-annual-report-2025.pdf, Business areas — Metal Markets, p.16-17].
- **Hydro Extrusions:** `Revenue = extrusion tonnes sold × price per tonne`, where price is negotiated per customer/application rather than set purely by LME [integrated-annual-report-2025.pdf, Business areas — Extrusions, p.18]. FY2025 external sales volumes were 978 kmt [integrated-annual-report-2025.pdf, Key figures, p.36].
- **Hydro Energy:** `Revenue = power volume sold × power price`, from ~18 TWh/year of owned and contracted Nordic/Brazilian hydropower, most of which is used internally to feed the smelters rather than sold externally [integrated-annual-report-2025.pdf, Business areas — Energy, p.15].

Volume is set by smelter/refinery capacity and utilization (Hydro's total primary-aluminium capacity is about 2.1 million tonnes/year) [integrated-annual-report-2025.pdf, p.16]. Price is set almost entirely outside the company's control — by the LME 3-month aluminium price (FY2025 average around USD 2,573/mt realized) and by product premiums that shift with regional supply/demand [integrated-annual-report-2025.pdf, p.36; Q1 2026 report/deck]. Margin is driven by where Hydro sits on the industry cost curve: management states Hydro's primary-aluminium smelting placed at the 16th percentile of the global cost curve in 2025 (lower is cheaper, better), with alumina, power and carbon anodes together making up 80-85% of smelting cash cost, and Alunorte's refining costs dominated by bauxite, energy and caustic soda (about 85% of cash cost) [integrated-annual-report-2025.pdf, p.14, p.16].

## 3. Business Type Classification

Vertically-integrated commodity aluminium producer — bauxite mine to alumina refinery to primary smelter to recycler/extruder — with a captive hydropower business that funds and de-risks its own energy input costs.

**Data gap called out:** reserve life and grade/strip ratio are both absent from the pool. This caps how confidently `business-quality` and `moat` can assess the durability of the Bauxite & Alumina segment's resource base — a mine-dependent business analyzed without a stated reserve life is an incomplete read on that specific point, and downstream modules should treat mine-life durability as "not proven from available data" rather than assume permanence.

**Sector red flags to watch (per overlay):** cost inflation eroding the margin — the annual report itself flags Brazilian wage/inflation exposure in bauxite mining and caustic-soda/energy exposure in alumina refining [integrated-annual-report-2025.pdf, p.14]; reserve depletion without replacement — not assessable given the reserve-life data gap above; single-asset/single-commodity concentration — Bauxite & Alumina is built around one mine (Paragominas) and one refinery (Alunorte), both in Brazil [integrated-annual-report-2025.pdf, p.14]; price taken at the peak — Hydro's realized prices move with the LME cycle and can be booked into hedges months ahead (e.g., "2026: 345 kt remaining hedged at ~USD 2,800/t") [nhy-presentation-q1-2026.pdf, p.736].

**Valuation norm for this type:** mid-cycle-normalized free-cash-flow DCF (never a single peak or trough year), EV/EBITDA measured across the cycle, and — where reserve data exists — a net-asset-value check on the reserve base. Given the reserve-life gap noted above, the NAV leg of that valuation norm is **not assessable** from this pool; this should be flagged to the valuation module rather than skipped silently.

## 4. What Drives Variance

The two biggest swing factors are the LME 3-month aluminium price and regional product premiums — both set externally, not by Hydro — since realized aluminium price on its own moved from USD 2,218/mt (2023) to USD 2,374/mt (2024) to USD 2,573/mt (2025) [integrated-annual-report-2025.pdf, p.36]. The next biggest factor is input cost, especially the price of alumina, electricity/carbon anodes for smelting (together 80-85% of smelting cash cost), and energy/caustic soda for alumina refining (about 85% of refining cash cost) [integrated-annual-report-2025.pdf, p.14, p.16]. Currency also matters directly — Hydro reports in NOK but sells largely at USD-linked prices, so the realized USD/NOK exchange rate (10.39 in 2025 vs 10.75 in 2024) changes reported revenue and margin even if underlying USD prices and volumes are flat [integrated-annual-report-2025.pdf, p.36]. Volume/utilization (smelter curtailments or ramp-ups) and mix (how much output goes to higher-value casthouse/extrusion products versus standard ingot) are secondary but still material drivers, particularly for Hydro Extrusions and Metal Markets [integrated-annual-report-2025.pdf, p.16-17].

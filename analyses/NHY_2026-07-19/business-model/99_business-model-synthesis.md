# Business Model Reality Check — NHY (Synthesis)

## Abstract

Norsk Hydro mines bauxite, refines it into alumina, smelts it into aluminium, and shapes some into extruded products, backed by captive Nordic and Brazilian hydropower. Profit is dominated by Hydro Aluminium Metal, 39.5% of FY2025's NOK 28,889 million adjusted EBITDA (earnings before interest, tax, depreciation, amortization) despite only 7.1% of revenue. Its strongest positive: a 16th-percentile global cost-curve position from captive bauxite and hydropower gives it the best EBITDA margin (23.7%) among three named peers. Its strongest negative: near-total price-taking drove a business-quality score of 34/100, with group ROE swinging from 24.9% (2022) to 2.6% (2023). No disqualifier triggered; verdict is cyclical, worth deeper work only with a timing edge.

## 5. Simple Summary

- **What it does:** Mines bauxite, refines it to alumina, smelts it into aluminium, and turns some into extruded products (window frames, car parts, cooling tubes) — plus runs a captive hydropower business that powers its own smelters [`02_business-identity.md`].
- **How it makes money:** Sells aluminium and alumina at prices set by the London Metal Exchange and public alumina indexes, plus a regional/product premium it partly controls; margin comes from being cheap to produce (16th-percentile global cost curve), not from pricing power [`02_business-identity.md`; `06_value-chain.md`].
- **Whether each new unit creates value:** Yes, at current pricing — Aluminium Metal earns a 14.4% after-tax return on capital (ARoaCE) against a 10% group hurdle, but the margin cushion (~USD 532/mt) is smaller than a single 20% price swing, so the "creates value" answer is cycle-dependent, not guaranteed [`04_unit-economics.md`].
- **Which segment matters most:** Hydro Aluminium Metal, 39.5% of FY2025 profit despite only 7.1% of revenue — but this rotates with the alumina cycle and was Bauxite & Alumina's turn in FY2024 [`03_segment-map.md`].
- **Whether it has a moat, and against whom:** A narrow moat against RUSAL, Chalco, and Hindalco — built on captive bauxite and hydropower giving the best margin of the three — but it is eroding at the whole-company level as Hydro Extrusions' margin turns negative and five European plants face closure [`09_moat.md`].
- **What external variables it depends on:** The LME aluminium price, the USD/NOK exchange rate, EU carbon policy (CBAM/ETS), and geopolitical supply shocks — all outside management's control, cushioned only partly by hedging and a low-cost position [`10_external-dependency.md`].
- **Whether capital is allocated well:** Reasonably — one bolt-on deal in five years plus active divestment of non-core bets, falling share count, and low leverage (net debt/EBITDA 0.24x) — but an unquantified Brazilian tax/Alunorte contingency and a thin impairment cushion on the one recent acquisition (Alumetal) are live, unresolved risks [`11_capital-allocation-governance.md`; `12_red-flags-sweep.md`].
- **Whether it deserves deeper work:** Only with a strong, evidenced view on where the aluminium/alumina price cycle is headed — the business model itself is a cyclical, price-taking commodity producer, not a durable compounder.

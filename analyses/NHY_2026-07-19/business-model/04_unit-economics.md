# Unit Economics — NHY

## 1. Natural Unit

The natural economic unit for Hydro Aluminium Metal (the dominant segment by profit — 39.5% of FY2025 group Adjusted EBITDA, per `03_segment-map.md`) is **one tonne of primary aluminium produced and sold** (metric tonne, "mt"). This is a manufacturer, so the unit is a physical unit of output, not a customer or account — the closest parallel to "manufacturer, per unit produced or ton" in the natural-unit reference list.

Hydro is a genuinely integrated value chain (no segment exceeds 85% of profit), so four secondary unit types matter and are not built out below: bauxite/alumina per tonne (Hydro Bauxite & Alumina), extruded product per tonne (Hydro Extrusions, whose EBIT margin turned negative in FY2025 per the segment map), recycled casthouse product per tonne (Hydro Metal Markets recyclers), and NOK/MWh of power sold (Hydro Energy).

## 3. Value Creation Read

**Creates value at current pricing, with a plausible (but not fully proven) through-cycle case.** The segment's own return metric — Adjusted return on average capital employed (ARoaCE), "the profit it earns on the capital tied up in the business, after tax" — was **14.4% for Hydro Aluminium Metal in FY2025**, clearing the group's stated hurdle of >10% ARoaCE "over the course of a business cycle" [FY2025 Integrated Annual Report, Adjusted RoaCE per business area chart, p.33; Profitability roadmaps, p.36]. Cash margin per tonne is positive and rising (≈USD 532/mt FY2025, rising to ≈USD 980/mt by Q1 2026 per the quarterly deck), and the segment sits in the cheap 16th percentile of the global cost curve, meaning even at lower prices it should stay profitable relative to higher-cost competitors [FY2025 Integrated Annual Report, p.15]. That said, the segment's *own* differentiated cost-of-capital hurdle (the report states hurdles are "differentiated for each business area" but does not give the Aluminium Metal-specific number) is not disclosed — so the margin above hurdle cannot be pinned exactly, only inferred against the group-wide 10% floor. The single most valuable missing disclosure to firm this up is the segment-specific cost-of-capital / ARoaCE hurdle rate.

## 4. Sensitivity

The realized LME aluminium price is the input most likely to swing the value-creation read if it moves 20%: a 20% move on the FY2025 realized price (USD 2,573/mt) is roughly USD 515/mt — bigger than the entire FY2025 cash margin per tonne (≈USD 532/mt) and close to double the FY2024 margin (≈USD 441/mt), meaning a 20% price fall could push the unit close to break-even at the cash-margin level even before capex and other costs. This is also the historically most volatile input: the segment's reported EBIT margin ranged from 3.6% to 31.0% over FY2020-FY2025 (per `03_segment-map.md`), and the realized LME price itself moved from USD 2,552/mt to USD 2,996/mt within calendar 2025 alone, a swing the annual report attributes partly to "an increased share of speculative trading" and a "bullish copper market" rather than purely fundamental supply/demand [FY2025 Integrated Annual Report, Market development and outlook, p.27]. By contrast, the implied all-in cash cost per tonne moved a smaller ≈11% (USD 2,225-2,500/mt) across the three most recent quarters disclosed — cost is the more stable side of the unit-economics equation, price is the volatile one.

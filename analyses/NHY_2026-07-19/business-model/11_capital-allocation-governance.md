## 2. Classification

**Standard professional management.**

No row scores in the high-severity range for a hard governance flag (auditor, restatements, pledging, ownership are all clean or not applicable), and the acquisition pattern does not meet the serial-acquirer bar — one bolt-on deal in five years plus active divestment of non-core bets is portfolio discipline, not empire-building. At the same time this is not a clean "owner-operator" profile: capex has run consistently above depreciation, the Q1 2026 working-capital jump is unexplained in the pool, and Hydro carries real, only partly quantified, off-balance-sheet exposure (Brazilian tax disputes and Alunorte litigation) that a pure discipline read would not have. The combination is a professionally run, state-anchored industrial company with ordinary-course capital allocation and a handful of items — contingent liabilities and the receivable spike — that warrant monitoring rather than alarm.

## 3. Most Material Signal

The off-balance-sheet contingent liabilities row is the one that would change the classification if it deteriorated. Hydro's own filing puts ~NOK 3.6bn of Brazilian tax exposure at "possible" loss and states it "is not possible... to provide a range of possible outcomes" for the Alunorte litigation, which spans a dismissed-then-appealed Rotterdam case and multiple pending Pará collective and criminal actions [FY2025 Annual Report, p.7801-7840, p.12022-12075]. None of this is provisioned beyond existing balance-sheet amounts, so an adverse ruling — particularly the appealed criminal penalty or a reversal on appeal in the Rotterdam case — could convert a currently unquantified contingency into a cash outflow with no advance warning in the numbers reviewed here. That is the one item that could push this from "standard professional management" toward "governance red flags" if it worsens.

## 2. Dominant Segment

**Hydro Aluminium Metal is the dominant segment by profit** — 39.5% of FY2025 group Adjusted EBITDA (NOK 11,409m of NOK 28,889m) [FY2025 Integrated Annual Report, Note 1.4 / Alternative Performance Measures, p.149-151, p.232-233], and an even larger 48.9% of group reported EBIT (NOK 7,036m of NOK 14,401m) because two other segments (Metal Markets and Extrusions) posted reported EBIT **losses** in FY2025. Aluminium Metal is dominant despite being only the fourth-largest segment by revenue (7.1%) — the classic upstream-margin, downstream-volume pattern of an integrated aluminium producer.

This dominance is not stable across time: in FY2024, Hydro Bauxite & Alumina was actually the largest Adjusted-EBITDA contributor (41.0% vs Aluminium Metal's 36.7%), driven by a spike in alumina prices [FY2025 Integrated Annual Report, Alternative Performance Measures, p.232-233]. The two upstream segments alternate as the top profit driver depending on where the alumina-vs-aluminium price spread sits in the cycle — Q1 2026 data reinforces the current rotation toward Aluminium Metal, whose Adjusted EBITDA (NOK 5,034m) was roughly 6.7x Bauxite & Alumina's (NOK 747m) in that quarter [Q1 2026 report, Note 2, p.26]. Hydro is not a single-segment business (no segment exceeds 85% of revenue or profit) — it is a genuinely integrated value chain where the profit center rotates with commodity-price cycles even though the revenue mix (dominated by Metal Markets and Extrusions, 73.9% combined) barely moves year to year.

## 3. Segment Disclosure Quality

Disclosure quality is good on breadth but has two flags. First, segment reporting covers revenue (external and internal), EBIT, EBITDA, Adjusted EBITDA (via the Alternative Performance Measures note), depreciation & amortization, capital expenditure and total assets for every segment — a materially fuller picture than revenue-only disclosure [FY2025 Integrated Annual Report, Note 1.4, p.149-151]. Second, segment definitions have been reasonably stable since FY2022 (five-way split: Bauxite & Alumina, Energy, Aluminium Metal, Metal Markets, Extrusions, plus Other/Eliminations), but the FY2020, FY2021 and FY2023 columns in the Capital IQ segment export are explicitly marked "Reclassified" [Norsk Hydro ASA OB NHY Financials.xls, Segments tab], and a "Rolling" business area with NOK 17,138m of assets existed in FY2020 and disappeared entirely from FY2021 onward — evidence of a real structural change (the Rolling activities were divested), not just a relabeling. The "Other and Eliminations" bucket is immaterial for revenue (~0.0%) and profit (0.5%) but houses NOK 17,254m of assets in FY2025 — 8.3% of the group's NOK 208,295m total assets — a large unallocated-asset pool investors cannot attribute to an operating segment; this falls short of the >10% "Other" flag threshold but is close enough to note. Most importantly for forward comparability: Hydro has told investors it will **stop presenting EBIT and EBITDA as segment performance measures** once IFRS 18 takes effect (annual periods beginning on or after 1 Jan 2027), replacing them with a new metric, "Adjusted OPDAI" (Operating Profit before Depreciation, Amortization and Impairment, adjusted), plus the equity-accounted share of profit/loss [FY2025 Integrated Annual Report, Note 10.5, p.206]. Downstream agents (`unit-economics`, `competitive-map`) should expect a discontinuity in segment profit metrics from FY2027 reporting onward and should not silently splice Adjusted OPDAI onto the historical EBITDA series without reconciliation.

## What This Module Found

Norsk Hydro (an aluminium and alumina producer) can comfortably survive a normal downturn, but its debt is drifting the wrong way. Net debt to EBITDA — how many years of core earnings it would take to repay debt net of cash, where EBITDA means earnings before interest, tax, and depreciation — has risen every period shown on the conservative "strict" basis: 0.49x (FY2023) to 0.80x (FY2025) to 1.02x on the latest trailing-twelve-month figure (net debt NOK 22,503m, EBITDA NOK 21,976m through 31-Mar-2026). It stays low in absolute terms and inside the company's own under-2.0x target, and both S&P (BBB) and Moody's (Baa2) rate it investment grade and stable.

The single most important support is liquidity — the cash and committed credit already in hand. NOK 40,980m of committed liquidity gives a runway of roughly 100 months (over 8 years) before the company would need outside help, and it covers the entire next-12-month cash needs bucket about 1.7x with no credit taken for future cash generation at all. The near-term debt maturity wall — bills coming due — is self-funded: the NOK 8,250m due within 12 months is covered 1.36x by cash on hand alone, so NHY does not need to refinance to pay it.

The single most important risk is not the numbers on the page but a disclosure gap behind them. No numeric covenant threshold — the leverage or coverage limit a lender sets — is disclosed anywhere for the debt that actually carries covenants (loans held by part-owned subsidiaries), and 75.6% of the debt stack (NOK 27,634m) carries no disclosed rate type, so the true floating-rate exposure cannot be pinned down. A live warning sign is already visible: Q1 2026 posted negative operating cash flow of NOK (1,891)m against positive Adjusted EBITDA of NOK 8,668m — cash running behind reported profit in the most recent quarter.

## What Would Change This Read

**Would strengthen it:** confirmed, disclosed covenant thresholds showing wide actual headroom; a reversal of the rising strict-basis leverage trend; disclosed rate-type detail showing low true floating exposure.

**Would weaken it:** a confirmed tight covenant at the part-owned-subsidiary level; evidence that a large share of the undisclosed 75.6% rate-type debt is actually floating while rates rise; a sustained (not single-quarter) negative operating cash flow trend; a large adverse ruling on the ~NOK 3.6bn "possible" Brazil tax exposure or the active Alunorte litigation.

**Data needed:** the underlying credit-agreement / trust-deed covenant package for the part-owned-subsidiary loans referenced in Note 7.4, and instrument-level rate-type disclosure for the 75.6% of debt currently unclassified.

## Bottom Line

- **Verdict: Solid.** Low absolute leverage, deep liquidity, and stable investment-grade ratings; capped short of Fortress only by the missing covenant disclosure.
- **Could be better than it looks:** NOK 40,980m of committed liquidity covers next-12-month uses ~1.7x with no future cash flow needed, and the structure only truly breaks at a ~69–71% earnings collapse, far beyond a normal 30–60% recession.
- **Could be worse than it looks:** leverage has risen every period on the strict basis (0.49x to 1.02x) as cash went to shareholder payouts against softening earnings (alumina prices down 40% year-on-year), and Q1 2026 already showed negative operating cash flow.
- **Evidence missing:** the actual covenant thresholds on the loans that carry them, and the rate type (fixed vs floating) on 75.6% of the debt stack.
- **Watch next:** whether the negative operating cash flow of Q1 2026 becomes a trend, and any adverse move on the ~NOK 3.6bn Brazil tax and Alunorte litigation exposures.

## Plain-English Glossary

- **EBITDA** — earnings before interest, tax, and depreciation; a rough measure of core operating profit.
- **Net debt to EBITDA** — debt net of cash divided by that operating profit; how many years of earnings it would take to clear the debt.
- **Strict basis (net debt)** — the conservative way to count net debt, netting only cash, not other near-cash items; gives a higher (more cautious) figure than the company's own measures.
- **Maturity wall** — the debt coming due within a set window (here 12 and 24 months).
- **Covenant** — a rule a lender sets (for example a maximum leverage limit); breaking it can force repayment or a waiver.
- **Covenant headroom** — how much room there is before a covenant would be breached; here "Not assessable" because the limits are not disclosed.
- **EBITDA/interest cover** — how many times operating profit covers the interest bill.
- **Investment grade** — a credit-rating band (BBB / Baa2 or better) signalling relatively low default risk.

## 2. What the Company Does

Norsk Hydro digs up bauxite (the raw ore), refines it into alumina, smelts that into aluminium metal, and shapes some of it into finished parts like window frames and car parts (its Extrusions unit). It powers much of this with its own cheap hydropower in the Nordics and Brazil, which makes it one of the lowest-cost aluminium producers in the world — it sits at the 16th percentile of the global cost curve (cheaper than about 84% of the industry) [`business-model/09_moat.md`].

The revenue model in one line: **profit ≈ (aluminium price − Hydro's cost per tonne) × tonnes sold, then converted from USD into NOK.** Two outside numbers Hydro does not control — the LME aluminium price (the world benchmark price set on the London Metal Exchange) and the USD/NOK exchange rate — drive the result. A NOK 1.00 move in USD/NOK swings yearly Adjusted EBITDA (a rough profit measure, explained in the glossary) by about NOK 4,900m, roughly 17% [`business-model/10_external-dependency.md`]. So this is a price-taker: when aluminium is dear, it earns a lot; when aluminium is cheap, it earns little.

## 3. The Variant Perception (what we might see that others don't)

**What everyone already knows:** Hydro is a state-linked, vertically integrated aluminium producer whose earnings ride the LME aluminium price and the USD/NOK rate, and it is currently enjoying a price spike caused by Middle East supply disruption.

**What is probably already in the price:** At NOK 84.96 the market needs Hydro's high FY2025 profit margin (Adjusted EBITDA margin 13.9% — profit as a share of sales) to *hold roughly flat through 2030*, not fade back to the 13.0% mid-cycle level the cash-flow model uses. A reverse-DCF (working out what the price is already assuming) calls that a "stretch, tilting aggressive" ask — a demanding assumption, not a cheap one [`valuation/05_reverse-dcf.md`].

**What the engine thinks the market may be missing:** The sell-side (the brokers who publish price targets) has a mean target of NOK 99.35 across 17 analysts (average call: Hold, 2.94/5), implying about 17% upside. This run's own base case is lower, NOK 81.83 (−3.7%). The gap is not secret information; it is facts already in the same data pool that the targets seem to underweight: (1) two-thirds of Q2 2026 volume is already sold forward at USD 3,000/mt versus a USD 3,467/mt quarter-end spot price — the company's own order book already shows the price fading; (2) the near-term profit consensus was cut to match management's own cost guidance, not out of independent caution; (3) the Extrusions unit's operating margin is already negative (−2.2% in 2025) with no proven turnaround; (4) a sudden Q1 2026 jump in unpaid customer bills is unexplained.

**What would prove the edge is real:** The 22-Jul-2026 result (three days out). If the Adjusted EBITDA margin falls back below ~15% (from the Q1 2026 peak of 17.2%) while unpaid bills normalize and operating cash flow turns positive, this run's cautious base case is vindicated. If instead the margin holds near 17%+ and cash flow is clean, the more bullish sell-side read wins and this caution was overdone.

**Edge score: 30/100.** This is a real but modest divergence built on reading public facts correctly, not proprietary information. It does not clear the 50-point bar for a high-conviction edge, which is why conviction is only 35.

## 4. Why It Could Work — Bull Case

The full dossier keeps this as a genuine steelman, not a token nod. The drivers that most move the thesis up:

1. **The price spike lasts.** If the Middle East supply shock keeps the LME aluminium price elevated and the 13.9%–14.0% margin holds through 2030 rather than fading, the bull case (NOK 107.70, +26.8%) is in play. About 33% of Q2 volume is still unsold at the elevated spot price [`earnings/05_beat-miss-setup.md`]. This is the single biggest upside driver.
2. **An earnings beat.** Revenue has beaten consensus four quarters running, and the near-term profit bar was cut mechanically — so a beat on both revenue and profit at the 22-Jul print is credible [`earnings/05_beat-miss-setup.md`].
3. **The market re-rates it toward peers.** On clean, restated multiples Hydro trades 18%–35% below the peer median (RUSAL, Chalco, Hindalco) despite lower debt and mid-to-best margins. If the market simply started paying it what it pays weaker peers, the peer method alone implies +10.2% even after a 10% quality discount [`valuation/03_relative-valuation-peers.md`].
4. **Balance-sheet firepower.** About NOK 40,980m of committed liquidity and a ~100-month runway give Hydro room to buy back shares hard into any dip or self-fund growth without issuing new shares [`balance-sheet-survival/99`].
5. **A delivered Extrusions turnaround.** If the five proposed European plant closures actually lift the margin back above zero, the lowest-valued segment re-rates [`catalyst/01_catalyst-calendar.md`].

The most credible single bull argument: that the sell-side (NOK 99.35 target) is right and this run's base case (NOK 81.83) is too conservative. The one piece of evidence that would most move it that way is FQ2 2026 showing the margin holding at or above 15% with unpaid bills normalizing.

## 5. Why It Could Fail — Bear Case & the Killer Risk

The drivers that most move the thesis down:

1. **The margin was a one-off spike, and it reverts.** Management itself calls the Middle East price spike "not run-rate" (not repeatable). A USD 400/mt price reversal costs about NOK 6.0bn — roughly 21% of FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA [`earnings/03_margin-drivers.md`]. The forward order book already prices in partial reversion.
2. **Structural, not just cyclical, moat erosion.** Extrusions' operating margin is already negative (−2.2%), and the upstream cost advantage is exposed to growing Chinese and Gulf capacity. The business-model module calls this "not a tail risk." This is what drives the 46.9%-downside bear case (NOK 45.12) [`business-model/09_moat.md`].
3. **A cash-quality problem.** In Q1 2026 unpaid customer bills nearly doubled quarter-on-quarter (days-sales-outstanding 33.0 → 56.7) against only 6.7% revenue growth, and operating cash flow was negative (NOK −1,891m) while reported profit was positive. Unexplained in the data [`earnings/08_earnings-red-flags.md`].
4. **A likely write-down.** The Alumetal recycling unit sits on only 18% headroom over its NOK 2.7bn carrying value; management flags a future impairment (write-down) as "likely" [`business-model/12_red-flags-sweep.md`].
5. **An unquantified Brazil tax / Alunorte legal risk** of roughly NOK 3.6bn "possible" loss, which does not show up in the reported debt figures [`balance-sheet-survival/05`].

**The killer risk (one sentence):** The Norwegian State's 34.3–34.5% stake is held to keep headquarters, technology, and jobs in Norway — not to maximize what shareholders get per share — and the Board has formally declined to draft takeover-bid principles because of it, so any undervaluation has no built-in mechanism to correct itself.

## 6. Avoid-Big-Risks Check (§24)

**Filter 6 — unaligned controlling owner: TRIPPED (RF-OWN-004, High severity).** The Norwegian State owns 34.26%–34.49% and its own Active Ownership Report states its purpose is industrial policy (retaining HQ and technology functions in Norway), not per-share value; the Board declined to adopt takeover-bid-handling principles specifically because of the stake [`management-governance/04`, `05`]. **This is the binding cap: it holds the rating at Watchlist.** It is more restrictive than the separate "commodity thesis with a weak edge" cap, which would otherwise have limited this to Starter Position Only.

The other four tested filters did **not** trip: no integrity/fraud finding (Filter 1), no turnaround-as-pitch problem at the group level (Filter 2), no serial-acquirer pattern (Filter 4), no fast-changing-industry disruption risk (Filter 5).

**Survival read:** not a solvency worry. Net debt (total debt minus cash) is NOK 22,503m as of Q1 2026, and net debt/EBITDA is 1.02x — low, though it has risen every period since FY2023 (0.49x). This is **not a net-cash company** on any basis; it is low-debt and cash-rich, which is a real but weaker thing than net cash. The structure survives a 30–60% EBITDA fall with no covenant breach and only breaks at a ~69–71% fall — far beyond the equity bear case [`balance-sheet-survival/06`]. Credit ratings are investment-grade (S&P BBB, Moody's Baa2).

## 7. Valuation & Fair Value

| Case | Fair value | vs price NOK 84.96 |
|---|---|---|
| Bull | NOK 107.70 | +26.8% |
| **Base** | **NOK 81.83** | **−3.7%** |
| Bear | NOK 45.12 | −46.9% |

The base case is driven by **sum-of-the-parts** (valuing each segment separately and adding them up; 40% weight) — the only method tied entirely to Hydro's own audited segment numbers, and it ties within 0.3% of the enterprise-value bridge (the check that segment values add up to the whole company's value). The intrinsic DCF (discounted cash flow — today's value of future cash; 35% weight) sits lower at NOK 70.14 but is 78.1% terminal-value (most of its worth sits beyond the forecast years), so its confidence is capped at 60. The peer method (25% weight) sits highest at NOK 93.70 but stacks a discretionary currency conversion and a 10% quality discount on a three-name peer set.

**Margin of safety: −3.83%** — the price sits *above*, not below, base fair value, so there is no cushion (margin of safety is the gap between fair value and price that protects you if you are wrong).

**What is priced in:** the current price needs the FY2025 margin (13.9%) to hold flat, not grow (the implied 5-year free-cash-flow growth rate is actually −3.21%). Two earnings-side reads independently call that margin non-durable.

**Methods disagree, and the dossier does not average it away:** on the raw vendor screen Hydro looked ~49% cheaper than peers, but that is a Capital IQ accounting-reclassification artifact — once restated on audited figures those same multiples flip to a *premium*. A real 18%–35% discount survives on clean multiples, but it is treated as a possible value trap (a cheap-looking stock that stays cheap) because the State owner has no mandate to close it.

## 10. What Would Change Our Mind

Kill criteria — specific, observable events that would force a downgrade or (if held) an exit:

- **FQ2 2026 (22-Jul) margin holds near 17%+ AND unpaid bills keep building with operating cash flow staying negative** — the bull persistence case gains evidence while the cash-quality flag is confirmed at once.
- **An Alumetal write-down booked** in the FY2026 annual report (~Feb-2027) — confirms the 18%-headroom flag.
- **An adverse ruling on the ~NOK 3.6bn Brazilian tax dispute or Alunorte litigation** — an unquantified risk becomes a real cash hit.
- **The Norwegian State visibly blocks or resists a value-accretive action** — turns the value-trap risk from inferred to proven.
- **Extrusions margin still negative one year after the plant-closure announcements** — confirms the moat erosion is structural, not a dip.

A firmer *Avoid* would follow confirmation that the elevated margin was fully cyclical and is now reversing sharply (aluminium back toward its 2023–2025 realized band of USD 2,218–2,573/mt) while the Extrusions turnaround is still spoken of as delivered rather than proven.

## 13. Bottom Line

- **What it does:** one of the world's lowest-cost aluminium producers, powered by its own cheap hydropower; earnings ride the aluminium price and the USD/NOK rate.
- **Why it may go up:** the Middle East-driven price spike could last, revenue keeps beating, and the shares trade 18%–35% below clean peer multiples.
- **Why it may go down:** the price already assumes today's unusually high margin holds — management says it will not last; Extrusions is losing money; a recycling unit is near a write-down; and the downside (−46.9%) is far bigger than the upside (+26.8%).
- **What supports the thesis:** solid, well-evidenced across six modules — low debt, huge liquidity, investment-grade rating; this is not a survival worry.
- **What is missing:** loan-covenant thresholds, an insider-trading log, AGM vote tallies, and an explanation for the Q1 2026 jump in unpaid bills.
- **Buy now or wait:** **Wait.** The price sits above our own fair value (−3.83% margin of safety), expected return is negative (−8.4%), and the biggest shareholder is not trying to lift the share price.
- **The one thing to watch next:** the 22-Jul-2026 quarterly results — do they show the margin fading (as expected) or holding?

## 14. Plain-English Glossary

- **LME aluminium price** — the world benchmark price for aluminium, set on the London Metal Exchange; Hydro takes this price, it cannot set it.
- **USD/NOK** — how many Norwegian kroner one US dollar buys; Hydro sells in dollars and reports in kroner, so this rate swings its profit.
- **Adjusted EBITDA** — earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation, with one-offs stripped out; a rough measure of operating profit before financing and accounting charges.
- **Adjusted EBITDA margin** — that profit as a percentage of sales (here 13.9% in FY2025).
- **Cost curve / percentile** — a ranking of all producers from cheapest to dearest; Hydro's 16th percentile means it is cheaper to run than about 84% of the industry.
- **Moat** — a durable cost or product advantage that protects profits from competitors.
- **Net debt** — total debt minus cash; here NOK 22,503m (strict basis: cash only, no investments netted).
- **Net debt/EBITDA** — how many years of profit it would take to repay net debt; 1.02x is low.
- **Net cash** — more cash than debt; Hydro is *not* net cash, just low-debt and cash-rich.
- **Covenant** — a condition in a loan agreement that, if breached, lets lenders demand repayment.
- **Liquidity runway** — how long the company could keep operating on cash and committed credit; ~100 months here.
- **Sum-of-the-parts (SOTP)** — valuing each business segment separately and adding them up.
- **Enterprise value (EV) bridge** — the check that the segment values add up to the whole company's value (its market value plus debt, minus cash).
- **DCF (discounted cash flow)** — today's value of the cash the business is expected to throw off in future, discounted back for time and risk.
- **Terminal value** — the slice of a DCF that comes from all years beyond the explicit forecast; when it is most of the value (here 78.1%), the estimate is less reliable.
- **Reverse-DCF** — running a DCF backwards to work out what growth or margin the current price is already assuming.
- **Margin of safety** — the gap between fair value and price that protects you if you are wrong; here it is negative (−3.83%), meaning no cushion.
- **Peer multiples (EV/EBITDA, P/E, EV/Sales)** — valuation ratios used to compare Hydro against similar companies.
- **Re-rating** — the market deciding to pay more (or less) for the same earnings.
- **Impairment / write-down** — cutting the book value of an asset when it is worth less than recorded; a hit to reported profit and book value.
- **Days-sales-outstanding (DSO)** — the average number of days it takes to collect a customer bill; a jump (33.0 → 56.7) can signal a cash-collection problem.
- **Sell-side / consensus** — the brokerage analysts who publish forecasts and price targets; their average is the consensus.
- **Return on capital (ROCE / RoaCE)** — the profit earned on each unit of money put into the business; compare it to what that money costs to raise.
- **Value trap** — a stock that looks cheap but stays cheap because nothing is set up to close the gap.
- **Risk/reward** — the size of the potential gain against the potential loss; negative here (−0.18) means the weighted target sits below today's price.

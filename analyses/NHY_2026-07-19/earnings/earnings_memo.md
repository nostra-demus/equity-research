## What This Module Found

The top line is going backwards. Trailing-twelve-month revenue (to Mar-2026) is down 3.2% versus FY2025, and Q1 2026 revenue fell 11.7% year on year — driven mostly by a 41% drop in the realized alumina price, a global-oversupply story that is not quickly reversible.

The profit rebound investors see is a commodity spike, not a turnaround. Adjusted EBITDA margin (core operating profit as a share of sales) jumped from an 11.8% trough to a 17.2% peak in one quarter, almost entirely because of a Middle East supply-shock spike in the LME aluminium price (the global benchmark price) — quarter-end spot USD 3,467/mt versus a FY2023–25 realized band of USD 2,218–2,573/mt. Management itself calls this level not run-rate, and the company's own forward bookings already show it partly unwinding (two-thirds of Q2 volume locked at USD 3,000/mt, below the USD 3,467/mt spot).

**Single most important driver:** the LME aluminium price. A USD 400/mt reversal toward its pre-shock range would cost roughly NOK 6.0bn of adjusted EBITDA — about 21% of last year's group total.

**Single most important risk:** a Q1 2026 receivables build (money owed by customers not yet collected). Days sales outstanding — the average number of days to collect a sale — rose from 33.0 to 56.7, and accounts receivable jumped 82.3% quarter on quarter against only 6.7% revenue growth. That leaves the recovery investors are asked to extrapolate without confirmed cash behind it. [08, flag #1]

## What Would Change This Read

**Would upgrade it:** the FQ2 2026 cash-flow statement shows receivables normalizing and operating cash flow turning positive again, AND the LME price holds near or above USD 3,000/mt through Q3 without a demand-side explanation emerging, AND the Extrusions segment's reported operating (EBIT) margin turns positive.

**Would downgrade it:** FQ2 receivables keep building while operating cash flow stays negative (confirming a real cash-conversion problem); OR the LME price reverts toward the pre-shock USD 2,300–2,995/mt range faster than the booking data assumes, dragging the unbooked ~33% of Q2 volume down; OR the recurring restructuring charge grows again in FY2026, widening the reported-vs-adjusted gap.

**Data needed:** the FQ2 2026 quarterly filing and cash-flow statement (due 22-Jul-2026); an explanation of the Q1 receivables spike and the unexplained 55.7% year-on-year jump in Norway revenue; a Q3 2026 segment cost/price bridge once management issues one.

## Bottom Line

- **Verdict: Mixed earnings setup** — a commodity-conditional bounce, not a clean acceleration.
- **Why it could be better than it looks:** revenue has beaten consensus four quarters running, the profit bar was cut into the print, and two-thirds of Q2 volume is already booked at USD 3,000/mt — a real, quantifiable cushion even if the price reverses.
- **Why it could be worse:** the whole margin recovery rests on an aluminium-price spike management says will not last, and over half of the Q1 adjusted-EBITDA recovery (NOK 1,600m of NOK 3,050m) came from an opaque "Other" bucket including a one-off internal-margin release that should not repeat.
- **What's missing:** an explanation for the Q1 receivables spike and for the 55.7% Norway revenue jump — neither is addressed anywhere in the data pool, and both bear on whether the recovery is genuine.
- **One thing to watch next:** the FQ2 2026 results due 22-Jul-2026 (three days out) — specifically whether operating cash flow turns positive and receivables normalize. This risk resolves in either direction very soon.

## Plain-English Glossary

- **LME aluminium price:** the global benchmark market price for aluminium (London Metal Exchange); Hydro is a price-taker on it.
- **Adjusted EBITDA / EBITDA margin:** core operating profit before interest, tax, depreciation and one-offs; the margin is that profit as a share of sales.
- **EBIT margin:** operating profit (after depreciation) as a share of sales.
- **Reported vs adjusted net income:** GAAP profit as filed versus the company's own figure after stripping out items it labels one-off.
- **Days sales outstanding (DSO) / receivables:** the average number of days to collect cash from a sale; receivables is money owed by customers but not yet collected.
- **Operating cash flow (CFO):** the actual cash the business generated from operations, as opposed to accounting profit.
- **Consensus bar:** the average of analysts' published estimates the company is measured against at results.
- **Net debt / net debt to EBITDA:** total borrowings minus cash; divided by a year of core profit, it shows how many years of profit the debt equals.
- **Capex:** capital spending on plant and equipment.

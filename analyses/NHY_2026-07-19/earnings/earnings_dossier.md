## 5. Earnings Setup Summary

### Revenue Setup

The current revenue trajectory is not sustainable as a run-rate in either direction shown over the past year. The FY2025 growth of +2.1% and the sharp Q1 2026 rebound in profit both sit on top of a top line that is trailing-twelve-month negative (−3.2%) and fell 11.7% year on year in the latest quarter — the decline is driven almost entirely by a 41% year-on-year collapse in the realized alumina price, a structural oversupply story (2.7–3.0 million tonne 2026 global surplus) that is not obviously reversible on a short horizon. The single factor that would flip revenue direction is the LME aluminium price holding or extending its current Middle East-driven spike long enough to outrun the alumina drag and the stronger krone — but the company's own forward booking data (two-thirds of Q2 volume locked at USD 3,000/mt, below the USD 3,467/mt quarter-end spot) shows the market itself pricing some reversal already. Reported revenue and underlying organic revenue tell materially different stories: end-market demand growth is running near +1% globally while realized aluminium prices moved +21.6% year on year — the gap between the price-driven headline and the roughly flat-volume underlying business is on the order of 20 percentage points.

### Margin Setup

Current margins are not at a sustainable level in either direction — Q1 2026's Adjusted EBITDA margin of 17.20% is a cyclical peak within the visible window, up from an 11.83% trough just one quarter earlier, and the swing is dominated by a single non-run-rate driver (the LME price spike) rather than a structural improvement. If the aluminium price reverses by even USD 400/mt toward its pre-shock range, Aluminium Metal — the segment contributing 39.5% of FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA — takes the largest single bite, roughly NOK 6.0bn, about 21% of FY2025 group Adjusted EBITDA. Hydro has no contractual margin-protection mechanism against this: it is a price-taker on both alumina (its main input, priced with a one-month index lag) and LME aluminium (its main output, realized with a 1.5-2 month lag), and its only levers are forward hedging — which dampens but does not eliminate the swing — and its position on the low end of the industry cost curve. The improvement program (NOK 6.5bn cumulative cost-out target by 2030, NOK 1.4bn realized to date) is the one durable, self-help offset, worth roughly 50 basis points of margin per year if delivered at the disclosed pace, but it is small relative to the commodity-price swings described above.

### Quality Check

The single largest gap between reported and economic earnings is the net income/EPS line, where reported figures sat 34% (FY2025), 84% (FY2024), and 180% (FY2023) below the company's own adjusted figures every year, driven by non-cash derivative timing, impairments, and a restructuring charge that recurs every year despite being labelled one-off (265 → 407 → 1,795 NOK million, FY2023-2025) — a widening, not narrowing, gap. These add-backs are not genuinely one-time: the rationalization charge has appeared in all three disclosed years and is growing, and the derivative mark-to-market swing is a structural feature of Hydro's hedge accounting that will recur every year the metal-price path moves. To model normalized earnings for next year, start from adjusted EBITDA/EPS rather than GAAP, since GAAP is dominated by non-operating derivative timing noise — but haircut the adjusted figure modestly for the recurring rationalization add-back, which the company's own disclosure pattern suggests understates the ongoing cash cost of running an aging asset base.

### Consensus Bar

For Hydro to beat the current bar by a material margin, the unbooked third of Q2 aluminium volume would need to realize near the quarter-end spot of USD 3,467/mt rather than reverting toward the booked USD 3,000/mt, or capex would need to land at management's guided NOK 13,500m rather than the Street's higher NOK 14,377m model — both plausible, evidenced paths, not speculative ones. The bar is most likely set correctly, not too low, on the profit line specifically: the recent EBITDA/EPS estimate cuts track management's own guided cost bridge (energy, carbon, fixed-cost seasonality) rather than reflecting independent Street caution, so the "low bar" read applies more to revenue (where the cut was not made) than to EBITDA/EPS. A meaningful share of the current consensus recovery is anchored to the Middle East supply shock and its knock-on effects on recycling spreads — both explicitly flagged by management as non-durable and already partially priced out in forward bookings.

## 6. Key Trend Summary

Revenue growth is **decelerating and has turned negative on a trailing basis**: FY2022's +38.9% commodity-price spike gave way to a −6.9% FY2023 correction, only modest +5.2%/+2.1% growth in FY2024/FY2025, and the latest TTM (ended Mar-2026) is down −3.2% versus FY2025, with Q1 2026 revenue down −11.7% year-on-year on a 40% year-on-year drop in the alumina price index (PAX) [first-quarter-report-2026.pdf, p.9]. Margins are **volatile rather than trending in one direction**: reported EBITDA margin bounced between 12.0% (FY2023) and 13.0% (FY2024) before easing to 12.4% (FY2025) on a company-reported basis, while adjusted EBITDA margin on a quarterly view fell from 16.7% (Q1'25) to a trough of 11.8% (Q4'25) before rebounding to 17.2% (Q1'26) — a swing driven substantially by non-cash unrealized-derivative adjustments rather than a steady operating trend (§4). There is **no seasonality read available** from this pool (§5) — the quarter-to-quarter swings visible in §3 track commodity-price and FX moves, not a repeatable calendar pattern. The clearest **inflection point** is Q4 2025: reported EBITDA fell to NOK 1,974 million (a 4.2% margin, the weakest point in the visible window) on a large unrealized LME-derivative timing loss, before both reported and adjusted metrics recovered sharply in Q1 2026 (adjusted EBITDA +55% QoQ, adjusted EPS +196% QoQ) — company commentary attributes the Q1 2026 rebound to higher all-in aluminium prices, higher alumina and metal sales volumes, and stronger Metal Markets/Extrusions results, partly offset by lower alumina prices, a stronger NOK, and reduced power production [first-quarter-report-2026.pdf, p.5]. Net debt on the company's own APM basis has also inflected twice in the visible window — rising from NOK 8.2bn (FY2023) to NOK 16.0bn (FY2024), falling back to NOK 9.7bn (FY2025), then rising again to NOK 12.9bn at Q1 2026 on operating-capital build — leverage (Adjusted net debt/Adjusted EBITDA) stayed within the company's own sub-2x target throughout at 0.2x–0.9x (§1, footnote 5).

## 1. Segment Decomposition Status

Segment decomposition applied — 5 operating segments (Bauxite & Alumina, Energy, Aluminium Metal, Metal Markets, Extrusions) plus an immaterial "Other and eliminations" bucket, from the business-model module [`analyses/NHY_2026-07-19/business-model/03_segment-map.md`]. ## 2. Revenue Driver Tree

| Business Type | Revenue Formula |
|---|---|
| Commodity producer | Production × realized commodity price |

Hydro does not fit one line of the standard template cleanly — it is a multi-segment integrated producer where revenue in each segment is driven differently. Company-specific formula, stated as a sum of segment drivers:

**Group revenue ≈ (Alumina volume × alumina-index price) + (Primary aluminium volume × [LME price + regional premium] × USD/NOK rate) + (Extrusion volume × extrusion selling price, itself LME-linked plus a conversion margin) + (Metal Markets trading/recycling volume × spread over LME), net of intercompany eliminations.**

The common thread across all five segments is that selling prices are directly or contractually tied to the US-dollar LME aluminium price (or, for alumina, an index price with a one-month lag that is itself correlated with the aluminium cycle) [`03_segment-map.md`, row 1: "the majority of alumina Hydro sells externally is contractually linked to either the LME prices or alumina index with one-month delay" — company language paraphrased in the segment map, sourced to FY2025 Integrated Annual Report, Note 1.4], then converted into NOK, the reporting currency, at the prevailing USD/NOK rate. This means the USD/NOK exchange rate is not a peripheral translation adjustment for Hydro — it is baked into how almost every segment's selling price becomes a NOK revenue line.

## 6. Revenue Growth Decomposition

Building an exact NOK-for-NOK revenue bridge from these pieces would require assuming a stable quarter-to-quarter segment revenue mix, which the evidence does not support (Bauxite & Alumina's revenue share swings materially with the alumina price cycle — its FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA share alone moved from 41.0% in FY2024 to 32.3% in FY2025 [`03_segment-map.md`, §2]). ## 7. The Single Biggest Revenue Driver

**The LME aluminium price is the single biggest revenue driver, and its current direction is a geopolitically-driven spike, not a normalized run-rate.** The price runs through nearly every segment's selling price, either directly (Aluminium Metal's realized price is LME-plus-premium) or contractually (alumina is sold on an index linked to the LME with a one-month lag; Metal Markets and Extrusions price their metal-linked products off LME-plus-conversion-margin) [`03_segment-map.md`, row 1; `first-quarter-report-2026.pdf`, p.13]. A 10-20% move in the LME price therefore does not stay contained to the 7.1%-of-revenue Aluminium Metal segment — it flows into the selling prices behind the 36.4%-of-revenue Metal Markets and 37.5%-of-revenue Extrusions lines too, even though those segments add conversion margin on top. The current direction is up sharply: the 3-month LME price moved from USD 2,995/mt to above USD 3,400/mt within Q1 2026 alone (+~16% intra-quarter), a move management attributes directly to the Middle East conflict and the closure of the Strait of Hormuz cutting off roughly 9% of global output and 4-5 million tonnes/year of exports [Q1 2026 transcript, prepared remarks, p.4]. This is explicitly not a demand-driven, repeatable move — it is a supply-shock spike, and the company's own forward booking data shows the market already pricing some reversal: only 67% of Q2 2026 primary production is booked, at USD 3,000/mt, below the USD 3,467/mt level the quarter ended at [Q1 2026 transcript, prepared remarks, p.6]. The USD/NOK exchange rate is the second-largest lever and works as a partial offset in the same period (a 12.3% weaker USD/NOK subtracted from the aluminium-price gain, per §6) — but it is a transmission mechanism for the same underlying price, not an independent driver of comparable size on its own disclosed sensitivity (NOK 4,900m of Adjusted EBITDA, a profit measure, per NOK 1.00 move [FY2025 Annual Report, p.38]).

**Cycle-position note (required for a commodity/policy-exposed business):** the latest reported period is not a normalized run-rate on two of its three biggest price inputs. The LME aluminium price (3-month average USD 3,192/mt in Q1 2026, quarter-end USD 3,467/mt) sits well above the FY2023-FY2025 realized-price band of USD 2,218-2,573/mt [Integrated Annual Report 2025, p.37] and is explicitly tied to a one-time geopolitical supply shock (Middle East conflict, Strait of Hormuz closure) rather than a structural demand shift — call this a price spike/peak, and label it non-run-rate; it can reverse quickly if the conflict resolves or curtailed Middle East and Chinese capacity returns [Q1 2026 transcript, prepared remarks, p.4-5]. The alumina price (Platts PAX USD 307/mt in Q1 2026) sits below the FY2023-FY2025 realized-price band of USD 359-462/mt [Integrated Annual Report 2025, p.37] — a trough, driven by structural oversupply from new Indonesian and Indian refinery capacity plus Middle-East-related demand destruction, not obviously reversible on the same short horizon. Extrusions' end-market demand — construction and industrial — is, in the company's own words, "stabilized at historically low levels" [`first-quarter-report-2026.pdf`, p.16], a cyclical trough consistent with the High industrial-cycle and consumer-cycle dependency the business-model module flags [`10_external-dependency.md`, rows "Industrial cycle" and "Consumer cycle"]. This reconciles with, and reinforces, the business-model module's own "Mostly externally driven" classification (External Dependency Risk Score 74/100, inverted — higher is worse) [`10_external-dependency.md`, §3-4]: none of the three biggest revenue inputs (aluminium price, alumina price, extrusion demand) is currently at a level that can be extrapolated forward without an explicit reversal risk flagged.

**Pass-through lag.** Hydro does not "pass through" costs to customers in a retail sense — it is a price-taker on both its main input (alumina, for the smelting segments) and its main output (LME aluminium) — but the pricing mechanisms carry stated, evidenced lags: alumina bought or sold externally is "priced with reference to alumina spot price indexes" with a one-month delay [Integrated Annual Report 2025, Note 8.2, p.186-187], and realized aluminium prices "lag the LME price developments by approximately 1.5-2 months," including the effect of Hydro's own LME strategic hedging program [first-quarter-report-2026.pdf, p.7, footnote 4]. This means a sudden LME move (such as the Q1 2026 spike from USD 2,995/mt to above USD 3,400/mt) shows up in realized results with a roughly 1.5-2 month delay, so its full effect is still flowing through into Q2 2026 rather than having been fully captured in the Q1 2026 print.

## 4. Margin Walk — Which Margin Level Matters Most?

**Adjusted EBITDA margin, read at the segment level, is the most useful metric for this business.** Reported EBITDA and EBIT are distorted quarter to quarter by large, non-cash, unrealized derivative mark-to-market swings on LME-linked contracts — the FY2023-FY2025 adjustment to EBITDA flipped sign each year (-1,033 in FY2023, -225 in FY2024, +3,193 in FY2025) and Q4 2025 reported EBITDA collapsed to NOK 1,974mn (a 4.2% margin) almost entirely on one such timing loss before recovering in Q1 2026 [`01_historical-financials.md` §3-4]. Gross margin is a CIQ-only construct with no company subtotal behind it. Adjusted EBITDA margin, and its segment-level Adjusted EBITDA components, is both the metric management itself uses to run the business (it is the profit-share basis in the segment map) and the metric least distorted by hedge-accounting timing — it is the right level to track whether the underlying price-cost spread, not derivative noise, is improving or worsening.

## 8. The Single Biggest Margin Driver

**The LME aluminium price — specifically, how much of its current level is a one-time supply-shock premium rather than a durable price.** Hydro Aluminium Metal is the largest single contributor to group Adjusted EBITDA (39.5% of FY2025, and roughly 6.7x Bauxite & Alumina's contribution in Q1 2026), and the company's own disclosed sensitivity is +NOK 150mn of Adjusted EBITDA per +USD 10/mt of aluminium price [`business-model/03_segment-map.md`, §2; `business-model/10_external-dependency.md`, §2]. The Q1 2026 price move — from USD 2,995/mt to above USD 3,400/mt within the quarter — was driven explicitly by a Middle East conflict, regional production curtailments (~40% of Qatalum), and the closure of the Strait of Hormuz, a region that supplies "some 9%" of global aluminium output and exports 4-5 million tonnes a year [Q1 2026 transcript, prepared remarks, p.4]. This is a textbook one-time, non-run-rate supply shock, not demand-led growth. If the shock unwinds — Gulf capacity resumes, the strait reopens, or China curtails more supply to rebalance the market as management itself flags as the open question for 2027 — a reversion of even USD 400/mt (back toward the pre-shock USD 2,995/mt-2,300/mt range) implies roughly NOK 6.0bn of Adjusted EBITDA downside on the disclosed sensitivity, about 21% of FY2025 group Adjusted EBITDA of NOK 28,889mn. The current level of realized aluminium price is therefore the single variable most likely, if it reverses, to compress group margins the most — and it is currently a tailwind resting on a geopolitical shock rather than a structural improvement.

## 6. Seasonality Read

`01_historical-financials.md` §5 states plainly that a formal seasonality table cannot be built for Hydro from this pool — only FY2023-FY2025 annual figures and 5 individual quarters (several approximate) exist, spanning a period of falling alumina and aluminium prices rather than a repeatable calendar pattern. The one seasonality-adjacent data point available is Extrusions volume, which rose 15.7% quarter-on-quarter from Q4 2025 to Q1 2026 and is described in that context as "seasonal" [`02_revenue-drivers.md` §5], and the company's own Q2 2026 bridge for Extrusions explicitly guides "higher sales volumes" versus Q2 2025 [`04_guidance-consensus.md` §2]. This is a mild, single-segment tailwind for the setup — not a group-level seasonality effect — and should not be read as evidence the whole print is seasonally favored.

## 7. Historical Pattern

`04_guidance-consensus.md` §6 shows revenue beating Street consensus in all four of the last four quarters (magnitudes +3.3%, +6.0%, +1.5%, +0.3% — shrinking, then re-widening slightly), which is a real, repeatable signal worth moderate weight for the revenue line specifically. EPS is a different story: two beats bracket two misses (+15.1%, −12.8%, −6.8%, +34.4%), with no clean sandbagging or overshooting pattern — the swings are large in both directions and, per `01_historical-financials.md` §4 and `06_earnings-quality.md` §4, are heavily influenced by non-cash, sign-flipping derivative timing adjustments rather than a consistent operating trend. The synthesizer should weight the revenue beat streak as a moderate positive signal, but should not extend that confidence to EPS — the historical pattern there is closer to a coin flip with wide variance, not a directional edge.

## 8. Setup Verdict

**Setup favors beat.**

The single most important factor is that FQ2 2026 EBITDA/EPS consensus was cut sharply (FQ2 EBITDA −9.5%, zero analysts raised FQ2 EPS) in the last 30 days specifically to match management's own guided Q2 cost bridge (energy, carbon, B&A fixed-cost seasonality) — a mechanical de-risking of the bar just before the print — while FQ2 revenue estimates stayed net-positive over the same window and the company's own booking data shows a third of Q2 aluminium production still unbooked at a spot price (USD 3,467/mt at Q1 quarter-end) well above the USD 3,000/mt already locked in for the booked two-thirds [`04_guidance-consensus.md` §4-5, §7; Q1 2026 transcript, prepared remarks, p.6]. The single biggest risk that could flip this verdict is the Metal Markets trading-gain reversal (§3) landing harder than management's own "normalizing" guidance implies, or the guided cost ranges (energy/carbon/fixed cost) landing above their top ends — either would erase the low-bar advantage on the metric (EBITDA/EPS) where the bar has moved lowest, even if revenue beats again.

## 9. Second-Quarter Look-Ahead

FQ3 2026 has materially less visibility: management's own quantified bridge covers only one quarter forward (Q2 2026 vs Q1 2026), so no comparable cost/price bridge exists for Q3 in this pool [`04_guidance-consensus.md` §2]. Two structural factors point toward a tougher setup by Q3 than Q2: the alumina oversupply is described as a multi-quarter, not obviously reversible trough (§5), and Extrusions demand recovery is explicitly pushed to "H2" with only ~1% full-year 2026 growth guided for both Europe and North America [`04_guidance-consensus.md` §2]. The LME price reversal risk (§3) also becomes more relevant by Q3, since a smaller share of Q3 production would likely be pre-booked at the current elevated hedge rate than Q2's already-locked two-thirds. Not proven from available data whether these effects net to a harder or easier Q3 bar — only that the visibility is lower and the known headwinds (alumina, extrusion demand) skew toward persistence rather than resolution within one quarter.

## 10. Pre-Mortem

If this "favors beat" setup fails, the most likely reason is that the Street's late estimate cuts did not fully capture the guided cost ranges — energy, carbon, and B&A seasonal fixed costs landed above their top-end guided figures, or the flagged Metal Markets trading-gain reversal was steeper than "normalizing" implied — eroding the low-bar advantage on EBITDA/EPS even as revenue kept its 4-quarter beat streak alive. A second plausible failure mode is that the Middle East supply shock unwound faster than the company's own booking data assumed, pulling the unbooked ~33% of Q2 aluminium volume down toward pre-shock spot levels before it could be sold at the elevated price this setup is counting on.

## 9. Earnings Quality Score

**Score: 55/100 — Mixed / average (41–60 band): material concerns from recurring "one-off" charges.**

The single most important reason for this score is that Hydro books "significant rationalization and closure costs" as an EBITDA/EBIT add-back **every single year**, with the amount growing (265 → 407 → 1,795 NOK m over FY2023–FY2025), alongside an impairment charge in every year [Integrated Annual Report 2025, p.36–37] — the module rule treats a charge that recurs every period as not genuinely one-off, and the band description (41–60: "material concerns…recurring one-offs") applies directly. This is compounded by a large, persistent gap between reported and adjusted net income/EPS (+34% to +180% across the three years, §7) driven by non-cash derivative and FX swings, which makes reported bottom-line EPS an unreliable stand-alone read of underlying earnings power in any single year. Offsetting factors that keep the score in the middle band rather than lower: cash conversion is decent on a multi-year average (81.3% 3-year avg CFO/EBITDA, 79.4% TTM — both above the 70% "healthy" threshold), working-capital days show no sustained build (§3), receivable factoring is disclosed with a hard cap and was not fully utilized, share-based compensation is immaterial, and every adjustment is transparently reconciled in the audited annual report rather than hidden or vaguely described.

## 10. The Single Biggest Quality Concern

The single biggest risk that reported earnings misstate the economic picture is not fraud or hidden cash weakness — it is that Hydro's reported net income and EPS are dominated, in every one of the last three years, by large non-cash items that sit below the EBITDA line: unrealized derivative mark-to-market timing on LME-linked hedges, impairments, and "significant" rationalization costs that recur annually despite being labelled one-off. Together these pushed reported net income *below* the company's own adjusted net income by 34% (FY2025), 84% (FY2024), and 180% (FY2023) [Integrated Annual Report 2025, p.36]. A reader who takes the headline reported EPS (NOK 1.77 in FY2023, for example) at face value without cross-checking the adjusted figure (NOK 4.26) or the cash-flow statement would badly misjudge the year's underlying earnings power. The company discloses this fully and audits it, so it is a volatility-and-interpretation risk rather than a concealment risk — but it means reported net income/EPS should never be used on a standalone basis for this company, and the recurring rationalization add-back (§8) means even the *adjusted* EBITDA figure likely overstates the ongoing cash cost of running Hydro's aging, high-capex asset base by a modest but persistent amount each year.

## 4. The Single Highest-Sensitivity Variable

The LME aluminium price is the single largest lever on earnings, with an average bull/bear swing of roughly NOK 5.25bn of Adjusted EBITDA — about 18% of FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA (NOK 28,889m) — on the company's own disclosed sensitivity of +NOK 150m per +USD 10/mt [FY2025 Annual Report, p.38]. Its current direction is sharply up: the 3-month average price moved from USD 2,995/mt to above USD 3,400/mt within Q1 2026 alone. This is an external variable Hydro does not control — management's only levers are forward hedging (which dampens but does not eliminate the swing; only 67% of Q2 2026 primary production is booked, at USD 3,000/mt, below the quarter-end spot) and its lower-cost position on the industry cost curve [`02_revenue-drivers.md` §4, §7]. For the bear case to materialize, the underlying cause — the Middle East conflict and the closure of the Strait of Hormuz, which curtailed roughly 9% of global aluminium output and 4–5 million tonnes/year of exports — would need to resolve, allowing curtailed Gulf capacity (including Hydro's own partly-owned Qatalum, currently ~40% curtailed) and any deferred Chinese supply to return to the market [Q1 2026 transcript, prepared remarks, p.4].

## 5. Interaction Effects

Three of the six variables move together rather than independently, and summing their bull or bear cases at face value would overstate the combined swing. First, the LME aluminium price and the USD/NOK rate are already offsetting each other in the current period: the +15% YoY gain in the USD-denominated realized aluminium price translated into only a +1% gain in NOK-denominated realized price, because the krone strengthened by a similar magnitude over the same period [`02_revenue-drivers.md`, §5, Aluminium Metal segment table]. A further aluminium-price spike accompanied by continued krone strength (both plausible if global risk-off flows both firm commodity prices and support NOK, a small oil-linked currency) would show up as a smaller NOK-earnings gain than the two sensitivities, summed independently, would suggest. Second, the LME aluminium price and the recycling-spread/premium-normalization variable share the same root cause — the Middle East supply shock — so their bull cases (price stays high, spread stays wide) and bear cases (price reverts, spread normalizes) are correlated, not independent; treating them as six separate probability-weighted events double-counts the same underlying catalyst. Third, the alumina-price bear case is partially linked to the same shock: the company's own ~3 million tonne 2026 oversupply estimate for alumina explicitly includes Middle-East-related demand loss [`02_revenue-drivers.md`, §5, Bauxite & Alumina table], so a resolution of the Middle East disruption that reverses the aluminium-price tailwind (variable #1's bear case) could simultaneously ease a small part of the alumina oversupply (a mild offset to variable #3's own bear case) — a weak, partial hedge rather than a clean one.

## 6. Non-Linear Or Asymmetric Risks

The recycling-spread/premium-normalization variable carries an explicit downside skew: the bull case (+NOK 108m, itself an unsupported inference) is roughly one-fifth the size of the bear case (−NOK 555m, a clean reversion to an already-observed prior-year level), and management's own guidance points toward the bear direction — trading activity is expected to "normalize" (i.e., fade) into Q2 2026. Extrusions also shows operating deleverage: the segment's reported EBIT margin fell from roughly +4% (2020–2023) to −2.2% (FY2025) even though the underlying volume decline was in the low single digits, and the company's own quarterly bridge shows a NOK 550m fixed-cost swing tied to comparatively modest scale changes [`03_margin-drivers.md`, §5–6] — a small further volume decline could again produce an outsized margin move in a segment already reporting negative margins. A third, disclosed asymmetry sits in Hydro's currency sensitivity table but outside the six variables tracked above: a EUR/NOK move of +NOK 1.00 produces only a −NOK 210m sustainable operating effect but a −NOK 3,310m one-off revaluation effect on financial items — roughly 16x larger — meaning EUR-denominated balance-sheet and derivative exposures carry a disproportionately large mark-to-market risk relative to their ongoing operating exposure [FY2025 Annual Report, p.38]. Finally, both alumina (a one-month index lag) and aluminium (a 1.5–2 month realization lag) carry pricing lags [`03_margin-drivers.md`, §3], so a sharp reversal in either spot price will not fully show up in reported results for one to two quarters — a reported quarter can look stronger than the underlying spot trend already implies, a timing-based non-linearity that risks misreading the trajectory for a quarter or two. Leverage-driven covenant risk is not currently a live asymmetric risk: Adjusted net debt/Adjusted EBITDA was 0.7x at FY2025, well inside the company's own sub-2x target [`01_historical-financials.md`, §1, footnote 5], so none of the above swings — even the bear cases summed naively — would approach a covenant-relevant leverage level on their own.

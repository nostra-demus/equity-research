# Earnings Module — NHY (Synthesis)

## Abstract

Norsk Hydro's underlying earnings trend is decelerating on the top line — trailing-twelve-month revenue is down 3.2% versus FY2025 and Q1 2026 revenue fell 11.7% year on year — even though reported and adjusted profit rebounded sharply in the latest quarter. That rebound is driven almost entirely by a Middle East supply-shock spike in the LME aluminium price, which management itself calls non-repeatable and which forward bookings already show partially unwinding. Consensus for the 22-July print was cut mechanically to match management's own guided cost bridge rather than out of independent caution, weakening the "low bar" read for profit specifically even as revenue stays beatable. The biggest risk is a Q1 2026 receivables build (days sales outstanding rose from 33.0 to 56.7) that leaves the recovery's cash backing unconfirmed. The verdict is a mixed, commodity-conditional setup, not a clean acceleration.

- **Disconfirming evidence already visible:** `08_earnings-red-flags.md` documents that over half (NOK 1,600m of NOK 3,050m) of the Q1 2026 QoQ Adjusted EBITDA recovery came from an opaque "Other" bucket including a one-off internal-margin-elimination release that should not repeat, and that the largest revenue segment (Extrusions, 37.5% of FY2025 revenue) still posted a −2.2% reported EBIT margin, masked at the group level by the aluminium-price spike [`08_earnings-red-flags.md`, flags MG1, MG3].

## 3. Reconciliation

- **06_earnings-quality vs business-model 11_capital-allocation-governance on the Q1 2026 cash-flow event.** `06_earnings-quality.md` characterizes Q1 2026's negative operating cash flow (NOK (1,891)m against positive Adjusted EBITDA of NOK 8,668m) as "an ordinary working-capital cycle... not accrual manipulation." The business-model module's `11_capital-allocation-governance.md`, cross-referenced via `08_earnings-red-flags.md`, quantifies the same event more sharply: days sales outstanding nearly doubled (33.0 → 56.7 days) and accounts receivable rose 82.3% quarter on quarter against only 6.7% revenue growth, scoring it 40/100 severity and calling it "unexplained." This is not a factual disagreement — both describe the same cash-flow print — but a materiality gap. **Reconciled view (conservative default, CLAUDE.md §4):** weight the more granular, quantified business-model read. The receivables build is treated in this synthesis as an unresolved cash-quality flag, not dismissed as routine, and the earnings-quality score of 55/100 is treated as a ceiling pending the FQ2 2026 cash-flow statement (due 22-Jul-2026), per `08_earnings-red-flags.md` §5-6.
- **05_beat-miss-setup's "favors beat" verdict vs 08_earnings-red-flags' caution (flag GC1).** `05` calls the setup "favors beat" based substantially on the FQ2 2026 EBITDA/EPS bar having been cut in the 30 days before the print. `08` flags that this cut tracks management's own guided Q2 cost bridge rather than independent Street conservatism — meaning if actual costs land at the guided midpoint rather than the low end, the post-cut consensus is accurate, not conservative, and the "low bar" advantage evaporates for EBITDA/EPS specifically. **Reconciled view:** "favors beat" is retained for the revenue line (a real, repeatable 4-for-4 beat streak) but downgraded to a coin-flip characterization for EBITDA/EPS, consistent with the historical pattern in that line (+15.1%, −12.8%, −6.8%, +34.4% surprise over the last four quarters).
- **00_earnings-data-triage vs 04_guidance-consensus on the consensus data-as-of date.** `00` dated the consensus data "Apr-29-2026." `04` corrected this to 2026-07-15, showing the Apr-29 timestamp belonged to the transcript's own auto-generated cover page, not the live, continuously-updated Consensus/Trends/Revisions tabs. **Reconciled view:** 04's correction is used throughout this synthesis; the consensus data is current (three days before the 22-Jul-2026 print), not stale.
- **CIQ vendor EBITDA/EBIT reclassification vs the company's own audited EBITDA/EBIT.** `01_historical-financials.md` found the CIQ workbook's EBITDA/EBIT fields do not reconcile to the audited figures (a ~NOK 27bn gap on FY2025 EBITDA) and used the company's own audited figures throughout instead. This synthesis follows that same resolution — every EBITDA/EBIT figure cited here is the company's own audited/APM figure, never the CIQ reclassification.

## 5. Earnings Setup Summary

### Revenue Setup

The current revenue trajectory is not sustainable as a run-rate in either direction shown over the past year. The FY2025 growth of +2.1% and the sharp Q1 2026 rebound in profit both sit on top of a top line that is trailing-twelve-month negative (−3.2%) and fell 11.7% year on year in the latest quarter — the decline is driven almost entirely by a 41% year-on-year collapse in the realized alumina price, a structural oversupply story (2.7–3.0 million tonne 2026 global surplus) that is not obviously reversible on a short horizon. The single factor that would flip revenue direction is the LME aluminium price holding or extending its current Middle East-driven spike long enough to outrun the alumina drag and the stronger krone — but the company's own forward booking data (two-thirds of Q2 volume locked at USD 3,000/mt, below the USD 3,467/mt quarter-end spot) shows the market itself pricing some reversal already. Reported revenue and underlying organic revenue tell materially different stories: end-market demand growth is running near +1% globally while realized aluminium prices moved +21.6% year on year — the gap between the price-driven headline and the roughly flat-volume underlying business is on the order of 20 percentage points.

### Margin Setup

Current margins are not at a sustainable level in either direction — Q1 2026's Adjusted EBITDA margin of 17.20% is a cyclical peak within the visible window, up from an 11.83% trough just one quarter earlier, and the swing is dominated by a single non-run-rate driver (the LME price spike) rather than a structural improvement. If the aluminium price reverses by even USD 400/mt toward its pre-shock range, Aluminium Metal — the segment contributing 39.5% of FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA — takes the largest single bite, roughly NOK 6.0bn, about 21% of FY2025 group Adjusted EBITDA. Hydro has no contractual margin-protection mechanism against this: it is a price-taker on both alumina (its main input, priced with a one-month index lag) and LME aluminium (its main output, realized with a 1.5-2 month lag), and its only levers are forward hedging — which dampens but does not eliminate the swing — and its position on the low end of the industry cost curve. The improvement program (NOK 6.5bn cumulative cost-out target by 2030, NOK 1.4bn realized to date) is the one durable, self-help offset, worth roughly 50 basis points of margin per year if delivered at the disclosed pace, but it is small relative to the commodity-price swings described above.

### Quality Check

The single largest gap between reported and economic earnings is the net income/EPS line, where reported figures sat 34% (FY2025), 84% (FY2024), and 180% (FY2023) below the company's own adjusted figures every year, driven by non-cash derivative timing, impairments, and a restructuring charge that recurs every year despite being labelled one-off (265 → 407 → 1,795 NOK million, FY2023-2025) — a widening, not narrowing, gap. These add-backs are not genuinely one-time: the rationalization charge has appeared in all three disclosed years and is growing, and the derivative mark-to-market swing is a structural feature of Hydro's hedge accounting that will recur every year the metal-price path moves. To model normalized earnings for next year, start from adjusted EBITDA/EPS rather than GAAP, since GAAP is dominated by non-operating derivative timing noise — but haircut the adjusted figure modestly for the recurring rationalization add-back, which the company's own disclosure pattern suggests understates the ongoing cash cost of running an aging asset base.

### Consensus Bar

For Hydro to beat the current bar by a material margin, the unbooked third of Q2 aluminium volume would need to realize near the quarter-end spot of USD 3,467/mt rather than reverting toward the booked USD 3,000/mt, or capex would need to land at management's guided NOK 13,500m rather than the Street's higher NOK 14,377m model — both plausible, evidenced paths, not speculative ones. The bar is most likely set correctly, not too low, on the profit line specifically: the recent EBITDA/EPS estimate cuts track management's own guided cost bridge (energy, carbon, fixed-cost seasonality) rather than reflecting independent Street caution, so the "low bar" read applies more to revenue (where the cut was not made) than to EBITDA/EPS. A meaningful share of the current consensus recovery is anchored to the Middle East supply shock and its knock-on effects on recycling spreads — both explicitly flagged by management as non-durable and already partially priced out in forward bookings.

## 5b. Leverage & Capital Structure

Leverage is within normal range and did not change materially during the period — no dedicated treatment required. Adjusted net debt/Adjusted EBITDA was 0.7x at FY2025 (up from 0.2x at FY2022, down from 0.9x at FY2024), comfortably inside the company's own sub-2x target throughout the visible window, and neither the absolute-leverage trigger (>3.0x) nor the leverage-change trigger (>1.0x YoY ratio increase, >50% total-debt increase, or >2x absolute net-debt change) is met on the data available [`01_historical-financials.md` §1, footnote 5]. Net debt did rise from NOK 9,669m (FY2025) to NOK 12,860m (Q1 2026, +33.0%), a point-in-time balance-sheet move tied to the same operating-capital build discussed in §1A and §8, but this falls short of the >2x absolute-change trigger and leverage remains a secondary consideration for this module, not a standing constraint on capital allocation.

## 8. Note To The Final Synthesizer

- Dominant earnings trend: revenue is decelerating and trailing-twelve-month negative (−3.2%), while the latest quarter's profit rebound is a cyclical, non-run-rate spike tied to a Middle East supply shock in the LME aluminium price — the two trends point in different directions and neither should be extrapolated on its own.
- Earnings are not cleanly cash-backed in the most recent quarter: a large, unexplained receivables build (DSO 33.0 → 56.7 days) sits directly under the base quarter the "favors beat" thesis depends on, and this synthesis treats the 55/100 earnings-quality score as a ceiling pending the FQ2 2026 cash-flow statement, not a settled read.
- The consensus bar is not uniformly low: it is genuinely beatable on revenue (a real 4-for-4 streak) and on capex (Street models more spend than guided), but the EBITDA/EPS bar was mechanically lowered to match management's own guided cost bridge rather than out of independent Street caution — do not treat "favors beat" as applying equally across the P&L.
- Next-quarter setup favors beat on revenue and on the capex line; is closer to a coin flip on EBITDA/EPS given the historical surprise pattern (+15.1%, −12.8%, −6.8%, +34.4%) and the bar-lowering caveat above. Visibility drops materially beyond FQ2 2026 — no comparable cost/price bridge exists for Q3, and the two known structural headwinds (alumina oversupply, Extrusions demand) skew toward persistence rather than resolution within one quarter.
- Top sensitivity variable: the LME aluminium price, currently a tailwind but explicitly a one-time geopolitical spike that could reverse if the Middle East conflict resolves or curtailed Gulf/Chinese supply returns — a USD 400/mt reversal implies roughly NOK 6.0bn of Adjusted EBITDA downside, about 21% of FY2025 group Adjusted EBITDA.
- No hard partial-data caps applied in this module (consensus, cash flow, transcripts, and revision history are all present); the only soft limitation is an incomplete 8-quarter P&L series (several Q2/Q3 2025 figures are chart-derived or vendor-only, not filing-sourced), which limits trend/seasonality precision but did not trigger a scoring cap.
- Biggest missing data point: an explanation for the Q1 2026 receivables spike and for the unexplained 55.7% YoY jump in Norway revenue — neither is addressed anywhere in this data pool, and both bear directly on whether the current earnings recovery is genuine.
- Red-flag severity verdict (`08_earnings-red-flags.md`, reported verbatim): **Material concerns** — 15 flags triggered (0 Critical, 5 High, 6 Medium, 4 Low). The five High-severity flags: (1) the Q1 2026 receivables spike; (2) over half of the Q1 2026 QoQ Adjusted EBITDA recovery explained by an opaque "Other" bucket including a one-off internal-margin release; (3) the recurring "one-off" restructuring charge plus large non-cash FV/MTM swings driving a 34%-180% reported-vs-adjusted net income gap; (4) the FQ2 2026 EBITDA/EPS bar cut tracking guided costs, not independent conservatism; (5) the "favors beat" framing risk of being read as structural acceleration despite TTM revenue being negative and the recovery resting on a one-time geopolitical price shock. All five are surfaced above in §1A, §3, or §8; none are omitted.
- What would change the verdict: see §7 — the decisive near-term evidence is the FQ2 2026 cash-flow statement (due 22-Jul-2026, three days after this report's as-of date).

## 9. Simple Summary

- Revenue is shrinking on a trailing basis (−3.2% TTM, −11.7% in the latest quarter) even though the headline commodity price is spiking — the driver is price and currency, not more demand.
- Margins swung from an 11.8% trough to a 17.2% peak in one quarter, almost entirely because of a Middle East-driven aluminium price spike management itself says will not last.
- Earnings are not fully clean: a "one-off" restructuring charge shows up every single year and keeps growing, and reported profit has run 34%-180% below the company's own adjusted profit figure in each of the last three years.
- The market's bar is easy to beat on revenue and on spending (capex), but was cut to match management's own guided cost bridge on profit — so "easy to beat" does not apply evenly across the numbers.
- Next quarter (results due 22-Jul-2026) probably beats on revenue; profit is closer to a coin flip given the mixed history and the bar-lowering caveat.
- The single biggest swing factor is the aluminium price — a further USD 400/mt reversal would erase about a fifth of last year's adjusted profit.
- Earnings volatility is high (70/100, worse than most) because the two biggest levers — the aluminium price and the Norwegian krone — are both outside management's control.
- This module is useful for the final synthesizer: it identifies a real near-term catalyst (FQ2 2026 results, three days out) and a specific, checkable risk (the receivables build) that will resolve in either direction very soon.

## 4. Read

MGM is concentrated on the geography side but not the customer side. No single named customer accounts for a material disclosed share of revenue; the business collects from millions of individual transacting patrons. On geography, the United States accounts for 70.8% of revenue, with the Las Vegas Strip alone representing roughly 48% of consolidated revenues — a level the company itself flags as a risk compared to more dispersed gaming operators. China/Macau is the second geography at 25.5% and is growing fastest (up from 20% three years ago). The concentration is not contractually secured in any meaningful sense: all patron relationships are transactional, high-end gaming is explicitly called out as driving income variability, and the Macau gaming concession — the legal right underlying China revenue — runs only to December 2032 and can be terminated or not renewed by the Macau government. The single biggest dependency the synthesizer should know about is Las Vegas Strip, which at ~48% of group revenue sits in one metropolitan market where air access disruption, Strip-level competition from new entrants, or a US consumer spending contraction would hit the majority of MGM's earnings base simultaneously.

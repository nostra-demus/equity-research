## 3. Classification

**Partly externally driven** — material exposure but real management levers (pricing, product mix, omnichannel marketing, hedging, active regulatory engagement).

MGM is not a commodity pass-through: management actively shapes outcomes through property positioning, premium product investment, event programming, convention mix, digital expansion, and targeted hedging. However, the business cannot escape three structural dependencies: (1) consumer willingness to spend on discretionary leisure and gaming, which is cyclical; (2) government-granted licenses and tax rates in every market it operates; and (3) mainland Chinese visitor flows and policy into Macau, which represent roughly 25% of total revenue and are substantially outside management's control. The Las Vegas Strip Resorts and Regional Operations (combined ~$12.2B revenue, ~70% of total) do have meaningful management levers — pricing, programming, convention mix, brand investment — but all require an underlying willing consumer base. MGM China is more exposed: visitor arrivals, currency control policy, and the concession terms all sit with Beijing and the Macau government.

## 4. External Dependency Risk Score

**52 / 100** (higher = worse; material external exposure, mixed mitigation)

Rationale:
- Consumer cycle dependency is genuine and demonstrated — Las Vegas Strip Resorts fell 4% in 2025 on lower visitor volume even without a recession.
- Government / regulatory dependency is structural and multi-layered (concession, taxes, sports betting policy, Japan IR license).
- Geopolitical exposure via Macau (~25% of revenue) is real but partially mitigated by MGM's market share strength and premium-mass positioning.
- FX and interest rate exposure is measurable and partially hedged; management has active tools here.
- Management does have real levers: product mix, convention business, omnichannel marketing, operational cost discipline (FTEs down in 2025 despite revenue), share buybacks. These collectively prevent the score from sitting in the 61–80 "mostly externally driven" band.

---

## 5. The Single Biggest Lever

**Consumer spending cycle**: a 20% adverse move in US and Asian consumer discretionary spending — triggered by a recession or severe macro shock — would simultaneously compress Las Vegas Strip RevPAR and casino volumes (~$8.4B revenue), pressure Regional Operations (~$3.8B), and could coincide with reduced mainland Chinese visitor flows to Macau (~$4.5B). The combined effect would hit all three of MGM's major cash-generating segments at once, and the $1.8B in fixed annual triple-net rent payments would remain non-negotiable regardless of revenue performance.

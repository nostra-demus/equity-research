# Competitive Map — MGM

## 1. Dominant Segment

Las Vegas Strip Resorts: 48.1% of FY2025 consolidated revenue ($8.44B of $17.54B) and 54.7% of profit-positive segment EBITDAR at a 33.9% segment EBITDAR margin — the highest margin among any reportable segment. [FY2025 10-K, Item 7 MD&A, pp.37–39]

---

## 2. Named Competitors

### Competitor A — Caesars Entertainment, Inc. (NasdaqGS: CZR)

- **Ticker / listing:** NasdaqGS: CZR
- **Where they compete:** Las Vegas Strip (Caesars Palace, Harrah's, Horseshoe, Paris, Bally's, Planet Hollywood, Nobu), regional US casinos across 18 states, and online sports betting / iGaming in 42+ North American jurisdictions. Direct overlap with MGM on the Las Vegas Strip and in multiple regional markets (including New Jersey and Maryland).
- **Scale:** LTM total revenue $11.56B (filing date April 28, 2026). [Capital IQ Comparable Analysis, Financial Data sheet, data as of 2026-07-03]
- **Profitability / return on capital:** LTM EBITDA margin 30.3%; LTM EBIT margin 18.1%; LTM net income margin -4.2% (loss-making at the net level due to heavy interest burden). Return on capital not separately disclosed; LTM Total Debt/EBITDA 7.0x (highly leveraged). [Capital IQ Comparable Analysis, Operating Statistics sheet, data as of 2026-07-03]
- **Source named in:** Named in the Capital IQ Default Comps peer set for MGM Resorts International [Capital IQ Comparable Analysis, Business Description sheet, data as of 2026-07-03]. The MGM 10-K does not name Caesars by name in its competition section but describes the competitive set as "major hotel casinos on or near the Las Vegas Strip" including "major competitors" that have recently expanded room capacity and convention space. [FY2025 10-K, Item 1, Customers and Competition, p.3]
- **One-line read:** Caesars is MGM's most direct Las Vegas Strip rival by property count and convention footprint, operating a comparable portfolio of large integrated resorts but carrying significantly more debt (~$25B net debt vs. ~$29B for MGM, which includes MGM's lease obligations) and currently loss-making at the net income line; its Las Vegas Strip EBITDA margins are broadly similar to MGM's before rent, though Caesars also sold its Strip real estate to VICI.

### Competitor B — Las Vegas Sands Corp. (NYSE: LVS)

- **Ticker / listing:** NYSE: LVS
- **Where they compete:** Macau integrated resorts (The Venetian Macao, The Londoner Macao, The Parisian Macao, The Plaza Macao / Four Seasons Hotel Macao, and The Sands Macao) and Singapore (Marina Bay Sands). LVS no longer operates Las Vegas Strip properties (sold The Venetian / Palazzo in 2021) but competes directly with MGM China in Macau, where it is the largest single operator by gaming revenue. LVS holds one of the six Macau gaming concessions.
- **Scale:** LTM total revenue $13.74B (filing date April 24, 2026). [Capital IQ Comparable Analysis, Financial Data sheet, data as of 2026-07-03]
- **Profitability / return on capital:** LTM EBITDA margin 35.2% (the highest among the comp set and above MGM's consolidated 12.8% — though the gap narrows substantially when MGM's ~$2.3B rent charge is added back to EBITDA as an apples-to-apples EBITDAR comparison); LTM EBIT margin 24.5%; LTM net income margin 13.4%; LTM Total Debt/EBITDA 3.2x (materially less leveraged than MGM or Caesars). [Capital IQ Comparable Analysis, Operating Statistics sheet, data as of 2026-07-03]. Return on capital (as reported by Capital IQ): not separately broken out in this data set; at 35.2% EBITDA margins and 3.2x leverage the underlying returns on invested capital are substantially above MGM's consolidated level.
- **Source named in:** Named in Capital IQ Default Comps peer set [Capital IQ Comparable Analysis, Business Description sheet, data as of 2026-07-03]. The MGM 10-K identifies LVS implicitly through the statement that "our key competitors in Macau include five other gaming concessionaires" — LVS (Sands China subsidiary) is one of those six. [FY2025 10-K, Item 1, MGM China Competition section, p.3]
- **One-line read:** LVS is MGM's primary competitor in Macau (where scale and mix of premium-mass vs. VIP are the key battlegrounds) and earns materially higher consolidated margins and carries far less debt — the clearest evidence that owning casino real estate (LVS owns Marina Bay Sands and its Macau properties) produces higher asset-level returns but also higher capital intensity than MGM's sale-leaseback model.

### Competitor C — Wynn Resorts, Limited (NasdaqGS: WYNN)

- **Ticker / listing:** NasdaqGS: WYNN
- **Where they compete:** Las Vegas Strip (Wynn Las Vegas, Encore at Wynn), Macau (Wynn Palace on Cotai, Wynn Macau on the Peninsula — two of the six Macau concessions), and Encore Boston Harbor (regional). Wynn's Las Vegas properties sit at the ultra-luxury end of the Strip, directly competing with MGM's Bellagio and Aria for the same high-end gaming and hospitality customer.
- **Scale:** LTM total revenue $7.29B (filing date May 7, 2026). [Capital IQ Comparable Analysis, Financial Data sheet, data as of 2026-07-03]
- **Profitability / return on capital:** LTM EBITDA margin 24.1%; LTM EBIT margin 15.7%; LTM net income margin 5.1%. LTM Total Debt/EBITDA 6.7x. Revenue grew 4.7% LTM. [Capital IQ Comparable Analysis, Operating Statistics sheet, data as of 2026-07-03]. Return on capital not separately broken out in this data set; at 24.1% EBITDA margins and 6.7x leverage, returns are below the LVS level but comparable to MGM at a segment-EBITDAR basis for the Las Vegas Strip.
- **Source named in:** Named in Capital IQ Default Comps peer set [Capital IQ Comparable Analysis, Business Description sheet, data as of 2026-07-03]; implicitly one of the five Macau concession rivals [FY2025 10-K, Item 1, MGM China Competition section, p.3]. MGM's CFO Jonathan Halkyard referenced "the only other U.S.-based Macau operator" in the Q4 2025 earnings call when discussing MGM China branding fee terms — that reference is to Wynn Resorts. [Q4 2025 Earnings Call, Feb 05, 2026, prepared remarks]
- **One-line read:** Wynn is MGM's closest luxury peer on the Las Vegas Strip (both targeting premium-mass and ultra-high-end table customers) and also shares the Macau concession market — its smaller scale and focus on fewer, ultra-premium properties give it higher per-property revenue concentration and similar EBITDA margins to MGM at the Strip segment level, though Wynn's consolidated balance sheet is also heavily leveraged at 6.7x EBITDA.

## 3. Competitive Position

**Macau (second-largest segment):** MGM China held a 16%+ Macau gaming gross revenue market share for the full year 2025, which management described as a record annual share level. Share was maintained above 16% through Q4 2025 as well (16.5% in Q4). [Q4 2025 Earnings Call, Feb 05, 2026, prepared remarks — "we achieved a 16.5% market share during the fourth quarter and impressively maintained share of over 16% for the full year, a record market share level for an annual period."] With six concessionaires and an equal-share baseline of ~16.7%, this is consistent with an at-or-slightly-below average share, but MGM management characterizes it as above-indexed given their limited room inventory. Macau position: Holding, with marginal upward trend on record share in FY2025.

**Las Vegas Strip (dominant segment):** Market share data for the Las Vegas Strip is not publicly disclosed by any regulator or operator in a way that allows direct competitor share calculation. The proxy available is RevPAR and revenue vs. the city aggregate. Las Vegas Strip net revenues fell 4% in FY2025 against city-wide visitor volume down 8% [FY2025 10-K, Item 7 MD&A] — suggesting MGM may have held share on a revenue-per-visitor basis even as total volume fell, though this cannot be confirmed without competitor disclosures. MGM's Strip occupancy was 92% in FY2025 [FY2025 10-K, Item 7 MD&A]. Management claimed "outsized room occupancy share" in the Q4 2025 call, but no specific share statistic was cited. **Strip position vs. peers: Not disclosed in primary sources.**

**Regional Operations:** Regional casino markets are fragmented by state; share disclosures are not published by the company or aggregators at a national level. Revenue grew 2% in Q4 2025 and full-year slot win set a record in FY2025. Regional position: Not disclosed.

## 4. Competitive Shape

The Las Vegas Strip integrated resort market is an oligopoly, dominated by three operators — MGM Resorts, Caesars Entertainment, and Wynn Resorts — that together own the majority of large casino-resort properties on the Strip. The Nevada Gaming Control Board does not publish HHI for the Strip, but the physical real estate is finite (the Strip is a 4.2-mile stretch of Las Vegas Boulevard) and the three large operators control most of the hotel room and casino floor capacity. New large-scale entrants require billion-dollar capital investment, a Nevada gaming license, and available Strip land — all high barriers. The Macau market is a government-administered oligopoly of exactly six concessionaires (MGM China, Sands China / LVS, Wynn Macau / Wynn Resorts, Galaxy Entertainment, SJM Holdings, and Melco Resorts) under licenses valid through December 2032; no new entrant can legally operate. This makes the Macau oligopoly even more rigid than Las Vegas: the six players are set by government decree, and only share within that fixed set can shift. US regional gaming markets are more fragmented: hundreds of casinos operate across 18+ states, often under state-specific licensing regimes, making any single operator's share in the aggregate national regional market low. The dominant competitive tension in each of MGM's three profit-positive segments is therefore oligopolistic discipline in Las Vegas and Macau, and fragmented but local-monopoly conditions in individual regional markets.

## 5. Caveat

The MGM 10-K does not name specific Las Vegas Strip competitors (Caesars, Wynn, etc.) by name in its competition section — it uses general descriptions ("major hotel casinos on or near the Las Vegas Strip," "major competitors"). The competitor identifications in this report for the Las Vegas segment rely on the Capital IQ Default Comps peer export (a Tier-5 source) and on industry knowledge, not on MGM's own filings naming those peers. The Macau concession competitors (five others out of six total) are confirmed by the 10-K but not individually named; the named Macau peers (LVS / Sands China, Wynn Macau) are identified from the Capital IQ peer set and from the Q4 2025 earnings call reference to "the only other U.S.-based Macau operator" (Wynn). For the moat agent's peer economics comparison: all profitability figures for Caesars, LVS, and Wynn are sourced from the Capital IQ Comparable Analysis export (data as of 2026-07-03) — these are data-vendor figures and should not be attributed to the peers' own filings without cross-checking against those filings directly.

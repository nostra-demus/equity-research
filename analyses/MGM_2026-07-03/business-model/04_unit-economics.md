# Unit Economics — MGM

## 1. Natural Unit

The natural economic unit for Las Vegas Strip Resorts is a hotel room (measured per available room per night, or "room-night").

Secondary unit types exist in the business: for MGM China the relevant unit is a gaming table (measured by table games drop and win per table); for Regional Operations the gaming machine (slot handle per machine) is also used. This table focuses on the dominant unit — the Las Vegas Strip room-night — because Las Vegas Strip Resorts generates 48.1% of consolidated revenue and 54.7% of positive-segment EBITDAR (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, amortisation, and triple net lease rent — the pre-rent cash profit each segment keeps before corporate and real estate costs).

## 3. Value Creation Read

The Las Vegas Strip room-night creates value at current operating scale, but the margin has deteriorated over two consecutive years and the true per-room return — once the triple net lease rent is included — is thinner than the reported EBITDAR margin of 33.9% implies.

The positive case rests on these disclosed numbers: RevPAR of $229, occupancy of 92%, and segment EBITDAR of ~$77,240 per available room per year (FY2025). The segment EBITDAR margin of 33.9% has been consistently positive across FY2023–FY2025, meaning that for each dollar of segment revenue, MGM retains 34 cents before rent and depreciation. At the consolidated level, cash from operations was $2.5B in FY2025, confirming that the portfolio — dominated by the LV Strip — genuinely converts revenue to cash.

The important caveat is that "segment EBITDAR" deliberately excludes the ~$2.3B per year in triple net lease rent (paid to VICI and Bellagio REIT) that sits in "Corporate and other." That rent is a real, fixed cost of the LV Strip and Regional operations. When rent is included, the all-in cash profit margin for the operating business is materially below 33.9% — the exact rent attribution per room is not disclosed at the segment level, preventing a clean per-room contribution margin calculation after rent. The disclosure gap most relevant to completing the unit economics is the per-segment rent allocation between LV Strip and Regional Operations.

Direction is deteriorating: RevPAR fell from $245 (FY2024) to $229 (FY2025), a decline of $16 or 6.5%, driven by an 8% decline in Las Vegas visitor volume and a room renovation at MGM Grand Las Vegas. Segment EBITDAR margin compressed by 130 basis points (one basis point = 0.01%) year on year.

**Verdict: Creates value — but at deteriorating per-unit returns.** The EBITDAR-level unit economics are positive and the segment has been profitable across three disclosed years. However, the FY2025 RevPAR and margin represent a weaker year following a convention-heavy FY2024 peak. Stating the FY2024 EBITDAR margin of 35.2% as a "normal" or structural level would be premature; through FY2023, FY2024, and FY2025 the range has been 33.9%–36.2%, suggesting a mid-cycle figure closer to 34–35% is more realistic than the FY2024 peak.

---

## 4. Sensitivity

RevPAR is the single input that would most change the value-creation read if it moved 20%. MGM's LV Strip room base is large and fixed (37,000 rooms), its lease rent to VICI is substantially fixed (approximately $2.3B per year consolidated, escalating annually at ~2% per the master lease terms), and its labour cost is partly fixed by union agreements (approximately 37,000 US employees are covered by collective bargaining agreements expiring in 2026). In that cost structure, RevPAR drops flow disproportionately to EBITDAR because the fixed cost base — rent, labour, gaming floor overheads — does not shrink with lower occupancy. A 20% RevPAR decline from $229 would bring RevPAR to approximately $183, comparable to pre-2022 levels; at a rough 50–55% flow-through on RevPAR changes (consistent with the observed FY2025 EBITDAR decline of 8% on a 6.5% RevPAR decline), segment EBITDAR could fall by $275–300M or roughly 10%. RevPAR has historically been most volatile at times of convention calendar weakness (as in 2025) and macro demand shocks — the 8% Las Vegas visitor-volume decline in FY2025 shows that volatility remains real even in a normalised operating environment.

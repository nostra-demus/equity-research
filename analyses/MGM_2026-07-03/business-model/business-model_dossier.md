# Business Model Reality Check — MGM (Synthesis)

## Abstract

MGM Resorts International is a US-listed gaming and hospitality operator that owns and runs casino resorts, primarily on the Las Vegas Strip, in regional US markets, and in Macau through a ~56% controlling stake in MGM China. It makes money by operating casinos (gaming win), hotels (room revenue at $249 ADR on the Strip), food and beverage, and entertainment, with Las Vegas Strip Resorts generating 48% of total FY2025 revenue of $17.5 billion and 55% of positive-segment cash profit. The strongest business-model positive is the oligopolistic structure of its two key markets — the Las Vegas Strip is a physically finite 4.2-mile corridor with no room for new large-scale entrants, and Macau is capped by government decree at exactly six concessionaires — giving MGM protection from outside competition that very few consumer businesses enjoy. The strongest business-model negative is an unusually rigid and escalating cost structure: $1.8 billion per year in non-negotiable triple-net rent to VICI, plus an uncapped capital call of roughly $2.1 billion remaining for the Osaka development, compressing free cash flow precisely when Las Vegas visitor volume fell 8% in FY2025. No automatic disqualifier was triggered; the business is an average cyclical leisure operator that deserves deeper work only if valuation adequately compensates for high operating leverage and a looming Macau concession clock.

## 3. Reconciliation

**Lease rent treatment:** `09_moat.md` and `11_capital-allocation-governance.md` both present two views of leverage — the ex-lease (post-leaseback) view at ~1.7x EBITDA and the gross-capital (lease-included) view at ~4.0x lease-adjusted EBITDA. The two are not contradictory; they measure different things. This synthesis uses the gross-capital view as the primary economic reality (the $1.8B/year rent is non-negotiable and fixed regardless of revenues) while noting the ex-lease view for comparison. The more conservative reading is the gross-capital basis.

**Moat verdict vs. competitive map:** `09_moat.md` calls the moat "narrow" while `08_competitive-map.md` describes the Las Vegas Strip as an oligopoly dominated by three incumbents. There is no real disagreement: an oligopolistic structure protects incumbents against new entry but does not prevent share battles within the incumbent set. The moat is real but operates at the industry level (no new outside entrants), not at the intra-oligopoly level. This synthesis reconciles the two readings as: narrow moat from external new-entry barrier; no competitive advantage within the oligopoly group.

**RevPAR trend:** `04_unit-economics.md` and `07_business-quality.md` both note the two-year RevPAR decline (FY2023: $237 → FY2024: $245 → FY2025: $229). The FY2024 peak was elevated by a strong convention year; FY2025 was softer. The preferred read is that FY2024 was a cyclical peak and FY2025 was a return toward mid-cycle, not a structural decline — but the data window is only three years and the more conservative reading is used: deteriorating unit economics pending evidence of recovery.

**No material disagreements between specialists** on the underlying numbers, segment definitions, or disqualifier determinations. Minor rounding differences in revenue share percentages are immaterial.

## 4. Note To The Final Synthesizer

**Mandatory Red-Flag Propagation.** All flags from `12_red-flags-sweep.md` with severity ≥ 40 are surfaced below. All six new flags cleared the ≥ 40 threshold; all six appear here:

- **Osaka uncapped funding commitment (severity 62):** MGM has already diluted from 50% to ~39% ownership in April 2026 while retaining the uncapped completion guarantee and pledging its entire stake as collateral; $2.1B remains unfunded through 2028 at ~$138M per quarter in current draws. This is an open-ended capital call whose total cost cannot be bounded from filings, layered onto a balance sheet with ~$4.2B in reported net debt and $25B in lease liabilities.

- **Operating income -22% on +4% revenue in Q1 2026 (severity 60):** The decline was driven by fading cyber insurance ($8M vs $49M in Q1 2025), self-insurance reserve build ($46M), gaming tax increases at MGM China, and higher payroll. These are now structural cost additions, not one-time items — and they are landing on a business where the $1.8B/year VICI rent amplifies every dollar of cost inflation into a larger EBITDAR impact.

- **Collective bargaining wave in 2026 (severity 58):** ~37,000 US employees under contracts expiring in 2026, with labor the largest controllable operating cost. New agreements at higher wage rates flow directly into the fixed-cost base with no automatic revenue recovery mechanism.

- **Shortfall guarantee on $6.01B of REIT debt (severity 55):** MGM backstops $3.01B (Bellagio REIT Venture, maturity 2029) and $3.0B (Mandalay Bay/MGM Grand Las Vegas) of third-party REIT indebtedness. The notional exposure is correlated with the same gaming downturn that would hit MGM's own revenues. Recorded at fair value under ASC 460 (currently "immaterial") but notional exposure is large and uncapped in a forced-liquidation scenario.

- **Cybersecurity CISO vacancy and fading insurance (severity 52):** CISO position remained vacant as of Feb 11, 2026 filing date; state gaming commission investigations ongoing; business interruption insurance proceeds are declining to near-zero ($8M in Q1 2026 vs $49M in Q1 2025), meaning the P&L tailwind is exhausted while remediation costs and reserve builds persist.

- **Multi-employer pension at Borgata, "critical status" (severity 42):** UNITE HERE Legacy Plan in critical status; withdrawal liability would be "significant" and is not quantified in the filing; risk compounds with the 2026 labor contract renewals.

- **New York integrated resort competition (severity 48):** New York Gaming Commission issued three integrated resort licenses in 2025 that will compete directly with Borgata (NJ) and any remaining Empire City operations; MGM already withdrew its commercial gaming license application and wrote off $256M in Empire City goodwill. The competitive damage to MGM Regional Operations (21.5% of revenue) is not future — the licenses have been awarded.

**Strongest business-model positive (with evidence):** The oligopolistic market structure in both key segments — the Las Vegas Strip (physically finite land, multi-year licensing, multi-billion-dollar entry cost) and Macau (government-capped at exactly six concessionaires since January 2023) — provides genuine protection from outside new entry that most consumer businesses lack. This is the foundational reason the Strip's occupancy stays at 92% and the Macau market share is contestable only within six players, not from the outside [FY2025 10-K, Item 1, pp.3, 7–9; Item 1A, pp.14–17, 26–27].

**Strongest business-model negative (with evidence):** A cost structure that is structurally more rigid than the revenue base it supports: $1.8B/year in non-negotiable VICI rent (with annual escalators), $46M in self-insurance reserve additions per quarter (post-cyber), union contracts expiring across 37,000 employees, and a $2.1B uncapped Osaka commitment drawing ~$138M/quarter — all landing on a business where Las Vegas visitor volume fell 8% in FY2025 and operating income already declined 22% in Q1 2026 despite 4% revenue growth [Q1 2026 10-Q, MD&A, p.22; FY2025 10-K, Item 7, MD&A, pp.35–45].

**Most important segment:** Las Vegas Strip Resorts — 48.1% of revenue, 54.7% of positive-segment cash profit, 33.9% EBITDAR margin, and the segment where MGM's convention-driven midweek demand and brand advantages at Bellagio and Aria concentrate. MGM China is the growth engine and concession-clock risk, but the Strip is where the majority of durable earnings sits.

**Cleanest unit-economics read:** The Las Vegas Strip room-night is the most fully disclosed unit: RevPAR $229, ADR $249, occupancy 92%, segment EBITDAR per available room ~$77,240/year. The read is clean on the revenue and EBITDAR-before-rent basis. It cannot be completed at the true all-in level because the $2.3B rent to VICI is not allocated to individual segments in the filings — this is a disclosed structural limitation, not an opacity flag.

**Where MGM sits vs named peers on margin / ROIC:** MGM's consolidated GAAP EBIT margin (5.7% in FY2025) lags all three named peers — LVS (24.5% LTM), Wynn (15.7% LTM), Caesars (18.1% LTM) — almost entirely because MGM's $2.3B rent sits in "Corporate and other" before arriving at EBIT. On segment EBITDAR (before rent), MGM Strip (33.9%) is broadly comparable to Wynn's Strip-level performance but below LVS's integrated resort margins (35.2% consolidated). Through-cycle ROIC of 10.2% (ex-lease IC) is marginally above the estimated WACC of ~9–10% — a thin margin that disappears when the economic substance of the lease is included in invested capital (3.6% gross-capital ROIC vs. any reasonable WACC estimate).

**Main external dependency:** Consumer spending cycle and the Macau gaming concession together represent the two existential external dependencies. A simultaneous US consumer slowdown (hitting ~$12.2B in domestic revenue) and any adverse Macau government action (hitting ~$4.5B and growing) would hit all three profit-positive segments simultaneously, while the $1.8B in annual rent remains non-negotiable.

**Most important capital allocation or governance signal:** The share buyback program (32% share count reduction in three years, $2.6B deployed in FY2024–2025) is the clearest positive capital-allocation signal in the filings. The countervailing concern is that buybacks are occurring alongside a $2.1B uncapped forward capital commitment to Osaka, lease-adjusted leverage of ~4.0x, and a digital segment that has not achieved positive EBITDA three years post-acquisition. The net signal is that per-share value is being created through buybacks but the capital structure has limited optionality if the consumer cycle turns.

Acquisition-pattern severity was 48/100 (below the 70 threshold for Filter 4); industry rate-of-change scored 60/100 (above the 40 threshold for Filter 5); no integrity/fraud evidence for Filter 1. ## 5. Simple Summary

- **What it does:** MGM Resorts runs casino resorts — people pay to gamble, sleep, eat, and see shows — with 16 US properties centered on the Las Vegas Strip and two Macau properties under a government-issued concession expiring December 2032.

- **How it makes money:** Gaming (table drop × hold percentage, slot handle × win percentage) plus hotel rooms ($249 ADR on the Strip, 92% occupancy), food and beverage, and entertainment; Las Vegas Strip Resorts generates ~48% of revenue and ~55% of segment cash profit.

- **Whether each new unit creates value:** The Las Vegas Strip room-night creates value at reported EBITDAR margins of 33.9% and RevPAR of $229 — but the margin is deteriorating (down from 36.2% in FY2023) and the true per-room return after rent cannot be cleanly derived from the filings because the $2.3B annual VICI rent is not allocated to the segments that generate it.

- **Which segment matters most:** Las Vegas Strip Resorts by a large margin. MGM China is the growth story and the concession-clock risk. MGM Digital is loss-making and subscale.

- **Whether it has a moat, and against whom:** Narrow moat — regulatory and license barriers prevent any new large-scale entrant on the Las Vegas Strip (finite land, multi-year licensing, billion-dollar capital cost) and in Macau (government-capped at six concessionaires). The moat protects the incumbent set from outside competition; it does not protect MGM from Caesars, Wynn, or LVS within the oligopoly. The moat is eroding: EBIT has fallen three consecutive years and the Macau concession clock is ticking.

- **What external variables it depends on:** Consumer willingness to travel and spend on gaming and leisure (primary), Macau government policy on the gaming concession and mainland Chinese tourist flows (~25% of revenue), US and state gaming tax policy, labor union negotiations for ~37,000 employees in 2026, and JPY/USD for the $2.1B unfunded Osaka commitment.

- **Whether capital is allocated well:** Buybacks are excellent and disciplined (32% share count reduction in three years). Acquisitions have been mixed: Cosmopolitan is strategically logical but LeoVegas has not produced positive EBITDA in three years and Empire City was fully written off. The Osaka commitment ($2.1B remaining, uncapped) is the most uncertain forward capital call. Economic leverage of ~4.0x on a lease-adjusted basis limits balance sheet flexibility.

- **Whether it deserves deeper work:** Yes — but only if the valuation adequately compensates for high operating leverage, the Macau concession clock, the cost-structure rigidity, and the Osaka capital call. At a price that assumes a repeat of FY2024's stronger convention year or ignores the lease-adjusted leverage, it does not clear the bar.

# Business Identity — MGM

## 1. What The Company Actually Does

MGM Resorts International is a US-listed (NYSE: MGM) gaming and hospitality operator incorporated in Delaware. It owns and operates casino resorts — physical properties where customers pay to gamble (slots and table games), stay in hotel rooms, eat and drink, attend concerts and shows, and attend conventions. As of December 31, 2025, it operates 16 casino properties in the United States (concentrated on the Las Vegas Strip and in regional markets such as Detroit, Atlantic City, and Maryland) and holds a ~56% controlling interest in MGM China, which operates two casino resorts in Macau under a government-issued concession. Through its fully consolidated subsidiary LeoVegas (headquartered in Sweden), MGM also sells online casino games and sports betting to customers in Europe and Brazil under the MGM Digital segment. The customer MGM is selling to ranges from the mass-market slot player at a regional property to the high-end "VIP" table-games player at Aria or the Bellagio, and to the convention-goer who fills a hotel midweek. The problem the customer pays to solve is entertainment and leisure: the casino provides the thrill of gambling in a controlled, licensed environment, combined with high-end food, lodging, and live events that are hard to replicate elsewhere. MGM does not own the real estate under most of its domestic properties; it sold these assets to VICI Properties and leases them back under long-term triple-net leases — paying approximately $2.3 billion in annual rent — in exchange for the capital returned to fund buybacks and debt reduction. [FY25 10-K, Item 1, Business Overview; Item 7, MD&A Overview; Note 11]

## 2. How The Company Makes Money

**Segment 1 — Las Vegas Strip Resorts (physical gaming + hospitality)**
`Revenue = visitors × (casino spend per visitor + room rate × occupied nights + food & beverage spend + entertainment spend)`

Reported as four revenue lines at each property: casino, rooms, food & beverage, and entertainment/retail/other. The Strip is distinctive because non-gaming revenues (rooms, food & beverage, entertainment) are typically the majority of net revenue — the 10-K states "over half of the net revenue from our Las Vegas Strip Resorts is typically derived from non-gaming operations." [FY25 10-K, Item 1, p.2] LV Strip Resorts net revenue FY2025: $8.44 billion; Segment Adjusted EBITDAR margin: 33.9%. [FY25 10-K, Item 7, MD&A, p.37, p.39]

**Segment 2 — Regional Operations (gaming-first)**
`Revenue = slot handle × slot win% + table games drop × table win% + ancillary hotel/F&B`

Regional casinos are gaming-primary: "the majority of net revenue from our Regional Operations is typically derived from gaming operations." [FY25 10-K, Item 1, p.2] Regional Operations net revenue FY2025: $3.77 billion; Segment Adjusted EBITDAR margin: 30.8%. [FY25 10-K, Item 7, MD&A, p.37, p.39]

**Segment 3 — MGM China (Macau concession gaming)**
`Revenue = main floor table games drop × table win% + VIP baccarat volume × hold`

MGM China revenue is almost entirely casino. Net revenue FY2025: $4.46 billion; Segment Adjusted EBITDAR margin: 27.0%. Gaming taxes in Macau are a large cost. MGM China holds a government concession valid through December 2032. [FY25 10-K, Item 1, p.2; Item 7, MD&A, p.37, p.39]

**Segment 4 — MGM Digital (online iGaming and sports betting via LeoVegas)**
`Revenue = active players × average revenue per player`

LeoVegas offers iGaming and sports betting, primarily in licensed European markets and Brazil. MGM also holds a 50% stake in BetMGM North America Venture (unconsolidated, accounted for under the equity method), which generated $60 million of income to MGM in FY2025 — its first profitable year. MGM Digital net revenue FY2025: $654 million (+19% year-over-year), but the segment ran a $90 million Adjusted EBITDAR loss. [FY25 10-K, Item 7, MD&A, p.37, p.39, p.40]

**What drives volume, price, and margin:**
Volume is driven by visitor counts (Las Vegas visitor volume fell 8% in 2025 per the Las Vegas Convention and Visitors Authority) and, in Macau, by Chinese tourist arrivals (up 15% in 2025 per the Macau Statistics and Census Service). Price on the gaming side is determined by hold percentage (the fraction of money wagered that the house keeps) — for table games this was 25.2% on the Las Vegas Strip in FY2025 and 25.5% at MGM China, neither of which is fully controllable by management; for slots the win rate is more stable (~9.4% Strip, ~10.1% regional). Hotel price (ADR) on the Strip was $249 in FY2025, down from $260 in FY2024, partly reflecting a remodel disruption at MGM Grand Las Vegas. Margin is squeezed by the $2.3 billion annual triple-net lease obligation to VICI (which sits in "Corporate and other" and causes reported consolidated adjusted EBITDA of $2.43 billion to look much lower than segment-level profitability). Labor, gaming taxes, and energy are the other key cost drivers. [FY25 10-K, Item 7, MD&A, pp.37–41]

## 3. Business Type Classification

Integrated resort operator and gaming concessionaire with asset-light domestic lease structure, high operating leverage to visitor volume, and a nascent loss-making online gaming segment.

**Sector-specific red flags to watch:**
- High-end gaming volatility: win percentage swings on baccarat (MGM China) and table games can move quarterly earnings materially — a single bad quarter of hold is not a structural deterioration.
- Government concession risk: Macau gaming concession runs to December 2032; non-renewal or adverse government action would impair the China segment permanently.
- Triple-net lease as quasi-debt: the $2.3B/year rent obligation to VICI is economically similar to debt service and does not appear in net-debt calculations; it depresses free cash flow materially.
- Visitor volume sensitivity: Las Vegas Strip revenues fell 4% in FY2025 on an 8% decline in Las Vegas visitor volume — the business has high fixed costs and operating leverage.

**Valuation norm for this type:** EV/EBITDA (on Consolidated Adjusted EBITDA, noting that this excludes the $2.3B rent which must be added back when comparing to unlevered companies), EBITDAR multiples at the segment level, and property-level NAV (particularly for Macau). FCFF DCF is appropriate but must normalize for the lease structure and Macau concession timing.

## 4. What Drives Variance

When revenue or margins move at MGM, the most likely cause is visitor volume and gaming hold percentage: in FY2025 Las Vegas Strip revenues fell 4% primarily because Las Vegas visitor volume fell 8%, while rooms revenue (down 9%) and ADR (down 4% to $249) also reflected a major room remodel disruption at MGM Grand Las Vegas. MGM China moves on Macau tourism policy and baccarat hold — China net revenues rose 11% in FY2025 driven by a 15% increase in Macau visitor arrivals and higher main floor table games drop ($15.8B vs $14.7B), with hold percent also ticking up to 25.5% from 25.0%. FX is a secondary factor (USD-denominated MGM China debt creates translation volatility, as the $288M FX loss in "other, net" for FY2025 shows). Cost inputs are dominated by labor (unionized; ~37,000 US employees covered by collective bargaining agreements as of December 31, 2025, with several contracts expiring in 2026) and the fixed rent line to VICI, which limits margin leverage to the upside when revenues grow. [FY25 10-K, Item 7, MD&A, pp.36–41; Item 1A, Risk Factors]

## 3. Segment Disclosure Quality

Segment definitions are consistent year-over-year. MGM Digital was added as a fourth reportable segment beginning in 2023 (LeoVegas acquisition in September 2022 created the critical mass); the segment note and MD&A disclose the change and provide restated prior-period comparisons. The segment profit measure — "Segment Adjusted EBITDAR" (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, amortisation, and triple net lease rent) — is consistently defined and applied across all segments, which is appropriate given MGM's asset-light structure (properties leased, not owned, under triple net leases). However, the triple net lease rent (~$2.3B annually) sits entirely in "Corporate and other" rather than being allocated to the Las Vegas Strip or Regional segments that generate the rents. This inflates each domestic segment's EBITDAR margin and makes cross-company comparisons with peers that own their real estate misleading; an analyst must add back rent to see the true all-in cost of operations. At-segment GAAP operating income and GAAP net income are not disclosed at segment level — only the adjusted EBITDAR measure is. "Corporate and other" is large in an absolute dollar sense (-$2.7B EBITDAR) but this reflects the rent flow-through, not a hidden business bucket of concern. The structural exclusion of BetMGM (50% JV) from all segments is disclosed and appropriate under US GAAP consolidation rules, but it means the digital gaming P&L that most investors associate with "MGM Digital" is not fully visible in segment results. Segment disclosure is generally transparent for a US GAAP filer, with one caveat: the rent allocation treatment requires a reader to understand the triple net lease structure before interpreting segment margins.

## 3. Value Creation Read

The Las Vegas Strip room-night creates value at current operating scale, but the margin has deteriorated over two consecutive years and the true per-room return — once the triple net lease rent is included — is thinner than the reported EBITDAR margin of 33.9% implies.

The positive case rests on these disclosed numbers: RevPAR of $229, occupancy of 92%, and segment EBITDAR of ~$77,240 per available room per year (FY2025). The segment EBITDAR margin of 33.9% has been consistently positive across FY2023–FY2025, meaning that for each dollar of segment revenue, MGM retains 34 cents before rent and depreciation. At the consolidated level, cash from operations was $2.5B in FY2025, confirming that the portfolio — dominated by the LV Strip — genuinely converts revenue to cash.

The important caveat is that "segment EBITDAR" deliberately excludes the ~$2.3B per year in triple net lease rent (paid to VICI and Bellagio REIT) that sits in "Corporate and other." That rent is a real, fixed cost of the LV Strip and Regional operations. When rent is included, the all-in cash profit margin for the operating business is materially below 33.9% — the exact rent attribution per room is not disclosed at the segment level, preventing a clean per-room contribution margin calculation after rent. The disclosure gap most relevant to completing the unit economics is the per-segment rent allocation between LV Strip and Regional Operations.

Direction is deteriorating: RevPAR fell from $245 (FY2024) to $229 (FY2025), a decline of $16 or 6.5%, driven by an 8% decline in Las Vegas visitor volume and a room renovation at MGM Grand Las Vegas. Segment EBITDAR margin compressed by 130 basis points (one basis point = 0.01%) year on year.

**Verdict: Creates value — but at deteriorating per-unit returns.** The EBITDAR-level unit economics are positive and the segment has been profitable across three disclosed years. However, the FY2025 RevPAR and margin represent a weaker year following a convention-heavy FY2024 peak. Stating the FY2024 EBITDAR margin of 35.2% as a "normal" or structural level would be premature; through FY2023, FY2024, and FY2025 the range has been 33.9%–36.2%, suggesting a mid-cycle figure closer to 34–35% is more realistic than the FY2024 peak.

---

## 4. Sensitivity

RevPAR is the single input that would most change the value-creation read if it moved 20%. MGM's LV Strip room base is large and fixed (37,000 rooms), its lease rent to VICI is substantially fixed (approximately $2.3B per year consolidated, escalating annually at ~2% per the master lease terms), and its labour cost is partly fixed by union agreements (approximately 37,000 US employees are covered by collective bargaining agreements expiring in 2026). In that cost structure, RevPAR drops flow disproportionately to EBITDAR because the fixed cost base — rent, labour, gaming floor overheads — does not shrink with lower occupancy. A 20% RevPAR decline from $229 would bring RevPAR to approximately $183, comparable to pre-2022 levels; at a rough 50–55% flow-through on RevPAR changes (consistent with the observed FY2025 EBITDAR decline of 8% on a 6.5% RevPAR decline), segment EBITDAR could fall by $275–300M or roughly 10%. RevPAR has historically been most volatile at times of convention calendar weakness (as in 2025) and macro demand shocks — the 8% Las Vegas visitor-volume decline in FY2025 shows that volatility remains real even in a normalised operating environment.

## 2. Input Cost Pass-Through

MGM operates a service business with no raw-material commodities in the traditional sense. Its three material input costs are labor, energy, and rent. None of these is passed through to customers via explicit contractual escalators on the revenue side.

**Labor** is the largest operating cost. As of December 31, 2025, approximately 37,000 US employees are covered by collective bargaining agreements, with contracts for "small groups of Las Vegas property and corporate employees" expiring in the first half of 2026 and regional agreements covering approximately 3,000 employees also expiring in 2026. [FY25 10-K, Item 1, Employees and Labor Relations] When union contracts renew at higher wage rates, those costs flow directly into operating expenses with no automatic recovery mechanism — MGM must absorb them or offset via price increases, efficiency, or volume. The 10-K explicitly lists union labor as a risk: "A significant portion of our labor force is covered by collective bargaining agreements." [FY25 10-K, Item 1A, Risk Factors, Business Industry Market Conditions summary]

**Energy** costs are explicitly flagged in the Risk Factors: "Our business is particularly sensitive to energy prices and a rise in energy prices could harm our operating results." [FY25 10-K, Item 1A, Risk Factors] No energy pass-through clause or hedging disclosure covering a material share of consumption appears in the 10-K.

**Rent** is the starkest example of a non-pass-through cost. MGM pays VICI approximately $2.3 billion per year in triple-net lease rent, with annual escalators baked into the lease structure. The 10-K confirms: "The leases for our operating properties have fixed rental payments (with annual escalators)." [FY25 10-K, Item 1A, Risk Factors p.14] This escalating rent obligation is absolute — it must be paid irrespective of revenue performance. MGM also provides shortfall guarantees on $3.01 billion and $3.0 billion of VICI landlord debt for Bellagio and Mandalay Bay / MGM Grand Las Vegas respectively. [FY25 10-K, Item 1A, Risk Factors pp.13–14]

**Supplier / input concentration:** The 10-K does not disclose the share of COGS or total purchases attributable to any single product supplier — the nature of the business (services, not manufactured goods) means there is no single-source raw material. The material supply-side dependencies are structural, not commodity: (1) VICI Properties is effectively the sole supplier of the physical space MGM occupies domestically, representing ~$2.3 billion of annual rent with no credible alternative counterparty at equivalent scale; (2) the Macau government is the sole grantor of the gaming concession that generates ~$4.46 billion of MGM China revenue. No percentage concentration figure for any goods supplier is disclosed; the filing does not quantify COGS from any named vendor.

## 3. Customer Pricing Power

MGM has partial pricing power on hotel rooms but limited pricing power on gaming, and its pricing authority depends heavily on the segment.

On the hotel side, MGM actively manages ADR (average daily rate — the average nightly price charged per occupied room, including complimentary rooms). Strip ADR was $249 in FY25, down from $260 in FY24, partly because a major room remodel disruption at MGM Grand Las Vegas pulled down the average. [FY25 10-K, MD&A p.38] The decline was attributed to volume and property-specific factors, not a secular loss of pricing ability — the 10-K language on Las Vegas Strip states that non-gaming revenues, which include rooms, are "typically the majority of net revenue" at the Strip properties, and 92% occupancy in FY25 demonstrates the rooms remain in demand. [FY25 10-K, Item 1, p.2; MD&A p.38]

On the gaming side, MGM does not set price in a traditional sense. The house edge is fixed by mathematics and game design (slot win rates ~9–10%; table hold ~25%). Players can choose which property or game to play, but the hold percentage is not individually negotiable — it is set by machine configuration and game rules. This means volume, not price, is the primary driver of gaming revenue.

There is no evidence in the last 24 months of a deliberate across-the-board gaming price increase (i.e., lifting hold percentages or minimum table-bet requirements) and a documented volume response. Las Vegas Strip casino revenues fell approximately 4% in FY25 on an 8% drop in visitor volume [FY25 10-K, MD&A p.37], suggesting MGM absorbed a demand shortfall rather than compensating with pricing. In Macau, revenue grew 11% in FY25, driven by volume (visitor arrivals +15%) and a modest improvement in table hold (25.5% vs 25.0%) — a hold-percentage shift in the company's favor, not a deliberate pricing action. [FY25 10-K, MD&A p.39]

MGM uses its loyalty program (MGM Rewards) and data analytics to personalize offers and target upsell, which management describes as a mechanism to "enhance customer acquisition, engagement, and value over time." [FY25 10-K, Item 1, Technology] This is a revenue-optimization tool, not a price lever in the conventional sense.

---

## 4. Economic Control Verdict

**Mixed** — MGM controls pricing and customer relationships on the output side (brands, gaming odds, hotel rates, loyalty program) but is structurally price-taking on two critical inputs: real estate (VICI leases at fixed-escalating terms with no exit) and gaming concessions (government-set terms in Macau). On the customer side, the company faces no individual counterparty with concentrated power — gaming revenue comes from millions of discrete retail transactions — giving it effective price-setting authority in the casino and hotel. But on the input side, it has committed to $2.3 billion of rent annually plus $6.01 billion of landlord shortfall guarantees that it cannot renegotiate at will, and it operates in Macau entirely at the pleasure of a government with unilateral termination rights. The result is a company that sets the odds at the table but cannot set its own rent or concession terms.

---

## 5. The Single Biggest Bargaining Risk

The VICI triple-net lease relationship: if VICI increases escalators beyond what the current contracts allow, refuses to extend leases, or exercises lease termination rights, MGM loses the ability to operate its US casino properties — which generate $12.2 billion of the company's $17.3 billion total net revenue — with no credible alternative landlord for comparable real estate at comparable scale. [FY25 10-K, Item 1A, Risk Factors p.14; MD&A p.37]

## 4. Read

MGM is a high-quality physical asset operator in a privileged location (Las Vegas Strip) grafted onto a franchise model with two structurally different risk profiles: the domestically leased, convention-supported Strip portfolio, and the Macau concession that represents a government-granted monopoly in an Asian gaming market. As a durable compounder, it falls short on three of the most important quality dimensions: cyclicality is high (proven by a near-total earnings collapse in 2020 and meaningful softness in every economic slowdown), regulatory dependence is extreme (the Macau concession is existential and binary), and competitive intensity is elevated and rising (iGaming expansion is directly eating into the volume that historically funneled through physical regional casinos, even if the Strip's unique experience provides some insulation). The business is better described as a premium cyclical leisure operator with a regulated-franchise overlay, not a durable compounder.

The single quality factor a buyer should track over the next 24 months is **regulatory dependence** — specifically, the Macau gaming concession trajectory toward its December 2032 expiry. As that date approaches, the market will increasingly apply a concession-term discount to MGM China earnings, and any signal from the Macau government about renewal terms, local content requirements, or concession structure changes will be a first-order earnings driver. The concession represents not just 25% of current revenue but most of the growth optionality in the story, so how that regulatory risk is resolved (or not) dominates the quality assessment of the whole enterprise.

## 4. Competitive Shape

The Las Vegas Strip integrated resort market is an oligopoly, dominated by three operators — MGM Resorts, Caesars Entertainment, and Wynn Resorts — that together own the majority of large casino-resort properties on the Strip. The Nevada Gaming Control Board does not publish HHI for the Strip, but the physical real estate is finite (the Strip is a 4.2-mile stretch of Las Vegas Boulevard) and the three large operators control most of the hotel room and casino floor capacity. New large-scale entrants require billion-dollar capital investment, a Nevada gaming license, and available Strip land — all high barriers. The Macau market is a government-administered oligopoly of exactly six concessionaires (MGM China, Sands China / LVS, Wynn Macau / Wynn Resorts, Galaxy Entertainment, SJM Holdings, and Melco Resorts) under licenses valid through December 2032; no new entrant can legally operate. This makes the Macau oligopoly even more rigid than Las Vegas: the six players are set by government decree, and only share within that fixed set can shift. US regional gaming markets are more fragmented: hundreds of casinos operate across 18+ states, often under state-specific licensing regimes, making any single operator's share in the aggregate national regional market low. The dominant competitive tension in each of MGM's three profit-positive segments is therefore oligopolistic discipline in Las Vegas and Macau, and fragmented but local-monopoly conditions in individual regional markets.

**The economic moat test (required).** A moat must produce a return on capital above the cost of capital. Peer-superiority alone is not a moat.

> Return on capital **above** cost of capital on the ex-lease IC basis: **10.2% through-cycle ROIC** (FY2023–FY2025 average, ex-lease invested capital) vs **~9–10% estimated WACC** (CAPM inference: risk-free rate 4.3% + beta 1.3 × ERP 5.5% = 11.5% cost of equity; after-tax cost of debt 4.6% at 5.7% avg fixed rate × 80%; blended at ~22% equity weight / ~78% debt+lease weight ≈ 6.1% — Inference, not from filings) — gap is narrow and **regime-dependent**: on the gross capital basis (lease obligations included in invested capital), through-cycle ROIC of 3.6% is materially below even the lowest WACC estimate. The economic moat test passes marginally on a post-leaseback ex-lease IC basis but fails if the economic substance of the $25B VICI lease obligation is treated as invested capital.

**Important qualifications:**

1. **Ex-lease vs. gross-capital divergence.** MGM sold its Las Vegas Strip real estate to VICI Properties in 2022 and now pays ~$2.3B/year in triple-net rent. Stripping the lease liabilities from invested capital makes the denominator appear smaller and ROIC higher. On a gross-capital basis that recognizes the $25B lease obligation as economically equivalent to debt secured by those properties, through-cycle ROIC is 3.6% — materially below the cost of capital. Both views are presented; the ex-lease view is what management implicitly uses; the gross-capital view is more conservative and reflects the economic substance of the arrangement.

2. **FY2025 as a weak year.** Business quality (`07`) scored cyclicality at 32/100 (high cyclicality). FY2025 Strip revenues fell 4% on 8% visitor volume decline. The through-cycle (FY2023–2025) average ROIC of 10.2% is used in preference to the single-year FY2025 figure of 7.1%.

**Relative to peers:** Company sits **at the median to bottom of named peers on EBIT margin** — MGM's FY2025 EBIT margin of 5.7% (consolidated GAAP) trails LVS (24.5% LTM) and Wynn (15.7% LTM) materially, and is below Caesars (18.1% LTM). ## 5. Moat Verdict

**Narrow moat** — some advantage exists, but it is limited in scope, regime-dependent in economics, and under medium-term pressure.

The strongest moat sources are the oligopolistic market structure of the Las Vegas Strip (finite physical land, high-cost entry barrier, government licensing requirements) and the Macau government-administered six-concessionaire oligopoly, which together make new competitive threats from outside the current incumbent set effectively impossible over any normal investment horizon. Within the incumbent set — where the real competition actually occurs — MGM's advantages are real but narrow: the Bellagio and Aria brands command genuine pricing power for premium-mass table gaming customers, and the convention capacity at center-Strip properties creates structural group-booking advantages. These translate into through-cycle ROIC marginally above cost of capital on a post-leaseback capital basis, but the advantage evaporates when lease obligations are treated as invested capital.

The durability test this moat must pass over the next 5 years: (1) the Macau gaming concession (25% of revenue) expires December 2032 — renewal on unfavorable terms or with new local-content obligations would directly compress returns; (2) the VICI master lease requires ~$2.3B/year in rent regardless of revenue, and a sustained consumer spending downturn would expose the operating leverage this creates; (3) iGaming and online sports betting must not structurally erode the regional casino volume base faster than BetMGM grows to replace it. If any of these three tests fail simultaneously, the narrow moat disappears.

**CLAUSE.md §24, Filter 5 check (fast-changing industry):** `07_business-quality` scored industry rate-of-change at 60/100 — moderate. The physical Strip business is slow-changing; the digital and Macau components are faster-changing. This does not trigger the automatic cap (score ≤40), but does require discounting the durability of the digital moat component in line with the module rules.

**Moat trajectory — eroding.** The moat's direction over the last 3–5 years is eroding on three measures: (a) return on capital is declining — FY2023 EBIT of $1,891M > FY2024 EBIT $1,450M > FY2025 EBIT $1,002M on rising revenue, indicating cost structure deterioration under fixed rent and rising D&A; (b) Las Vegas Strip ADR fell from $260 (FY2024) to $249 (FY2025), a 4.2% decline, suggesting weakening pricing power even at the premium end; (c) the Macau concession clock is ticking — with expiry in December 2032, every passing year reduces the certainty-weighted value of the China segment and creates market-discount pressure. The entry barrier (licenses/location) has not changed, but the return above cost of capital is compressing year by year. An eroding moat at this trajectory feeds the permanent-impairment / declining-perpetuity risk trigger in downstream valuation modules (CLAUDE.md §24, Filter 5).

## 2. Classification

**Capital allocation concerns** — MGM's share buyback program is decisive (32% share count reduction in three years, $2.6B deployed in FY2024-FY2025) and the VICI lease structure has freed capital, but the company carries concerns across two dimensions: (1) the 2022 acquisition burst (Cosmopolitan + LeoVegas) produced impairments and persistent digital-segment losses ($90M EBITDA loss in FY2025), indicating capital was not recovered at the hoped return; and (2) the economic leverage position, when the $1.8B annual triple-net rent is capitalised alongside $6.3B in reported debt, creates a total-claim structure that is large relative to the business's earnings power. The governance picture is cleaner — long-tenured unqualified auditor, no related-party concerns, no restatements, no insider selling — and does not independently reach red-flag territory.

## 3. Most Material Signal

The debt level and trajectory signal (severity 52) is the most material single indicator. The reported net debt / Consolidated Adjusted EBITDA of ~1.7x understates the true capital structure burden because the $1.8B annual triple-net rent payment to VICI is structurally fixed (it must be paid under any revenue scenario, with escalators, and carries all property-cost obligations on top) and is economically equivalent to a debt-service obligation. Capitalising that rent at 8x implies approximately $14.4B of imputed lease obligations; adding reported net debt of ~$4.2B gives a total claims stack of approximately $18.6B against a lease-adjusted EBITDA of roughly $4.7B — a ratio of approximately 4.0x. If the gaming cycle turns or Las Vegas visitor volumes continue their current softness (Las Vegas visitor volume was down 8% in FY2025), this leverage stack amplifies downside. The signal would cross into a more serious classification if: the MGM Osaka commitment ($2.3B) requires incremental debt financing rather than internal cash flows; or BetMGM reverts to loss-making (it turned marginally profitable in FY2025 for the first time after three years of losses); or the VICI lease rent escalators outpace revenue growth. Either of those three developments would increase the effective leverage meaningfully and could force asset sales or equity issuance that would undermine the buyback-driven per-share value creation thesis.

## 4. Cross-Cutting Patterns

Three individual flags converge into a pattern the synthesizer should treat as a single amplified risk. First, the cybersecurity incident (September 2023) produced an ongoing CISO governance vacancy, fading insurance recoveries masking real cost inflation, and self-insurance reserve build that is now eating ~$37M per quarter of EBITDAR. Second, the multi-employer pension at Borgata (UNITE HERE Legacy Plan, "critical status") sits on top of a labor base where ~37,000 US employees face contract renewals in 2026. Third, the 22% operating income decline in Q1 2026 on 4% revenue growth shows these costs are already flowing through earnings. The common thread is that MGM's cost structure has become more rigid and less predictable at exactly the time its revenue growth is slowing: cybersecurity remediation costs, labor inflation from new collective bargaining agreements, MGM China gaming tax increases, and rising self-insurance reserves are all fixed or quasi-fixed cost additions landing on a business with $1.8B/year in non-negotiable lease rent. The operating leverage that amplified upside during the post-COVID recovery will amplify downside in a slower-growth environment, and the current earnings trajectory already shows this mechanism activating.

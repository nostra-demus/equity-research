## 2. Classification

**Capital allocation concerns** — MGM's share buyback program is decisive (32% share count reduction in three years, $2.6B deployed in FY2024-FY2025) and the VICI lease structure has freed capital, but the company carries concerns across two dimensions: (1) the 2022 acquisition burst (Cosmopolitan + LeoVegas) produced impairments and persistent digital-segment losses ($90M EBITDA loss in FY2025), indicating capital was not recovered at the hoped return; and (2) the economic leverage position, when the $1.8B annual triple-net rent is capitalised alongside $6.3B in reported debt, creates a total-claim structure that is large relative to the business's earnings power. The governance picture is cleaner — long-tenured unqualified auditor, no related-party concerns, no restatements, no insider selling — and does not independently reach red-flag territory.

---

## 3. Most Material Signal

The debt level and trajectory signal (severity 52) is the most material single indicator. The reported net debt / Consolidated Adjusted EBITDA of ~1.7x understates the true capital structure burden because the $1.8B annual triple-net rent payment to VICI is structurally fixed (it must be paid under any revenue scenario, with escalators, and carries all property-cost obligations on top) and is economically equivalent to a debt-service obligation. Capitalising that rent at 8x implies approximately $14.4B of imputed lease obligations; adding reported net debt of ~$4.2B gives a total claims stack of approximately $18.6B against a lease-adjusted EBITDA of roughly $4.7B — a ratio of approximately 4.0x. If the gaming cycle turns or Las Vegas visitor volumes continue their current softness (Las Vegas visitor volume was down 8% in FY2025), this leverage stack amplifies downside. The signal would cross into a more serious classification if: the MGM Osaka commitment ($2.3B) requires incremental debt financing rather than internal cash flows; or BetMGM reverts to loss-making (it turned marginally profitable in FY2025 for the first time after three years of losses); or the VICI lease rent escalators outpace revenue growth. Either of those three developments would increase the effective leverage meaningfully and could force asset sales or equity issuance that would undermine the buyback-driven per-share value creation thesis.

## 4. Capital Allocation Score /100

**52 / 100**

The score reflects:
- Very strong share buyback execution (32% share count reduction, consistently funded from operating cash flow) — pulls the score up materially.
- Dividend suspension replaced by buybacks is a rational decision given the company's leverage and the preference for flexibility.
- Active but imperfect M&A in 2022-2023: Cosmopolitan's strategic value is plausible (Strip scale), but LeoVegas/digital has not delivered positive EBITDA three years post-acquisition; Empire City was a full goodwill write-off.
- Economic leverage (lease-adjusted ~4x) limits the score materially; the business does not carry the optionality of a debt-light balance sheet.
- Governance signals are clean (no auditor issues, no related-party concerns, no restatements) and pull the score modestly upward.
- The Osaka off-balance-sheet commitment ($2.3B, denominated in JPY with hedging in place) is a real and large forward capital call.

The acquisition-pattern row was scored at 48 severity — below the ≥70 threshold — so the rejector-filter cap (Capital Allocation Score max 50) is NOT triggered. The score of 52 reflects that the overall picture is mixed-to-slightly-below-average: strong buyback execution alongside imperfect deal returns and high economic leverage.

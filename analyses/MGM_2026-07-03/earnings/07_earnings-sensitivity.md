## 1. Variable Selection

The seven variables below were selected from the driver tables in `02_revenue-drivers.md` and `03_margin-drivers.md` by taking every driver rated High magnitude at the segment or consolidated level, then supplementing with Mid-magnitude drivers where the business-model external-dependency output (`10_external-dependency.md`) confirmed structural importance. Las Vegas Strip RevPAR sits at the top because it is rated High in both the revenue and margin driver tables, drives 48% of consolidated revenue, and has a demonstrated 80–85% incremental flow-through to segment EBITDAR. The triple-net lease rent (VICI) is included because it is the most consequential fixed cost and creates the operating-leverage asymmetry that amplifies all other moves. Table games hold percentage is included as a Mid-rated statistical driver that regularly causes quarterly EPS noise of $50–100M. MGM China main-floor table drop, Macau brand fee, labor costs from union renewals, and MGM Digital losses round out the list. BetMGM (unconsolidated JV) is excluded — it does not appear in consolidated revenue and is not scored as a driver in the upstream tables.

## 4. The Single Highest-Sensitivity Variable

**Las Vegas Strip RevPAR** is the variable that moves consolidated Adjusted EBITDA the most. A 7% move in either direction — matching the FY2024-to-FY2025 observed swing — translates to roughly $200M in Consolidated Adjusted EBITDA, or about $0.53 per diluted share (Inference applying approximate tax and interest bridge). The current direction is deteriorating but showing early signs of stabilization: Las Vegas Strip net revenue grew year-over-year in Q1 2026 for the first time in six consecutive quarters, driven by convention calendar improvement and the return of MGM Grand room inventory after its remodel. However, two forces could reverse this: a US consumer spending pullback (RevPAR is mechanically exposed to visitor volume via the occupancy component, currently at 92%) or a further decline in Canadian and international inbound traffic. The 30–40% drop in Canadian visitors already visible in Q1 2026 table drop data (-3.4% YoY) is partly a RevPAR headwind that is geopolitically driven and not within management's control. For RevPAR to swing to the adverse case ($213, -7% from base), the occupancy rate would need to fall below 88% — a level not seen since 2021 COVID recovery — and/or ADR would need to soften from its current flat trend. Las Vegas Strip RevPAR is the primary external variable that dominates the earnings range.

## 5. Interaction Effects

Las Vegas Strip RevPAR and US consumer spending (the largest external dependency) are mechanically linked — weaker consumer confidence drives lower visitor volumes and lower occupancy, which is exactly how RevPAR has been declining (occupancy from 94% to 92%, not ADR). Table games hold percentage and Las Vegas Strip gaming volume can both compress simultaneously in a consumer downturn: lower visitor counts reduce the drop base that hold is applied against, compounding the revenue hit beyond what either variable alone would suggest. In Macau, the brand fee headwind (variable 6) is partially offset by volume growth (variable 4): if China table drop grows 10%, the incremental EBITDAR largely absorbs the $78M brand fee. However, these two China variables do not offset each other at consolidated level since the brand fee is intercompany-eliminated. The most consequential compounding scenario is a simultaneous US consumer slowdown (hurting LV Strip RevPAR) and a China travel restriction or anti-corruption campaign shock (hurting MGM China drop volume) — the external-dependency module flags this as the single biggest lever because it would hit all three major cash-generating segments concurrently while the $1.87B fixed rent remains non-negotiable.

## 6. Non-Linear Or Asymmetric Risks

Three non-linear or asymmetric effects are material for MGM:

**Operating deleverage on the fixed rent.** The $1.87B triple-net lease rent to VICI escalates regardless of revenue. When revenue grows, the fixed rent base creates operating leverage: each additional dollar of Las Vegas Strip revenue converts at roughly 80–85% to segment EBITDAR (historical flow-through rate, FY2024–2025 reverse implied). When revenue falls, the same mechanism works in reverse with equal severity — a 4% revenue decline in FY2025 produced an 8% EBITDAR decline for the LV Strip segment. There is no variable cost offsets to the rent, which means small revenue declines below break-even (approximately the rate of revenue growth needed to absorb the annual rent escalation) generate disproportionately large EBITDA compression.

**Hold-percentage non-linearity.** Table games hold (the house win-rate) is statistically random around a normalized mean, but it is mechanically amplified by the drop base. If US or Macau gaming volumes fall (table drop contracts) and hold percentage also moves unfavorably in the same quarter — a plausible negative correlation since soft consumer periods can affect player behavior — the combined revenue impact is the product of two negative moves, not the sum. The Q2 2025 quarter (-$20M estimated hold impact) and Q1 2026 China February hold weakness illustrate that both can occur simultaneously.

**MGM Digital Brazil investment overshoot.** Management explicitly flagged on the Q1 2026 call that Brazil investment may exceed original FY2026 guidance. The Digital segment has a narrow cost base ($182.7M Q1 2026 revenue), meaning a $20–30M Brazil cost overshoot would represent a proportionally large hit to segment losses and could push the full-year loss toward the bear case of -$130M rather than the targeted -$45M. This is a small tail risk in absolute terms but non-linear in the sense that management guidance has already been flagged as uncertain.

## 7. Earnings Volatility Score

**Score: 58 / 100** (higher = WORSE — more volatile and sensitive to small input changes)

*This is an inverted score: 58 is in the "Material sensitivity — earnings can swing meaningfully" band (41–60). Lower is better.*

Rationale: MGM's Consolidated Adjusted EBITDA has been remarkably stable in dollar terms ($2,336M → $2,411M → $2,426M over FY2023–FY2025 despite 490 bps of gross margin compression) — but this stability has required Macau volume recovery to offset Las Vegas softness, a dynamic that cannot be expected to persist indefinitely. The fixed-rent structure means any simultaneous demand softness across LV Strip and Macau would compress EBITDA sharply. GAAP EPS is far more volatile ($3.19 → $2.40 → $0.76 over FY2023–FY2025, driven by goodwill impairment, D&A step-up, and buyback arithmetic) — but GAAP EPS is not the company's primary metric. At the EBITDA level, a RevPAR swing of 7% alone would move EBITDA by ~8%, and a concurrent hold percentage move of 1pp adds another ~4%. The score of 58 reflects: (a) meaningful sensitivity of both revenue and EBITDA to RevPAR and gaming hold; (b) the asymmetric fixed-rent structure that amplifies downside; (c) partial mitigation from geographic diversification (Macau partially offsets LV softness) and ADR pricing power (LV ADR flat even as occupancy fell); (d) hedged interest rate exposure (87% fixed-rate debt); and (e) the fact that most drivers are external (consumer cycle, geopolitics, hold variance) rather than company-controlled.

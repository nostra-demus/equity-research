# Earnings Module — MGM (Synthesis)

## Abstract

MGM's dominant earnings trend is decelerating: Consolidated Adjusted EBITDA (the profit measure before interest, taxes, and depreciation) has fallen year-over-year in four of the last five comparable quarters, with the LTM figure 1.7% below FY2024 despite revenue growing 2.8%. The biggest driver of this pressure is the Las Vegas Strip's fixed-cost trap — approximately $1.87 billion in annual rent owed to VICI Properties escalates regardless of revenue, so the 6.5% RevPAR (revenue per available room) decline in FY2025 translated directly into an 8% segment profit decline. The consensus bar has been cut 33% from 18 months ago (FY2026 normalized EPS from $2.90 to $1.95) and sits at "fair" rather than depressed, meaning a beat requires operational improvement rather than a low hurdle. The single biggest near-term risk is that the apparent Las Vegas Strip gaming recovery in April-May 2026 was driven by statistical hold variance rather than genuine visitor volume growth, while Macau June 2026 GGR fell 12.1% year-over-year, removing the segment that has been offsetting Strip weakness all year. The verdict is mixed earnings setup: partial signs of stabilization are real, but the structural cost trajectory and critical red flags prevent any confident inflection call.

## 1A. Module Disconfirmation *(CLAUDE.md §8)*

**Strongest bear point:** EBITDA has fallen year-over-year in four of the last five comparable quarters (Q4 2024: -10.1%, Q1 2025: -6.8%, Q2 2025: -7.1%, Q3 2025: -15.1%, Q1 2026: -8.9%), while the $1.87B fixed rent escalates 1–2% per year regardless of revenue. At current FY2025 Adj EBITDA of $2,426M, the rent consumes approximately 77% of the headline EBITDA figure; if LV Strip RevPAR weakens again, the residual EBITDA above rent collapses faster than the top-line would suggest. [FY2025 10-K, p.4436; Q1 2026 10-Q, p.1293]

**Strongest bull point:** Las Vegas Strip net revenue grew year-over-year in Q1 2026 for the first time in six consecutive quarters, convention room-night mix is rising mid-single digits in 2026, MGM Grand rooms are fully back online (54,000 incremental room-nights vs prior year), the Q1 2026 self-insurance charge ($46M) is at least partly one-time, and the FY2026 consensus EPS bar has been reset 33% lower from 18 months ago — creating the conditions for a modest beat if the Strip holds. [Q1 2026 10-Q, p.146; Q4 2025 earnings call, Feb-05-2026, CFO; Capital IQ 06_Trends.xls]

**Single killer risk specific to earnings quality and beat/miss setup:** The Q2 2026 "beat" scenario rests on two signals that are both of low quality: unverified NGCB gaming win data for April/May (which cannot separate hold effects from volume) and management's assertion that the $46M Q1 self-insurance charge was one-time, while the filing language ("an increasing cost in our business") and the business-model red-flag sweep characterize it as structural. If both signals are wrong simultaneously, Q2 misses and the FY2026 EBITDA consensus ($4,624M) needs a further cut.

**Disconfirming evidence already visible:** (a) Macau June 2026 GGR fell 12.1% year-over-year per two convergent unverified external sources (DICJ via worldcasinodirectory.com and asgam.com, Jul-01-2026) — the last month of FQ2 and the segment that has been the sole EBITDA offset to LV Strip weakness throughout FY2024–FY2025; (b) the $341M MGM Digital goodwill has only 7% fair-value headroom and is flagged as a Critical Audit Matter by Deloitte, with Brazil investment potentially overshooting guidance. [05_beat-miss-setup §3; FY2025 10-K, Critical Audit Matter, p.53–54]

## 3. Reconciliation

**Disagreement 1 — Self-insurance characterization (High severity, unresolved):**
- `03_margin-drivers` and `05_beat-miss-setup` characterize the Q1 2026 $46M self-insurance charge as one-time per CFO Q1 2026 call language ("one-time true-up, not expected to recur").
- `08_earnings-red-flags` and the business-model `12_red-flags-sweep` characterize it as structural and ongoing, citing the CFO's own qualifier ("an increasing cost in our business") and the cumulative reserve-build mechanism.
- **Reconciled view:** The conservative reading favors the structural interpretation. A one-time "true-up" implies a discrete catch-up, but the filing language supports reserve-building as a trend. The synthesis therefore does NOT incorporate a $77M YoY tailwind for Q2 2026 as a high-confidence beat driver; rather, it treats it as uncertain. This is the most consequential unresolved agent conflict for the near-term quarter.

**Disagreement 2 — Beat/miss verdict framing (Low severity, resolved):**
- `05_beat-miss-setup` said "balanced"; `04_guidance-consensus` said "bar is fair / not obviously depressed." These are phrased differently but are substantively aligned — both conclude neither a beat nor a miss is the base case.
- **Reconciled view:** No real disagreement. Both feed into the "Balanced" next-quarter setup verdict.

**Disagreement 3 — CIQ EBITDA vs Company Adj EBITDA definitions (Low severity, resolved):**
- Multiple agents noted that CIQ EBITDA and Company Consolidated Adjusted EBITDA differ in sign and magnitude across periods.
- **Reconciled view:** Company Consolidated Adjusted EBITDA is the primary metric throughout this synthesis, consistent with the company's own disclosure and the approach taken by `01_historical-financials`. CIQ EBITDA is used only where the company's figure is unavailable (FY2021–FY2022).

**Red-flag agent's overall Severity Verdict** per `08_earnings-red-flags` is: **"Critical concerns — critical flags present; earnings setup is unreliable or should be downgraded."** This conflicts with the `05_beat-miss-setup` "Balanced" characterization and the `04_guidance-consensus` "Fair bar" assessment. Both upstream agents were assessing the FQ2 2026 quarter-level setup; the red-flag agent was assessing the overall earnings module quality. The conflict is surfaced explicitly: the quarter-level setup is genuinely balanced on a noise-to-noise basis (hold could go either way; self-insurance timing is uncertain); but the structural earnings trajectory — which the red-flag agent correctly identifies as directionally negative across four of five quarters — makes "Mixed earnings setup" the defensible module-level verdict rather than any inflection category. The final reader should weigh both.

## 5. Earnings Setup Summary

### Revenue Setup

The 2022–2023 post-COVID revenue surge is finished. MGM's revenue grew 1.7% in FY2025 and 4.2% in Q1 2026 year-over-year — the latter entirely driven by Macau volume recovery and MGM Digital growth, not the Las Vegas Strip. The current revenue trajectory is not sustainable as a growth narrative: it depends on Macau's post-COVID recovery continuing at roughly the same pace while the Strip stabilizes, but Macau June 2026 GGR already fell 12.1% year-over-year with World Cup demand diversion as the stated cause. The single factor that would flip revenue direction upward is a sustained Las Vegas leisure recovery — specifically occupancy returning above 93% from its current 92% — driven by either a reversal of the Canadian visitor decline (geopolitically driven and outside management's control) or a US consumer re-engagement with discretionary travel. On the downside, the reported and organic revenue stories are nearly identical: there are no material M&A effects in FY2025 (Northfield Park sale closed April 2026 and removes approximately $400M from Regional from Q2 2026 onwards) and no FX effects (MOP/HKD pegged to USD). The gap between headline and underlying is therefore small, but the Northfield divestiture will create a structural regional revenue gap in FY2026 comparisons that consensus may not fully reflect.

### Margin Setup

The current Consolidated Adjusted EBITDA margin of 13.8% (FY2025) is not a cyclical peak — it has already compressed 20 basis points from FY2024 and 60 basis points from FY2023. But it is also not a trough, because the structural rent escalation (up to 2% per year) means that even if revenue flatlines, EBITDA will compress annually. The segment that would inflict the largest single bite on consolidated margins if it moves adversely by 10–20% is Las Vegas Strip, where revenue declined 4% in FY2025 and EBITDAR fell 8% — an approximately 85% incremental flow-through rate. Management has no contractual pass-through mechanism on costs: rent escalates on a fixed schedule, union contract wages cannot be reduced, and gaming taxes are a fixed percentage of win. The company is a price-taker on its largest fixed costs. The only protection is operating leverage on the upside — every incremental Strip revenue dollar converts at very high rates — but that lever requires revenue growth, which has been absent.

### Quality Check

The single largest gap between reported and economic earnings is the $1.87 billion in annual cash rent paid to VICI that does not appear in Consolidated Adjusted EBITDA. This is fully disclosed and is not a manipulation — but it means the $2,426M FY2025 Adj EBITDA is approximately 77% pre-committed to landlords before capex, debt service, or equity returns. A normalized "EBITDA after rent" figure for FY2025 is approximately $556M, not $2,426M. The FY2025 GAAP net income of $206M is also unrepeatable — a -85.5% effective tax rate driven by a non-recurring valuation allowance release, IRS refund, and Macau tax exemption will not recur in FY2026. The property transaction add-back has appeared every year for three consecutive years (a gain in FY2023, charges in FY2024 and FY2025), so calling it non-recurring systematically overstates Adj EBITDA. For modeling normalized earnings next year, the correct starting point is Consolidated Adjusted EBITDA (not GAAP) because GAAP is too distorted by the FX loss, D&A step-up, and goodwill impairment — but the analyst must then deduct the full annual rent of approximately $1.87–2.3B to arrive at an economically meaningful figure.

### Consensus Bar

For the consensus bar to be beaten materially, Las Vegas Strip gaming revenues would need to sustain the April-May 2026 momentum (Strip win up 6.6% and 13.2% year-over-year), the Q1 2026 self-insurance charges would need to not recur, and Macau volume growth would need to absorb the brand-fee headwind. The bar is most likely set incorrectly on the upside for FY2026 EPS — not because the revenue bar is too high (revenue estimates have actually drifted up $162M over six months) but because the cost trajectory is worse than the consensus embeds: gross margin has fallen 490 basis points since FY2022 on a cost base growing faster than revenue. The clearest anchoring error in current consensus is the assumption that the FY2025 GAAP tax benefit (-85.5%) will normalize without a large reset in FY2026 GAAP EPS. Approximately 50–60% of current FY2026 consensus is anchored to the convention calendar recovery (a near-term event) and the Macau visitor-arrivals tailwind (whose momentum has visibly slowed in June 2026).

## 5b. Leverage & Capital Structure

**Trigger assessment:**
- Trigger A: Net debt / Adjusted EBITDA on a strict basis = $4,167M / $2,426M = **1.7x** (FY2025, strict basis). This is below the 3.0x threshold. Trigger A does NOT fire on the strict basis.
- On the broad basis (including ~$25.1B operating lease liabilities per Capital IQ), total "debt" is approximately $31.4B and broad net debt / EBITDA is approximately **12.1x**. However, this reflects ASC 842 accounting for the VICI lease right-of-use liabilities, not traditional financial debt. The synthesis notes this broad-basis figure because it represents the true economic obligation but does not apply the leverage trigger using it — the trigger compares financial debt leverage consistently.
- Trigger B: Net debt (strict basis) changed from $3,947M (FY2024) to $4,167M (FY2025), a $220M increase year-over-year on a base of $3,947M (+5.6%). Neither the 1.0x ratio increase nor the 50% debt increase nor the 2x net-debt change threshold is triggered.

Neither Trigger A nor Trigger B fires on the strict-basis financial leverage. However, the master synthesizer should be aware that the economically meaningful leverage picture is the lease-adjusted basis where approximately $29.3B in broad net debt (including operating lease liabilities) sits against $2,426M Adj EBITDA — an approximately 12x broad leverage ratio that is structurally embedded and non-reducible unless properties are sold back from VICI. The rent obligation of $1.87B per year is senior to all equity returns, is contractually escalating, and is secured by first lien on operating licenses. This is the most important capital structure fact for the master synthesizer, even though it does not trigger the formal leverage section criteria under strict-basis accounting.

Leverage is within normal range on strict financial debt basis and did not change materially during the period under the standard triggers — no dedicated treatment required under the formal criteria. See Section 8 for the synthesis note on the economic leverage reality.

## 8. Note To The Final Synthesizer

- **Dominant earnings trend:** Decelerating, not inflecting. EBITDA fell year-over-year in four of the last five comparable quarters. The Q1 2026 partial LV Strip improvement is real (first revenue growth in six quarters) but narrow — convention-driven, not leisure-driven, on a "short booking cycle" per the CEO's own words.

- **Red-flag agent's Severity Verdict (mandatory):** **Critical concerns** — "critical flags present; earnings setup is unreliable or should be downgraded." This is the `08_earnings-red-flags` agent's verbatim overall verdict and must not be softened. Three Critical flags: rent exclusion from Adj EBITDA; FY2025 -85.5% tax rate non-recurring; LV Strip RevPAR (primary EBITDA variable) trending below FY2024 level.

- **Whether earnings are clean and cash-backed:** Cash conversion is technically healthy (CFO/EBITDA 104%). But the reported EBITDA of $2,426M is structurally misleading: after deducting the $1.87B annual rent obligation to VICI — which flows through the financing section of the cash flow statement, not the operating section — the actual EBITDA available to equity holders is approximately $556M before capex. This is the most important accounting fact in this module. The rent is not hidden; it is fully disclosed. But an investor using the headline EBITDA multiple without rent-adjusting is making an invalid comparison to any peer that owns its own real estate.

- **Critical flag propagation (required):**
  - *Critical Flag 1 — Rent exclusion from Adj EBITDA:* See Section 1 "Biggest earnings risk" and Section 5 "Quality Check." The headline EBITDA is 77% pre-committed to landlords.
  - *Critical Flag 2 — FY2025 -85.5% tax rate non-recurring:* No forward EPS model should use FY2025 GAAP net income ($206M) or GAAP EPS ($0.76) as a base. Normalized rate is approximately 5–11%. This structurally depresses FY2026 GAAP EPS vs FY2025 even if operations improve.
  - *Critical Flag 3 — LV Strip RevPAR #1 EBITDA variable still below FY2024 level:* RevPAR on an LTM basis ($238 Q1 2026 vs $242 Q1 2025) has not recovered to FY2024's $245 average. One quarter of convention-driven growth does not confirm a trend reversal. See Section 7 for reversal criteria.

- **High flag — self-insurance / one-time vs structural contradiction:** The most consequential unresolved conflict in this module. CFO says one-time; filing says "increasing cost in our business"; business-model red-flag sweep says structural. The $77M implied Q2 2026 tailwind (which drives the "beat" scenarios in `05`) is uncertain. Apply the conservative view — do not model the tailwind as high-confidence. See Section 3, Disagreement 1.

- **High flag — Macau June GGR -12.1% YoY:** Two convergent unverified sources (worldcasinodirectory.com, asgam.com citing DICJ, Jul-01-2026) confirm a World Cup-driven June decline. This is the last month of FQ2 and covers MGM's second-largest segment (25% of revenue). If confirmed in the MGM Q2 print, the segment that has provided the sole offset to LV Strip weakness throughout FY2024–FY2025 will not provide that offset in Q2 2026.

- **High flag — Osaka uncapped completion guarantee:** $2.1B remains unfunded as of March 2026; MGM's stake was diluted to approximately 39% in April 2026 and is pledged as collateral. A 10% adverse JPY move adds $228M to the funding obligation. This is a capital call risk outside EBITDA that the earnings module cannot size precisely but the master synthesizer must carry.

- **High flag — Macau concession terminable unilaterally by Macau government (Dec 2032):** Low near-term probability but catastrophic impact — 25% of consolidated revenue at risk. This caps any long-run EBITDA multiple regardless of near-term recovery.

- **Consensus bar assessment:** Fair, not low. The bar has been reset 33% on EPS from 18 months ago but EPS revisions are still net-negative (-8 down vs +4 up for FY2026 over 3 months). Revenue estimates have crept up while EPS estimates fell — a cost-compression signal that is inconsistent with an EPS beat unless the cost trend reverses sharply.

- **Next-quarter setup and second-quarter look-ahead:** FQ2 2026 setup is balanced; outcome driven by whether Las Vegas Strip gaming win improvement was volume-driven (beat) or hold-driven (noise). FQ3 2026 looks materially weaker: Q3 is MGM's structurally lowest-margin quarter (11.3%–14.2% historically), Macau entered July under World Cup pressure, and US summer leisure demand visibility is limited on a short booking cycle.

- **Top sensitivity variable:** Las Vegas Strip RevPAR (±$200M Adj EBITDA per 7% move). Currently deteriorating on a year-over-two-year basis. Hold percentage is the dominant quarterly noise variable (±$100M per 1pp swing).

- **Partial-data cap:** No full caps applied — all data categories present. Partial caveat: labor sensitivity (variable 5 in `07`) is Low-confidence as payroll is not disclosed; energy sensitivity is unquantified. Earnings volatility confidence is Medium overall.

- **Biggest missing data point:** Maintenance vs growth capex split. Total capex was $1,069M in FY2025 with Osaka now drawing approximately $138M per quarter; recurring FCF may be materially overstated if Osaka capex is a significant fraction of that total.

- **Thesis type classification:** Sector-cycle / Macro-conditional. Strip RevPAR, Macau GGR, Canadian visitor flows, and gaming hold are all external variables outside management's control. This is primarily a bet on the US consumer, global gaming conditions, and geopolitical stability — not a company-specific earnings story.

- **What would change the earnings verdict:** See Section 7. Sustained LV Strip RevPAR recovery to FY2024 levels over two or more consecutive quarters, confirmed by occupancy lifting toward 94%, would be the clearest upgrade trigger.

## 9. Simple Summary

- Revenue is barely growing (1.7% FY2025; +4.2% Q1 2026 driven by China and Digital, not the Strip) — the post-COVID surge is over
- Margins are compressing at every level: gross margin down 490bps since FY2022; EBITDA margin down 60bps in two years; the $1.87B fixed rent is the structural weight that prevents recovery
- Earnings are cash-backed in terms of CFO conversion (104% FY2025) but the headline EBITDA overstates economic earnings by roughly 3–4x because the landlord's $1.87B cash claim sits above equity holders
- The consensus bar is fair (reset 33% over 18 months) but not obviously beatable — EPS revisions are still running net-negative, and Q2 has the Macau June GGR headwind in its final month
- Q2 2026 setup is balanced: Strip gaming win improved April-May but on an easy hold-impacted comparable; Macau June fell 12.1% year-over-year; outcome depends on which effect dominates
- Las Vegas Strip RevPAR is the single biggest earnings variable; at ±7% it moves EBITDA by approximately $200M; current direction is below FY2024 levels and the recovery is convention-driven (fragile)
- Earnings volatility is material (58/100 inverted score) driven by hold variance, the fixed-rent amplifier, and macro/geopolitical exposures that management cannot control
- This module is useful for the master synthesizer: it provides a clear signal (mixed, leaning decelerating on structural cost trajectory), three Critical red flags, and a well-evidenced sensitivity framework — but any valuation work must rent-adjust the EBITDA before applying a multiple

# Maturity Wall & Refinancing — EMAR

*Emaar Properties PJSC (DFM: EMAAR) — Dubai / UAE real-estate developer. Reporting standard **IFRS**; reporting currency **AED millions**; fiscal year ends **31 December**. The dirham is pegged to the US dollar at **AED 3.6725/USD**, so the group's USD sukuk (Islamic bonds — ~64% of debt) carry effectively **no dirham FX risk**. Debt totals are **Q1 2026 (31 Mar 2026)**; the instrument-by-instrument maturity detail is the **FY2025 audited as-reported** disclosure (Q1 discloses totals only) — the latest instrument-level breakdown available, and it ties to the audited notes. Filings are in English — no translation gap.*

*Plain-English note (first use): **maturity wall** = how much debt must be repaid or refinanced in each year; **weighted-average maturity (WAM)** = the average number of years until the debt comes due, weighted by size; **coupon** = the interest/profit rate the company pays; **basis point (bps)** = one hundredth of a percent; **refi step-up** = how much more it would cost to replace old debt with new debt at today's rates; **fixed vs floating** = whether the rate is locked or moves with a benchmark.*

*Data completeness: a full year-by-year, instrument-level maturity schedule IS disclosed (FY2025 audited Notes 24–25 + CIQ Capital Structure Details). The partial-data rule (build from short-term/long-term split only, cap confidence to Low) does **not** apply here — the schedule is complete and reconciles to gross debt.*

## 5. Refinancing Read

The wall is a single 10-year sukuk — **AED 2,752.6m (Sukuk 3, 3.64%) due 15 September 2026, ~26% of gross debt** — and after it clears the remaining book (Sukuk 4 2029, Sukuk 5 2031) is laddered at a ~3.6-year WAM. Refinancing that sukuk at today's rates would cost roughly **+165 bps** (from ~3.64% to ~5.2–5.5% on a new 5Y USD sukuk), i.e. ~AED 45m/yr more — trivial against 52x interest cover and more than offset by higher yields on the group's ~AED 35bn cash. The single biggest refinancing risk is not funding but **timing/optics** — a bunched near-term IFRS current portion (~AED 5.3bn, half of it rolling EM revolvers and term-loan amortisation) can *look* heavy — yet it is covered many times over by unrestricted cash.

**Market-closure test (12 months, no new unsecured issuance).** Emaar **survives comfortably.** The only material bullet in the window, Sukuk 3 (AED 2,752.6m, 15 Sep 2026), is covered **4.4x by unrestricted free cash on hand (AED 12,179.5m)** before touching the ~AED 3,669m undrawn committed revolver, the ~AED 22.5bn short-term-investment pool, or any operating cash flow — so **no new issuance is required to clear the next 12 months.** *Labeled assumption:* the small EGP/PKR revolvers (~AED 1.0bn) are assumed to keep rolling at the subsidiary level; even if they did not, that incremental amount is still covered several times over by free cash. This is the evidence-based read, not an assertion.

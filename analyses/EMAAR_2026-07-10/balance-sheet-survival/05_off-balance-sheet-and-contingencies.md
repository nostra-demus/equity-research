# Off-Balance-Sheet & Contingencies — EMAR

*Emaar Properties PJSC (DFM: EMAAR) — Dubai / UAE real-estate developer. Reporting standard **IFRS**; reporting currency **AED (UAE dirham)**. The Guarantees & Contingencies (Note 30) and Commitments (Note 31) figures are the **FY2025 audited** note (as at 31 Dec 2025) — the latest full commitments-and-contingencies disclosure; balance-sheet items (pension, equity, leases) are shown at **Q1-2026 (31 Mar 2026)** where noted. All amounts in **AED millions** unless stated. Filings are in English — no translation gap.*

*Plain-English note (first use): a **guarantee** is a promise to pay or perform if someone else fails; a **contingent liability** is a possible obligation that only becomes a real cash bill if a specific event happens (a lawsuit lost, a project not delivered); a **commitment** is money the company has contractually agreed to spend (buy land, pay a builder) but has not yet paid; **RERA escrow** = customer home-purchase payments locked in regulated bank accounts, releasable only against construction milestones; **recognized** = already sitting on the balance sheet as a liability; **maximum exposure** = the worst-case gross amount if the item fully crystallized.*

**Reporting currency: AED millions (IFRS).** Cross-module note: this agent reads `01_capital-structure-and-leverage.md` (leases, pension, RERA flagged there) and `business-model/11_capital-allocation-governance.md` (litigation / related-party context). Both were available and are reconciled below.

**Reads.**
- **Leases are not an incremental item.** IFRS 16 already capitalises Emaar's leases; the AED 778.0m lease liability sits inside 01's gross debt, so it is flagged here only to avoid double-counting — there is **no** hidden operating-lease pool to add (contrast US GAAP, which keeps operating leases off the debt line).
- **The AED 35.5bn commitment book is the single largest off-balance-sheet number, but it is capital spending, not a contingent loss.** It is the value of signed contracts to buy land and build, net of what has been invoiced [FY2025 AR, Note 31]. It grew from AED 25.0bn (FY2024) to AED 35.5bn (FY2025) — a rising forward capital call as Emaar launches its AED 200bn masterplan pipeline [business-model/11, capex row]. It is funded by customer presale advances (AED 43.3bn sits in RERA escrow earmarked to these projects) and operating cash flow, not by new borrowing — so it is a use of the company's own cash, not a debt-like claim that a third party can call.
- **Pension/OPEB is genuinely recognized** (already on the balance sheet at AED 210.7m) and small — 01 kept it out of gross debt; it is shown here for completeness. Its maximum exposure is close to its recognized value because it is a measured actuarial provision, not an open-ended contingency.
- **Not a debt-like item (flagged to prevent misreading):** the group's large **unearned-revenue** liability is customer presale money settled by delivering finished homes, not by paying cash — self-liquidating operating float, already handled in 01 §2, excluded here.

| Group's share of associate / JV contingent liabilities | Nil | AED 130.7 (2024: 120.2) | Associates & joint ventures | FY2025 AR, Note 16 |
| **Total guarantees + JV contingent liabilities** | **Nil recorded** | **AED 9,451.1** | | FY2025 AR, Notes 30 & 16 |

**Reads.**
- **The RERA guarantee (AED 9.0bn) is 95% of the guarantee stack and is the largest genuine contingent item — but it is structurally backed.** Under Dubai's escrow law, a developer guarantees project delivery to RERA; the guarantee would turn into a cash outflow only if Emaar failed to complete presold projects. Those same projects hold **AED 43.3bn of customer money in RERA escrow** (01 §3) and a forward revenue book, so the guarantee is collateralised by the very cash and inventory it relates to. It crystallizes as a loss only in a systemic failure-to-deliver scenario, not in the normal course. It grew ~18% YoY (7,615 → 9,008) as the project pipeline expanded — track it against the pipeline, not as free-standing risk.
- **Everything else is small:** financial guarantees/LCs fell by more than half (472.2 → 203.6); India performance guarantees are AED 103.2m and are already cash-collateralised (banks hold AED 679.9m of Emaar deposits under lien — the "deposits under lien" 01 §3 flagged as restricted).
- **None of the guarantees carries a recorded provision** — the company treats them as performance/contingent obligations, not probable cash losses.

**Reads.**
- **Two active matters, both Emaar-India, both unprovided.** The largest single claimed number is the **DDA counterclaim of AED 593m**; against it Emaar is itself claiming AED 581m, so the net downside to the group is roughly the AED 593m counterclaim plus the AED 75m guarantee already invoked. The EHTPL/TSIIC matter puts ~AED 190m of Indian JV assets under a dealing-restraint. Business-model/11 frames the legacy Emaar-India litigation at a peak exposure of ~AED 0.6bn, consistent with the DDA counterclaim being the binding number.
- **Live, not dormant:** both carry current-year procedural movement (NCLAT ruling, Supreme Court appeal, arbitration underway) — they are distinguished here from the ordinary-course contractor claims the company itself deems remote.
- **No recorded provision** is disclosed against either active matter (management asserts the claims are not justifiable) — so if either crystallized it would hit earnings unprovided. The amounts are small relative to equity (see §4).

**Reconciliation to 01 and business-model/11:** the RERA AED 9.0bn, JV contingent-liability AED 130.7m, the ~AED 35bn commitment book, and the ~AED 0.6bn Emaar-India litigation all tie to the figures 01 §2 and business-model/11 flagged. **Disclosure caveat (not a language gap — §27):** Emaar elects the **IAS 24 government-related-entity exemption** and does **not** itemise the bulk of its financing, land-purchase and construction dealings with the Dubai-Government / ICD ecosystem [FY2025 AR, Note 33; business-model/11]. That is a related-party-disclosure limitation (a governance item carried by business-model/11), not a gap in the contingencies note itself — the Guarantees & Contingencies note (Note 30) is specific and quantified. Because the C&C disclosure is detailed and Emaar is net cash (not a highly-levered or systemically-litigious name), the partial-data solvency cap for "thin contingencies disclosure" is **not** triggered. The residual point to watch is that undisclosed related-party guarantees to the state ecosystem cannot be fully ruled out under the IAS 24 election.

## 5. Contingency Read

The largest off-balance-sheet number is the **AED 35.5bn project-commitment book** (land + construction contracts), but that is contractual capital spending funded by AED 43.3bn of customer escrow and presales — a use of the company's own cash, not a third-party claim; the largest **genuine contingent exposure** is the **AED 9.0bn RERA performance guarantee**, which is live but structurally backed by the same escrow and presold inventory, so it becomes a cash loss only in a systemic failure-to-deliver scenario. **Active litigation is real but small:** two legacy Emaar-India matters (DDA counterclaim AED 593m; TSIIC/EHTPL restraint over ~AED 190m of JV assets), both unprovided, together under 1% of equity. Even the full AED 10.2bn contingent-loss stack (ex-commitments) is **9.9% of equity** and is covered 2.4x by the group's AED 25bn broad net cash — none of the disclosed off-balance-sheet or contingent items threatens solvency if it crystallized.

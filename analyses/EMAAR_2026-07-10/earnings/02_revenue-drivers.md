# Revenue Drivers — EMAR

*Emaar Properties PJSC (DFM: EMAAR). Dubai/UAE developer; IFRS; currency AED millions unless stated (AED pegged to USD at ~3.6725). Fiscal year ends 31 December. Latest reported period: Q1 2026 (quarter ended 31 Mar 2026). This agent identifies what MOVES revenue, not what revenue was — the levels come from `01_historical-financials`.*

**Upstream / limitations.** `01_historical-financials` present and used. Business-model `03_segment-map` and `10_external-dependency` present and used. **No verbatim earnings-call transcript in the pool** — management driver colour comes from two FAB Securities "Earnings Call Insight" notes (4Q25, 3Q25), used as a **verdict-stripped, unverified sell-side proxy** (Rating/Target/vs-our-estimate stripped per MODULE_RULES); every driver **number** is traced to a primary document (company press release / annual report / CIQ). Quarterly data is available, so QoQ/seasonality is analysed (not "Not available").

Plain-English glossary (first use): **off-plan** = home sold before/during construction, paid in instalments; **POC (percentage-of-completion)** = revenue booked in proportion to construction cost incurred to date ÷ total estimated cost; **backlog** = value of signed sales not yet recognised as revenue; **pre-sales / bookings** = new sales contracts signed in the period (a leading indicator, not yet IFRS revenue); **ASP** = average selling price; **ADR** = average daily hotel room rate; **GLA** = gross leasable area; **pp** = percentage points; **bps** = basis points (100 bps = 1.0%).

## 2. Revenue Driver Tree

| Business Type | Revenue Formula |
|---|---|
| Multi-segment developer + landlord | Sum of segment revenue drivers |
| Real Estate (~80%) — build-to-sell developer | Σ(off-plan units sold × price × % construction completed in period) — recognised over time by POC |
| Leasing/Retail (~15%) — landlord | Leasable area × occupancy × rent per sq m + turnover rent + service charges |
| Hospitality (~5%) | (rooms × occupancy × room rate) + attraction tickets + F&B + hotel-management fees |

**Company-specific one-line formula:**
Reported revenue ≈ **opening sold backlog converted by construction progress (POC) on the ~80% development engine** + **recurring mall rent and hotel income on the ~20% annuity.** The swing factor is the development line, and its revenue this year is *bookings made 1–4 years ago* being recognised as buildings rise — so **reported revenue lags demand.** The true leading indicators are **contracted pre-sales** and the **revenue backlog**, not current-quarter recognised revenue [FY2025 AR, Auditor's Report — Key Audit Matter, revenue recognition; Q1 2026 press release, 11 May 2026].

**This is organic demand, not acquired or FX-driven growth.** FY2025 +39.6% and Q1-26 +23% were UAE development revenue recognised on previously-sold, contracted backlog — no material acquisition or currency contribution (Section 6).

**Cycle-position read (Hard Rule — cyclical/policy-exposed business).** The latest reported period sits **at or near a Dubai property-cycle peak on the leading indicators, with a lagging revenue cushion underneath:**
- *Bookings and demand are at a record (peak signal).* FY2025 was Dubai's strongest year on record; Emaar's property sales hit an all-time high AED 80.4bn; group return on invested capital (ROIC — profit earned per AED of capital) rose to ~13.7% LTM from ~4.7% in FY2021, a near-decade high [ciq_facts multi_year_trajectory]. These are **not a normalised run-rate**; the base rate for Dubai property is boom-bust (2009; the 2015–2019 downturn) [10_external-dependency].
- *Momentum is already decelerating.* Backlog growth slowed from +39% YoY (Dec-25) to +29% (Mar-26); pre-sales growth cooled to +16%; the UAE core grew +9% [Q4 PR; Q1 PR]. Consensus long-term growth is −14.8% and out-year estimates imply an earnings **peak ~2029, then decline** [04_guidance-consensus].
- *But reported revenue has a 3–4 year cushion.* The AED 163.4bn contracted backlog (~3.3× annual revenue; ~94% of the under-construction pipeline already sold) means recognised revenue can keep rising for ~3–4 years even if new sales roll over [Q1 PR; FAB 3Q25 proxy]. So the **near-term revenue driver (backlog conversion) is far more stable than the medium-term driver (new demand).**
- *One-time items.* There is **no one-time policy/subsidy tailwind inflating revenue** — the new UAE Corporate Tax + 15% Domestic Minimum Top-up Tax (DMTT), and the Q3-25 MD120 tax relief, are **profit-line** items, not revenue [10_external-dependency; FAB 3Q25 proxy]. The non-run-rate element is the **record-cycle demand backdrop itself**, which sets bookings and therefore future revenue.

This agrees with `10_external-dependency` (score 63/100, "mostly externally driven") — no divergence.

## 7. The Single Biggest Revenue Driver

**Real Estate backlog conversion — the pace at which the AED 163.4bn contracted, sold backlog is recognised as revenue via percentage-of-completion (construction/delivery progress on already-sold units).** This is the single biggest lever because Real Estate is ~80% of revenue and its revenue is mechanically the conversion of sold backlog as buildings rise: a 10–20% change in the pace of Real Estate recognition moves total revenue ~8–16%, more than any other driver. Its **current direction is Improving** — backlog is at a record AED 163.4bn (+29% YoY), ~94% of the under-construction pipeline is already sold, and unit deliveries are set to "ramp materially" through 2026–28 [Q1 PR; FAB 4Q25 proxy]. Because the backlog is contracted and covers ~3–4 years of revenue, the **next-12-month revenue is highly visible and biased up**, largely insulated from a demand slowdown.

The one caveat that separates near-term from medium-term: backlog conversion is fed upstream by **new Dubai off-plan pre-sales**, which are at a cyclical record and already decelerating (backlog growth +39%→+29% YoY; consensus long-term growth −14.8%). So the biggest driver of the *next year* (backlog conversion) is safe and rising; the biggest risk to the *medium term* (2027+) is whether Dubai demand keeps replenishing the backlog once today's record cohort is delivered — the classic developer split between a visible, contracted near term and a cycle-exposed out-year.

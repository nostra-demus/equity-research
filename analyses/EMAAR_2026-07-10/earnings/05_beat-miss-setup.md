## 1. Next Quarter Context

Emaar gives **no formal numeric guidance**, so the bar is set entirely by Street consensus, which 04 judges **Low**: FQ2 2026 revenue **~AED 12,007m** and EPS-normalized **~AED 0.47**, both cut hard over three months, then stabilised and ticked back up in the last 30 days [04 §4, §7].

## 6. Seasonality Read

Seasonality is a mild headwind to the print's optics but not to the clean year-on-year comparison. Per the seasonality table in 01 §5, Q2 averages ~21.2% of annual revenue and was FY2025's softest quarter (19.6%, AED 9,736m — below Q1-25's AED 10,098m). So Q2-26 will likely print roughly flat-to-lower QoQ versus Q1-26's AED 12,398m, which is the normal seasonal shape, not deterioration. The clean read is YoY: consensus AED 12,007m is ~+23% over Q2-25, coverable by backlog conversion plus the 2026–28 delivery ramp [02 §4]. The sharper point: Q2 is the one quarter in the last four where BOTH revenue and EPS missed (Q2-25: −4.6% / −5.0%) [04 §6], so seasonality and the year-ago precedent put the top-line (and even EPS) miss risk at its seasonal high in exactly this quarter — partly offset because the bar has since been cut ~9–16% [04 §4].

## 7. Historical Pattern

Emaar has a real, positive, but narrowing beat pattern. It beat in 4 of the last 5 fiscal years (only FY2022 missed) and 3 of the last 4 quarters, and the beats land on the profit line — Q1-26 cleared EPS +9.6% / EBITDA +16% despite a revenue miss [04 §6; ciq_facts surprise_history]. Two features temper how hard the synthesizer should lean on it: (i) revenue is lumpy and misses recur in soft quarters (Q1-26 −2.4%, Q2-25 −4.6%), so a single-quarter top-line miss is live even in a good year; and (ii) the annual EPS beat magnitude has shrunk each year (+30% → +22% → +15% → +11%) as analysts catch up [04 §6]. Weight it as a moderate positive for a profit-line beat — discounted for the shrinking magnitude and the specific Q2 revenue-miss precedent — not as a high-conviction lock.

## 8. Setup Verdict

**Setup favors beat** — on the profit line specifically. The single most important factor: the bar was cut hard and has stopped falling (FY2026 EPS-normalized reset 2.32→1.95, and the near-term FQ2 / FY26 revisions turned back UP in the last 30 days [04 §4]), while Emaar's beats reliably land on EPS/EBITDA — it just beat EPS +9.6% in Q1-26 on a revenue miss, powered by margin plus ~AED 2bn of net finance income [04 §6; 03 §5] — so against a low, stabilised bar and a record contracted backlog, the profit line is the most likely place to clear again. The single biggest risk that could flip it: a single-quarter revenue miss on POC timing in the seasonally soft Q2 — the exact quarter that missed BOTH lines a year ago (Q2-25: revenue −4.6%, EPS −5.0%) — especially if paired with decelerating pre-sales that turn even a clean profit beat into a "good print, bad signal" reaction.

## 9. Second-Quarter Look-Ahead

FQ3 2026 (reported ~Nov 2026) looks slightly harder on the profit line. FQ3 is a seasonally heavier quarter (~25% of revenue and rising) and the 2026–28 delivery ramp keeps supporting revenue recognition [01 §5; 02 §4] — but the year-ago Q3-25 base was flattered by the one-off MD120 tax reversal that drove a +23–26% beat, so the FQ3-26 profit comparison laps an inflated base and the EPS beat is harder to repeat at the same magnitude [04 §6]. Net: the near-term (Q2) profit-beat setup is cleaner than the quarter after it, where a tougher tax / one-off comp raises the bar even as revenue stays supported.

## 10. Pre-Mortem

If this setup fails, the most likely reason is that we over-weighted the mechanically-visible, backlog-driven current-quarter P&L (the easy part to get right) and under-weighted that Emaar's stock and forward estimates move on the leading indicators — property sales and backlog growth, both decelerating (+16% sales; backlog +39%→+29%) [02 §3; 04 §4]. A clean profit beat could still meet a sell-off if Q2 pre-sales / backlog growth steps down further, making our "beat" right on the print but wrong on what mattered. The second most likely miss: a POC-timing revenue shortfall in the soft Q2 that we dismissed as timing noise but that lands alongside the profit line for a rare double miss, as in Q2-25.

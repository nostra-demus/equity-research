# Valuation Module Memo — EMAR

*Emaar Properties PJSC (Dubai; DFM: EMAAR). Reports under IFRS in AED (UAE dirham), which is pegged to the US dollar at 3.6725, so currency risk is negligible. Memo date: 2026-07-10. This condenses the valuation synthesis (`99_valuation-synthesis.md`); it adds no new numbers, evidence, or analysis, and carries the synthesis's verdict, scores, and caps unchanged.*

**Verdict: Modestly undervalued** — base-case fair value (what one share is really worth) is AED 15.00, which is +23.0% above the AED 12.20 price and a +18.7% margin of safety (the cushion of price below worth). But the discount is held at "Modestly undervalued" — not "Materially undervalued" — because a value-indifferent government owner can keep the price cheap indefinitely (filter RF-OWN-004). This is a masked cheap asset and a value trap at the same time. [synthesis §1]

## 2. What This Module Found

Emaar screens modestly below a defensible fair value: base-case worth is AED 15.00 against a pool-verified AED 12.20 price (US$3.32, last close 2026-06-28, 12 days stale) — about +23% upside, an +18.7% margin of safety. [synthesis §1, price/capital `01`]

The single most important driver is **sum-of-the-parts** (SOTP — valuing each business separately, then adding them up). A wholly-owned Dubai mall business throws off steady rent — an annuity, a recurring income stream — worth about AED 76.8bn of enterprise value (EV, the whole business priced as equity plus debt, less cash). That is roughly 79% of Emaar's entire current EV, from just 22% of its profit, hidden inside a cyclical property developer. Malls plus net cash (cash on hand exceeds all debt) of ~AED 25bn come to ~AED 102bn — close to the whole ~AED 107.8bn market value — so the market pays almost nothing for the #1 Dubai developer, which earns 72% of profit. [SOTP `06`]

At AED 12.20 the market is pricing a collapse. Working backward from the price (a reverse-DCF), it implies free cash flow — the cash the business throws off after spending to run and grow — shrinking about 13.4%/yr for a decade, at a through-cycle profit margin (the average across good and bad years) near 21%. That is below Emaar's own worst recent trough (FY2021 operating margin 23.5%) and below its clean peer Aldar (27.2%), and pays just 64% of the intrinsic DCF value. A AED 163.4bn sold order backlog (homes already sold but not yet booked as revenue — 94% pre-sold, 3.3× revenue, converting over 3–4 years) says that collapse is not achievable, so on fundamentals the gap is upside. [reverse-DCF `05`, synthesis §5]

The single most important risk is the **government-owner value trap**. The controller is the value-indifferent Government of Dubai (Dubai Holding group, 29.73% stake), which has no incentive to unlock the hidden mall value or lift the payout — so the cheapness can persist for years (dead money, not a loss). Downside is a ~20% fall to a real Dubai cyclical trough at AED 9.75, floored near AED 6–8/share by net cash plus the wholly-owned mall annuity before any developer value. [synthesis §1, §5]

## 3. The Specialists, Briefly

One line per specialist the synthesis rolled up (name → its main finding). [synthesis §2]

- **Data triage (`00`)** — "Sufficient": all five methods can run; the one binding cap (RF-OWN-004) is carried in from governance; price pool-verified.
- **Price & capital structure (`01`)** — Price AED 12.20, pool-verified; net cash on every basis. The cash-basis choice moves EV by ~AED 22.9bn (~19%): broad EV AED 96,657m (canonical) vs strict EV AED 119,511m; AED 43,338m of RERA escrow (buyer money held in trust) excluded.
- **Multiples vs own history (`02`)** — Roughly fair on its own history (~AED 12.5, assuming no re-rating). The 0th-percentile "discount" is peak earnings on an up-cycle-only window under a government owner — a value trap, not a margin of safety; its reversion table is illustrative-only (zero-weighted).
- **Relative to peers (`03`)** — Modestly cheap vs the one clean comp (Aldar) on peak metrics, roughly fair through-cycle; base ~AED 16.9. The forward-P/E gap to Aldar is only −10%.
- **Intrinsic DCF (`04`)** — Base intrinsic ~AED 18 (grid AED 15–22). The market EV roughly equals the value of the next 10 years' cash flow alone, assigning ~zero to everything after 2035; terminal value is only 36% of EV.
- **Reverse-DCF (`05`)** — What's priced in is conservative/undemanding: free cash flow −13.4%/yr for a decade, ~21% through-cycle margin, paying 64% of the `04` intrinsic value.
- **Sum-of-the-parts (`06`)** — Parts worth more than the whole: base AED 16.77 after a 20% holdco/state-owner discount (band AED 11.0–23.8); the mall annuity alone is ~79% of current EV.
- **Scenario & fair value (`07`)** — "Modestly undervalued": base AED 15.00, bull AED 21 / bear AED 9.75; margin of safety +18.7%, downside-to-bear +20.1%.

**Main disagreement (from Reconciliation):** the methods span more than 40% — base points run AED 12.5 (`02`) to AED 18 (`04`), a 44% spread; the full range is ~AED 9.6 to ~AED 23.8. It comes down to one question: value Emaar on its **assets** (SOTP + DCF → ~AED 17–18) or on **normalized through-cycle earnings at a fair multiple** (→ ~AED 11–12.6)? The synthesis resolves it by trusting SOTP most (the right method for a holding-company-like hybrid, and the only one that already prices the government-owner discount), with the DCF corroborating; it discounts `02`'s reversion table and `03`'s peak-earnings headline. The base was pulled to AED 15.00 — about 9% below the AED 16.52 mechanical blend — because two inputs carry no owner discount and sit on peak metrics. This is carried as-is, not re-adjudicated. ## 4. What Would Change This Read

Upgrade and downgrade triggers, carried from the synthesis's "what would change the verdict" table. [synthesis §6]

**Could make it cheaper (→ Materially undervalued):**
- Credible evidence the government owner will crystallise the masked mall value — a mall spin-off, a REIT, a higher payout, or minority-friendly related-party terms.
- The Dubai up-cycle extending past FY2028 with developer operating (EBIT) margin holding ~37%.
- A clean second UAE comp confirming an Aldar-parity fair multiple.

**Could make it more expensive (→ Fairly / Modestly overvalued):**
- A genuine Dubai downturn (developer earnings −~35% off peak, per the boom-bust base rate) troughing toward the AED 9.75 bear.
- The ~21% priced-in margin proving structural — cheap-land advantage gone, the new 15% tax a one-way ratchet.
- Mall cap rates (the yield property investors demand; higher rate = lower value) widening as rates and supply rise.

**Data that would settle it:** governance evidence on capital-return intent and IAS 24 related-party quantification (the accounting rule governing disclosure of deals with the controlling owner); a ~3-year peer-multiple history; and a through-cycle Dubai developer-margin series to test the terminal margin.

## 5. Bottom Line

- **Verdict: Modestly undervalued** — base fair value AED 15.00 vs AED 12.20, +18.7% margin of safety, held at this level (not higher) by the government-owner value trap. [synthesis §1]
- **Biggest reason it could be better than it looks:** a wholly-owned Dubai mall annuity (~79% of enterprise value) plus ~AED 25bn net cash roughly equal the whole market value — and the price bakes in a ~13%/yr free-cash-flow runoff that the AED 163.4bn, 94%-sold backlog contradicts. [SOTP `06`, reverse-DCF `05`]
- **Biggest reason it could be worse:** the Government-of-Dubai owner (29.73%) has no incentive to unlock the hidden value, so the stock can stay cheap for years (dead money); developer earnings also sit at a Dubai-cycle peak that consensus already marks down ~15%. [synthesis §1]
- **What evidence is missing:** primary-source evidence on how the state owner treats minority holders — its capital-return intent and the size of the IAS 24-hidden related-party channel. This is the swing between "masked annuity" and "perennial value trap." [synthesis §7]
- **One thing to watch next:** any credible signal the owner will crystallise the mall value (spin / REIT / higher payout). A ~8% dividend yield pays holders to wait. [synthesis §5, §6]

## 6. Plain-English Glossary

Terms as they first appear in this memo:

- **Fair value / base-case fair value** — what one share is really worth; the "base" is the central estimate between bull and bear.
- **Margin of safety** — how far the price sits below fair value; the cushion if you are wrong.
- **Sum-of-the-parts (SOTP)** — value each business separately, then add them up.
- **Annuity** — a steady, recurring income stream (here, mall rent).
- **Enterprise value (EV)** — the whole business priced as equity plus debt, minus cash.
- **Net cash** — cash on hand is larger than all debt.
- **Reverse-DCF** — work backward from today's price to see what growth and margins the market is assuming.
- **Free cash flow** — the cash a business throws off after spending to run and grow itself.
- **Through-cycle margin** — the profit margin averaged over good and bad years, not just today's.
- **Operating (EBIT) margin** — operating profit as a share of sales.
- **Sold backlog** — homes already sold but not yet delivered or booked as revenue.
- **DCF / intrinsic value** — add up expected future cash flows in today's money.
- **Terminal value** — the slice of a DCF's value that comes from all years beyond the explicit forecast.
- **EV/EBITDA** — enterprise value against pre-tax operating cash earnings.
- **P/E** — price against earnings per share; forward uses next year's estimate, trailing uses the last year's.
- **P/BV** — price against book (accounting) net worth.
- **Cap rate** — the yield a property investor demands; a higher cap rate means a lower property value.
- **Dividend yield** — the annual dividend as a percent of the share price.
- **Value trap** — a stock that looks cheap but stays cheap because nothing forces the price toward its worth.
- **RERA escrow** — buyer deposits held in a regulated trust account, not free company cash.
- **IAS 24 related-party** — the accounting rule requiring disclosure of transactions with the controlling owner and affiliates.
- **NCI (non-controlling interest)** — the share of a subsidiary owned by outside minority holders, not by Emaar.

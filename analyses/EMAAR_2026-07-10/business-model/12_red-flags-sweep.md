## 3. Most Severe New Flag

**Minority-interest leakage (severity 52).** It is the largest genuinely-uncovered item and the one that most directly distorts the numbers synthesis and valuation will use. Roughly **one in every five dirhams of consolidated profit — AED 4.7bn of AED 22.3bn — belongs to outside shareholders, not to Emaar Properties owners**, and because the minority-held businesses (the separately-listed Emaar Development and Emaar Misr) run at higher returns than the wholly-owned assets, the leakage (12% of equity but 21% of profit) is concentrated in exactly the cyclical developer earnings that are already peak-flattered. The fix is simple once flagged — value the parent on its ~79% share of EBITDA and earnings, deduct the minority claim in any EV build, and do not read consolidated EV/EBITDA of 4.0x as the multiple on *owned* cash flows — but if the synthesizer carries consolidated figures unadjusted it will overstate what the equity actually owns by about a fifth. It is fully disclosed and not deteriorating, so it is a valuation-accuracy flag, not a governance red flag — but it must not be averaged away.

## 4. Cross-Cutting Patterns

Individually minor, three items rhyme into one pattern: **maximum capital being committed into new Dubai supply at the top of the cycle, of which the owner keeps only ~79%.** (1) An **AED 200bn single-district masterplan** (4.5m sqm GFA) was announced Jun 2026, on top of a guided ~AED 65bn 5-year capex and AED 120bn of gross-development-value land bought in 2025 at market prices — pro-cyclical deployment (2) into a record-supply market (648 launches, 167,000+ units) where consensus already models **−14.8% long-term growth**, (3) while ~21% of the resulting (peak-flattered) profit leaks to minorities. The second, ownership-side thread — government control just re-shuffled (ICD → Emirates Power / Dubai Holding), most government-ecosystem related-party dealings left unquantified under IAS 24, a founder-MD running a live competitor, and a fresh CFO change — is largely owned by capital-allocation, but the **new Eagle Hills competition and the NCI leakage plug into it**: the minority holder is asked to fund a pro-cyclical land-and-build cycle, keep four-fifths of a peaked return, and trust an owner and a founder whose competing interests are only partly visible. None of this is a disqualifier; together it argues for anchoring returns to mid-cycle and adjusting headline economics to the parent's real ownership before any rating.

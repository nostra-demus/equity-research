**Absolute FY2025 figures (AED'000), Note 3:** Revenue — Real Estate 39,550,371; Leasing/Retail 7,681,261; Hospitality 2,325,631; Total **49,557,263**. Segment result (profit before tax, before impairment and before unallocated items) — Real Estate 19,751,867; Leasing/Retail 5,320,151; Hospitality 868,821; Others 446,511; subtotal **26,387,350**. Below that subtotal sit unallocated selling/general/admin costs of (1,313,953) and unallocated net finance income of 583,037, giving group profit before tax of **25,656,434** [FY2025 Annual Report (IFRS), Note 3 — Segment Information, p.187–188].

*Profit-share basis:* each segment's result ÷ the AED 26,387,350 sum of the four segment results (i.e. before the unallocated corporate items). Using group PBT (AED 25,656,434) as the denominator instead moves Real Estate to 77.0% — the ranking does not change.

*Margin note (why "flattered"):* the Real Estate segment result includes AED 2,769,841 thousand of finance income (interest earned on its large cash and receivables) against only AED 864,912 thousand of finance costs [Note 3, p.188]. Strip finance income back out and the Real Estate operating margin is ~45%, not ~50%. Downstream `unit-economics` should not read the headline ~50% as pure development margin.

## 2. Dominant Segment

**Real Estate is the dominant segment, and it is not close** — 79.8% of FY2025 revenue and 74.9% of segment profit (AED 39,550m of 49,557m revenue; AED 19,752m of 26,387m segment result) [Note 3, p.187–188]. It leads on revenue, profit, and assets (80.1% of segment assets), so profit and revenue agree on the winner. The most recent quarter confirms the mix holds: Q1 2026 Real Estate revenue was AED 9,803m of AED 12,398m total (79.1%) [CIQ Financials→Segments, quarterly, Q1 Mar-31-2026].

This is **not** a single-segment business (Real Estate is below the 85% line), and the second segment matters for a specific reason: **Leasing, Retail & Related is the highest-margin, most stable engine** — a ~69% segment margin on recurring mall rent versus Real Estate's cyclical ~50% (~45% ex-finance-income). So Emaar is really two businesses bolted together: a cyclical Dubai off-plan **developer** that drives the profit today, sitting on top of a recurring mall-and-hotel **landlord** (Leasing 15.5% + Hospitality 4.7% of revenue) that carries the quality and cushions the downside. The developer sets the direction of the numbers; the landlord underwrites them.

## 3. Segment Disclosure Quality

Disclosure here is good and granular. The three reportable segments plus "Others" are defined identically in FY2025 and the FY2024 comparative in the same note, and the CIQ standardized history shows the same three-segment structure running back to FY2020 — **consistent year-over-year, no redefinition in the last three years** [FY2025 Annual Report, Note 3, p.187–188; CIQ Financials→Segments, annual FY2020–FY2025]. The **"Others" bucket is small and not a hiding place** — it carries no segment revenue (its income sits in other operating income), 1.7% of segment profit, and 2.4% of segment assets, all well under the 10% level that would worry me. **Profit is disclosed at segment level**, not just revenue: the note gives each segment's result (PBT before impairment), plus capex, depreciation, finance costs, finance income, assets and liabilities — more than many issuers provide. Two caveats for downstream users: (1) segment "result" is struck at a pre-tax, post-finance level, and the Real Estate line is materially lifted by ~AED 2.77bn of finance income (see §1 note) — it is not a clean operating-profit (EBIT) margin; and (2) unallocated corporate SG&A (AED 1,314m) and unallocated net finance income (AED 583m) sit outside the segments, so segment results do not sum to group PBT without them. One labeling mismatch to be aware of, not a defect: management's narrative and ESG sections name more units — "UAE Development, Emaar Malls, Emaar Hospitality, Leisure, Commercial Leasing, and Entertainment" — than the three audited reportable segments they roll up into [FY2025 Annual Report, Reporting Scope and Boundary section]. Geographic disclosure also exists (UAE 93% / International 7% of FY2025 revenue) but that is `customer-geography`'s call, not analyzed here.

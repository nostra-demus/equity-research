## What This Module Found

Emaar master-plans Dubai land into branded communities and sells homes off-plan — buyers pay in instalments during construction, before the home exists. That build-to-sell business is ~80% of FY2025 revenue (AED 39.6bn of AED 49.6bn); the rest is mall rent and hotels.

The edge is the funding model, not just the brand. Buyers forward-fund construction through regulated escrow — a controlled account their money sits in until building milestones are met (~AED 44bn held) — so the customer finances Emaar's working capital (the day-to-day cash a business normally ties up). Add ~AED 25bn net cash (cash and liquid investments above total borrowings, on the data-vendor's broad basis) and a ~AED 163bn contracted backlog (signed sales not yet booked as revenue — about four years' worth), and this is a business that can keep building through a downturn without outside finance. The downside is about *returns*, not survival.

The single most important driver is one variable: Dubai residential demand (price × volume × how fast off-plan units sell). It sets ~80% of revenue, and 93% of all revenue is UAE — up from 86% two years ago [`05_customer-geography.md`; `10_external-dependency.md`].

The single most important risk is that this concentration meets a cycle peak. Through-cycle return on capital (ROIC — the profit earned on each dirham invested), normalized for the new 15% UAE minimum tax, is ~7.5–9.5% against a ~11–12% cost of capital (what that money costs to raise) — it clears the hurdle only at the cyclical top (13.4% normalized over the last twelve months) [`09_moat.md`]. The "peak" read is already visible in the numbers: group gross margin (revenue left after the direct cost of building and selling) fell from 63% in FY2023 to 55% in FY2025; 4Q25 gross margin was down 251 basis points (2.51 percentage points) year on year on mix; new land is now bought at market prices; and consensus already prices −14.8% long-term growth into a record Dubai supply pipeline (648 launches, 167,000+ units) [`ciq_facts.json`; `07_business-quality.md`; `10_external-dependency.md`].

## What Would Change This Read

The synthesis says deeper work pays off only with a strong view on the Dubai cycle's timing. The observable events that would move the read:

**Toward "avoid" / worse:**
- Dubai pre-sales momentum rolling over — bookings and developer margin turn first, while the backlog holds *reported* revenue up for a while.
- A large, richly priced asset bought from the government parent, funded with fresh share issuance (dilution).
- A visible swing from net cash toward funding state projects.

**Toward "higher-quality" / better:**
- Through-cycle ROIC holding above the cost of capital even as the cheap-land advantage narrows.
- A rising share of recurring (mall and hotel) income.

Governance note: today the government related-party channel is not visibly extractive — it is the thing that would change the classification if it deteriorated.

## Bottom Line

- **Verdict:** Cyclical business — worth deeper work only with a strong timing edge. No disqualifier tripped, no §24 filter tripped, verdict not locked.
- **Could be better than it looks:** the funding model is downturn-resistant — buyer escrow (~AED 44bn), ~AED 163bn backlog (~4 years of revenue) and ~AED 25bn net cash mean it can build through a downturn without outside finance; the brand and Downtown-Dubai location out-earn listed peer Aldar by ~18 operating-margin points. Downside is about returns, not solvency.
- **Could be worse than it looks:** through-cycle ROIC ~7.5–9.5% is at or below the ~11–12% cost of capital, so most of today's headline return is the cycle near its top, not a durable spread. The killer risk is a Dubai downturn — a ~20% adverse move in Dubai residential demand hits ~80% of revenue and 93% of the geography at once and stops backlog replenishment.
- **Missing evidence:** a clean annual count of homes sold and their average selling price (would split price from volume and read the cycle turn directly); secondary — cap rate / WALE / FFO for the recurring segment, and the absent ownership/insider export.
- **Watch next:** Dubai pre-sales momentum — bookings and developer margin turn before reported revenue does.

## Plain-English Glossary

Terms in order of first appearance; plain meaning only, no new numbers.
- **Net cash:** cash and liquid investments exceed total borrowings (shown here on the vendor's broad basis, which nets in short-term investments — not the strict debt-minus-cash figure).
- **Backlog:** signed sales not yet recognized as revenue.
- **Off-plan:** selling a home before it is built; buyers pay in instalments during construction.
- **Escrow:** a regulated account the buyer's money is held in until construction milestones are met.
- **Working capital:** the day-to-day cash a business ties up to operate; here buyers pre-fund it.
- **Return on capital (ROIC):** the profit earned on each dirham of capital invested.
- **Cost of capital:** what the money used in the business costs to raise.
- **Gross margin:** revenue left after the direct cost of building and selling, as a percent of revenue.
- **Basis points:** hundredths of a percentage point (251 bps = 2.51 points).
- **Contribution margin:** revenue left after the variable costs of one unit.
- **EBIT / operating margin:** operating profit as a percent of revenue.
- **EV/EBITDA:** enterprise value (equity plus debt, less cash) divided by earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortization — a whole-company price on operating cash earnings.
- **Minority-interest (NCI) leakage:** the part of a group's profit that belongs to outside shareholders of part-owned subsidiaries, not to the parent.
- **EPS:** earnings per share.
- **Dilution:** issuing new shares, which shrinks each existing share's slice.
- **Cap rate / WALE / FFO:** standard measures for valuing rental property (yield, average lease length, and recurring property cash flow) — named here as missing for the recurring segment.
- **IAS 24:** the accounting rule on related-party disclosure; its government-related exemption lets Emaar leave those deals unquantified.

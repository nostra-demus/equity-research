This is a judgment-weighted aggregate, not a straight average (the simple mean of the 11 rows is ~55). **Cyclicality carries the heaviest weight (~30%)** because it defines what Emaar is — a leveraged bet on one city's property cycle, currently at a peak — and it drags hardest (27). **Margin stability and competitive intensity are the secondary drags (~20% combined).** Against them, a quality floor of ~50% weight — pricing power (62), low disruption risk (72), net-cash-driven capital intensity (64), commodity independence (62), and good disclosure (68) — keeps the score out of the Weak band. **Band anchor (fix F43):** the second-lowest row is margin stability at 44, so the aggregate is capped at ~64; 53 sits comfortably below that ceiling and above the 27 cyclicality floor, reconstructable from the rows.

## 4. Read

Emaar is a **high-quality but deeply cyclical single-city real-estate developer at a cyclical peak**, wrapped around a smaller recurring mall-and-hotel annuity (~20% of revenue) that carries the quality and cushions the downside. The assets, the Downtown-Dubai brand, the net-cash balance sheet (Total Debt/EBITDA 0.40x), and the capital-light off-plan model (buyers pre-fund construction through escrow) are all genuine strengths — but they sit on top of one city's property cycle, so the business classifies as **cyclical, not durable compounder.** Crucially, this is a **sector/property-cycle bet, not a technology-disruption bet**: the industry rate-of-change row scored 72 (well above 40), so §24 Filter 5 does **not** trip — the danger is the cycle turning, not the model being disrupted, and RF-BQ-005 is **not** emitted.

The headline returns are at a cyclical high and must be anchored, not extrapolated: **LTM ROIC of 13.7% (the profit earned on each AED 100 of capital) is a peak** — the FY2021–FY2025 span ran 4.7% → 5.7% → 8.5% → 9.8% → 12.5%, so a **through-cycle ROIC is nearer high-single-digits (~8–10%)**, and today's ~55% gross margin / ~49% EBITDA margin are similarly flattered by the wide spread between historically cheap legacy land and current selling prices — a spread that narrows as Emaar buys new land at market prices (AED 120bn gross development value acquired in 2025). The strong current pricing power (row 62) does **not** override the low cyclicality row (27); the two reconcile as "premium pricing power *while the Dubai cycle is up*."

**The single factor a buyer should watch over the next 24 months: Dubai pre-sales momentum and the developer margin.** New bookings (property sales, AED 80bn in FY2025) are the true leading indicator — they turn *before* IFRS revenue, which the AED 155bn backlog carries for ~4 years. If Dubai sales cool, bookings and margin roll first while reported revenue stays elevated on the backlog; that gap is where the peak-cycle risk shows up.

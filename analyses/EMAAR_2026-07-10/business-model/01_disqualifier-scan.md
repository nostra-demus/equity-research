## 2. Triggered Disqualifiers — Detail

No disqualifier triggered.

**Soft signals (no verdict-lock; routed to the named modules, not discarded):**

- **Government / unaligned-owner risk (§24 filter 6).** The Dubai government controls Emaar via Dubai Holding / ICD (group ~29.73%; single largest holder 22.27%). The May-2026 transfer of ICD's 22.27% stake to Emirates Power Investment LLC for AED 23.9bn [Key Developments 2026-05-11] is a move between government-related vehicles, not a third-party change of control. A controller whose objective may not be long-term minority per-share value is a structural cap, not a discount to arbitrage — route to management-governance and valuation.
- **RP transparency (IAS 24 exemption).** Because the Group does not quantify transactions with other Dubai-government / ICD-controlled entities, the disclosed RP figure (0.35% of revenue, 1.2% of expenses) is a partial view. This is a disclosed, standard IFRS election for a state-controlled issuer — not a language/opacity issue and not a threshold breach — but it lowers RP disclosure completeness. Route to management-governance (disclosure quality).
- **India Enforcement Directorate contingency.** Disclosed, sub-threshold (AED 190m attached assets, ~0.2% of Group assets), management deems non-material [FY2025 AR, Note 30]. Route to red-flags-sweep / balance-sheet-survival as a tracked contingency.
- **Group Head of Finance change.** Hesham Heikal departed 20 May 2026; Pawan Chindalia appointed as successor [Key Developments 2026-05-22] — a routine change with a named replacement, not a resignation red-flag pattern. Route to management-governance track-record.

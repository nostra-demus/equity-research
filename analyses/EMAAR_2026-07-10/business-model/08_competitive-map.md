# Competitive Map — EMAR

*Emaar Properties PJSC (DFM:EMAAR). UAE / Dubai issuer, IFRS, reporting currency AED; the dirham is pegged to the US dollar at ~3.67 AED/USD. Both required upstreams are present — `02_business-identity.md` and `03_segment-map.md` — and are inherited below. Peer economics are captured for the downstream `moat` agent's "earns more than peers" check; peer margins that are not publicly disclosed are marked as such and never invented.*

## 1. Dominant Segment

**Real Estate — the build-and-sell (off-plan) developer engine: 79.8% of FY2025 revenue and 74.9% of segment profit, concentrated in Dubai residential** [inherited from `03_segment-map.md`; FY2025 Annual Report (IFRS), Note 3, p.187–188]. "Off-plan" means homes sold before/during construction and paid in instalments. Competitors below are chosen for this segment — Dubai (and wider UAE) residential development — not for the smaller mall-and-hotel annuity.

## 2. Named Competitors

Three most-credible rivals in the dominant segment: the two largest direct Dubai off-plan developers after Emaar (DAMAC, Sobha), plus the one listed UAE developer peer that carries disclosed economics (Aldar) so the moat has a clean peer-margin anchor.

### Competitor A — DAMAC Properties

- **Ticker / listing:** Private since 2022 (formerly DFM:DAMAC; taken private by founder Hussain Sajwani's Maple Invest for ~AED 2.19bn) [Web: DAMAC Properties / Wikipedia, 2026 — unverified]. Its DIFC debt issuer, Damac Real Estate Development Ltd, is rated by S&P.
- **Where they compete:** Dubai off-plan residential — ultra-luxury branded residences (Versace, Cavalli tie-ups); same forward-funded, instalment-sale model as Emaar; expanding into Iraq/Saudi/international.
- **Scale:** AED 36bn property sales in 2025 (a record; the largest of Dubai's *private* developers, ~45% of Emaar's AED 80.4bn) [Web: Gulf News, 9-Feb-2026; Time Homes Real Estate ranking, 2026 — unverified]. 2024 revenue is reported inconsistently across web sources (~AED 13.1bn vs ~AED 18.7bn); the higher figure is paired with ~AED 4.8bn net profit [Web: apilproperties / Owler, 2026 — unverified].
- **Profitability / return on capital:** Not publicly disclosed in audited detail (private). Web-reported ~AED 4.8bn net profit on ~AED 18.7bn 2024 revenue implies a ~26% net margin — **unverified**, figures conflict, treat as directional only [Web, 2026 — unverified]. For the moat: DAMAC is a partial anchor at best; ROIC/ROE not disclosed.
- **Source named in:** Industry press consistently ranks DAMAC the clear #2 Dubai developer behind Emaar [Web: Gulf News; Time Homes; Oliva "Emaar vs DAMAC", 2026]. Not named in Emaar's own filings; not in the CIQ comp set (private).
- **One-line read:** The direct #2 in Dubai by sales — branded-luxury off-plan, roughly half Emaar's booking volume, and the sharpest private rival for the same buyer.

### Competitor B — Sobha Realty

- **Ticker / listing:** Private (UAE operating arm of the Sobha group; holding company PNC Investments rated Moody's Ba3→upgraded Ba2, with a USD 500m sukuk — Islamic bond) [Web: Moody's via Sobha Realty press, 2024–2025 — unverified]. Not the separately-listed Indian Sobha Ltd (BOM:532784).
- **Where they compete:** Dubai off-plan residential — vertically-integrated luxury communities (Sobha Hartland, Siniya Island); 11 UAE masterplans; same off-plan instalment model.
- **Scale:** AED 23bn sales in 2024 (+~50% YoY); ~AED 30bn in 2025 on stated targets (AED 20bn Dubai + AED 10bn Siniya Island) [Web: Sobha Realty press; Construction Week, 2024–2026 — unverified].
- **Profitability / return on capital:** Not publicly disclosed (private); revenue and net margin not broken out. Only the sukuk rating (speculative-grade Ba2) and sales are public. ROIC/ROE **not disclosed** — not a usable peer margin anchor.
- **Source named in:** Industry press ranks Sobha the #3 Dubai developer and puts its 2024 share at "~10% of Dubai's real estate market" [Web: Sobha Realty press; royalpproperties / miva rankings, 2025–2026 — unverified]. Not named in Emaar's filings; not in the CIQ comp set.
- **One-line read:** A pure Dubai off-plan luxury rival at ~10% share — smaller than DAMAC by sales, but the same buyer and the same forward-funded model.

### Competitor C — Aldar Properties PJSC

- **Ticker / listing:** ADX:ALDAR (Abu Dhabi Securities Exchange) — the largest listed UAE developer, so the only rival here with audited, comparable economics.
- **Where they compete:** UAE residential development, historically Abu Dhabi-centric, now expanding into Dubai; a larger recurring / asset-management and construction mix alongside build-to-sell.
- **Scale:** FY2025 revenue AED 33.8bn (+47% YoY); group sales/bookings AED 40.6bn (+21%), of which UAE AED 35.5bn [Web: Aldar FY2025 results via Zawya / The National / Khaleej Times, 9-Feb-2026]. About two-thirds of Emaar's AED 49.6bn revenue and roughly half Emaar's AED 80.4bn bookings.
- **Profitability / return on capital:** FY2025 EBITDA AED 11.2bn (~33% margin — cash-profit before interest, tax, depreciation) and net profit after tax AED 8.8bn (+36%), a ~26% net margin [Web: Aldar FY2025 results, 9-Feb-2026]. CIQ comp cross-check (LTM): gross 34.9%, EBITDA 28.9%, **operating (EBIT) margin 27.2%, net income margin 23.0%** [CIQ "Company Comparable Analysis" → Operating Statistics, as-of 2026-06-28]. **ROE/ROIC not disclosed in available sources** (not carried in the CIQ comp export; no clean equity figure sourced) — the profit-margin anchor stands in for it. **Peer anchor for the moat:** Aldar earns an operating margin of ~27% and net margin of ~23–26%, versus Emaar's ~45% EBIT / ~36% net margin and ~13% ROIC — Emaar out-earns its one listed peer by a wide gap [Emaar: CIQ Operating Statistics; ciq_facts.json `multi_year_trajectory` ROIC 12.5% FY2025 → 13.7% LTM].
- **Source named in:** In the pool CIQ comp set (the sole other UAE developer among 11 names) and named in Emaar's own FY2025 Annual Report — as a co-participant in the "Unity for Change" retail-sustainability initiative and as counterparty to a Dubai Holding–Aldar mixed-use JV [FY2025 Annual Report, ESG / Board sections; CIQ "Company Comparable Analysis" → Business Description].
- **One-line read:** The listed UAE peer and the moat's clean margin benchmark — comparable revenue scale, but development-plus-asset-management economics that run at roughly half Emaar's operating margin.

## 3. Competitive Position

**Holding — Emaar is the clear #1 in Dubai residential development and stays there by a wide margin, though fast-growing private rivals keep the gap from widening.** Developer-level market share is not officially published, so booking (property-sales) value is the proxy: Emaar sold AED 80.4bn in FY2025 (+16% YoY), roughly double #2 DAMAC's AED 36bn; over Jan–Aug 2025 Emaar's ~AED 51bn was again ~2x DAMAC's ~AED 24bn [Q4 2025 press release, 12-Feb-2026; Web: Time Homes / Gulf News rankings, 2026 — unverified]. Supporting proxies point the same way: a revenue backlog (sold, not yet recognised) of AED 163.4bn at 31-Mar-2026 and 90–100% absorption on FY2025 launches [Q1 2026 press release; Q4 2025 earnings call summary]. The reason this is "holding" and not clearly "gaining": DAMAC (record AED 36bn) and Sobha (+~50% in 2024) grew faster in percentage terms off smaller bases, so Emaar's ~2x lead is stable rather than expanding.

## 4. Competitive Shape

**Consolidated-but-competitive, led by one dominant player — a "leader-plus-cluster" oligopoly of branded developers sitting above a state-owned master-developer bloc.** A handful of large names supply most new Dubai off-plan stock: by 2025 sales value, Emaar AED 80.4bn, DAMAC ~36, Sobha ~30, Binghatti ~26, with state-owned Nakheel (~AED 13bn Jan–Aug) and Meraas (~AED 10bn Jan–Aug) — both now consolidated under Dubai Holding Real Estate — behind them [Q4 2025 press release; Web: Time Homes / royalpproperties rankings, 2025–2026 — unverified]. Sobha alone put its 2024 share at ~10%, which frames the order of magnitude: no single firm monopolises, but the top ~6 branded developers dominate new supply. A formal concentration index (HHI) and official per-developer shares are not published — the Dubai total of 270,000+ transactions worth AED 892bn in 2025 mixes resale and all segments, a different base from developer primary sales, so it is not a clean share denominator [Q4 2025 earnings call summary]. One structural twist: the state-owned bloc is both rival and partner — Emaar's UAE Development and Rove Hotels run as joint ventures with Meraas (co-opetition) [FY2025 Annual Report, Note / segment and hospitality sections].

## 5. Caveat

Two limits on this map, both disclosed rather than papered over:
1. **Emaar's own filings do not name competitors in a rivalry sense.** Aldar, Meraas and Nakheel appear only as JV partners, an ESG co-participant, or in director biographies — not in a "our competitors are…" disclosure. Competitor identification therefore leans on the in-pool CIQ comp set (Aldar) plus industry press (DAMAC, Sobha, Binghatti).
2. **The two largest *direct* Dubai rivals (DAMAC, Sobha) are private, so their margins and returns are not audited-public.** DAMAC's revenue/profit figures conflict across web sources and are unverified; Sobha discloses only sales and a sukuk rating. Only Aldar carries a clean, sourced peer margin — so the moat's per-peer profitability comparison rests mainly on Aldar, with DAMAC/Sobha as directional at best. **What would resolve it:** DAMAC's DIFC sukuk-issuer accounts (Damac Real Estate Development Ltd) and Sobha/PNC Investments sukuk disclosures for audited peer margins and returns; Dubai Land Department developer-level sales data for hard market shares.

# Capital-Allocation Scorecard — EMAR

*Emaar Properties PJSC (DFM: EMAAR). Jurisdiction: UAE / Dubai Financial Market. Reporting standard: IFRS. Reporting currency: AED (dirham, pegged to USD at ~3.6725). Fiscal year ends 31 December. Window: FY2021–FY2025 (5 audited years) + LTM to 31 Mar 2026. Multi-year history is available — this scorecard is NOT single-period, so no partial-data cap applies. Annual reports are filed in English; no translation gap.*

*Scope: this agent grades how management spent shareholders' money over 3–5 years — M&A, buybacks, dividends, capex, debt. Ownership/insider alignment (Filter 6, RF-OWN-004), compensation, and full related-party scoring belong to agents 03/04/05/06 and are referenced only as context. This module runs BEFORE valuation, so buybacks are NOT judged against intrinsic value.*

**Plain-English glossary (first use):** CFO = cash generated by operations; FCF = free cash flow = CFO − capital spending; ROIC = return on invested capital, the profit earned on each AED 100 of capital put to work; incremental ROIC = the return on the *new* capital added over a period; NOPAT = operating profit after tax; NCI = non-controlling interest (the minority holders of a listed subsidiary such as Emaar Development); RPT = related-party transaction; net cash = cash and liquid investments minus total borrowings.

**Read of the mix.** Over five years Emaar generated **AED 107.3bn of operating cash** and used it in a strikingly conservative pattern: it returned **AED 25.2bn as dividends (23.5%)**, paid down **AED 9.3bn of net debt (8.7%)**, spent only **AED 8.0bn on capex plus development land (7.5% combined)** — because an off-plan developer funds growth through customer pre-payments (unearned revenue rose ~AED 8bn/yr), not through fixed-asset capex — did **zero buybacks**, and spent almost nothing net on outside M&A (**0.9%** of CFO in cash). The single largest destination of cash was the balance sheet itself: **~AED 65.7bn (61%) of five-year CFO was retained as cash and term deposits/securities** (AED 46.9bn cash build + AED 18.8bn into financial assets). ## 4. Dividends & Organic Reinvestment

**Correction to the upstream read (primary-source, §4/§5).** Agents 01 and business-model/11 state the dividend was "held flat at AED 1.00/share across FY2023–FY2025." The audited **FY2025 AR Note 32** and **FY2024 AR Note 32** show otherwise: the parent dividend was **AED 0.50/share for FY2023** (50% of share capital, AED 4,419,395k) and **AED 1.00/share for FY2024 and FY2025** (100% of share capital, AED 8,838,790k each). The cash-flow line reconciles exactly — FY2024 cash dividends of AED 7,289,411k = parent AED 4,419,395k (FY2023 dividend) + subsidiary/NCI AED 2,870,016k. So the dividend **grew** (AED 0.25 FY22 → 0.50 FY23 → 1.00 FY24 → 1.00 FY25), which is a stronger, not weaker, distribution story than "flat" — but the flat-at-AED-1.00 claim is a misread and is corrected here.

**Read.** The dividend is **growing, deeply covered, and funded by real cash** — parent payout ~50% of FY2025 earnings, covered ~3x by free cash flow and ~2x even on normalised operating cash, on a net-cash balance sheet. It is sustainable (RF-CAP-003 "dividends not covered by FCF" is NOT triggered — Green). Two caveats: (1) a structural **AED 2–3bn/yr leaks to the non-controlling holders of listed subsidiaries** (Emaar Development, Emaar Misr) before it reaches Emaar Properties shareholders; and (2) coverage is flattered by presale advances — on a mid-cycle basis (consensus models an earnings peak ~2029, then a −14.8% long-term decline) the deep cover would compress, though a flat AED 1.00 dividend would still be affordable. **Organic reinvestment is where the per-share value was actually created:** ROIC rose from 4.7% to 13.7%, and because growth is funded by customer advances (negative working capital) rather than fresh equity/debt, the return on *incremental* capital is very high (~45%+ derived) and comfortably above the cost of capital — but that is heavily cycle-aided (Dubai upcycle + legacy cheap land + a FY2025 tax one-off), so credit it as cycle-strong reinvestment, not proof of through-cycle skill.

## 5. Capital-Allocation Read

Over FY2021–FY2025 Emaar turned AED 107bn of operating cash into a **net-cash balance sheet, a dividend that quadrupled per share (AED 0.25→1.00) and is covered ~3x by free cash flow, and organic development returns that lifted ROIC from 4.7% to 13.7%** — capital was allocated to per-share value, and every classic value-destruction trap was avoided (no debt-funded acquisition spree, no buybacks at high prices, no uncovered dividend, no runaway capex). The **best use** was the combination of deleveraging to net cash and a growing, deeply-covered dividend, funded by high-return, customer-advance-financed development; the **worst use** was the **AED 7.5bn Dubai Creek Harbour purchase from the controlling owner (Dubai Holding), part-paid in newly-issued Emaar shares to that same owner with no arm's-length valuation shown to minorities**, sitting alongside ~AED 3.6bn of legacy overseas-expansion write-offs (FY21–23) that are the visible cost of the pre-2013 empire-building era. The **single biggest capital-allocation signal is a governance one, not a deployment failure**: the related-party capital-allocation channel that IAS 24 leaves largely unquantified under government control — today it is not extractive (distributions are pro-rata, returns are rising, the balance sheet is net cash), but it is the lever that would flip this from disciplined to value-destructive if a future asset were bought richly from the parent and funded by dilution. Two efficiency caveats keep it out of the top bucket: the record is heavily **cycle-aided** (consensus models an earnings peak ~2029), and **~61% of five-year operating cash now sits as cash and low-yield financial assets** — strategic optionality for the ~AED 65bn forward capex pipeline and the AED 200bn masterplan, but a drag on blended returns.

**Conclusion: value-creative / disciplined on the capital actually deployed — with material governance caveats (related-party asset-injection channel + government control) and a cycle-aided record.**

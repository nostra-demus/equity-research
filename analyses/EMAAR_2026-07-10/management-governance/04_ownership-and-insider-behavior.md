**Unaligned-owner test (CLAUDE.md §24, Filter 6) — RF-OWN-004 FIRED.** Emaar's controller is the **Government of Dubai** (via Dubai Holding group, 29.73%). This is structure (a) of the filter — government control — where the state pursues multiple non-per-share objectives (city masterplanning, strategic projects, directed capital into national developments). The evidence traces to pool filings: the FY2025 Annual Report names "two government-related entities collectively hold ~29.8%" and the May-2026 block transfer is a move *within* the state's own vehicles. Concrete alignment frictions already visible: (1) the IAS 24 exemption makes the largest related-party channel unquantifiable to a minority holder by construction; (2) the founder-MD chairs a **competing** developer; (3) board seats are held by Dubai Holding officers. This is a **structural cap, not a discount to arbitrage** — I lower the Control-structure-fairness component and apply the **shareholder-friendliness cap (max 55)** and **governance-risk floor (55)** per MODULE_RULES to the synthesis. **Value-trap note handed to valuation:** persistent cheapness under a government owner (EV/EBITDA ~4.0x, 0th percentile of its own 16-quarter range per `ciq_facts.json`) is not, on its own, a margin of safety — a state controller can keep a name cheap and keep it cheap; price the misalignment, do not assume it re-rates. Mitigants worth stating plainly: single share class (one-share-one-vote), dividends flow **pro-rata** to all holders (AED 1.00/share held FY2023–25, ~AED 8.84bn), net-cash balance sheet, and no *visible* extraction to date — the channel is a watch item, not a proven leak.

## 5. Ownership / Insider Read

Skin in the game is **near zero and static**: the people running Emaar own **0.03%** of it, the founder-MD's 2,700,500 shares are legacy 1997 equity (not bought with cash, and the LTIP pays in cash-settled Phantom Shares that deliver no real stock and no dilution), and **directors executed no trades in FY2025** — a neutral signal, neither conviction-buying nor red-flag selling. The dominant fact is **who holds the votes**: the **Government of Dubai (Dubai Holding group, 29.73%)** controls the company, the founder-MD chairs a **competing** developer, and the IAS 24 exemption keeps the state-ecosystem related-party channel unquantified — a structural minority-alignment risk (**RF-OWN-004**, shareholder-friendliness capped at 55, value-trap note to valuation). The offsetting comforts are real but do not remove the cap: a single one-share-one-vote class, **no share pledging**, ~70% free float with credible global institutions (Vanguard, BlackRock, Baillie Gifford), stable institutional breadth, and pro-rata dividends — so the biggest risk to a minority holder is not theft on today's evidence, it is a controller whose objective may not be per-share value.

**True (not just legal) independence — the core issue.** By UAE rules the board is majority-independent, but the "independence" is from *management*, not from the *controlling shareholder*. So the directors who are "independent of management" are not independent of the **government owner** — the board is structurally aligned with the controller, not with minorities. **Read on leakage.** On the **disclosed** figures, related-party dealing is small and pointed the safe way: RP income ~0.35% of revenue, RP expenses ~0.76% of revenue (~1.2% of total expenses), receivables *from* related parties trivial (AED 17.5m), payables *to* JVs declining, and key-management pay flat. No loans/guarantees to related parties, no royalty/brand fee to the government owner. `business-model/01_disqualifier-scan` tested the hard RPT bar and found **N** (0.35% of revenue / 1.2% of expenses — far below the 25% disqualifier). **The problem is what is *not* quantified.** Under the **IAS 24 government-related-entity exemption**, Emaar "has elected not to disclose" its transactions with the wider ICD/Dubai-government ecosystem — explicitly **"financing and operational (power, utilities, land purchases, infrastructure services, construction services etc.)"** — stated only as "commercial terms." These are the economically *large* flows, and Emaar's entire model rests on acquiring cheap Dubai land, much of it from the government sphere. So a minority holder cannot see whether the "cheap land" edge is an arm's-length purchase or a controller subsidy that could be repriced. This is a **disclosed, standard, legal election for a state-controlled issuer — not opacity in the language/§27 sense and not a threshold breach** — but it removes visibility over the single biggest potential value-transfer channel. Routed to agent 06 (disclosure completeness); it caps the group-leakage-protection sub-score but does **not** fire a hard RPT red flag on an unquantified amount (conservative default, §4).

**Founder-MD conflict (self-dealing channel).** MD Alabbar chairs **Eagle Hills**, a directly competing developer, while **chairing Emaar's Investment Committee** (which reviews Emaar's new investments, feasibility and financing — 9 meetings in 2025, the most active committee). The AR requires conflict disclosure and recusal, but **no separation protocol for Emaar↔Eagle Hills deal-flow is disclosed** to minorities. No actual Emaar↔Eagle Hills transaction is in the pool, so this is flagged as an **Amber** conflict channel (High materiality, unresolved), routed to agents 04/06 — not a fired RF on current evidence.

## 5. Minority-Shareholder Protection Read

On paper the board ticks the UAE boxes — 55.6% independent, an independent-chaired audit committee (2/3 independent, 100% attendance, reviewing every related-party deal), an independent-chaired nomination & remuneration committee, one-share-one-vote with statutory cumulative voting, no poison pill, no staggered board, no dual-class, and no share dilution — so it is **not** a case of classic management entrenchment. But box-ticking is the wrong test here: **the board is not independent of the party that actually controls Emaar.** The Government of Dubai controls the company (~29.73% via Dubai Holding/ICD, with Note 33 confirming "significant influence"), and every nominally "independent" director is a serving Dubai-government official or an executive of a government-controlled entity, with the controller's own Group CIO seated at the table — a board built to serve the owner, not to check it for minorities. The single biggest self-dealing/leakage risk is the **IAS 24 exemption that leaves the largest related-party flows — land bought from, and utilities/construction/financing paid to, the government ecosystem — entirely unquantified**, compounded by a founder-MD who chairs a directly competing developer (Eagle Hills) while chairing Emaar's most active committee (Investment). Per CLAUDE.md §24 Filter 6 (RF-OWN-004, structurally unaligned government owner — formally owned by agent 04), a board of the controller's own appointees cannot offset an owner whose objective is Dubai's development rather than per-share value; minority protection is therefore **weak / entrenched** — entrenchment of the *controller*, not of management. **Conclusion: weak / entrenched.**

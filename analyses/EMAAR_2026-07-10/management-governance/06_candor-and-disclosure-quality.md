# Candor & Disclosure Quality — EMAR

*Emaar Properties PJSC (DFM: EMAAR). Jurisdiction: UAE / Dubai Financial Market. Reporting standard: IFRS. Currency: AED (dirham, pegged to USD at ~3.6725). Fiscal year ends 31 December. Latest audited year FY2025; latest quarter Q1 2026 (released 11 May 2026). Annual reports are filed in English — no translation gap, and no home-language disclosure is treated as opacity (CLAUDE.md §27).*

**Partial-data rule applied up front.** The pool holds **no verbatim earnings-call transcript and no shareholder letter.** The two "Earnings Call Summary" files are **FAB Securities sell-side notes** ("Earnings Call Insight" 3Q25 / 4Q25, carrying Rating BUY and a Target Price) — an analyst's paraphrase of the call, not management's own words under questioning. A sell-side note **and** a company press release both fail the verbatim test, so **tone under questioning, Q&A-evasiveness, and owning-the-miss live are Not assessable** from them. Those components are marked **Insufficient Data** and the Disclosure-Candor score is **capped at 65**. What *is* assessable, and used here: the audited annual reports (FY2023–FY2025 MD&A and notes — primary), the company's own earnings **press releases** (primary written communications, Q3'25 / Q4'25 / Q1'26), and the `earnings/06_earnings-quality` cross-check on non-GAAP aggressiveness (present — no cross-check gap).

The checkable operational promises (sales, backlog, dividend, deliveries) were **mostly kept** [01_management-and-track-record.md]. The genuine soft spots and how the *written* record handled them:

The pattern: **the audited/mandatory layer discloses the hard facts; the voluntary promotional layer does not foreground them.** How management handled misses *verbally, under analyst questioning,* cannot be judged — no verbatim transcript.

## 2. Non-GAAP / Adjustment Aggressiveness

This is Emaar's strongest candor positive. The cross-check from `earnings/06` is unambiguous: **no adjusted EPS, no adjusted net income, no restructuring or stock-comp add-backs** — the company reports straight audited IFRS EPS.

| Signal | Detail | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Recurring "one-off" addbacks | **None on the P&L.** No adjusted-earnings measure exists. The only "recurring one-off" is a **cash-flow** item — the customer-advance inflow (cash buyers pre-pay for off-plan homes) that flatters CFO/FCF — surfaced and normalised by the earnings module, not a management adjustment. **RF-DISC-002 NOT triggered.** | earnings/06 §4, §5 |
| GAAP-to-adjusted gap (magnitude / trend) | The only non-GAAP figure is a company-defined **"EBITDA"** (rough operating cash profit) of AED 25.6bn vs CIQ standardized EBITDA AED 24,132m — a **~AED 1.5bn / ~6% optimistic gap** because it folds in net finance income (~AED 2.0bn) + share of JV results. It reconciles transparently to profit-before-tax; it is not used to manufacture an earnings number. Audited IFRS EPS AED 1.99 is reported straight. | earnings/06 §4, §7; Q4/FY2025 press release |
| Does adjusted framing flatter reality? | **Direction runs conservative, not aggressive.** FY2021–FY2023 impairments/provisions *depressed* reported earnings; they were not reversed aggressively to flatter the present. Investment property is held at **cost** (no fair-value gains inflating profit); stock comp is negligible (AED 1.68m). Earnings-quality score **81/100** — cash-backed even after stripping the advance tailwind. | earnings/06 §5, §8, §9 |

The one honest asterisk is not an *earnings* adjustment but a *cash* one: reported FCF (free cash flow = operating cash − capex) of ~AED 31.0bn LTM overstates steady-state cash by ~27% versus normalised ~AED 22.6bn, because the customer-advance build and a corporate-tax payment lag are cyclical tailwinds. Management does not headline FCF, so it makes no misleading FCF claim — but neither does it flag the cyclicality of its reported cash generation.

## 3. Disclosure Transparency

The **mandatory** disclosure is above-average and granular: three IFRS-8 segments with segment profit and geographic split (Real Estate 80% / Leasing-Retail 15% / Hospitality 5%; UAE 93% of revenue), full non-controlling-interest (NCI — the slice of consolidated profit owned by outside shareholders of listed subsidiaries) attribution (owners of parent AED 17,599m vs NCI AED 4,726m = 21.2% of net profit), the India Enforcement Directorate contingency quantified (AED 190m, ~0.2% of assets), the UAE corporate-tax step-up explained, and the KPIs that actually matter for a developer — property sales (the leading indicator), revenue backlog, land bank, deliveries, occupancy, absorption rates — all reported consistently [FY2025 AR notes; business-model/12_red-flags-sweep.md; ciq_facts.json]. The single material transparency gap is the **IAS 24 government-related-entity election**: Emaar discloses only ~0.35% of revenue / 1.2% of expenses as related-party and elects **not to itemise the bulk of its dealings with the Dubai-government / ICD ecosystem** (land purchases, utilities, construction, power) — the very channel through which the AED 7.5bn Dubai Creek Harbour asset was bought from parent Dubai Holding in 2022. This is a disclosed, IFRS-permitted election (not opacity, not a language gap), but for a state-controlled issuer it leaves the largest related-party channel unquantified to a minority holder. No disclosure got **less** transparent over time — no KPI was dropped or redefined after underperformance (RF-MGT-003 not triggered) — but the abrupt Group-CFO exit (effective 20 May 2026, announced 22 May, **no reason disclosed**) is a completeness/timeliness ding on the executive-change axis.

## 4. Tone in Bad Times

The worst recent operational spot is the Q1'26 quarter (revenue a touch light vs consensus; March hospitality soft): here management's **written** tone is **mixed** — the release owns the March hospitality softness with a specific cause ("affected by the ongoing regional situation") yet wraps the quarter in promotional framing ("strong start," "well-positioned," "disciplined capital allocation," "prudent capital allocation," "resilient"), and the founder's quote pivots to **macro-political praise** of UAE government stability rather than any Emaar-specific accountability. The Q4'25 release, covering the margin-fade quarter, is more one-sided — "record," "highest-ever," "healthy margins across all business lines" — and does not surface the −251 bps EBITDA-margin decline in its own words. **Whether the tone is candid or evasive in the live Q&A is Not assessable** — the pool has no verbatim transcript, only a sell-side paraphrase, which reflects the analyst's selection, not management's answers under questioning.

## 5. Candor Read

**Mixed.** The accounting *substance* is genuinely clean and points toward candid — Emaar reports straight audited IFRS EPS with **no adjusted-earnings engineering**, its one-off history runs conservative (past impairments depressed, not flattered, earnings), and the mandatory notes disclose the hard facts (margin-by-segment, 21% NCI leakage, contingencies, tax step-up); the single biggest candor signal is that the most common tool of managerial deception — a flattering "adjusted" number — is simply **absent** here (`earnings/06` = 81/100). Pulling the other way: the *voluntary* layer is promotional and does not own the fades (the Q4'25 margin decline framed as "healthy margins"), the **IAS 24 election leaves the largest related-party channel unquantified**, there is **no formal numeric bar** to be held to, and the **verbal record is unverifiable** (no transcript). The routed integrity signal was **cleared** by agent 01 (RF-MGT-005 not emitted; no unresolved adverse integrity signal), so it does **not** compound against the trust read — but the government-control / founder-competitor / IAS 24 alignment overhang keeps this short of "high-trust." Net: management can broadly be trusted on the audited numbers, less so on the framing around what is going wrong. Verdict: **mixed.**

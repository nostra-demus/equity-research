## What This Module Found

The people running Emaar have largely done what they promised and turned the capital they deployed into real value per share, but their alignment with minority (outside) shareholders is structurally thin, so the module's read nets to **standard / mixed**. On the money actually deployed the record is value-creative: earnings per share (EPS — the profit attributable to each share) roughly quadrupled from AED 0.52 to AED 1.99, the dividend grew from AED 0.25 to AED 1.00 per share, and return on capital (ROIC — the profit earned on each dirham invested) rose from 4.7% to 13.7%, all on a net-cash balance sheet (more cash than debt, ~AED 25bn) [FY2025 AR, Note 29 / Note 32]. The dividend is covered about 2.9–3.7 times by free cash flow (the cash left after running and reinvesting in the business), and the team avoided every classic value-destroying trap — no debt-funded acquisition spree, no buybacks at high prices, no uncovered dividend [02-008, 02-010]. Promises on property sales (AED 80.4bn), order backlog (AED 163.4bn — the value of homes already sold but not yet booked as revenue) and deliveries were kept, though the record leaned on a strong Dubai property upcycle, cheap legacy land and a FY2025 tax one-off, so it reads as cycle-strong execution rather than proof of through-cycle skill [FY2023 AR; Q1 2026 PR].

The single most important risk is **RF-OWN-004**: the Government of Dubai, through the Dubai Holding group, controls 29.73% (single largest holder 22.27%), the board is staffed entirely by government officials — so no "independent" director is actually independent of the owner — and an IAS 24 accounting election leaves the largest related-party channel (land, utilities and construction bought from the state) unquantified to minorities [FY2025 AR, Annex I p.153 / Note 33]. The clearest illustration is the AED 7.5bn Dubai Creek Harbour asset bought from the parent Dubai Holding, part-funded by issuing new Emaar shares to that same owner, with no arm's-length (fair-market) valuation shown to outside holders [02-005]. Importantly, no extraction is proven today: dividends flow pro-rata to every holder, there is a single one-share-one-vote share class, no share pledging, share count flat since FY2023 (after a disclosed 2021–22 share-funded-M&A step-up), and the accounting is clean with no "adjusted" earnings engineering (earnings quality 81/100) [04-006, 04-007, 06-001]. The risk is the channel and the controller's objective — Dubai's development, not minority value per share — which §24 Filter 6 treats as a structural cap, not a discount to trade around. Incentives are split: a genuine share-price-linked long-term incentive plan (LTIP — pay that vests over years and tracks the share price) covers the CEO and CFO (~75% at-risk), but the founder Managing Director takes AED 61m (~USD 16.7m, three times the CEO) with no share link and owns just 0.03%; separately, the group CFO left abruptly in May 2026 with no reason disclosed (RF-MGT-001, watch) [FY2025 AR, Annex D; Key Developments 2026-05-22].

## What Would Change This Read

**Toward "Aligned & competent" (upgrade triggers):**
- Quantified or ranged IAS 24 government-ecosystem related-party flows that confirm arm's-length terms.
- A disclosed Emaar↔Eagle Hills separation protocol (ring-fencing the founder-MD's competitor).
- A CFO-exit explanation plus a permanent appointment.
- Disclosed incentive metrics that are returns-based.
- An independent director with no government tie.

**Toward "Misaligned or weak" / "Serious governance concerns" (downgrade triggers):**
- A new asset injected richly from the parent and funded by dilution — a repeat of the Dubai Creek Harbour structure.
- Evidence of below-market land or subsidy being repriced against minorities.
- Capital directed to national projects at below-cost returns.
- A Critical related-party-leakage or enforcement finding.

**Data needed:** IAS 24 government-ecosystem transaction detail; Emaar↔Eagle Hills related-party disclosure; DFM AGM vote tallies; the CFO-exit rationale; a verbatim earnings transcript; a granular insider-trade feed.

## Bottom Line

- **Verdict: Standard / mixed (Watchlist).** Governance Score 59; confidence-adjusted 41. Competent operators, structurally thin alignment. Not upgraded, not downgraded here.
- **Why it could be better than it looks:** on deployed capital this team created real value per share (EPS AED 0.52→1.99, ROIC 4.7%→13.7%, net cash ~AED 25bn) with zero value-destruction traps and clean, un-engineered accounting (earnings quality 81/100).
- **Why it could be worse than it looks:** the controller is a government whose goal is Dubai's development, not minority value per share; the board cannot check it (every "independent" is a state official); and the biggest related-party channel is left unquantified — a value-trap risk, not a discount to arbitrage.
- **What's missing:** the IAS 24 government-ecosystem related-party detail (land / utilities / construction / financing with the state) — the one channel that could flip capital allocation from value-creative to extractive. Second-highest: a verbatim earnings transcript, which would lift the candor cap.
- **Watch next:** why the CFO left in May 2026 and whether a permanent successor is named (RF-MGT-001), and whether the new controlling vehicle (Dubai Holding) changes minority terms or the direction of capital.
- **Caveat carried:** no abuse is proven today — dividends are pro-rata, no pledging, clean audit — so the cap reflects a structural conflict, not a demonstrated leak.

## Plain-English Glossary

Terms in order of first appearance; only terms this memo uses. No new numbers or facts.

- **Watchlist** — a "hold and monitor" rating: worth tracking, not yet a buy.
- **Governance Score** — the module's single 0–100 headline for stewardship quality.
- **Confidence-Adjusted Governance Score** — that headline scaled down by how good the evidence is (here 59 × 70% = 41).
- **Inverted score** — one where a higher number is worse (here, governance risk).
- **EPS (earnings per share)** — the profit attributable to each share.
- **ROIC (return on capital)** — the profit earned on each dirham of capital invested.
- **Net cash** — more cash than debt (the opposite of net debt).
- **Free cash flow** — the cash left after running and reinvesting in the business; used here to test dividend cover.
- **Backlog** — the value of homes already sold but not yet booked as revenue.
- **Related-party transaction** — a deal with an insider or the controlling owner, where the price may not be a fair market price.
- **IAS 24** — an accounting rule; its "government-related entity" election lets Emaar skip itemising deals with other state-owned entities.
- **Arm's-length** — priced as if between unrelated parties, i.e. a fair market price.
- **Pro-rata** — shared out in proportion to holding, so every shareholder gets the same amount per share.
- **One-share-one-vote** — each share carries exactly one vote; no super-voting insider class.
- **LTIP (long-term incentive plan)** — pay that vests over several years and tracks the share price.
- **Earnings quality** — how faithfully reported profit reflects real cash economics (higher is cleaner).
- **§24 Filter 6** — the constitution's "unaligned controlling owner" risk filter, which caps scores when the controller's goals differ from minority value.

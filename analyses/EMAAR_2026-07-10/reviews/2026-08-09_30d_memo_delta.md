## 6. Stage-One sheet comment
Emaar's Q2 2026 print, reported 7 August, confirmed the exact risk we flagged as our reason to stay on the sidelines. We wrote a specific test in July: if pre-sales held above 16 percent year-on-year growth, the priced-in earnings collapse would be disproven; if not, the Dubai cycle was turning. Pre-sales fell 84 percent year on year instead, and EBITDA missed consensus by 5 percent. This is not a surprise that hurts us — it is the risk we declined to bet against. We took no position, and the shares fell from 12.20 to 11.50 dirhams, about 5.7 percent, roughly 5 points worse than the Dubai market index over the same stretch. Separately, Emaar and its state parent unveiled a new 200 billion dirham development plan, which commits fresh capital rather than returning the trapped mall value to minority holders — a mild negative for the part of our thesis that depends on the owner eventually acting differently. We are keeping the Watchlist rating. The mall-annuity value argument is untouched by this print; what changed is confirmation that the cyclical risk gating it is real and already showing up in the numbers.

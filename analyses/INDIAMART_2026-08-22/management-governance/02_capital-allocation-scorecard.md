**Incremental ROIC derivation (Inference — approximate, labelled).** Because IndiaMART sits on a treasury book (cash + short-term investments + trading securities) larger than its total capital, a textbook "equity + net debt" invested-capital base is negative and not meaningful. Instead, **operating invested capital** is built as Net PP&E + Goodwill + Other Intangibles + Long-term Investments (the M&A/venture book) — i.e. capital genuinely committed to the operating and investing franchise, excluding the treasury book [CIQ export, Balance Sheet tab]. NOPAT = EBIT × (1 − effective tax rate); the effective tax rate is the company's own disclosed figure for FY24 (23.69%), FY25 (21.44%) and FY26 (24.38%) [FY25 & FY26 Annual Reports, Income Tax notes]; FY22/FY23 use an assumed 25% (not disclosed in this pool — **flagged as an estimate**).

```
Operating invested capital (₹mn): FY22 4,771.7 | FY23 10,649.9 | FY24 10,601.4 | FY25 11,818.9 | FY26 13,269.9
NOPAT (₹mn, approx.):             FY22 2,219.3 | FY23 1,806.6  | FY24 2,250.1  | FY25 3,866.7  | FY26 3,793.0

Incremental ROIC FY23 vs FY22: ΔNOPAT −412.7 / ΔIC +5,878.1  = −7.0%  (Busy Infotech goodwill/investment added, NOPAT fell)
Incremental ROIC FY24 vs FY23: ΔIC ≈ −48.4 (roughly flat)     = not meaningful (near-zero denominator)
Incremental ROIC FY25 vs FY24: ΔNOPAT +1,616.6 / ΔIC +1,217.5 = +132.8% (operating-leverage snapback, not capital-driven)
Incremental ROIC FY26 vs FY25: ΔNOPAT −73.7 / ΔIC +1,451.0    = −5.1%  (NEGATIVE — capital kept growing into the
                                                                        long-term-investment book while operating NOPAT fell)
```

**Reading it.** The FY26 print is the one that matters most for the "is capital still creating per-share value" question: operating invested capital grew by ₹1,451mn (mostly the venture-investment book, §2) while NOPAT on the operating base actually *fell*, producing a negative incremental return. The FY25 figure (+132.8%) is not a capital-allocation signal at all — it reflects the FY24→FY25 EBITDA-margin snap-back (cost discipline / operating leverage, per `earnings/01_historical-financials.md` §6), not returns on newly deployed capital. Company-reported absolute "Return on Capital %" (a CIQ ratio using the full, treasury-inflated capital base) ran 10.3% (FY22) → 7.5% (FY23) → 9.5% (FY24) → 15.4% (FY25) → 13.5% (FY26) [CIQ export, Ratios tab] — directionally consistent (a dip, then a snap-back, then a pullback) but understated in absolute magnitude by the huge treasury-cash denominator, so it is shown only as a cross-check, not the primary read.

**Reinvestment / capex.** Capex is trivial and falling (₹44.2mn FY22 → ₹70.0mn FY26, well below D&A every year) — consistent with an asset-light marketplace model, not with under-investment signalling distress [`business-model/11_capital-allocation-governance.md` §1]. The real "growth reinvestment" line for this business is the M&A/venture-investment book scored in §2, not PP&E capex.

## 5. Capital-Allocation Read

Capital allocation at IndiaMART is **mixed**: disciplined and genuinely shareholder-fair on the distribution side, diffuse and largely unproven on the deployment side. The best use of capital has been the combination of near-zero capex (asset-light model, capex consistently below D&A) with disciplined, all-shareholder-economics buybacks (two fixed-price tender offers, promoters capped at pro-rata participation, funded from reserves with zero debt) and a documented, consistently-paid, well-covered dividend (never above 44% of FCF on a cash-paid basis in any single year, and only exceeding CFO in the one year — FY24 — that combined a large tender buyback with the prior year's dividend, funded from the net-cash balance sheet, not debt). The worst use of capital is the recurring pattern of small minority-stake venture investments (~19 deals across fleet management, HR SaaS, legal tech, e-commerce and procurement software, an estimated $250–300mm cumulative over a decade) justified with bare "ecosystem" language and carrying no disclosed aggregate return — a pattern that has already produced one struck-off subsidiary (Livekeeping), one nominally-divested position (Ten Times Online), and FY2025 impairment/diminution charges, set against one moderately-sized control deal (Busy Infotech) that is improving but still loss-making after four years. The single biggest capital-allocation signal is the **FY26 incremental ROIC turning negative** (≈−5.1% on an operating-capital base that excludes the treasury book) — for the first time in this five-year window, capital kept flowing into the investment book while the operating return on that growing capital base fell, which is exactly the "growth for its own sake" pattern this scorecard exists to catch, even though it has not yet reached the scale (debt-funded, near/above own market value) that would trigger a hard serial-acquirer cap under CLAUDE.md §24 Filter 4. Conclusion: **mixed** — not value-destructive (the balance sheet, dividend and buyback conduct are genuinely clean), but not yet "disciplined" either, given the diffuse venture-investment program and the FY26 return signal.

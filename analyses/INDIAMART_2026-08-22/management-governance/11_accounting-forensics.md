## 3. Dechow F-Score & Accrual Battery (A8-17, A8-18) — computed with python3, computation shown

All three balance-sheet dates required (t=FY26, t−1=FY25, t−2=FY24) are present in the pool (Section 1 + footnote). RSST accruals follow the Richardson (2005) decomposition, operationalised for this balance sheet as: **WC** = (Current assets excl. cash/ST-investments/trading securities) − (Current liabilities excl. current lease/debt); **NCO** = (Total assets − Current assets − LT investments) − (Total liabilities − Current liabilities − LT lease); **FIN** = (ST investments + Trading securities + LT investments) − (Current + LT lease liabilities). This split is this agent's own operationalisation of the standard Richardson accrual model to available line items (labelled, not a company-disclosed sub-total — CLAUDE.md §15) — shown in full below for auditability.

```

FY25: WC=-11,479.16  NCO=-1,725.05 FIN=34,322.27
FY26: WC=-12,816.40  NCO=-2,481.08 FIN=38,497.10

dWC = WC_t - WC_t-1 = -12,816.40 - (-11,479.16) = -1,337.24
dNCO = NCO_t - NCO_t-1 = -2,481.08 - (-1,725.05) = -756.03
dFIN = FIN_t - FIN_t-1 = 38,497.10 - 34,322.27 = 4,174.83
avg TA_t = (46,246.03 + 41,336.85)/2 = 43,791.44
RSST = (dWC+dNCO+dFIN)/avg TA_t = 2,081.56/43,791.44 = 0.0475  (4.75% of avg assets)

dREC = (Rec_t - Rec_t-1)/avg TA_t = (314.86-449.39)/43,791.44 = -0.0031  (-0.31%)

cash_sales_t-1 = Sales_t-1 - (Rec_t-1 - Rec_t-2) = 13,883.44 - 50.61 = 13,832.83
dCASH_SALES = (15,824.95/13,832.83) - 1 = 0.1440

avg TA_t-1 = (41,336.85+34,486.03)/2 = 37,911.44
ROA_t   = NI_t/avg TA_t     = 4,746.82/43,791.44 = 0.1084

     = -5.8688
probability = e^pred/(1+e^pred) = 0.002818

```

## 12. Forensics Read

Both computed batteries clear Green with room to spare: Beneish M = **-3.29** (composite floor for Green is -2.22; zero of seven banded components sit in the manipulator zone) and Dechow F = **0.76** (Green floor is <1.00), computed from two fully-cited consecutive audited annual filings with a genuine three-balance-sheet-date history for the components that need it. The single measured fact that would worry a forensic reader most in a different company — cash from operations exceeding net income in every one of the last five years (CFO/PAT 113%–167%) alongside deferred revenue that has never once declined — is here the strongest possible signal of genuine, cash-backed earnings, not a red flag: it is the direct opposite of the Satyam pattern (profits the cash never confirms) and the direct opposite of channel-stuffing (receivables actually *fell* while revenue grew, and collections growth outpaced revenue growth). The one item that rhymes, faintly, with a governance-lens concern is the recurring ~30–40%-of-PBT treasury FVTPL gain (Section 8) that `earnings/06_earnings-quality.md` already flagged as its single biggest earnings-quality caveat — it is fully disclosed, Ind AS-mandated, and not manipulative, but it means diluted EPS is a materially noisier number than EBIT/EBITDA for this company, and a reader who anchors on EPS alone would be misreading the trend. Cash authenticity grades Amber, not Green or Red: the implied yield on the company's small (₹804mn, 1.7%-of-assets) operating cash balance runs about two-thirds of the period risk-free rate — a real but modest gap, structurally explained by a cash mix dominated by non-interest-bearing current accounts, and nowhere near the <50%-of-risk-free or trust-custody thresholds that would signal a fabricated pile; the company's real liquid wealth sits in a separately-verified, quoted, ~₹30bn treasury book, not in the tested cash line. The sector overlay does not apply (this is not a bank/NBFC/insurer); no regulator has ever found a divergence between IndiaMART's reported numbers and an inspection; and the balance sheet carries no leverage to misuse — the one leverage-adjacent item worth naming (a new ₹897mn placement of inter-corporate deposits with third-party NBFCs) is ordinary-course treasury diversification to investment-grade counterparties, not a related-party leakage channel. A rational minority holder should conclude: these books are not showing the patterns that precede blow-ups, and the one place to keep watching is the growing wedge between statutory PBT/EPS and the underlying EBIT/EBITDA trend, and the treasury-book exposure it implies.

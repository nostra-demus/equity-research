## 3. Stated Strategy & Targets

Management describes the core marketplace strategy as deepening monetisation of the existing paying-supplier base — moving suppliers from the entry-level Silver package into higher-value Gold/Platinum tiers via category-based pricing — while working through an above-normal churn problem on newer, first-year Silver-tier suppliers that has suppressed net supplier additions since FY24 [Q1 FY27 transcript, Jul-21-2026, Q&A, Dinesh Agarwal]. Alongside the core marketplace, the group is investing in a second growth leg, the Busy Infotech accounting-software subsidiary (acquired 2022 for ~₹500 crore / $66.93mm), and has just incorporated a new lending subsidiary, IndiaMART Finance Limited (certificate of incorporation 04-Aug-2026), aimed at MSME working-capital financing for platform users [Corporate Timeline.xls, 04-Aug-2026 and 21-Jul-2026 entries]. The company does not issue formal numeric guidance for consolidated revenue, EBITDA, or EPS [`earnings/04_guidance-consensus.md` §2, citing Q1 FY27 board-outcome press release]; the checkable targets in the record instead come from specific, dated statements management has made on quarterly calls — these are what Section 4 tests.

**Reading the record.** Three of the specific, dated targets management set on the core marketplace business (margin, supplier net-adds, revenue-growth aspiration) were missed, in two cases by a wide margin; one narrower target (10% collection growth) was approximately met; the Busy Infotech deal's own deliberately low bar ("don't lose money") is being approached but not yet cleared. ## 5. Management Read

Leadership stability at the very top is real: both founders have run this company for 27 years, hold roughly 49% of it directly, and the one CFO change in a decade was announced in advance and executed on schedule — this is not a management team in flux. But the checkable promise record on the metrics that matter most (EBITDA margin, paying-supplier net-adds, revenue-growth aspiration) shows three material misses against specific, dated targets the CEO himself set on quarterly calls, against one target approximately met and one management-transition promise delivered exactly as stated — a mixed-to-weak record on delivery. The single biggest signal, on balance, is the pattern of specific misses followed by management withdrawing formal guidance rather than resetting a lower, achievable one — a real accountability gap sitting alongside genuinely candid, non-defensive real-time disclosure of the underlying problems (the CEO repeatedly told analysts he was "not confident" to give a number rather than papering over the churn issue).

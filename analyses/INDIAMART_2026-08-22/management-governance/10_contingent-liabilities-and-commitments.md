## 11. Read

Total contingent liabilities are genuinely small — ₹521.86mn against ₹24,003.75mn of consolidated net worth [FY26 Annual Report, Note 36; Consolidated Balance Sheet] is 2.17%, nowhere near the 5% materiality line this checklist uses, let alone the 25% Red band — so on pure solvency size this is a clean Green, consistent with `balance-sheet-survival/05`'s independent read. But the size test is the wrong place to stop: the note's biggest live item, a ₹203.80mn GST demand over transitional input-tax credit, sat at an **unchanged** ₹219.18mn combined headline figure across FY25 and FY26 while its first-ever appellate ruling — a dismissal by the Commissioner (Appeals), Noida, on 2 April 2026 — went against the company in between. Management disclosed the dismissal candidly in the note's own footnote prose (this is not concealment), but a reader who checks only the summary table would see "no change" where the real story is "lost the first round." The company's provisioning stance has not moved: still labeled "possible," still unprovided, with only the largest remaining forum (the Appellate Tribunal) left before a second loss would make this a genuine RF-CL-002 serial-appeal-loss case. Two smaller findings round out the picture: a ₹13.84mn unexplained drop in the CESTAT matter's already-deposited amount between the FY25 and FY26 auditor's reports, with no narrative anywhere addressing it, and a first-time, unquantified mention of "slight delays in a few cases of Provident Fund" in the FY26 CARO report that was absent from FY25's clean-deposits language. Neither is material in rupee terms, but both are the kind of small, unexplained movements this checklist exists to surface before they compound. What a rational minority holder should watch next: the outcome of the GST TRAN-1 appeal at the Appellate Tribunal (the single most consequential unresolved fact in this note) and whether next year's CARO report shows the PF-delay language persisting or clearing.

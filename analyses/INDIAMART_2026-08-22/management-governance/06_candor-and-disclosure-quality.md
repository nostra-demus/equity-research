# Candor & Disclosure Quality — INDIAMART

**Data availability note.** This read is built from **verbatim** earnings-call transcripts (S&P Global Market Intelligence transcripts, tier 3 per this module's source hierarchy) — 22 quarterly transcripts spanning Q3 FY21 through Q1 FY27 are present in the data pool, including the two most recent (Q4 FY26, 30-Apr-2026, and Q1 FY27, 21-Jul-2026), both read in full for this report. No shareholder letter exists in the pool (IndiaMART does not publish one; the FY26 Annual Report's Chairman/MD statements were reviewed for tone as a supplement). **No partial-data cap applies to this report** — the verbatim-transcript requirement in this module's Partial-Data Rule is met, so the tone, Q&A-evasiveness, and owning-the-miss components below are fully assessable, not capped at Insufficient Data. Cross-module inputs used: `earnings/06_earnings-quality.md` (non-GAAP read, present and read in full) and `earnings/04_guidance-consensus.md` (guidance/beat-miss record, present and read in full).

**Reading the pattern.** Across four consecutive years of misses on the metrics that matter most (margin, net-adds, growth aspiration), management's real-time communication style is consistent: name the miss in the same terms as the target was set, quantify the drivers with specific splits rather than vague "headwinds," and let the underlying KPI keep printing every quarter rather than smoothing it into a blended or adjusted figure. The one genuine communication gap — flagged already in `01` (finding 01-012) — is that after the FY24 net-add and margin misses, management stopped giving ANY forward numeric target on net-adds rather than resetting one at an achievable level; that is an accountability gap (nothing to be held to next), but it is an openly stated, reasoned withdrawal ("we would come back with a better guidance" once churn improves), not a quiet one — see §4A.

## 3. Disclosure Transparency

Segment economics are disclosed with real numbers, not boilerplate: every quarterly call breaks out Busy Infotech billing, revenue-from-operations, deferred revenue, cash-from-operations, and license counts separately from the core marketplace, and the core-platform KPIs (paying-supplier count and net change, unique business inquiries, collections, deferred revenue, EBITDA margin) have been reported on a consistent basis across all 22 transcripts in the pool, including through the FY24–FY27 deterioration — management did not stop reporting the metrics that got worse, only the forward guidance built on them (§4A). One transparency reduction to note: traffic-source detail (organic vs performance-marketing vs direct) was explicitly withheld in Q4 FY26 ("I won't be able to give you how much traffic is coming through organic search... I can't provide you a split") — a real information gap, but framed candidly as "too new to disclose reliably" rather than denied outright, and it is operational granularity below the KPI level rather than a KPI itself going dark.

## 4. Tone in Bad Times

The worst recent print by the numbers is Q4 FY26 (Apr-30-2026): EPS missed consensus by ~52% (the sharpest miss of the last 5 quarters per `earnings/04_guidance-consensus.md` §6), net paying suppliers declined 1,200 for the quarter, and the CEO used the phrase "worry of falling into a vicious loop" — a founder-CEO volunteering the negative framing himself rather than waiting for an analyst to suggest it. Q&A was answered directly: churn rates were given by tier and cadence (Platinum <1%/month, Gold 1–1.5%/month, Silver ~7%/month vs a prior ~5–6%), the net-add shortfall was split by named cause with rough percentages, and when an analyst's question broke up mid-call ("Swapnil, your voice is breaking"), management asked for a repeat rather than letting the question drop. The subsequent Q1 FY27 call (Jul-21-2026) continued the same register under continued pressure — an analyst put the "leaky bucket" framing to the CEO directly and he accepted it without deflecting, then answered "I can't really answer that for sure" rather than manufacture false certainty on whether the churn problem is structural. No instance of a declined or redirected question was found in either transcript; every question asked was answered, including ones the company had no good answer to.

## 5. Candor Read

Management is **candid / high-trust** in how it communicates bad results: across four years of real, material misses on the metrics that matter most (margin, net-adds, growth), the CEO and CFO consistently used the company's own words for its own shortfalls ("lowest ever" quarter, "worry of... a vicious loop," "I'm really not confident to give you any answer"), gave granular cause-by-cause splits rather than blended "headwinds" language, and — critically — kept reporting the deteriorating KPIs every single quarter rather than redefining, rebasing, or dropping them (A7-03: Green). The strongest evidence for trust is structural: there is no non-GAAP overlay to hide behind (§2) and no declined or deflected question was found in the two most recent verbatim transcripts (§4) even as analysts pressed directly on whether the core growth problem is structural. The strongest evidence against unconditional trust is the guidance-withdrawal pattern (A7-04: Amber, RF-MGT-003 already flagged upstream in `01`) — after missing specific numeric net-add and margin targets, management chose silence over a corrected, achievable target, which protects it from a future scored miss but leaves investors with less to hold it to; this is an accountability gap, not concealment, since the withdrawal itself was stated openly and repeated with the same reasoning across multiple calls, not buried. Separately, the one routed integrity item from `business-model/01_disqualifier-scan` (two self-reported SEBI PIT/Designated-Person compliance lapses) was investigated by `01_management-and-track-record` §4A and concluded non-adverse and self-disclosed — **RF-MGT-005 was NOT emitted upstream**, so there is no unresolved adverse integrity signal here to weigh against the trust read; if a future run identifies the unnamed individuals or finds a broader pattern, this conclusion should be revisited. On balance: **candid / high-trust**, with the guidance-withdrawal accountability gap as the one qualifier worth tracking.

The single biggest candor signal: a founder-CEO who, four years running, reported the exact number that proved him wrong on his own prior quarterly call, in the same units he originally guided in, without changing what gets measured.

## 2. Dominant Segment

Web and related Services is the dominant — and effectively the only economically material — segment. It generated 91.96% of FY26 consolidated revenue (₹14,429.38m of ₹15,690.42m) and more than 100% of FY26 segment profit, because the Accounting Software Services segment posted a small operating loss (segment result of ₹(28.15)m) that year [FY26 Annual Report, Note 32]. On both the revenue test and the profit test, Web and related Services clears the 85% single-segment threshold: this is best read as a single-segment business with a small, currently loss-making, fast-growing bolt-on (Accounting Software Services), not a genuinely multi-segment company. At the standalone (parent-only) level the company does not disclose segment information at all — "the Company has disclosed the segment information only as part of consolidated financial statements" [FY26 Annual Report, Note 32, Standalone Financial Statements] — underscoring that the marketplace business is effectively the whole of the standalone entity.

## 3. Segment Disclosure Quality

Segment definitions have been consistent for the trailing three fiscal years (FY24, FY25, FY26 all report the same two segments, "Web and related Services" and "Accounting Software Services," with identical wording of the segment description note) [FY26 Annual Report, Note 32; Q1 FY27 Interim Report, Note 29]. The structure did change further back: FY21 was reported as a single combined segment ("Business-To-Business E-Marketplace"), and the Accounting Software Services segment first appears in FY23 following the Busy Infotech acquisition [Financials (1).xls, Segments tab] — this predates the trailing-three-year window and is noted for completeness, not as a current disclosure gap. There is no "Other" or residual revenue bucket — 100% of consolidated revenue is allocated between the two named segments in every disclosed year. The one unallocated item is on the balance sheet, not revenue: an "Unallocable" assets line of ₹7,629.29m in FY26, 16.5% of total segment assets of ₹46,246.03m [FY26 Annual Report, Note 32] — likely unattributed cash/investments, but the filing does not itemise it, so this is flagged as a minor gap. Profit is disclosed at segment level down to "Segment results" (operating profit before finance cost, D&A, other income, associate losses and tax), but the company does NOT split net profit after tax, finance cost, other income, or the associate-loss line (which is material — ₹(547.72)m in FY26, almost entirely attributable to the Vyapar associate) by segment; only consolidated totals are given below the segment-result line [FY26 Annual Report, Note 32]. Segment-level capital expenditure has not been disclosed since FY21 (only Assets and Liabilities are broken out for FY22 onward) [Financials (1).xls, Segments tab], which limits how precisely capital intensity can be judged per segment (see Section 1 note above). Downstream agents (`unit-economics`, `competitive-map`, `business-quality`) should treat the segment-level margin and capex reads as directional, not precise, given these gaps.

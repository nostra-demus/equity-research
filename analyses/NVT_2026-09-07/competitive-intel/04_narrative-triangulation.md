### 3a. The market-share cross-check (the "everyone claims share gains" test)

This test runs whenever the subject and **at least one peer** both make a share claim. **It cannot run here, and it fails the trigger on both sides at once:**

1. **The subject makes no explicit share claim.** Across both FY2026 calls and every filing in the bound generation, nVent never states that it is gaining share, and it names no competitor anywhere in its own filings [FY24 10-K, Item 1 — Competition, p.2]. The nearest adjacent claim — "New products contributed over 30 points to our sales growth" [Q2 FY26 transcript, prepared remarks] — is a **product-vintage** metric, not a share metric, and its basis is undefined anywhere in this pool. Treating it as a share-gain claim would be inventing the subject's side of the test.
2. **No peer makes any claim at all**, share or otherwise, because no peer call is in the corpus.

So there is **no simultaneous-gain finding to adjudicate** — neither a contradiction nor a "jointly possible" note, because there are no gain claims to place beside each other. For the record of what a future run must do rather than assume: nVent's own numbers alone would **never** prove a share gain. Growing infrastructure sales $409.3m → $882.5m in a vertical whose whole end-market is expanding is entirely consistent with flat or even falling share, and a share bridge would require the total market's size and every material participant's revenue in the same segment and window — **including the two rivals that will never disclose it, Rittal and Hitachi Energy**. Absent an exhaustive bridge or arithmetic showing claimed gains exceed what all other players could have ceded, simultaneous gains by nVent and any peer must be recorded as *"jointly possible — unsampled competitors may be ceding share"*, **not** a contradiction, and must not be routed to candor or §8.

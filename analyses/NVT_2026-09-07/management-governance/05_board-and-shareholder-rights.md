## 5. Minority-Shareholder Protection Read

The board is a real check, not a rubber stamp: 8 of 9 directors are independent, all three key committees are 100% independent with two designated financial experts, the independent directors met without management at **every** board and committee meeting in 2025, the 2025 evaluation was run by an outside third party with written assessments and one-to-one interviews, and there is no controller, no promoter, no dual-class share and **no related-person transaction at all in 2025** on the proxy's own statement — corroborated by the absence of any ASC 850 note in the audited financials and by `07`'s independent network sweep, which found zero `disclosable-and-omitted` subjects among 53. Rights are close to intact: one share one vote, annual election of every director on a majority standard that actually fails a loser, a 10% threshold to requisition a meeting, proxy access, no poison pill in force, and the Irish Takeover Rules' ban on frustrating action — which is a stronger real-world constraint on a defensive share issue than most US charters carry.

The two things that stop this being unqualified. First, **the CEO also chairs the board**, and the counterweight — a designated independent Lead Director with genuine enumerated powers — is a mitigation, not a substitute; the same person leads the body that reviews her succession, and no successor or emergency plan is disclosed. Second, the Constitution keeps three board-side levers alive that shareholders never voted for individually: **the power to adopt a poison pill without a shareholder vote, 20 million blank-cheque preferred shares whose terms (including voting) the board sets alone, and a Delaware-203-style 15% / three-year business-combination freeze** that the 10-K itself says would discourage a premium bid. None is in use; all are available. Add the softer items: the Lead Director carries a Minor-concerns grade from `07` on her Tupperware chairmanship through that company's restatement and Chapter 11; the audit-committee chair sits on four public boards and chairs a fifth company; and the company-secretary-equivalent seat has been without a publicly named permanent occupant since 2026-04-01.

**Conclusion: adequate — at the upper end of adequate, and short of strong protection only because of the combined Chair/CEO role and the dormant-but-unilateral board defenses.** `04_ownership-and-insider-behavior` runs in the same layer and its output does not exist as I write; on my own read there is **no structurally unaligned controlling owner** — nVent is widely held, not government-controlled, not a listed subsidiary of a value-maximising parent, and not a sprawling unrelated conglomerate (two segments in one industry) — so **RF-OWN-004 / §24 Filter 6 does not trip and the "no better than weak" floor does not apply**. If `04` finds a control bloc the filings do not show, that conclusion must be revisited. **The single biggest entrenchment risk to minority holders is not a current abuse but a standing capability: a board that can adopt a poison pill and set the terms of 20 million preferred shares on its own, chaired by the chief executive it is meant to supervise.**

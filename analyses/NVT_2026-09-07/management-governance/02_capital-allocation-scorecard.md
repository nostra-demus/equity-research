**Serial-acquirer + opportunity-cost test (CLAUDE.md §24, Filter 4) — the filter TRIPS.**

Four material deals in four years — ECM ~$1,100m (2023), Trachte $687.5m (2024), EPG $979.6m (2025), Maverick $1,750m + up to $550m (agreed 2026) — against FY2025 revenue of $3,893.1m. Cumulative cash acquisitions of $3,017.2m over FY2021–FY2025 were **158.3% of five-year free cash flow**; add Maverick and the four-year committed deal total reaches roughly **$4.5bn**. Most M&A destroys value, and that base rate — not the company's own narrative — is the yardstick here.

The **opportunity cost** is not hypothetical. A whole reporting segment earning a 20.5% operating margin was sold, and the proceeds went straight into EPG, of which **89.4% of the purchase price** is goodwill and intangibles. Group-wide, goodwill ($2,676.3m) plus other intangibles ($1,793.3m) now total **$4,469.6m — 62.5% of $7,146.3m of total assets and more than the $3,986.9m of total equity**, leaving tangible book value at **−$482.7m** [CIQ Financials→Balance Sheet, 2026-06-30]. And the ECM deal's tracking basis has been removed by a segment re-cut: the segment it was bought into no longer exists as a reported line.

**Two things that argue the other way, named rather than averaged away (CLAUDE.md §3).** First, **no goodwill or intangible impairment has ever been recorded** — "no impairment expense recorded in 2024, 2023 or 2022 related to goodwill" and none on identifiable intangibles [FY2024 10-K, Note 2 and Note 5 accounting-policy notes]. There is no accounting confession of overpayment yet. Second, the Maverick release is **not** bare synergy language: it states a multiple (11.5×/10.5×), a revenue base, and a falsifiable first-year accretion test. That is more disclosure than the previous three deals carried, and it is why **RF-CAP-001 ("large acquisition with vague rationale") is NOT triggered** — the rationale is thin on returns but it is numerically checkable. What is still missing across all four deals is any **return-on-invested-capital target or post-close return disclosure**, which is why no deal in the table can be scored "delivered" on evidence.

**Verdict on the filter:** serial-acquirer pattern **confirmed on the deal count, the cash intensity relative to internally generated cash, and the funding source (a divested segment)**. It is at the milder end of the band on one dimension — the deals are not "near or above the company's own value" ($4.5bn of deals against a $27,703.6m market capitalisation at a $171.16 share price [CIQ Financials→Key Stats, Current Capitalization]) and three of four were funded from disposal proceeds and cash rather than stretched debt. That mitigates severity; it does not switch the filter off. **RF-CAP-004 fires at High severity**, the M&A-discipline component is capped, and the module Score Cap Rule "Serial-acquirer pattern → Capital allocation max 50" is applied to the total in §5.

## 5. Capital-Allocation Read

Over five years nVent put **$3,017.2m into buying other companies — 158.3% of everything the business generated after capex** — and $312.9m, one-ninth of that, into its own plants; the gap was funded by $610.8m of net new debt and by selling a segment that was earning a **20.5% operating margin** on $622.7m of sales, which is the textbook shape of selling the geese to buy a hen. The **best** use of capital was the buyback: $591.3m spent at 4.7× the stock-comp charge, cutting the diluted share count 3.4%, with the largest single year ($253.1m) executed at an average of **$52.73** into the H1-2025 drawdown and the buying deliberately throttled to $50.4m at ~$126 and then to zero as the shares ran to $171.16 — that is price conduct visible in the filings, not a claim. The **worst** was the acquisition programme: four material deals in four years, not one of them carrying a published return target or a post-close return disclosure, 89.4% of the EPG price landing in goodwill and intangibles, group intangibles now 62.5% of assets against negative tangible book, and an incremental return on the money actually deployed of **7.6% over FY2022–FY2025** against peers computed at 13–16% — the 22.6% incremental return of the last 18 months is real but is measured after a disposal shrank the capital base and inside a demand upswing that produced +47% organic growth in a single quarter.

**The single biggest capital-allocation signal: the serial-acquirer pattern (RF-CAP-004), and specifically that the next deal is the first one to be funded with fresh debt.** Maverick Power at $1,750m plus up to $550m of earnout, funded with cash on hand and new borrowing under a Bank of America bridge, would take a balance sheet currently at 1.24× net debt/EBITDA toward the top of management's own stated 2.0–2.5× range — while the three deals already done still have no disclosed return to show for them.

**Conclusion: MIXED.** Per-share value has been created — normalised diluted EPS went $1.27 (FY2021) → $2.12 (FY2025) → $2.92 (LTM Jun-2026), book value per share $15.03 → $24.63, share count down 2.5%, dividend never cut and raised three times, indexed total shareholder return 296.3 at end-2024 against 197.5 for the S&P MidCap 400 Industrials — but the mechanism that produced most of it was an end-market surge and a well-timed disposal, not demonstrated returns on the $3bn of acquisitions, and the returns evidence that does exist puts nVent 3.7–6 points below its named peers on return on capital, a gap that has persisted five years.

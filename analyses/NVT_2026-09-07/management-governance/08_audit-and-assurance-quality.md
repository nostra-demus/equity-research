**The untested test that would have mattered most.** A4-07's low-balling trigger — a fee that FALLS while revenue grows more than 20% — is live in exactly this window: revenue rose about **29.5%** in FY2025 while the group bought EPG (~$979.6m) and sold Thermal Management ($1.7bn), a scope change on both sides at once. ## 10. Read

The assurance chain here is able and unafraid on every test a reader can actually run: a Big-4 firm in place since the company's incorporation, unqualified opinions on FY2022–FY2024 and on internal control, no restatement in five years attested on the 10-K cover page itself, no auditor resignation and no Item 4.01 8-K ever filed, an audit committee that met eight times and sat in executive session without management at each meeting, and an auditor whose reappointment drew 0.48% of votes against. The gatekeeper lead the triage handed over dissolves on the evidence — the CFO "departure" was a lateral move to a segment presidency with a successor named 20 days ahead, so there is no exit cluster at all.

What cannot be tested is independence in money terms, and that is the weakest link. **The audit-fee ratio is unreadable** — the FY24 10-K routes Item 14 to a proxy nobody collected, and three direct retrievals of the public proxy truncated before the fee table — so the one Enron-shaped question, whether the auditor earns more from consulting than from auditing, has no answer here. It bites hardest because the very period in question is the one where revenue rose about 29.5% while a ~$979.6m business came in and a $1.7bn one went out; a fee that failed to move with that scope is exactly what A4-07 exists to catch, and it is exactly what this pool cannot show. Two smaller gaps sit alongside it: the **FY2025 auditor's report and ICFR scope are absent**, so the first-year control exclusion for the Electrical Products Group is invisible; and the **signing partner is never named**, so the unit of accountability — and the five-year lead-partner rotation the US regime does require — cannot be checked at all.

To trust these numbers more, three documents would do it: the DEF 14A fee tables for FY2023–FY2025 (the ratio and its trend against scope), the FY2025 Form 10-K (the missing opinion, the missing ICFR scope, and the second CAM that would turn one data point into a recurrence test), and the PCAOB Form AP for the nVent engagement (the partner's name, and a partner-level discipline sweep to replace the coverage-limited row).

**Conclusion: mixed — watch the named link (the unreadable audit-fee ratio, and the FY2025 audit report behind it).** Nothing in the readable record is adverse; the reservation is about what is unreadable, not about what was found.

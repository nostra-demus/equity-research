## 5. Management Read

nVent is run by a CEO who has been in the seat for the company's entire ~8.4-year independent life and a senior team whose median time at the company is about 8.4 years, refreshed in one deliberate step in March 2025 rather than churned — the only CFO change in eight years moved the incumbent sideways into running the biggest segment rather than out of the door, and the Chief Accounting Officer and the auditor both stayed put through it, so the gatekeeper-exit pattern that this test exists to catch is simply absent. On promises, the record is **mostly kept and, in 2026, beaten hard**: adjusted EPS came in 17.2% and 26.1% above the top of the guided range in two consecutive quarters, the ~20% adjusted operating margin target was met at 20.2% in 2025, the $1.7bn Thermal Management sale was agreed and closed within six months, and a specific segment-margin recovery promised in May was delivered in July — set against one clear miss (Q3 FY2024, −22.2% on adjusted EPS), one year of organic growth below the then-standing target (+2.4% in FY2024), and a cash-conversion target quietly cut from ~100% to ~95% while every other target was raised.

The single biggest management signal is **positive but must be read with its qualifier attached**: this team beats the numbers it sets, and it has also been badly wrong about those numbers in the conservative direction — the FY2026 EPS midpoint rose 25.5% in five months — which means the delivery on show is operating execution inside an extraordinary AI data-centre demand upswing, not demonstrated forecasting skill. In the four years before this cycle, full-year adjusted-EPS surprise ran +0.5%, +1.0%, −0.4%, +0.6%; that, not 2026, is the base rate for how well they forecast. The one thing to watch, and it is a watch item rather than a warning: four material acquisitions in four years against a $3.9bn revenue base, executed by a team whose returns on capital (9.5% LTM) still sit several points below the peers it is buying alongside.

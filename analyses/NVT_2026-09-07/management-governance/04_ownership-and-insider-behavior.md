## 5. Ownership / Insider Read

**Skin in the game is thin and it was granted, not bought.** Directors and officers together hold roughly 0.5% of nVent — a figure derived from the 10-K cover page rather than read off a proxy, and cross-checked at 0.33% by an unverified web source — and every share of it that can be traced came out of the 2018 Omnibus Incentive Plan, where 14.98 million shares (9.1% of the company) sit issued or reserved at a $30.64 weighted-average option strike against a $171.16 market price. Not one open-market purchase by an insider appears in the pool or in the web record.

**The insider signal is net selling, but it is the ordinary kind and it cannot carry weight in scoring.** The visible pattern is cashless exercise-and-sell inside the post-results trading window the company's own policy defines, pre-cleared by the General Counsel, with no 10b5-1 plan adopted or terminated in any period the pool covers — and the May 2026 sales were followed by a Q2 that doubled EPS from continuing operations, so nothing here looks like selling ahead of bad news. Because that transaction record is web-sourced and not a pool filing, it fires no red flag and caps the recent-insider-behavior component to Insufficient Data (fix F20).

**The biggest risk to minority holders is not control abuse — it is that nobody is in control.** Pledging is contractually banned, hedging is banned, there is one share and one vote, no dual class, no bloc, no parent, and an Irish takeover regime that forbids the board from frustrating a bid. Against that: a combined Chair/CEO with no lead independent director disclosed anywhere in the pool, a board that may issue 20 million preferred shares — with voting rights of its own choosing — without asking shareholders, and insider ownership so small that no executive's own wealth is meaningfully tied to the share price except through the annual grant cycle. With the proxy absent, the pay design that would have to substitute for ownership alignment is unread in this run, and the honest verdict is that alignment here is **asserted by policy, not evidenced by stake**.

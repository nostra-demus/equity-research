## 1. RPT Intensity & the Applicable Regime (A5-01)

**Applicable materiality rule, named per period.** nVent is a US-reporting issuer, so **SEC Regulation S-K Item 404(a)** governs proxy-level related-person disclosure in every year read: any transaction over **$120,000** in which the company is a participant and a director, executive officer, 5%-holder or their immediate family has a direct or indirect material interest. The financial-statement note is governed by **ASC 850** (material related-party transactions must be disclosed in the notes). **nVent's own written policy sets a stricter line than the SEC floor — $50,000** — and routes every candidate transaction to the Governance and Sustainability Committee [nVent DEF 14A filed 2026-03-31, "Policies and Procedures Regarding Related Person Transactions", SEC EDGAR, retrieved 2026-09-07]. There was **no regime change across FY2023–FY2025**: the same Item 404(a) $120,000 floor and the same $50,000 company policy applied in all three years. The India LODR Reg 23 graded-slab change of December 2025 is **not applicable** to this issuer and is not used anywhere in this report.

One jurisdictional overlay does apply on top, because nVent is an Irish plc: under Irish company law **a guarantee or security for a loan, quasi-loan or credit transaction to a director or a connected person requires a special resolution of shareholders** (a 75% supermajority) [FY24 10-K, Exhibit 4 — Description of Securities, "Voting"]. That is a stronger structural gate on the A5-03 channel than the US default, and no such resolution appears on any AGM ballot in the period read.

## 14. Read

**Value moving to a controller or a related party: $0.0m a year, 0.0% of PAT, in each of FY2023, FY2024 and FY2025** — and the finding rests on three independent legs rather than one convenient sentence: the audited statements carry no ASC 850 note where a material flow would force one, the leases note explicitly denies any related-party lease, and the proxy states in terms "we had no related person transactions during 2025" against a self-imposed **$50,000** threshold that is stricter than the SEC's own $120,000 line. There is no controller to leak to — the largest holder is Vanguard at 5.24%, filed passive — so the whole promoter-channel apparatus (royalty, promoter vendor, promoter customer, sibling private company, insider on both sides of a trade) has no subject, and the brand estate that would otherwise be the obvious channel sits **inside** the consolidation at nVent Services GmbH at a zero external rate.

**The single largest channel, if one ever opens, is the group's own plumbing rather than any counterparty:** $1,592.1m of intercompany funding flowing from the Luxembourg note issuer down to non-guarantor subsidiaries — 99.5% of the Obligor Group's noncurrent assets, unchanged to the cent across two quarters, with rate and tenor undisclosed. Every counterparty on that line is a 100%-owned consolidated subsidiary, so nothing leaves the group; it is a visibility item, not a leakage item, and it is the one place a future related-party transaction would be hardest to see.

**Would the structure let leakage hide?** Partly. 137 named entities across 33 jurisdictions is above the >100 line, the ownership chain between them is not disclosed in this filing regime, no material subsidiary publishes its own numbers, roughly 31% of period-end cash sits where repatriation is limited by local rules or tax cost, and the perimeter moved twice in eighteen months ($1.7bn out, $979.6m in, $1.75bn pending). Set against that: the debt sits at one named issuer guaranteed by the listed parent with a published Obligor Group table, there are no variable interest entities and no unconsolidated special-purpose entities at all, the captive insurer is consolidated and named, and even a 5.2% shareholding is disclosed on an exhibit that does not require it.

**The one disclosure that would settle the biggest open question: the FY2025 Form 10-K Exhibit 21** — the audited subsidiary list as of 2025-12-31. It is the only document that would show the post-divestiture, post-EPG group perimeter as management and the auditor actually certify it, rather than reconstructed from a benefit-plan filing; it would confirm whether the Russian entity is still in the group; and it would give the entity count the next run should be judging, instead of a count that is eighteen months stale.

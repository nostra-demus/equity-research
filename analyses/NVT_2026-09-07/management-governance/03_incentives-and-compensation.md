### The two things that matter most in this section

**First: what IS disclosed is genuinely per-share, not size-based.** The only performance condition attached to any equity award in the last three grant years is *relative* total shareholder return — a per-share, market-relative measure that a company cannot clear by buying revenue. The annual bonus explicitly carries a free-cash-flow criterion, stated in the company's own current filing, not in a marketing document. Nothing readable in the pool pays management for revenue, absolute EBITDA, headcount, or deal count.

**Second, and this is the real gap: there is no return-on-capital metric anywhere in the disclosed design.** No ROIC, no ROCE, no return-on-invested-capital hurdle appears in Note 16, in any of the three filed award agreements, or in the free-cash-flow language. That matters here for a specific, dated reason:

- nVent's own return on invested capital is **9.54% (LTM to 30 June 2026)** — the profit it earns on each $100 of capital employed [CIQ Financials → Ratios tab, LTM 12 months Jun-30-2026 — tier-5 vendor export, as read in `business-model/08_competitive-map.md`, §2d].
- On **2026-08-24** nVent agreed to buy Maverick Power for **$1.75 billion**, plus **up to $550 million** of extra cash tied to performance in 2027 and 2028. The company's stated financial test in the announcement is that the deal will be **"accretive to adjusted EPS in the first year"** [`nVent-to-Acquire-Maverick-Power-2026.pdf`, press release, 2026-08-24]. No return-on-capital hurdle is disclosed.
- A large, partly debt-funded acquisition can raise earnings per share and lift relative TSR while earning **less than the cost of raising the money**. That is the classic gap between an EPS test and a returns test. **Nothing in nVent's disclosed pay design pushes back on it.**

**A third point, flagged rather than scored: the plan's authorised menu is far broader than the awards actually granted.** The filed PSU award agreement lists the measures the committee is permitted to choose from, and the list includes plainly size-based ones — "revenue growth; sales growth (including organic sales growth); … earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization; operating income; … market share" alongside the returns-based ones ("return on equity; return on investment; return on invested capital (including after-tax); total stockholder return") [FY24 10-K, **Exhibit 10.24 — Performance Share Unit Award Agreement**, footnote 1]. The committee has therefore *reserved the right* to pay on size without a further shareholder vote. It has not used that right in 2022, 2023 or 2024. Both facts belong in the record.

## 4. Incentive Alignment Read

**What the pay actually rewards, on the evidence that exists: beating a peer index on total shareholder return, and generating free cash.** The only performance condition on any equity award granted in 2022, 2023 or 2024 is three-year relative TSR against the S&P 400 Industrials, paying 0–200% — and nVent beat that exact index by 98.8 index points over five years, so management was paid for clearing the bar it was actually set. The annual bonus carries free cash flow as a stated criterion in the company's own current filing. Nothing readable pays for revenue, absolute EBITDA, headcount or deal count; equity dilution is small (0.25–0.42% annual burn, 9.07% overhang) and the share count is falling, not rising; the clawback, the double-trigger, the absence of gross-ups, and the outright ban on hedging and pledging are all real and all filing-sourced; and shareholders backed the pay design with ~95.2% support.

**The single biggest incentive signal is what is missing rather than what is there: no return-on-capital measure appears anywhere in the disclosed design.** A company earning 9.54% on invested capital has just agreed to pay $1.75bn plus up to $550m more for Maverick Power, justified publicly as "accretive to adjusted EPS in the first year" — a test a debt-funded deal can pass while still earning less than the money costs. Relative TSR and EPS accretion will both reward that deal if the share price cooperates; nothing in the pay plan penalises it if the returns do not.

**Conclusion: mixed.** The metrics that are disclosed are returns/per-share-based, not size-based — that part is a genuine positive and is stated without hedging. What holds the verdict at mixed is threefold and specific: the weights behind those metrics are unreadable from the pool, so "how much" of the pay each metric drives cannot be tested; there is no capital-efficiency gate on a management team in the middle of its largest acquisition; and the two magnitude signals that do exist — CEO pay growing ~6 percentage points a year faster than per-share earnings, and a ~307× pay ratio — both sit above where a reader would want them while resting on sources too weak to grade Red. **Incentive alignment is scored 50/100 under the no-proxy cap; the uncapped read on the evidence assembled here is 57.**

# Candor & Disclosure Quality — NVT

**Jurisdiction & regime.** nVent Electric plc is incorporated in Ireland, head-officed in London, and files as a **US SEC domestic filer** on the NYSE (Commission file 001-38265), reporting under US GAAP in USD, fiscal year ending 31 December [FY24 10-K, cover page]. The US form names (10-K, 10-Q, 8-K, DEF 14A) are therefore the correct local documents here (CLAUDE.md §27). Every pool document is in English; no translation issue arises, and **no language-based gap or opacity flag is recorded**.

**Evidence binding: frozen.** Every read resolved through extract generation `6db32848…1aecd1e6` (`manifest.json`, per-document extracts, `ciq_facts.json`, `relationships.json`). Live `data/NVT/` was not read; `data/NVT/` below is a citation label only.

**Partial-data status — the important one, stated first.** The pool contains **two verbatim earnings-call transcripts** (Q1 FY26, 2026-05-01; Q2 FY26, 2026-07-31), so the module's "no transcripts" cap does **not** apply and tone and Q&A ARE assessable. But both of those quarters were blowouts: adjusted EPS beat the top of the guided range by 17.2% and 26.1% [earnings/04_guidance-consensus.md §6]. **The one clear miss in the record — Q3 FY2024, adjusted-EPS surprise −22.2% — has no transcript in this pool**, and there is no shareholder letter, no proxy (DEF 14A), no FY2025 10-K and no 8-Ks [management-governance/01_management-and-track-record.md, header]. So the strongest form of the test this agent exists to run — *how do they talk when the print is genuinely bad?* — cannot be run on the genuinely bad print. It is run instead on the mildest available bad news (two segment-margin declines and one down organic year in the FY24 10-K MD&A), and that limitation is carried into the score, the confidence and the verdict rather than smoothed away.

This is standard US-industrial non-GAAP practice with two above-average marks and one below-average one — not a company hiding a deteriorating business behind add-backs.

## 3. Disclosure Transparency

**Segment economics: disclosed, and the granularity has gone UP, not down.** From Q1 FY26 the 10-Q discloses, per segment and with restated prior-year comparatives, not just net sales and segment income but the significant expense categories beneath them — cost of goods sold, SG&A, and R&D (Q2 FY26 Systems Protection: sales $1,072.1m, COGS $707.5m, SG&A $99.8m, R&D $16.6m, segment income $248.2m; Electrical Connections: $399.2m / $227.1m / $57.3m / $6.0m / $108.8m) [Q2 FY26 10-Q, Note 13]. That lets an outsider compute a segment gross margin the company never quotes — and, as §4 below shows, the answer is less flattering than the commentary. A company that hands you the numbers to check it with is not obscuring. Revenue is additionally disaggregated by segment × vertical and by segment × geography, and backlog is disclosed with a stated definition ("all orders from external customers, which represent firm commitments, and are supported by a purchase order or other legitimate contract"; $749.3m at 2024-12-31) [FY24 10-K, Item 1 — Backlog of Orders by Segment; Item 1A].

**Two things did get less granular, and neither follows the deterioration pattern the rule exists to catch.** (a) Reportable segments went from three to two — because Thermal Management was sold on 2025-01-30, which is a disposal, not a reporting choice. (b) The revenue-by-vertical disaggregation went from four categories to three in Q1 FY26: the **Energy** vertical was folded away. Test it properly — Energy was **rising**, not deteriorating: $60.5m (FY2022) → $69.8m (FY2023) → $81.7m (FY2024), and it was 2.7% of FY2024 sales; the company stated the reason ("based on benchmarking of industry peers and for purposes of how we assess performance") and **restated the prior-year comparatives** so the series stays comparable, noting the change "had no impact on our consolidated financial results" [FY24 10-K, Note — Vertical net sales; Q2 FY26 10-Q, Note 2]. A category dropped while growing, with comparatives restated, is the opposite of RF-MGT-003.

**The one genuine transparency gap is a KPI that only exists outside the filings.** "New Product Vitality" — quoted as **27% of sales, "up +7pp from last Investor Day"** [2026 William Blair deck, slide 6] — and its call-level companion, "new products contributed over 30 points to our sales growth" in Q2 (">20 points" in Q1) [Q2 FY26 transcript, prepared remarks; Q1 FY26 investor deck, Highlights], carry **no disclosed definition anywhere in the pool**: no stated window for what counts as "new," no reconciliation to any filed line, and no appearance in the 10-K or either 10-Q. That metric is used to attribute the majority of a 53%-growth quarter, and a reader cannot audit it. Separately and more mildly, nVent **names no competitor anywhere in its own filings** — the competition section is entirely generic [FY24 10-K, Item 1 — Competition; business-model/08_competitive-map.md, header], and management declined to break the $2bn data-centre revenue between liquid cooling and the rest ("certainly, we haven't broken that down yet") [Q2 FY26 transcript, Q&A — Jeff Hammond]. Neither is unusual for a US industrial; both are places where the reader is asked to take a number on trust.

## 4. Tone in Bad Times

**The honest headline: the worst period in this pool is not a bad period.** Both available transcripts cover quarters with 47–53% sales growth and record earnings, so the tone read is necessarily made on a bull-market sample. Within that sample, the tone is specific rather than promotional — inflation offsets are quantified to the dollar ("price plus productivity offset inflation of nearly $60 million, including approximately $40 million in tariff impact" in Q1; "more than $50 million… more than $30 million in tariff impact" in Q2), the all-in tariff burden was given on the spot when asked ("incremental is $80 million this year followed from $90 last year. So, $170 million all-in… the US tariff environment remains highly fluid"), and the raised tariff cost ($80m → $100m) was announced with its reason ("largely, this is driven by our significantly higher volume growth") rather than slipped in. The plainest tell is the refusal to speculate favourably: asked whether tariff relief might be coming, the CEO said only "We've kept to our current outlook, and I guess we'll wait and see."

**Q&A was mostly answered directly, with one exception that an analyst named on the record.** On the Q1 call Jeff Sprague of Vertical Research put it explicitly — "I think, somebody tap-danced around this a little bit. Right? But you clearly would have had factory inefficiencies in the quarter. You also would have had more inflation than you expected" — and the CFO's reply addressed neither factory inefficiencies nor the unexpected inflation, opening instead with "we were very pleased with the margin expansion that we saw in the quarter from Systems Protection." That is a deflection, and it is worth more than its size because it is the only hard question in the pool. A softer version appeared in Q2, when Jeff Sprague asked why the guide implies **sequentially declining** revenue in Q3 and more so in Q4: the CFO answered by switching metrics to the two-year stack ("we're going to significantly accelerate 50% in Q3 at the midpoint versus 46% growth in the first half") without addressing the sequential decline — though the CEO then answered it squarely ("this is just our planning, and we're being very prudent… as we ramp 2 new facilities").

**One place where the commentary is more flattering than the company's own segment data, and it should be named rather than averaged away (CLAUDE.md §3).** Management attributed Systems Protection's Q2 ROS gain to "strong volume and productivity" — "Return on sales of 23.2% increased 150 basis points year-over-year on strong volume and productivity" [Q2 FY26 transcript, prepared remarks]. Do the arithmetic from the segment note the company itself now publishes: Systems Protection **gross margin FELL 246 bps**, from 36.47% (230.5 ÷ 632.0) to 34.01% (364.6 ÷ 1,072.1). The ROS gain came entirely from operating leverage on fixed costs below the gross line — SG&A fell 363 bps as a share of sales (12.94% → 9.31%) and R&D 29 bps (1.84% → 1.55%). The build reconciles exactly: −246 + 363 + 29 = **+146 bps**, which is the "150 basis points" quoted [Q2 FY26 10-Q, Note 13 — derived]. The 10-Q's own MD&A *does* disclose the gross-margin drivers honestly (inflation, unfavourable product mix, capacity investment, partly offset by volume leverage and productivity), so the filing is candid and the call statement is not false — "productivity" is a genuine named offset. But a listener told the margin rose on "volume and productivity" would not know the gross line went backwards by 246 bps. **That is selective emphasis, not a contradiction: RF-DISC-001 is tested and does NOT trigger.**

## 5. Candor Read

**Conclusion: mixed, leaning candid — with the decisive test unrun.** On everything that can be checked, this management tells the truth in a checkable way: it puts the bad number before the mitigation (Electrical Connections ROS −390 bps, then the copper explanation, then a dated forward promise it went on to keep sequentially); it separates bought growth from earned growth in the one year earned growth was negative (FY2024 EFS: volume −1.7%, price +0.2%, organic −1.5%, acquisition +12.8%); it raised guidance three times while restating the prior range each time and disclosing the tariff cost going up with it; it leaves stock-based compensation inside adjusted earnings; and in Q2 FY26 it removed a $25.8m GAAP *benefit* from its own adjusted numbers, publishing adjusted EBITDA below GAAP EBITDA. It also now publishes segment cost lines detailed enough for an outsider to catch it framing a margin gain generously — which is what §4 above did.

The strongest evidence the other way is threefold and none of it is trivial: **four "one-off" adjustment categories recur in every single year on record**, holding adjusted EPS 28.8% above audited GAAP EPS in FY2025 with 87% of the gap being the amortisation cost of the acquisition engine itself; a headline growth-attribution KPI ("New Product Vitality," 27%; "new products contributed over 30 points to our sales growth") exists only in decks and calls with **no definition and no reconciliation to any filed number**; and the only genuinely hard analyst question in the pool drew a non-answer that the analyst himself labelled "tap-danced around."

**The single biggest candor signal is the one I could not test.** Both verbatim transcripts in this pool cover quarters that beat the top of guidance by 17.2% and 26.1%; the one real miss in the eight-quarter record — Q3 FY2024, −22.2% on adjusted EPS — has no transcript, no letter and no 8-K here. Candor is measured in bad times, and this company's bad time is missing from the evidence. No adverse integrity signal was routed from `business-model/01_disqualifier-scan` (it states explicitly that none was found), so nothing compounds against the trust read under CLAUDE.md §24 Filter 1 — but nothing resolves the untested half of it either. Treat the candid read as **provisional and cycle-conditioned**, and re-test it on the first quarter this company misses.

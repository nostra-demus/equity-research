# Accounting Forensics — NVT

**Company:** nVent Electric plc (NYSE: NVT). **Jurisdiction / regime:** US SEC domestic filer, Irish-incorporated. **Reporting standard:** US GAAP. **Currency: USD, in millions except per-share.** **Fiscal year ends 31 December.**

**Evidence binding.** All reads went through the frozen extract generation `6db32848…1aecd1e6` (`manifest.json`, `corpus.txt`, `ciq_facts.json`, `relationships.json` and its `extract` references). Live `data/NVT/` and `analyses/NVT_2026-09-07/_pool_extracts/` were NOT read; `data/NVT/…` below is a citation label only. `NOSTRA_CONTINUATION_RUN_ROOT=analyses/NVT_2026-09-07`, so `balance-sheet-survival/01` was resolved inside that exact root and no prior run was searched.

**Plain-English glossary for the technical terms used below (§21).** **CFO** = cash from operations, the cash the business actually collected from trading, $501.0m in FY2024. **EBITDA** = operating profit before the non-cash charges for wearing out plant and writing down acquired intangibles, $675.6m in FY2024. **Accruals** = the part of reported profit that has not yet turned into cash. **M-score** (Beneish) = an 8-input statistical screen for earnings manipulation; NVT computes at **−2.67**, and more negative is cleaner. **F-score** (Dechow) = a misstatement-probability screen scaled so 1.00 is the average listed company; NVT computes at **1.92**. **RSST accruals** = the total accrual across working capital, long-lived operating items and financial assets, **2.23%** of average assets here. **Soft assets** = the share of the balance sheet that is neither cash nor plant — i.e. the part built on estimates — **92.9%** here. **Effective tax rate (ETR)** = tax charge ÷ pre-tax profit, **43.9%** in FY2024. **Net debt (strict basis, §15)** = total borrowings − cash, **$1,236.4m** at 30-Jun-2026.

Anyone quoting a Beneish score above the cutoff for nVent's FY2025 has almost certainly made this mismatch.

**Sensitivity on the two vendor-dependent inputs.** Setting `ΔCASH_SALES` and `ΔROA` to zero gives **F = 1.80** — Amber, not Red. So the vendor-sourced inputs move the composite by 0.12 and sit on either side of the 1.85 line. The composite's placement is therefore not robust to that one basis choice, and this is stated rather than rounded away.

**Decomposition — what actually carries the 1.92.** The computation was re-run with terms switched off:

| Configuration | F-score |
|---|---:|
| Every accrual input at nVent's actual value, but soft assets = 0 and no issuance | **0.11** |
| Soft assets and the issuance dummy at nVent's values, every accrual input = 0 | **1.76** |
| Soft assets alone (no issuance) | **0.63** |
| A company with every input at zero and no issuance (the model's floor) | **0.10** |

**Read that table before reading the headline.** nVent's *accrual* content — the part of the model that measures profit booked ahead of cash — scores **0.11, about one-tenth of an average company**. The entire trip over 1.85 comes from two structural terms: a balance sheet that is 92.9% soft assets (weight 1.979, contributing +1.838) and a binary dummy for having issued any security at all (weight 1.029). A company with perfectly clean accruals that is intangible-heavy and issues a bond scores ~1.76 before it books a single dollar of anything. That is the model behaving as specified, not a finding about nVent.

**What the soft-asset number does mean.** It is not nothing. At 31-Dec-2024, goodwill of $2,221.8m plus net intangibles of $1,587.0m is **$3,808.8m — 56.6% of total assets and 117.6% of the $3,237.6m of shareholders' equity** [S1, p.41]. Add $1,276.1m of assets held for sale and the non-cash, non-plant share reaches 92.9%. Concretely: most of what nVent's balance sheet says it owns is management's estimate of the future cash flows of businesses it bought, tested annually for impairment — and Deloitte's single Critical Audit Matter for FY2024 was precisely that class of estimate (the $206.6m Trachte customer-relationship intangible, valued on a multi-period excess-earnings model with management-set margin, growth, discount and attrition assumptions) [S1, Report of Independent Registered Public Accounting Firm, 18-Feb-2025]. That is an impairment and valuation exposure, and it belongs in the risk register. It is not evidence that revenue or profit is fabricated, and the accrual half of this same battery says the opposite.

**Corroboration, FY2025, tier-5 vendor basis:** **F = 1.91**, also above the 1.85 line, on RSST 6.01% (Amber, between the 3% and 10% lines), ΔREC 4.80%, ΔINV 1.64%, soft assets **88.07% — falling** from 92.89%, ΔROA +5.31pp, ISSUE = 1 ($275.0m of long-term debt drawn) [S4]. The ΔREC of 4.80% sits just under its 5% Red line, and the band's own carve-out applies: stripping the $97.7m of receivables acquired with EPG takes it to **3.36%** [S3, Note on Acquisitions]. The one input that got worse — RSST 6.01% — is the EPG purchase accounting itself: $975.7m of cash acquisitions adding goodwill and intangibles to `ΔNCO` [S4, CIQ Cash Flow, `Cash Acquisitions`].

**A8-18 graded Amber, and why not Red.** The letter of the Red band is met — securities were issued in a year whose F composite is ≥1.85. The band's stated purpose is "books dressed for a raise," and every piece of evidence points the other way: TATA is **−0.0386** (cash exceeded reported profit by $260.2m, the opposite of the flagged direction), CFO/PAT was **2.08×**, cash taxes paid rose to $120.2m, and the $500.0m issue is disclosed acquisition financing for Trachte, a deal whose purchase accounting Deloitte examined as its Critical Audit Matter. The trigger is also partly circular here: the issuance dummy is itself one of the two terms that pushes F over 1.85. Graded **Amber**, with the divergence stated rather than resolved silently. **RF-ACC-004 is not emitted.** It would be emitted if a future year showed issuance alongside a positive TATA or a materially higher RSST.

## 12. Forensics Read

**The accrual evidence says these books are clean, and it says so on every measure that matters.** The Beneish M-score computed from the FY2024 10-K is **−2.67 with zero of seven components in the manipulator zone** — receivables fell relative to sales, gross margin was flat, the depreciation rate accelerated, and total accruals were **negative $260.2m** (cash exceeded reported profit). CFO/PAT has run 0.92–2.08× for five years, CFO/EBITDA 69–79% for four, cash taxes paid rose 37.7% while revenue rose 31.0%, the $2.5bn backlog management quotes is the same number the 10-Q discloses as a contractual ASC 606 performance obligation, and the effective tax rate in the last audited year was **43.9%, eighteen points above statutory** — fabricated profit does not pay $120.2m of cash tax at above the statutory rate.

**The one number that trips a red band is the Dechow F-score at 1.92, and the decomposition matters more than the headline.** Re-running the model with terms switched off shows nVent's *accrual* content scores **0.11 — about a tenth of an average company**; the entire trip over 1.85 comes from two structural terms, a balance sheet that is 92.9% soft assets and a binary dummy for having issued any security at all, which together produce ~1.76 before a single dollar is booked. Set the two vendor-dependent inputs to zero and F falls to 1.80, below the line. So RF-ACC-001 is recorded honestly, and it is equally honestly reported as a model artefact of an intangible-heavy acquirer rather than a manipulation signal. **The Score Cap Rules floor does not apply** — it requires RF-ACC-001 with ≥3 Beneish manipulator-zone components (nVent has zero) or a cash-authenticity failure (none).

**The real finding underneath that artefact is worth keeping.** Goodwill plus intangibles of $3,808.8m is **117.6% of shareholders' equity**, tangible book value is **negative $571.2m**, and Deloitte's single Critical Audit Matter was exactly this class of estimate. Most of what this balance sheet says nVent owns is management's forecast of what four acquired businesses will earn, and the Maverick Power deal will add more. That is an impairment and valuation exposure for the risk register — not a revenue-recognition one. The three softer signals sit alongside it: bad-debt coverage has fallen from 2.93% to 1.69% of receivables while the book doubled (small, ~2% of profit, but moving the wrong way); "one-off" restructuring and acquisition costs recur every single year; and leverage touched the 3.00× Red line at FY2024, was rescued by a divestiture rather than by repayment, and is heading back to ~2.4× on the next deal — a capital-allocation question for `02`, not an accounting one.

**What a rational minority holder should conclude, and what should keep them honest.** On the evidence available, nVent's reported earnings are confirmed by its cash, its revenue is confirmed by its tax and its collections, its order book is contractual, and its auditor gave a clean opinion on both the accounts and the internal controls. The binding limitation is not the company's — it is the pool's: **the entire primary battery runs on a 20-month-old FY2024 Form 10-K, because the FY2025 10-K (filed 17-Feb-2026) was never collected.** The FY2025 corroboration is tier-5 vendor data, and on that basis the M-score drifts into Amber at −2.07, driven by an acquisition's opening receivables. Collect the FY2025 10-K and re-run both batteries before anyone treats this Green as current.

## 3. Most Severe New Flag

**The measure that sets management's incentive-compensation targets excludes the amortisation of the intangibles that management's own acquisitions create.** The synthesizer should weight this because it converts `11`'s §24 Filter-4 finding from a *pattern* into a *mechanism*. `11` established that nVent spent $3,017m on acquisitions FY2021–FY2025 against $2,091m of free cash flow, sold a whole reporting segment to help pay, and has $4,469.6m of goodwill and intangibles on a $7,146.3m balance sheet — but with no proxy in the pool it could not test pay design at all and scored the related rows at the conservative default. The filing itself supplies the missing piece: reportable segment income is defined "exclusive of intangible amortization, acquisition related costs, costs of restructuring activities… impairments", and the CEO uses that measure for "setting incentive compensation targets" [Q2 FY26 10-Q, Note 13, p.19–20; identical in FY24 10-K, Note 15, p.69]. In the first half of 2026 that removed **$100.6m of cost — 15.6% of the $644.9m reported as segment income** — and the amortisation piece alone grew 28.2% year on year *because of* the Electrical Products Group deal. The structural consequence is the one that matters: **the more the company buys, the more flattering its own performance yardstick becomes, and an impairment on a bad deal would be excluded from the measure too.** Nothing here is hidden — the full reconciliation to income before income taxes is printed, and this is a common industrial practice, which is why the severity is 55 and not higher. But it is the reason a synthesizer should not treat rising *segment* income as independent confirmation that the acquisition programme is working; the confirmation has to come from consolidated returns on the whole capital base, which `11` measures at 7.2% in FY2025 rising to 9.5% LTM only alongside a demand surge.

`RF-RFS-001 (aggressive accounting practice pattern)`

*The tag is emitted for the second Section 2 row (severity 52): the $93.2m Luxembourg deferred-tax-asset benefit recognised in FY2023 and written off by a $92.8m valuation allowance in FY2024 — a "more likely than not" recognition judgment that reversed on 99.6% of its value within twelve months after flattering FY2023 by 24.8% of that year's pre-tax income — together with the pension mark-to-market swing, the $12.5m guarantee-liability release booked to income, and the $25.8m IEEPA reimbursement recognised in Q2 FY26 gross profit against unnamed and unsized offsetting tariffs. Emitted once, per the rule. It is a source emission for the cross-module forensic mosaic (CLAUDE.md §13), not a claim of concealment: every item is disclosed with its amount and its reason, none is cash, and the FY2024 audit opinion and internal-control opinion are both unqualified.*

## 4. Cross-Cutting Patterns

**One pattern, and it is about where the growth is being recorded versus where it is being paid for.** Take five of the six rows above together with what `11` already found. Capital goes out as acquisitions ($3,017m FY2021–FY2025, plus $1.75bn agreed for Maverick) and, increasingly, into working capital that customers used to fund (contract liabilities −9.6% while revenue +53.1%; receivable days 61.6 → 76.1). The cost of that capital — $82.2m of intangible amortisation in six months, up 28.2% — is then removed from the profit measure the CEO uses to set bonus targets. Meanwhile the spending that would build growth organically instead of buying it is shrinking as a share of sales (R&D 2.2% → 1.74%), and the physical capacity behind the fastest-growing product line is concentrated in three sites in one Minnesota metro area with a plan that reaches only 2028. None of these is a violation of anything; each is disclosed. Together they describe a business whose reported operating performance is being measured on a basis that systematically excludes the cost of the strategy producing it.

**The number that argues against the pattern, named (§3).** Consolidated cash generation has not broken: LTM operating cash flow of $690.6m is up 48.3%, LTM free cash flow (cash from operations minus total capex, §15) is $577.7m, net debt/EBITDA fell from 3.07x at Dec-2024 to 1.24x at Jun-2026, and no goodwill impairment has ever been recorded [figures as compiled in `11_capital-allocation-governance.md` §1 and §3 from the Capital IQ Cash Flow, Balance Sheet and Ratios tabs — tier-5 vendor export; "no impairment expense recorded in 2024, 2023 or 2022 related to goodwill", FY24 10-K, Note 2]. That is a genuine counterweight and it stops the pattern short of a governance-level finding. It does not dissolve it, for two reasons: the cash strength is being generated inside the same demand surge that makes every bought business look good, and the FY2025 10-K's absence means the last full audited year of it cannot be verified from this pool at all.

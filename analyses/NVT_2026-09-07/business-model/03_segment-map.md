# Segment Map — NVT

**Regime:** US SEC domestic filer (Form 10-K / 10-Q), US GAAP, reporting currency USD in millions, fiscal year ends 31 December. No FX conversion is used anywhere in this report. [FY24 10-K, cover page; Q2 FY26 10-Q, cover page]

**Evidence note (read this before the table).** The FY2025 Form 10-K is **not in the data pool**, so there is no audited FY2025 segment note available to this run [`analyses/NVT_2026-09-07/business-model/00_data-triage.md`, §3]. Three different periods are therefore cited below, each at its own evidence tier, and they are never blended:
- **H1 FY26 (six months ended 2026-06-30)** — a filing, but **interim and unaudited** [Q2 FY26 10-Q, Note 13, p.20–22]. This is the most recent and most relevant picture.
- **FY2025** — **Capital IQ vendor export (tier 5), not a filing** [Capital IQ, `nVent Electric plc NYSE NVT Financials.xls`, `Segments` tab, column 12 months Dec-31-2025].
- **FY2024** — the only **audited annual** segment note in the pool [FY24 10-K, Note 15 (Segment Information), p.69–71].

## 2. Dominant Segment

**Systems Protection dominates, on both measures, and its dominance is recent and accelerating.** It is 72.5% of revenue and 70.0% of reportable segment income in H1 FY26 [Q2 FY26 10-Q, Note 13, p.20]. Two years earlier, on the audited FY2024 numbers, the same segment was 60.7% of revenue and only 53.2% of segment income [FY24 10-K, Note 15, p.70] — so this is not a stable structure being described, it is a company that has been remade in six quarters by one end market. The engine of that shift is disclosed: Systems Protection revenue grew 72.5% in H1 FY26, of which 56.7 points were organic and 14.6 points came from the Electrical Products Group acquisition [Q2 FY26 10-Q, MD&A, p.29], and infrastructure — mostly data centres — went from 44.8% to 65.5% of the segment's sales [Q2 FY26 10-Q, Note 2, p.9].

This is **not** a single-segment business by the >85% test: Electrical Connections is still 27.5% of revenue and 30.0% of segment income. Do not treat NVT as one business.

**The number that disagrees, named (CLAUDE.md §3).** The segment that dominates is *not* the higher-margin one. Electrical Connections earns 25.9% of its sales as segment income against Systems Protection's 22.9% in H1 FY26, and it has out-earned Systems Protection on margin in every period in the tables above (FY22 27.8% vs 17.0%; FY23 31.1% vs 21.6%; FY24 30.0% vs 22.1%; FY25 28.7% vs 20.7% on vendor data; H1 FY26 25.9% vs 22.9%) [FY24 10-K, MD&A, p.25 and p.27; Q2 FY26 10-Q, MD&A, p.29–30; Capital IQ `Segments` tab]. That does not overturn the verdict — Systems Protection still produces 2.3x the absolute segment profit (451.3 vs 193.6) and all of the growth — but it does mean the mix shift is dilutive to blended margin at the segment line, and the two margin lines are moving in opposite directions (SP +1.7 points year on year, EC −2.6 points). Anyone modelling group margin from Systems Protection's improvement alone will get the wrong answer.

**The reportable-segment structure does not describe the actual driver.** The thing that is changing this company — data centres — is a *vertical* that cuts across both segments, not a segment. Management expects total data-centre sales above $2 billion in 2026, "more than double last year's sales" [Q2 FY26 transcript, prepared remarks]. Set against FY2026 guided reported sales growth of 37–39% on FY2025 revenue of 3,893.1 — that is roughly $5,334–5,411 million of group revenue — data centres would be about **37%** of the company (2,000 ÷ 5,372 at the midpoint). *That ratio is derived arithmetic from a transcript figure and a guidance range, not a disclosed number — inference, not from filings.* No audited or filed segment line isolates it, and nothing in the pool splits it between the two segments.

**One more structural change is already contracted.** On 2026-08-24 nVent agreed to buy Maverick Power — engineered power distribution and switchgear for data centres — for $1.75 billion plus up to $550 million of earn-out, with estimated 2026 revenue of roughly $700 million, funded with cash and new debt and expected to close in Q4 2026 [`nVent-to-Acquire-Maverick-Power-2026.pdf`, press release dated 2026-08-24]. The release does not name the segment it will sit in. The precedent is that the Electrical Products Group's enclosures, switchgear and bus systems were placed "predominantly within our Systems Protection reporting segment" [Q2 FY26 10-Q, MD&A, p.24], so Maverick most likely lands there too and pushes Systems Protection past 75% of revenue — *inference, not from filings.*

## 3. Segment Disclosure Quality

**Definitions changed materially in the last three years — twice — and the current definitions are only about six quarters old.** Thermal Management stopped being a reportable segment when it was agreed for sale in July 2024 and became a discontinued operation restated across all periods presented [FY24 10-K, Note 15 and Note 6]; the company went from three segments to two. Then in Q1 2025 both survivors were renamed — Enclosures to Systems Protection, Electrical & Fastening Solutions to Electrical Connections — with the FY24 10-K flagging the rename in advance and describing the same underlying businesses [FY24 10-K, Item 1, p.2 and Note 15, p.69]. Composition looks unchanged through the rename (FY2024 revenue of 1,823.3 / 1,182.8 appears identically under the old names in the 10-K and the new names in the vendor export), so the series is comparable — but a reader must not assume so from the labels alone. Separately, the *vertical* categories were re-cut in Q1 2026 for peer benchmarking, with 2025 comparatives restated and no effect on consolidated results [Q2 FY26 10-Q, Note 2, p.9] — so pre-2026 vertical splits from older documents are not comparable either.

**On the "Other" bucket: better than typical on revenue, weaker than it looks on profit.** There is no unallocated-revenue bucket at all — every dollar of sales is assigned to one of the two segments, so the revenue shares are clean. But *Enterprise and other* is a real cost pool: (73.7) in H1 FY26, equal to 2.7% of group revenue and **11.4% of reportable segment income** (73.7 / 644.9); it was (105.6) in FY2024, or 13.9% of that year's 757.6 [Q2 FY26 10-Q, Note 13, p.21; FY24 10-K, Note 15, p.70]. On top of that, **all intangible amortization is unallocated** — 82.2 in H1 FY26, another 12.7% of reportable segment income, and 94.7 in FY2024 [same sources]. For a company that has spent roughly $1.0 billion on the Electrical Products Group and has agreed $1.75 billion for Maverick Power, that matters: the segment margins in §1 are struck **before** the amortization of the intangibles those deals create, so they overstate what each segment earns on a fully loaded basis and the gap will widen as more deals close. Segment income also excludes restructuring, acquisition and integration costs, and — new in 2026 — IEEPA tariff reimbursements of 25.8 [Q2 FY26 10-Q, Note 13, p.19 and p.21]. Segment margin here is not an operating margin; do not use it as one.

**Profit is disclosed at segment level, and so is more than usual.** nVent gives, by segment: net sales, cost of goods sold, SG&A, R&D, reportable segment income, identifiable assets, depreciation and capital expenditure [Q2 FY26 10-Q, Note 13, p.20–22; FY24 10-K, Note 15, p.70–71]. It also discloses revenue **by segment and by geography** and **by segment and by vertical** — a genuinely useful second cut most industrials do not give [Q2 FY26 10-Q, Note 2, p.8–9]. Concentration is addressed: "No customer accounted for more than 10% of net sales in 2024, 2023 or 2022" [FY24 10-K, Note 15, p.70].

**What is genuinely missing, and it is the part that matters most.** (a) **No audited FY2025 segment note is in the pool** — the FY2025 10-K is absent, so every FY2025 segment figure in this report is a tier-5 vendor read, and the H1 FY26 note is unaudited [triage `00`, §3]. (b) **No segment-level cash flow, working capital, order intake or backlog.** Backlog is given only at group level ($2.5 billion), and only on an earnings call, not in a filing [Q2 FY26 transcript, prepared remarks] — for a business whose growth is now driven by lumpy long-cycle project orders, the absence of backlog by segment is a real gap. (c) **No disclosure of the data-centre business as a reportable line**, despite management sizing it above $2 billion for 2026; it is visible only through the infrastructure vertical, which also contains power utilities and other non-data-centre work. (d) **No segment split for the announced Maverick Power acquisition.** (e) Amortization is disclosed by segment only as *depreciation*; the amortization component sits unallocated. Downstream agents (`unit-economics`, `competitive-map`) should treat FY2025 segment profitability as vendor-sourced, treat H1 FY26 as unaudited, and treat any per-segment cash-return or backlog metric as **Not disclosed** rather than estimating one.

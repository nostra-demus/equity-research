# Business Model Reality Check — NVT (Synthesis)

## Abstract

nVent Electric makes the enclosures, cooling units, switchgear and connectors that house and link electrical equipment, sold mostly through electrical distributors and, increasingly, as engineered data-centre projects. Systems Protection now supplies 72.5% of revenue and 70.0% of reportable segment income. The strongest positive is growth: 46.9% organic in Q2 FY26, three to six times faster than every named peer. The strongest negative is that the growth has not yet earned its keep — through-cycle return on capital of 8.1% (computed NOPAT basis at a normalised 22.0% tax rate, FY2022–FY2025) sits about 190 basis points below the company's own disclosed 10.0% cost of capital. No automatic disqualifier triggered. The verdict is cyclical: worth deeper work only with a strong timing edge.

### Module Disconfirmation (CLAUDE.md §8)

- **Strongest bear point.** Through-cycle return on capital is **8.1%** (FY2022–FY2025 average, computed NOPAT basis at a normalised 22.0% tax rate) against the company's **own disclosed 10.0% weighted-average cost of capital** — a gap of about −190 basis points. It clears the rate only in the peak LTM year, only on the more generous of two measurement bases (11.88% computed; the vendor basis is 9.54%, still −46bp short), and management's own M&A slide sets the test as "Target ROIC > WACC in 3 years" — a statement in plain terms that the deals do not clear the cost of capital today [`09_moat.md` §3; FY24 10-K, MD&A — Critical Accounting Estimates, p.32; Q1 FY26 earnings presentation, 2026-05-01, slide 9].
- **Strongest bull point (the steelman, and I land bearish anyway).** The built business is genuinely value-creating and the arithmetic is not close: about **$0.10 of capex plus ~$0.21 of working capital per $1 of new annual revenue**, returning about **$0.254 of segment income a year** — roughly a 1.2-year pre-tax payback, which survives a 20% adverse move in every input tested [`04_unit-economics.md` §2 and §4]. And the share gain is real on delivered revenue: **+46.9% group organic in Q2 FY26** against Eaton Electrical Americas +18%, Hubbell +10.2%, Legrand +9.8% — three to six times every named rival, in an end market all of them are chasing [`08_competitive-map.md` §3].
- **Single killer risk.** Fixed capacity committed a year ahead of a lumpy order book. Three Minnesota liquid-cooling plants (the third, "Blaine 2", opening H1 2027), capex up 40%, and $1.75bn of cash and new debt for Maverick — against a backlog that gives visibility only "into 2027" and that fell sequentially in the last reported quarter. A volume shortfall hits twice: it strips the roughly 3.9 points of SG&A and R&D leverage that produced the entire segment-margin gain, and it strands capacity already paid for [`04_unit-economics.md` §4; `12_red-flags-sweep.md` §2, severity 45].
- **Disconfirming evidence already visible in the specialist outputs.** Three items, each already in the upstream work and none of them hypothetical. (a) **Order growth decelerated from about +40% in Q1 FY26 to "low double digits" in Q2 FY26, and backlog fell $2.6bn → $2.5bn**, while quarterly sales grew 47% organically — the order line already disagrees with the revenue line [`10_external-dependency.md` §1]. (b) **Gross margin per unit fell 2.1 points year on year** (36.3% → 34.2% in Systems Protection) while segment margin rose 1.7 points — every unit is less profitable before overheads than a year ago, and the margin gain is borrowed from volume [`04_unit-economics.md` §3]. (c) **The dominant segment is not the higher-margin one:** Electrical Connections earns 25.9% of sales as segment income against Systems Protection's 22.9%, and has out-earned it in every disclosed period — so the mix shift is dilutive to blended segment margin [`03_segment-map.md` §2].

## 3. Reconciliation

Five material items. Each is adjudicated, not averaged.

**(1) Fast growth versus sub-cost-of-capital through-cycle returns — the central tension, and it is not split.**
`08_competitive-map.md` finds nVent growing three to six times faster than every named peer. `09_moat.md` finds through-cycle return on capital of 8.1% (computed NOPAT, normalised 22.0% tax rate, FY2022–FY2025) against a company-disclosed 10.0% WACC, and `07_business-quality.md` scores quality at 41 with Filter 5 tripped. These are not in conflict and must not be netted into "mixed". **They are the same fact seen twice: nVent is buying and building revenue faster than anyone, at a return that does not yet cover what the money costs.** The reconciled view: growth is the strongest thing about this business and it is real on delivered revenue, but growth is not evidence of a moat, and the specific test it has failed is the decisive one — economic profit. Three supports: (a) the FY2025 return of 9.00% computed / 7.21% vendor is the first year clean of Thermal Management on the earnings side, is **not** a peak year, and is still below 10.0%; (b) management's own capital-allocation slide sets the M&A hurdle as "Target ROIC > WACC in 3 years", which concedes the deals do not clear it today [Q1 FY26 earnings presentation, 2026-05-01, slide 9]; (c) `09_moat.md`'s pro-forma arithmetic shows the peak-year spread of +47bp is erased by the Maverick purchase entering the capital base, and goes to −30bp with the earn-out. The share-gain read survives — on delivered revenue, labelled inference with no disclosed share figure — but it is explicitly **not** allowed to lift the moat verdict, and `08` itself names the three numbers that disagree with it (base effect, Eaton's $15.3bn backlog, and decelerating orders).

**(2) Two return-on-capital bases in circulation. Both are shown; the conservative one governs the verdict.**
`04_unit-economics.md`, `07_business-quality.md` and `08_competitive-map.md` all use Capital IQ's vendor "Return on Capital %" (9.5% LTM; 7.2% FY2025; 6.2–6.3% FY2022–24). `09_moat.md` reverse-engineered that line and found its numerator is struck **after** interest, which charges the cost of debt against an operating return; it therefore also computed a standard NOPAT ÷ average invested capital series (11.88% LTM; 9.00% FY2025; **8.10% through-cycle FY2022–FY2025**). **Reconciled view: both bases are reported side by side wherever the number appears, and the verdict is decided on whichever is less favourable at each test.** The computed basis is the more generous one, and the company still fails on it through the cycle (8.10% vs 10.0%); on the vendor basis it fails even at the LTM peak (9.54% vs 10.0%). No averaging, no split. The 22.0% normalised tax rate behind the computed series is itself a stated adjustment — it strips FY2024's $92.8m valuation-allowance charge, FY2023's roughly $174m Swiss deferred benefit, and the structurally unrepeatable pre-2024 rates of 12.8–14.9% superseded by OECD Pillar II — and that basis travels with the number here and downstream.

## 4. Note To The Final Synthesizer

- **Strongest business-model positive — the thing nVent actually does better than anyone named.** It converts capital into revenue extraordinarily cheaply when it builds rather than buys: roughly **$0.10 of capex plus $0.21 of working capital per $1 of new annual revenue, returning about $0.254 of segment income a year** — a payback a bit over a year pre-tax, which survives a 20% adverse move in every input tested [`04_unit-economics.md` §2, §4]. ## 5. Simple Summary

- **What it does.** Makes the metal and composite boxes, cooling units, switchgear and connectors that house, cool, connect and protect electrical equipment — brands HOFFMAN, SCHROFF, TRACHTE, CADDY, ERICO, ILSCO. Increasingly it also designs and builds whole engineered power and cooling systems for data centres.
- **How it makes money.** It sells physical units at a price. Two shapes: short-cycle catalogue parts shipped through electrical distributors within 90 days, and long-cycle engineered projects delivered out of a backlog. Growth splits in the company's own filing as organic + acquisitions + currency — Q2 FY26 was 52.8% total, of which 46.9 points organic.
- **Does each new unit create value? Yes when it builds, unproven when it buys.** Building a dollar of new annual revenue costs about $0.10 of capex plus $0.21 of working capital and returns about $0.254 a year — paid back in a bit over a year. Buying that same dollar cost $2.50 at Maverick Power — 25 times more. At group level, the through-cycle return on capital of 8.1% is about 190 basis points **below** what the company itself says its capital costs (10.0%).
- **Which segment matters most.** Systems Protection — 72.5% of revenue, 70.0% of segment profit, and essentially all the growth. Awkward detail: it is the **lower**-margin of the two segments, so the shift toward it dilutes blended segment margin.
- **Does it have a moat, and against whom? No moat proven.** The best source is its distributor channel, scored 45 out of 100 — and Hubbell, Legrand and ABB all use the same channel. Against Rittal, Hubbell, Legrand and Eaton it earns **less** on every measure that could be sourced, while growing three to six times faster. On four of the things a moat is usually made of, nVent's own annual filing says it is "not materially dependent" on them.
- **What external variables it depends on.** Above all, how much money hyperscalers and cloud operators decide to spend on data centres — about 94% of last quarter's organic growth came from that one vertical, and 85% of sales are in the Americas with almost no long-term contracts behind them. Then trade policy: roughly $190m of tariff cost, about 3.5% of guided sales. Then steel, copper and aluminium prices, which are **not** financially hedged at all.
- **Is capital allocated well? No — this is the weak point.** It spent $3,017m buying companies over five years while generating $2,091m of free cash flow, sold an entire division to help pay, and now has 63% of its balance sheet in goodwill and intangibles. Leverage is low today at 1.24x, but management has said it wants to run at 2.0–2.5x. The clean parts are genuinely clean: unqualified audit, same auditor since 2017, no restatement, share count down 3.4%, dividend covered five times over.
- **Does it deserve deeper work? Yes — but as a timing bet on the data-centre build, not as a durable compounder.** No disqualifier fired, the books are clean, and the growth is real. What is missing is proof that the growth earns its cost of capital outside a boom. The next test lands in late October 2026: backlog and gross margin, read together.

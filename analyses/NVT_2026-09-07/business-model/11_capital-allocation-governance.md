## 2. Classification

**Capital allocation concerns.**

Governance mechanics are clean on every item the pool can actually test: an unqualified audit opinion and an unqualified opinion on internal controls from Deloitte & Touche LLP, no material weakness, no error-correction restatement, no auditor change, no dilution (share count down 3.4% since FY2021), a dividend covered roughly five times by operating cash flow, and falling leverage. None of that supports a "governance red flags" label.

But the *use of capital* is a different question, and it fails the standing serial-acquirer test (CLAUDE.md §24, Filter 4). Over 2021–2025 nVent spent $3,017m of cash on acquisitions against cumulative free cash flow of $2,091m for the same five years (cumulative cash from operations $2,404.3m minus cumulative capital spending $312.9m) [CIQ Financials→Cash Flow, FY2021–FY2025 columns] — that is, the deals consumed more than everything the business generated, and the gap was bridged with debt and with the $1.7bn sale of the Thermal Management segment (completed 2025-01-30) [FY2024 10-K, Item 1 and Note 6]. Add the pending $1.75bn Maverick Power purchase (plus up to $550m of earnout) and the four-year deal tally reaches roughly $4.5bn.

The opportunity cost is not hypothetical here: a whole reporting segment was sold, and the proceeds recycled into a bought business. Filter 4 names exactly this — selling the geese to buy a hen. The 2026-08-24 release justifies Maverick with "great fit", "aligns with our strategy" and "complementing nVent's data center offerings"; under §24 that language is not evidence of value creation, and the only hard number offered is a purchase multiple of ~11.5x anticipated 2026 adjusted EBITDA (~$152m implied on the $1.75bn price — my arithmetic from the release's own two figures).

Nor is this "standard professional management". A typical listed industrial does not turn 63% of its balance sheet into goodwill and other intangibles ($2,676.3m + $1,793.3m = $4,469.6m against $7,146.3m of total assets at 30-Jun-2026, more than total equity of $3,986.9m) [CIQ Financials→Balance Sheet]. On the EPG deal, $875.4m of the $979.6m price — 89% — landed in goodwill and intangibles rather than tangible assets [Q2 FY26 10-Q, Note 5].

## 3. Most Material Signal

**The acquisition pattern, and specifically what the next deal does to the balance sheet.** Today's leverage genuinely is low — net debt/EBITDA of 1.24x at 30-Jun-2026, against 3.07x at Dec-2024 [CIQ Financials→Ratios] — and that is the single fact holding this classification out of the "governance red flags" band. But the CFO told the July call that 1.2x is "well below our target range of 2 to 2.5x, providing ample flexibility to invest in growth and acquisitions" and that the M&A pipeline is "very good" [Q2 FY26 earnings call, 2026-07-31, prepared remarks and Q&A]. That is a stated intention to re-lever. Doing the arithmetic on Maverick: $1.75bn funded from $256m of cash plus new debt takes net debt to roughly $3.1bn against pro-forma EBITDA of about $1,227m (LTM $1,074.6m plus the ~$152m implied for Maverick), i.e. about 2.5x — the top of their own target range, before any of the $550m earnout is paid *(Inference, not from filings — my calculation from the cited CIQ balance-sheet, CIQ EBITDA and news-release figures; it holds LTM EBITDA static and so is a bound, not a forecast, since 2026 EBITDA is still growing)*. If a further deal of Maverick's size were announced before that leverage came back down, or if any of these businesses required a goodwill write-down (there has been none to date: "no impairment expense recorded in 2024, 2023 or 2022 related to goodwill" [FY2024 10-K, Note 2]), the classification would move to governance-level concern and the score would fall further.

**The number that argues the other way, named.** The returns data currently supports management, not the sceptic: return on capital rose from 6.3% (FY2024) to 9.5% (LTM Jun-2026) and normalised diluted EPS from $1.62 to $2.92 over the same span [CIQ Financials→Ratios, Return on Capital %; CIQ Financials→Income Statement, Normalized Diluted EPS]. Two things stop that from overturning the read. First, return on capital sat at 6.2–6.3% right through FY2022–FY2024 — the years the ECM and Trachte money was going out — which is a weak return on each dollar invested by any plausible cost-of-capital standard, and the improvement only arrived with the FY2025–FY2026 data-centre demand surge (revenue +46% year on year in the LTM period) rather than with proven integration. Second, a bought-growth record measured during an unusually strong end-market cycle is exactly the kind of evidence Filter 4 tells you not to accept at face value.

**Working capital, adjudicated.** Receivable days rose 14.5 days from FY2024, which reads badly on its own. Against it: inventory days *fell* 17.2 days, inventory turnover improved from 4.99x to 6.50x, the full cash-conversion cycle widened only 3.6 days, and LTM operating cash flow rose 48.3% to $690.6m [CIQ Financials→Ratios; CIQ Financials→Cash Flow]. The FY2025 dip in operating cash flow to $465.2m is also less alarming than it looks: it is stated after a −$183.8m cash outflow from discontinued operations, most plausibly tax on the Thermal Management sale [CIQ Financials→Cash Flow, "Net Cash From Discontinued Ops.", FY2025]. So the receivables build is a project-mix and acquisition effect to watch, not evidence of a cash-conversion break — hence severity 40, not higher.

## 4. Capital Allocation Score /100

**50 / 100** (higher = better).

Build: the simple average severity across the twelve applicable rows is 29.5, which would invert to about 70. Weighting for what actually matters in this business — roughly a quarter of the weight on the acquisition-pattern row, since bought growth is where essentially all incremental capital has gone — takes the weighted severity to about 37 and the inverted score to about 63.

**Rejector-filter cap applied (CLAUDE.md §24, Filter 4; MODULE_RULES rejector table).** The acquisition-pattern row scored 72 severity — four material deals in four years (ECM ~$1.1bn, Trachte $687.5m, EPG $979.6m, Maverick $1.75bn plus up to $550m contingent), acquisition spend exceeding all internally generated free cash flow over the same five years, and a whole reporting segment divested to help fund the buying. That is a clear serial-acquirer pattern, so the Capital Allocation Score is capped at **50/100** regardless of the clean audit, the shrinking share count, the covered dividend and the currently low leverage. The cap is applied here explicitly and is the binding constraint on the score.

**What would lift it:** two to three years of segment-level evidence that the acquired businesses earn their cost of capital through a normal end-market cycle (not just the current data-centre surge), leverage returning below 1.5x after Maverick closes without a new deal announced, and a proxy statement in the pool that lets the related-party, insider-ownership and board-composition rows be scored on evidence rather than left at the conservative default.

**What would lower it:** another debt-funded deal of Maverick's size before leverage normalises; any goodwill or intangible impairment; the receivables build continuing without a matching rise in operating cash flow; or disclosure that the CFO change was not routine.

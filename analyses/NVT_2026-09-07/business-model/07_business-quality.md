## 4. Read

**This is a cyclical, capital-spending-driven industrial hardware maker riding one exceptional wave — not a durable compounder, and not yet proven to be anything else.** The base business (enclosures, cable management, connectors, grounding) is a decent, boring, code-driven manufacturer competing against ABB, Eaton, Hubbell, Legrand, Hitachi Energy and Rittal, plus "thousands" of local low-cost players by the company's own count [FY24 10-K, Item 1A, p.5; Capital IQ Competitors tab]. What has changed the company is not that business — it is the AI data-centre build, which took infrastructure from 12% of sales at spin to 58.1% of H1 FY26 net sales [Q2 FY26 transcript; Q2 FY26 10-Q, Note 2, p.9], and which is delivered on lumpy orders, in one region (Americas 85.0%), with essentially no contractual duration behind it (4.7% of FY2024 sales on contracts of a year or more).

**Anchor the returns — they are at a cyclical high, and the high does not override the cyclicality row (§16, §9).** Return on capital is **9.5% LTM Jun-2026** against 6.7% / 6.2% / 6.3% / 6.3% / 7.2% in FY2021–FY2025 — a through-cycle level of roughly **6.4%** on the four pre-boom years, meaning today's figure is about **1.5x** the recent normal [Capital IQ Financials → Ratios tab — tier-5 vendor export]. EBITDA margin is 22.2% LTM against 19.7% in FY2021 [same tab]. Both peaks are explained by the same thing the Cyclicality row scores 30 for: volume running ahead of installed capacity during a build-out, with 46.9% organic growth in Q2 FY26 [Q2 FY26 10-Q, MD&A]. The two reconcile rather than contradict — a peak return earned *because* the cycle is at its peak is evidence for the cyclicality read, not against it. Downstream (`09_moat`, and the valuation layer) must not capitalise the 9.5% or the 22.2% as a through-cycle level; the pre-boom base rate is the other anchor, and note also that the returns are struck on a balance sheet that has absorbed $3.0bn of acquisitions in five years and is about to absorb $1.75bn more.

**The single quality factor to keep an eye on over the next 24 months: cyclicality made visible through infrastructure order intake against the fixed capacity now being committed against it.** Three Minnesota liquid-cooling plants are committed, the third ("Blaine 2") opening H1 2027, with FY2026 capex up 40% to roughly $130m and $1.75bn agreed for Maverick Power [Q2 FY26 transcript, prepared remarks; `nVent-to-Acquire-Maverick-Power-2026.pdf`, 2026-08-24]. That is fixed cost and fixed capital being laid down against an order book management itself describes as lumpy, with $2.5bn of visibility that runs out "into 2027". The observable test is whether backlog and infrastructure order intake keep growing after that window — and note the disclosure problem attached to it: **backlog appears in filings only annually and never by segment**, so for four of the next six quarters the only read available will be a management number on a call, not a filed one.

**§24 Filter 5 — this is a sector / technology-cycle bet, not a durable compounder.** The industry rate-of-change row scored 40 (Weak). The growth, and all of the incremental capital, is aimed at data-centre liquid cooling and power distribution — a technology the CEO puts at only "10% to 15% of cooling in data centers" today, with the rack voltage architecture (800-volt DC) still evolving and chip roadmaps being rewritten out to 2030 [Q2 FY26 transcript, Q&A]. In that setting the long-run winners are not knowable in advance, and the thesis should be carried to synthesis and to the moat agent as a **sector / technology-cycle bet with discounted moat durability and capped conviction**, not as a compounding franchise.

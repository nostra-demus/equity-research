## 1. What The Company Actually Does

nVent makes the metal and composite hardware that houses, cools, connects and protects electrical equipment: standard and custom enclosures, liquid and air cooling units, pre-built control buildings, switchgear and bus systems, power-distribution gear, plus cable-management brackets, electrical connectors, grounding and fastening parts, sold under brands including HOFFMAN, SCHROFF, TRACHTE, CADDY, ERICO and ILSCO [FY24 10-K, Item 1 — Company Overview and Business and Products; Q2 FY26 10-Q, MD&A Overview]. It designs, manufactures, markets, installs and services these products [Q2 FY26 10-Q, MD&A Overview]. Buyers are data-centre operators (the CEO names hyperscalers, "neo-clouds" and multi-tenant operators), electric utilities, industrial original-equipment makers, panel builders, electrical contractors and electricians, and a large share of volume reaches them through electrical distributors rather than direct — the FY24 10-K states that because many products sell through distributors, retail, contractors and OEMs, success also depends on the channel and distribution network [FY24 10-K, Item 1 — Competition; Q2 FY26 transcript, prepared remarks (Wozniak)]. Sales are concentrated in the Americas: of FY2025 revenue of $3,893.1m, $3,158.3m was Americas, $587.3m Europe/Middle East/Africa and $147.5m Asia-Pacific [Capital IQ Financials → Segments tab, geographic segments, FY2025 — tier-5 vendor export, not a filing]. What the customer is paying for is uptime and installed cost: keeping mission-critical electrical equipment running in harsh or dense environments, meeting safety codes and certifications, and cutting the hours an electrician spends putting the system in [FY24 10-K, Item 1 — Company Overview; 2026 William Blair deck, 2026-06-03, slide 5 "nVent Value Proposition", where the labour-saving and power-efficiency figures are marked as nVent internal estimates]. The company is a 2018 spin-out of Pentair's electrical business and has since reshaped its portfolio through deals: it bought ECM Industries for roughly $1.1bn in 2023 and Trachte for roughly $687.5m in 2024, sold the Thermal Management business on 2025-01-30 for $1.6bn net cash, bought the Avail "Electrical Products Group" (enclosures, switchgear, bus systems) on 2025-05-01 for roughly $1.0bn, and on 2026-08-24 agreed to buy Maverick Power (data-centre power distribution, roughly $700m estimated 2026 revenue) for $1.75bn plus up to $550m of earn-out tied to 2027–2028 performance [FY24 10-K, Item 1 — History and Development; Q2 FY26 10-Q, Notes 4 and 5; "nVent to Acquire Maverick Power" release, 2026-08-24]. It runs two reported segments — Systems Protection and Electrical Connections (renamed in Q1 2025 from Enclosures and Electrical & Fastening Solutions) [FY24 10-K, Item 1 — Company Overview; Q2 FY26 10-Q, MD&A Overview].

## 2. How The Company Makes Money

The company sells physical units at a price. Its own filings decompose reported growth exactly this way:

**Group reported revenue growth = organic growth (volume + price/mix) + acquisitions + currency.**
Q2 FY26: 52.8% total = 46.9% organic + 5.4% acquisition + 0.5% currency. First half FY26: 53.1% = 41.2% + 10.7% + 1.2% [Q2 FY26 10-Q, MD&A — Net sales, components of change].

Two revenue lines with different shapes sit underneath that:

- **Systems Protection — part catalogue product, increasingly engineered project work:**
  `Revenue = standard units shipped (enclosures, air/liquid cooling) × price per unit + engineered project revenue delivered out of backlog (control buildings, switchgear, modular power)`.
  Q2 FY26 net sales $1,072.1m, +69.6% year on year, of which 62.0 points organic, 7.2 points acquisition, 0.4 point currency [Q2 FY26 10-Q, MD&A — Systems Protection].
- **Electrical Connections — short-cycle, distributor-sold attachment parts:**
  `Revenue = units sold through distributors / contractors × price per unit`, book-and-ship and seasonal.
  Q2 FY26 net sales $399.2m, +20.6% year on year, of which 17.9 points organic, 1.9 points acquisition, 0.8 point currency [Q2 FY26 10-Q, MD&A — Electrical Connections]. Demand rises in the Northern-Hemisphere spring and summer [FY24 10-K, Item 1 — Seasonality].

The split between the two shapes is disclosed, not inferred: "A majority of our revenues result from orders received and products delivered in the same month and products generally ship within 90 days… However, a growing portion of our backlog, particularly in the infrastructure vertical, has a longer design and manufacturing process, and can take more than one year" [FY24 10-K, Item 1 — Backlog of Orders by Segment]. That long-cycle portion has grown fast: total backlog was $749.3m at 2024-12-31 (up from $462.8m a year earlier, mostly from the Trachte deal) [FY24 10-K, Item 1 — Backlog], and management stated backlog of $2.5bn at the Q2 FY26 call, giving visibility "through the year and into 2027" [Q2 FY26 transcript, 2026-07-31, prepared remarks (Wozniak)].

**What drives volume:** capital spending by data-centre operators and electric utilities is the swing factor — management says infrastructure organic sales "more than doubled" in Q2 FY26 and that data-centre orders "tend to be large and lumpy"; new products were said to contribute over 30 points of sales growth with 14 launched in the quarter [Q2 FY26 transcript, prepared remarks — a management claim on a call, tier 6, not independently verified in the pool]. **What drives price:** deliberate, selective list-price increases taken to recover raw-material, labour and tariff cost — the filing says organic growth "includes selective increases in selling prices" but never quantifies the price contribution separately [Q2 FY26 10-Q, MD&A — Net sales]; on the call, price *plus* productivity together were said to offset more than $50m of inflation including more than $30m of tariff impact — a combined figure, not a measured price effect [Q2 FY26 transcript, prepared remarks (Corona)]. **What drives margin:** spreading fixed factory and SG&A cost over higher volume, against input-cost inflation, tariffs, product mix and the cost of adding capacity. The two margin lines moved in opposite directions in Q2 FY26 and both belong in the read: gross margin fell 70 basis points to 37.9% (hundredths of a percent; 100bp = 1 percentage point) on inflation, unfavourable product mix and capacity investment, while operating margin rose 410 basis points to 20.4% because SG&A fell 460 basis points to 15.8% of sales on volume leverage and restructuring savings [Q2 FY26 10-Q, MD&A — Consolidated Results of Operations, Gross profit, SG&A]. Deals then add a second, separate charge below segment income: intangible amortisation of $41.1m in Q2 FY26 versus $35.9m a year earlier [Q2 FY26 10-Q, MD&A — SG&A].

## 3. Business Type Classification

Electrical-hardware manufacturer selling standard product through distributors alongside a fast-growing engineered, backlog-delivered project business, now driven mainly by data-centre and power-utility capital spending — an operating company with a capex-cycle bet embedded in it.

## 4. What Drives Variance

Volume, not price, is the dominant swing: 46.9 of the 52.8 points of Q2 FY26 revenue growth were organic, and management attributes roughly 44.0 of those points to the infrastructure vertical, where data-centre orders are described as "large and lumpy" [Q2 FY26 10-Q, MD&A — Net sales; Q2 FY26 transcript, prepared remarks]. Price is a smaller, deliberate lever pulled to recover raw-material, labour and tariff cost rather than a growth engine, and its size is not separately disclosed [Q2 FY26 10-Q, MD&A — Net sales]. Currency is minor at this scale — 0.5 point of Q2 growth and 1.2 points of first-half growth — because roughly four-fifths of revenue is Americas-based [Q2 FY26 10-Q, MD&A — Net sales; Capital IQ Financials → Segments tab, FY2025 geographic split — vendor export]. On margin, the pull is between volume leverage on one side and input-cost inflation, tariffs and product mix on the other: gross margin fell 70bp in Q2 FY26 while operating margin rose 410bp, so the whole of the margin gain came below the gross line, from SG&A leverage and restructuring savings, not from the factory [Q2 FY26 10-Q, MD&A — Gross profit, SG&A]. Acquisitions are a step-change on top of all of this (5.4 points of Q2 growth, 10.7 points of first-half growth), and they carry their own drag through intangible amortisation [Q2 FY26 10-Q, MD&A — Net sales, SG&A].

### The economic moat test (required)

nVent is an **operating company**, not a financial, so the test is **ROIC against WACC** (the profit earned on each $100 of capital invested, against what that money costs to raise), per `frameworks/SECTOR_OVERLAYS.md`'s generic operating-company row as applied in `07_business-quality.md` §2b.

> **Return on capital BELOW cost of capital through the cycle: through-cycle ROIC 8.1% (FY2022–FY2025 average, computed NOPAT basis) vs 10.0% WACC — a gap of about −190 bps.** At the cycle **peak** the LTM figure is 11.88% computed (+188 bps above) or 9.54% on the vendor basis (−46 bps below) — i.e. the company clears its cost of capital only in its best year, only on the more generous of the two measurement bases, and only by a margin that its own pending acquisition arithmetic wipes out (below). [Cost of capital: FY24 10-K, MD&A — Critical Accounting Estimates, Goodwill, p.32. Returns: computed from Capital IQ Financials → Income Statement and Balance Sheet tabs — tier-5 vendor export; vendor ROIC from Capital IQ Financials → Ratios tab.]

**Where the 10.0% comes from, and why it is used.** nVent discloses it: *"Discount rate assumptions for each reporting unit take into consideration our assessment of risks inherent in the future cash flows of the respective reporting unit and **our weighted-average cost of capital**. We utilized a **10.0% discount rate for each reporting unit** in determining the discounted cash flows in our fair value analysis"* [FY24 10-K, MD&A — Critical Accounting Estimates, Goodwill and indefinite-lived intangibles, p.32]. This is a company-disclosed, auditor-reviewed rate, and it satisfies the scope match a CGU rate normally fails: **the same single rate is applied to every reporting unit**, so it spans the whole group; it is a USD, after-tax rate on after-tax cash flows, matching the currency and tax basis of the ROIC being tested. It is the FY2024 rate, because the FY2025 10-K is absent from this pool — that is the one scope limitation on it, and it is stated rather than hidden.

**Three independent cross-checks, per CLAUDE.md §16 (the rate must be reality-tested, not merely asserted).**
1. **Management's own hurdle agrees with it.** nVent's capital-allocation slide sets the M&A test as *"Target ROIC > WACC in 3 years"* [Q1 FY26 earnings presentation, 2026-05-01, slide 9 — investor presentation, tier 7]. That is management stating in plain terms that its acquisitions do **not** clear the cost of capital today and are underwritten to do so within three years. It corroborates both the rate and the verdict.
2. **The company's own DCF frame is internally consistent** — 10.0% discount rate with a 3.0% perpetual growth rate beyond 2030 [FY24 10-K, MD&A, p.32].
3. **A lower rate would be presumed wrong.** The stock's own yields run far below 10% — LTM earnings yield 1 ÷ 47.5x P/E = **2.1%**, levered FCF yield $468.2m ÷ $27,703.6m market capitalisation = **1.69%** [Capital IQ Financials → Multiples and Key Stats tabs, share price $171.16 — tier-5 vendor export]. Per §16 that is a market pricing in growth, not evidence of a 2% cost of capital; no CAPM build is attempted here because no beta or risk-free input exists in this frozen pool, and inventing one to undercut a company-disclosed rate is exactly the defect §16 bans.

**The vendor ROIC is cross-checked against a computed one, and they diverge materially — both are shown.** The 9.54% LTM figure that `07_business-quality.md` and `08_competitive-map.md` both use is Capital IQ's "Return on Capital %". Reverse-engineering it against the pool's own statements, its numerator is **after** interest (earnings from continuing operations ÷ average capital employed: $591.0m ÷ average of $5,848.4m and $6,032.9m = 9.95%, against the vendor's 9.54% on its own averaging window). That charges the cost of debt against an operating return, which is not the standard ROIC base. On the standard base — **NOPAT ÷ average invested capital (total debt + total equity)** — the figures are higher:

**The normalized tax rate used, and what was stripped (CLAUDE.md §15; this is the anchor `valuation/04_intrinsic-dcf` §1 must reconcile to).** NOPAT above uses a **normalized structural tax rate of 22.0%**, not any single year's reported effective rate. nVent's reported effective rate was **12.8% (FY2022), −22.5% (FY2023), 43.9% (FY2024)**, 22.1% (FY2025) and 22.2% (LTM Jun-2026) [FY24 10-K, MD&A — Provision (benefit) for income taxes, p.24; Capital IQ Financials → Income Statement tab, Effective Tax Rate %]. Three distortions were stripped to get to 22.0%: **(a)** FY2024's **$92.8m non-cash charge** to establish valuation allowances on deferred tax assets, which is what drove the 66.4-point jump to 43.9% [FY24 10-K, MD&A, p.24 — "$92.8 million of non-cash expense recorded in 2024 related to the establishment of valuation allowances on deferred tax assets"]; **(b)** FY2023's roughly **$174m deferred foreign tax benefit** from a Swiss intangible step-up, which produced the negative rate [Capital IQ Financials → Income Statement tab, Total Deferred Taxes FY2023 −$174.0m; FY24 10-K, MD&A, p.24]; **(c)** the pre-2024 rates of 12.8–14.9%, which are **structurally unrepeatable** because the OECD Pillar II 15% global minimum tax took effect 1 January 2024 in jurisdictions where nVent operates and was named as a driver of the FY2024 rate rise [FY24 10-K, Item 1A — tax risk factor; MD&A, p.24]. What remains — FY2025 at 22.1% and LTM at 22.2% — is the post-Pillar-II structural rate, and **22.0% is the rate used throughout this report**. Applying a distorted rate would have moved the verdict: FY2024's reported 43.9% would have put that year's ROIC at 6.7% instead of 7.83%.

**Through-cycle, not a single peak year (required).** `07_business-quality.md` scores cyclicality at **30/100** — its lowest row — and states plainly that today's 9.5% LTM return is about **1.5x** the ~6.4% pre-boom level, driven by volume running ahead of installed capacity during a build-out (46.9% organic growth in Q2 FY26) [`07_business-quality.md`, §1 cyclicality row and §4]. `10_external-dependency.md` scores external dependency at **60/100 (inverted — higher is worse)**, with roughly 94% of Q2 FY26 organic growth from one vertical [§4]. **The LTM figure is therefore labelled a cycle peak and is not used raw to support a moat verdict.** The through-cycle average (FY2022–FY2025) is the test figure: 8.10% computed / 6.49% vendor, both below 10.0%.

**Two basis caveats on that through-cycle number, stated in both directions.**
- *It is biased downward before 2025.* The FY2022–FY2024 capital base still carries the Thermal Management assets while the EBIT numerator excludes Thermal Management's earnings (discontinued operations, business sold 2025-01-30) [Q2 FY26 10-Q, Note 1, p.7]. So those three years' computed ROIC understates the continuing business. **This does not rescue the verdict**, because FY2025 — the first year clean of Thermal Management on the earnings side — prints **9.00%** on average capital and **9.07%** on ending capital ($493.5m ÷ $5,443.1m), still **below 10.0%**, and it is not a peak year.
- *No gross-capital adjustment is needed.* nVent is **not** net cash: net debt was $1,376.9m at 2026-06-30 on the strict total-debt-less-cash basis ($1,632.9m − $256.0m) [`ciq_facts.json`, `net_debt_m` = 1,376.9, `total_debt_m` = 1,632.9; Capital IQ Financials → Balance Sheet tab]. The capital base is not collapsed by cash netting, so there is no inflated denominator to correct.

**The one piece of arithmetic that erases the peak-year excess.** nVent has agreed to buy Maverick Power for **$1.75bn** plus up to **$550m** of contingent payments in 2027–28, against Maverick's estimated 2026 revenue of about **$700m** at an effective enterprise-value multiple of about **11.5x** — implying roughly **$152m of EBITDA** [nVent to Acquire Maverick Power press release, 2026-08-24]. Adding $1.75bn to the $5,531.5m capital base and, generously, the full $152m of EBITDA as incremental NOPAT-bearing earnings at the same 22.0% tax rate (EBIT ≈ $135m, NOPAT ≈ $105m) gives $762m ÷ $7,281m = **10.5%** — a spread of +47 bps at peak-cycle earnings. Include the $550m earn-out and it is $762m ÷ $7,831m = **9.7%**, back below 10.0%. *Inference from the two cited figures, not from filings.* On a through-cycle rather than peak numerator, the pro-forma return is materially further below.

## 4. Where The Company Sits

**1. Relative to peers.** **The company sits at the BOTTOM of its named peers on both margin and capital efficiency.** Group EBIT margin 17.43% LTM against Hubbell 20.7% GAAP and Legrand 20.7% adjusted (each basis stated); on the only matched-basis comparison available, Systems Protection's 22.9% segment return on sales sits **7.0 points below** Eaton Electrical Americas' 29.9% (FY2025) and 4.6 points below its Q2 2026 27.5%; on return on capital, 9.54% against a 13.3–13.6% third-party-computed peer band. That last gap is the durable one — it has persisted for five years (nVent vendor ROIC 6.7% / 6.2% / 6.3% / 6.3% / 7.2% for FY2021–FY2025) [all from `08_competitive-map.md`, §2d — figures inherited, not re-sourced]. **Nothing in the evidence supports a claim that nVent out-earns its named peers.** The single measure on which it leads is growth, not profitability: +46.9% group organic in Q2 FY26 against Eaton Electrical Americas +18%, Hubbell +10.2% and Legrand +9.8% [`08_competitive-map.md`, §3] — three to six times faster, off a base under a third the size of Eaton's, with a group backlog ($2.5bn) about a sixth of Eaton's Electrical Americas backlog alone ($15.3bn).

**2. Absolute — the economic moat test.** **The company earns a return on capital BELOW its cost of capital through the cycle: 8.1% through-cycle ROIC (FY2022–FY2025, computed NOPAT basis at a normalized 22.0% tax rate) against a 10.0% company-disclosed WACC — about −190 bps.** It clears the rate only in the peak LTM year, only on the computed basis (11.88%, +188 bps), and not on the vendor basis even then (9.54%, −46 bps). This is the decisive test, and it is decided against the company: whatever advantage the distribution, switching-cost and location rows describe, it has not shown up as economic profit in any year that was not a cycle peak.

## 5. Moat Verdict

# **No moat proven** — a moat in structure, not in economics

**Moat trajectory: STABLE** (evidence panel below; the panel is split 4–3, so neither "widening" nor "eroding" is confirmed).

**The strongest moat source is distribution (45/100)** — a dense electrical-distributor, contractor and OEM channel that nVent itself names as a condition of its success [FY24 10-K, Item 1 — Competition, p.2] — followed by narrow switching costs on the designed-in engineered work (40/100) and a freight-and-proximity location edge from regional manufacturing (40/100). None reaches the "strong" band, all three are occupied by every named rival, and **none of them lifts the return on capital above the cost of capital through the cycle** (8.1% against 10.0%), which is precisely the "moat in structure, not economics" case the verdict set defines. **The hard rule applies and is applied:** with a through-cycle return at or below the cost of capital, the verdict cannot be "Strong moat" regardless of anything else — and here there is not even peer-relative superiority to fall back on, because nVent earns less than every named peer on every sourced measure. **The durability test it would have to pass over the next five years is specific and measurable:** hold return on capital above 10.0% *after* the $1.75bn Maverick Power purchase enters the capital base and *after* data-centre order growth normalises — the arithmetic in §3 shows the peak-year spread of +47 bps pro-forma (or −30 bps including the earn-out) leaves no room for either. **Durability is discounted further for rate of change:** `07_business-quality.md` scores industry rate-of-change at **40/100**, tripping CLAUDE.md §24 Filter 5 — liquid cooling is at "10% to 15% of cooling in data centers" with the 800-volt DC rack architecture unsettled and chip roadmaps rewritten to 2030 [Q2 FY26 transcript, Q&A]. A moat in a fast-changing industry decays faster than the same moat in a boring one, and all of nVent's incremental capital is going into the fast part.

### Trajectory evidence panel

**Adjudicating the rows that disagree, by name (CLAUDE.md §3).** The panel splits **four supporting a widening read (EBITDA margin, EBIT margin, ROIC level, share proxy) against three contradicting it (gross margin, cash conversion, pricing)**, and neither side is a majority large enough to license a directional word. So the honest verdict is **stable — the direction is not established**, and specifically: *improving on the operating-leverage lines, flat-to-eroding on the pricing and cash lines, with the structural level unchanged.*

Taking the contradictions one at a time. **Gross margin down 129 bps over five years and 325 bps from the FY2024 peak** is the cleanest read on pricing power, because it is the line where price lands before any volume leverage touches it — and it went the wrong way while revenue nearly doubled. **Cash conversion down from 77.0% to 64.3%** (71.9% adjusting for the discontinued-operations drag, still down from FY2024's 95.2%) says the growing earnings are being funded into working capital: net working capital rose $219.7m in H1 FY26, mainly receivables, and the cash conversion cycle stretched to 85.6 days from 81.4 [Q2 FY26 10-Q, MD&A — Operating activities; Capital IQ Financials → Ratios tab]. **Pricing is simply not measurable** — nVent discloses no volume-versus-price split at any level [`07_business-quality.md`, §1 disclosure row], so "price plus productivity offset inflation" is a management claim on a call (tier 6), not a measurement.

Now the four that point the other way, and why they do not make this "widening". **EBITDA margin +255 bps and EBIT margin +215 bps are real and must not be dismissed** — but the whole of the Q2 FY26 improvement came *below* the gross line: operating margin rose 410 bps while SG&A fell 460 bps to 15.8% of sales on volume leverage and restructuring savings [Q2 FY26 10-Q, MD&A]. That is margin bought by running 46.9% organic growth through a fixed cost base, and it reverses when volume does. **The ROIC gap narrowing from −327 bps to −46 bps (or to +188 bps computed) is the strongest single argument for "widening"** and it is the one I weigh hardest against my own verdict — but it is the same cycle-peak effect measured a second way, it is a level that FY2025's clean 9.00% did not reach, and the Maverick arithmetic in §3 removes it. **The share-gain proxy is genuine on delivered revenue** — three to six times every named rival's organic growth — but it is an inference with no disclosed share figure behind it, and the order line already disagrees with it: backlog down sequentially and order growth decelerated from ~+40% to low double digits while revenue grew 47%.

Net: the *structural* position is where it was five years ago — a return on capital below the cost of capital, and below every named peer's, with no moat source scoring above 45. That is why the word is **stable**, not widening and not eroding, and why "erosion" must not be carried downstream as confirmed.

**Which way I would be wrong, and when I find out.** The single next data point is **nVent's Q3 FY2026 results, expected late October 2026** — specifically two lines read together: (a) infrastructure order intake and the group backlog against the $2.5bn at 2026-06-30, and (b) gross margin against the 37.9% printed in Q2 FY26. **Backlog back above $2.6bn with gross margin recovering above 37.9% would flip the read to widening** — it would mean the pricing line is turning up at the same time as the volume line, which is the one combination the current panel does not contain. **Backlog below $2.4bn with gross margin under 37.0% would flip it to eroding**, and would put the peak-year ROIC spread out of reach before Maverick even closes. Either outcome lands roughly seven weeks from this report's date. The slower confirming read is the **FY2026 10-K (expected February 2027)** — the first audited annual filing this engine will have since FY2024, and the first that will carry Maverick Power inside the capital base.

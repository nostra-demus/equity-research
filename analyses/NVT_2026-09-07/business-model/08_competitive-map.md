### Competitor A — Rittal GmbH & Co. KG

- **Ticker / listing:** none. Private; a company of the Friedhelm Loh Group, Herborn, Germany [CIQ Competitors export (NVT), retrieved 2026-09-07 — vendor export].
- **Where they compete:** industrial and IT enclosures, power distribution and climate control (air and liquid cooling), serving colocation, electrical engineering and automation, energy and telecom customers — the same product core as nVent HOFFMAN and SCHROFF inside Systems Protection. Germany-headquartered, sold globally; strongest in EMEA, where nVent is weakest (EMEA is $220.6m of Systems Protection's $1,966.9m H1 FY26 sales, 11.2%) [CIQ Competitors export — business description; Q2 FY26 10-Q, Note 2, p.8].
- **Scale:** LTM revenue **$1,756.30m**; the export leaves the LTM date blank for this row, so the period is unstated [CIQ Competitors export (NVT), retrieved 2026-09-07 — vendor export]. Parent Friedhelm Loh Group reported revenue of **€3.2 billion in 2025** with 12,900 employees, and Rittal is its largest company [Friedhelm Loh Group "Facts and Figures", retrieved 2026-09-07 — web source, unverified; no FX conversion applied, the euro figure is quoted as published]. Rittal alone is roughly **45%** of Systems Protection's annualised H1 FY26 run-rate ($1,756.3m against $1,966.9m × 2 = $3,933.8m) — derived arithmetic from the two cited figures.
- **Profitability / return on capital:** **not public / not disclosed.** A German GmbH & Co. KG inside a privately held group; no income statement, margin or return-on-capital figure for Rittal is present in the pool or in the sources checked. Do not assume one.
- **Source named in:** CIQ Competitors export, listed against nVent Electric plc; the disclosing document the vendor cites is a **Legrand SA (ENXTPA:LR) Form Doc** — i.e. a third company's filing named the pair, not nVent's own [CIQ Competitors export (NVT), retrieved 2026-09-07 — vendor export].
- **One-line read:** the closest product-for-product rival to Systems Protection's enclosure-and-cooling core, and the one whose economics cannot be tested at all, because it is private.

### Competitor B — Hubbell Incorporated (NYSE:HUBB)

- **Where they compete:** two segments — Utility Solutions (enclosures, connectors, switches, arresters, controls for grid transmission and distribution) and Electrical Solutions (enclosures, connector and grounding products, electrical equipment for industrial and commercial buildings). - **Scale:** LTM revenue **$6,223.60m** as of **Jun-30-2026** [CIQ Competitors export (NVT), retrieved 2026-09-07 — vendor export]. That is roughly **1.29x** nVent's LTM revenue of $4,834m [CIQ Income Statement, LTM 12 months Jun-30-2026 — vendor export], comfortably inside the ~5x scale test.
- **Profitability / return on capital:** FY2025 **GAAP operating margin 20.7%** (operating income $1,208.8m on $5,844.6m), **adjusted operating margin 22.7%**, net income attributable to Hubbell **$887.1m** = **15.2% of sales** (derived: 887.1 ÷ 5,844.6); by segment, Utility Solutions GAAP operating margin 21.5% and Electrical Solutions 19.3% [Hubbell FY2025 results release, 2026-02-03 — company release, unverified web copy]. Other third-party services computed materially different ROIC values for the same company (13.23% at GuruFocus, retrieved 2026-09-07 — third-party computed, unverified), so treat the ROIC as a **13–16% band**, not a point estimate, and do not build a precise gap off it.
- **Source named in:** CIQ Competitors export, listed against nVent Electric plc; disclosing document **Atkore Inc. (NYSE:ATKR) 2025 Form 10-K** [CIQ Competitors export (NVT), retrieved 2026-09-07 — vendor export].
- **One-line read:** the nearest public peer on scale and geography, earning a higher group operating margin and a higher return on capital than nVent, while growing about a quarter as fast organically.

## 3. Competitive Position

**Gaining share** in the dominant segment — on a growth proxy, not on any disclosed share figure. **No market-share number for nVent exists anywhere in the pool**: the FY24 10-K, the Q2 FY26 10-Q, both 2026 earnings calls and the William Blair deck contain no share claim, and no third-party share study is in the pool. The proxy used is organic revenue growth against the named peers **in the same reported quarter or half**, which removes both the acquisition effect and the period mismatch: nVent group organic growth was **+46.9%** in Q2 FY26 and **+41.2%** in H1 FY26, and Systems Protection's own organic contribution was **+62.0 points** in Q2 and **+56.7 points** in H1 [Q2 FY26 10-Q, MD&A — Net sales and Systems Protection, p.24 and p.29]. Over the same window, Eaton's Electrical Americas grew **+18% organically** in Q2 2026 [Eaton Q2 2026 release, 2026-07-30 — company release, unverified web copy], Hubbell **+10.2% organically** in Q2 2026 [Hubbell Q2 2026 release, 2026-07-28], and Legrand **+9.8% organically** in H1 2026 [Legrand H1 2026 release, 2026-07-29]. Growing three to six times faster than every named rival in an end market all of them are chasing is share gain; there is no other arithmetic that fits.

**The numbers that disagree, named (CLAUDE.md §3).** Three, and they matter. **(a) Base effect.** nVent's Systems Protection is running at roughly $3.9bn annualised against Eaton's Electrical Americas at $13.3bn in FY2025 — nVent is adding share off a base under a third the size, so a large percentage gain is a small absolute one. **(b) Committed work.** Eaton's Electrical Americas backlog was **$15.3bn** at end-2025 against nVent's **entire group** backlog of **$2.5bn** at 2026-06-30 [Eaton FY2025 release, 2026-02 — unverified web copy; Q2 FY26 transcript, 2026-07-31, prepared remarks — a management statement on a call, not a filing]. The largest named rival has about six times nVent's whole order book already committed. **(c) Orders, not revenue.** Eaton's twelve-month rolling average orders in Electrical Americas were **up 41%** [Eaton Q2 2026 release, 2026-07-30], much closer to nVent's growth rate than the revenue lines suggest — and nVent's own backlog was described by its CEO as "around the right level", i.e. roughly flat sequentially [Q2 FY26 transcript, Q&A — Joseph Ritchie, Goldman Sachs]. None of these overturns the gaining-share read on delivered revenue, but together they mean the gap is narrowing at the order line and the share gain should not be extrapolated. **Position vs peers on profitability is the opposite of the position on growth** — see §2d.

## 4. Competitive Shape

**Fragmented overall, with a consolidated top tier in the data-centre electrical niche — and the top-N share cannot be computed from this pool, so it is Not disclosed.** The fragmentation is nVent's own disclosure, not an inference: "We compete with **thousands of** smaller regional and local companies that may be positioned to offer products produced at lower cost than ours, or to capitalize on highly localized relationships", and "The number and size of competitors vary considerably depending on the product line" [FY24 10-K, Item 1A — Risk Factors, p.5–6; Item 1 — Competition, p.2]. The vendor export supports the same shape from the other direction: the rows tagged to nVent alone span fifteen distinct companies ranging from $41m (Haydon) to $35,752m (ABB) in revenue, across enclosures, conduit, transformers, switchgear, framing and racks — that spread is what a product-line-by-product-line market looks like, not a four-firm oligopoly [CIQ Competitors export (NVT), retrieved 2026-09-07 — vendor export]. The part of the market that now drives nVent — engineered power and cooling for data centres — is structurally different and much more concentrated: the credible suppliers at hyperscale volume are large integrated firms (ABB $35.8bn, Eaton $30.0bn, Legrand $11.7bn, Hubbell $6.2bn LTM, all per the same export) against nVent at $4.8bn LTM, with Rittal privately held and a small number of specialists such as Forgent Power at $1.2bn. **No HHI, no top-four share and no market-size figure exists in the pool or was sourced**, so the concentration of that niche is described, not measured; anyone needing it must buy a market study. What can be said from the filing is that the barrier is not scale alone — nVent lists the competitive factors as "technical expertise, reputation for quality and reliability, timeliness of delivery, new product innovation, previous installation history, contractual terms and price", plus the strength of the distributor channel [FY24 10-K, Item 1 — Competition, p.2].

## 5. Caveat

Competitors **were** identified, but the evidence chain behind them is weak in four specific ways, and each is fixable:

1. **nVent names no competitor in any filing or on any call in this pool.** Every name here rests on a tier-5 Capital IQ vendor export whose disclosing documents are *other companies'* filings (Atkore's 10-K, Forgent Power's 424B4, Legrand's and Hammond's own documents). *What would resolve it:* the FY2025 10-K, absent from the pool [`00_data-triage.md`, §3], or a sell-side or industry market-share study naming nVent's share of the enclosure and data-centre power markets.
2. **The competitor export is stale and noisy in places.** It still treats the divested RAYCHEM/Thermal Management business as a current subsidiary, prints Southwire's LTM revenue as $7.13m, and carries a Jun-30-2018 LTM date for Zekelman. Those rows were excluded, but the errors cap confidence in the rows that were kept. *What would resolve it:* a fresh CIQ Competitors pull, plus the CIQ **comps** export, which is missing from this pool entirely (`peer_ev_ebitda`, `shares_outstanding_m` and `current_price` are all flagged `missing` in `ciq_facts.json`) and would give like-for-like peer margins and returns from one vendor on one basis instead of the six different sources stitched together in §2d.
3. **Peer return-on-capital figures are third-party computed, not filed.** Hubbell's ROIC came back as 13.60% from one service and 13.23% from another; Legrand's ROCE is a service's calculation, not a figure in Legrand's own release. They are shown as a band and labelled unverified. *What would resolve it:* Hubbell's FY2025 10-K balance sheet and Legrand's FY2025 Universal Registration Document, from which both can be computed on a stated basis.
4. **No pure-play data-centre cooling or thermal-management specialist appears in the sourced competitor list at all**, even though liquid cooling is the capacity nVent is building three Minnesota plants for [Q2 FY26 transcript, prepared remarks]. Given the export's two-year, current-subsidiary scope, absence is not evidence of non-existence — but it means this map has a hole exactly where the growth is. No such company is named here, because naming one without a source would be inventing a competitor. *What would resolve it:* any filing, industry report or trade-press source that names the liquid-cooling suppliers nVent bids against.

**Downstream instruction for `moat`.** Use §2d, not the names alone. Compare segment margin to segment margin (Systems Protection 22.9% to Eaton Electrical Americas 29.9%/27.5%), group to group (nVent EBIT 17.43% to Hubbell 20.7% GAAP and Legrand 20.7% adjusted, each basis stated), and treat the peer ROIC/ROCE figures as a 13–16% band from unverified third-party calculations. On this evidence nVent earns **less** than its named peers on every sourced measure while growing far faster than all of them — any moat read must hold both facts at once.

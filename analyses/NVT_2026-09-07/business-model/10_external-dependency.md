# External Dependency Check — NVT

**Direction warning: the score in Section 4 is INVERTED — higher = WORSE (more dangerous external dependence).**

**Evidence base and its limit.** nVent Electric plc is a US-domestic SEC filer (Form 10-K / 10-Q), incorporated in Ireland, head office in London, reporting in US GAAP and US dollars [FY24 10-K, cover page]. The FY2025 Form 10-K is **absent from the data pool**, so **the current Item 1A Risk Factors used below are the FY2024 set** (year ended 2024-12-31, filed 2025-02-18), and the only disclosed market-risk sensitivities are the FY2024 ones in Item 7A. The Q2 FY26 10-Q points to a FY2025 10-K that exists but that this run cannot read [Q2 FY26 10-Q, Item 3: "refer to our 2025 Annual Report on Form 10-K"]. Everything after 2024 comes from the Q1 and Q2 FY26 10-Qs, the two FY26 earnings calls, the June 2026 William Blair deck, and the August 2026 Maverick Power release. Where a figure comes from the Capital IQ workbook it is named as a vendor export (tier 5), never as a filing.

All disclosed sensitivities are from the **FY2024** Item 7A (the FY2025 10-K is not in the pool). The Q2 FY26 10-Q states "there have been no material changes in our market risk during the quarter ended June 30, 2026" but points to the FY2025 10-K this run cannot read, so these coefficients are carried forward with that caveat.

| Variable | Move tested | Disclosed impact | What it hits | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Interest rates — fixed-rate debt | +100 bp / −100 bp | Fair value −$63.1m / +$67.5m | Fair value only; no cash interest, no cash flow | [FY24 10-K, Item 7A] |
| Interest rates — variable-rate debt | +100 bp / −100 bp | ±$8.7m of interest incurred | Cash interest | [FY24 10-K, Item 7A] |

Two restatements onto today's balance sheet, both labelled as arithmetic on filed balances rather than company disclosure:

- The $8.7m rate sensitivity was measured when the debt book was **60% fixed / 40% variable**. At 2026-06-30 it was **$1,300.0m fixed / $200.0m variable** (87% / 13%), so the same 100 bp move is worth roughly **$2.0m** of interest today. *Inference from [Q2 FY26 10-Q, Note "Debt"] — not a company-disclosed sensitivity.*
- Currency derivative cover changed: gross notional currency forwards **$259.3m at 2024-12-31 → $186.4m at 2026-06-30**, cross-currency swaps **$350.5m at 2026-06-30** [FY24 10-K, Item 7A; Q2 FY26 10-Q, Note "Derivatives"].

**No commodity-price sensitivity is published, and no commodity derivative is disclosed.** Item 7A covers currency and interest rates only [FY24 10-K, Item 7A]. That is a genuine disclosure gap on the input the company itself names first among its inflation drivers.

**The measured mitigation rate — the base case, not zero (CLAUDE.md §9).** nVent's own reported numbers show how much of an external cost shock it actually absorbed, so a zero-mitigation stress would be the wrong base case here. In Q2 FY26 the company carried **more than $50 million of inflation, including more than $30 million of tariff cost**, and still delivered **return on sales of 21.9%, up 110 basis points year on year** [Q2 FY26 transcript, prepared remarks]. That is a realised offset of more than 100% of the cost shock in that quarter, achieved through price and productivity. The honest caveat: this was measured in a quarter of 47% organic volume growth, when fixed-cost leverage was doing part of the work — the same offset should not be assumed at flat or falling volume. A zero-mitigation tariff stress ($190m straight to profit) is a **bound**, not a forecast.

## 3. Classification

**Partly externally driven** — material exposure with real management levers on the cost side, and effectively no lever on the volume side.

The evidence pulls two ways and both readings have to be stated:

- **Against a "wrapper" verdict:** nVent is not a price pass-through. It sets its own prices, absorbed >$50m of inflation in Q2 FY26 while raising return on sales 110 bp, has no customer disclosed above 10% of sales as of FY2024, hedges its currency translation, and has almost no floating-rate debt. New products contributed over 30 points of Q2 sales growth — company-controlled content, not market-controlled volume [Q2 FY26 transcript, prepared remarks]. Roughly 63% of FY26 sales are still outside data centres.
- **Toward a "wrapper" verdict:** the *marginal* dollar is close to a pure bet on AI data-centre capital spending. About 94% of Q2 FY26 organic growth came from one vertical; infrastructure went from 12% of sales at the 2018 spin to 45% in 2025 to nearly 60% of first-half 2026 sales; data-centre sales alone are guided above $2bn, more than 37% of company sales; the entire guidance raise was attributed to data centres; and the $1.75bn Maverick Power acquisition (2026 revenue ~$700m, data-centre power distribution) increases that share again from Q4 2026 [Q2 FY26 transcript; Maverick Power release, 2026-08-24].

So: the base business is a broad electrical-products company with normal industrial exposures. The growth, the earnings revisions and the guidance raises are a bet on one external spending cycle that management does not control. That is "partly externally driven" at the top of its range, one notch from "mostly externally driven".

**What would move it to "Mostly externally driven":** data-centre sales passing roughly half of group revenue (a plausible 2027 pro-forma outcome once Maverick closes — *inference, not from filings*), or a disclosed single customer above 10% of net sales.

Band: **41–60 — material external exposure, mixed mitigation.** Scored at the very top of that band, consistent with the "Partly externally driven" classification in §3 and one point below the "mostly externally driven" band.

| Component | Read | Effect on score |

## 5. The Single Biggest Lever

**AI data-centre capital spending by hyperscalers, neo-clouds and multi-tenant operators** — a 20% adverse move in that spending would strike a business where data centres are guided above $2 billion of ~$5.4 billion of 2026 sales and where roughly 94% of Q2 FY26 organic growth came from the infrastructure vertical, with a $2.5 billion backlog, a third Minnesota liquid-cooling plant opening in the first half of 2027, and a $1.75 billion data-centre acquisition still to be paid for.

# Beat / Miss Setup — INDIAMART

## 1. Next Quarter Context

The next quarter to report is Q2 FY27 (July–September 2026), expected around 21-Oct-2026, following the Q1 FY27 print (June quarter, reported 21-Jul-2026) [`04_guidance-consensus.md` §1]. IndiaMART gives no formal numeric guidance, so the bar is set entirely by sell-side consensus: revenue ₹4,222.5mn (₹422.25cr), up +1.67% in the last month, and normalized EPS ₹20.23, cut −5.47% in the last month — a cut that came *after* Q1 FY27's reported EPS beat, because analysts did not carry forward the one-off treasury gain that drove it [`04_guidance-consensus.md` §4, §6]. `04_guidance-consensus.md` calls the overall bar "fair" — not obviously low or high — because revenue and EBITDA revision trends are mildly positive while EPS and target-price revisions are mildly negative [`04_guidance-consensus.md` §7].

## 6. Seasonality Read

Seasonality neither helps nor hurts the setup meaningfully. Q2 (Jul–Sep), the quarter about to report, has averaged 24.86% of annual revenue across FY24-FY26 — squarely in the middle of the four quarters, below Q4's 25.88% but above Q1's 23.71%, a spread of only ~2 percentage points that `01_historical-financials.md` explicitly says does not clear its >30%/<20% seasonality flag [`01_historical-financials.md` §5]. Quarterly EBITDA margin for Q2 has swung far more year to year than quarter to quarter within a year (27.15% in FY24 Q2, 38.71% in FY25 Q2, 33.17% in FY26 Q2), confirming that the "year effect" (cost-cycle and spend decisions) — not the calendar quarter — is what will decide the margin outcome this time too [`01_historical-financials.md` §5-6].

## 7. Historical Pattern

IndiaMART's revenue line has beaten or matched consensus in each of the last four quarters by a tight 0% to +1.3% margin, including an exactly in-line Q1 FY27 print — this should be weighted as a real signal that the sell-side's revenue model is well calibrated, so a large revenue surprise in either direction next quarter would be unusual [`04_guidance-consensus.md` §6]. EPS, by contrast, has swung wildly (roughly −35%, +37%-+56%, −52%, +28%-+33% over the same four quarters) almost entirely because of the lumpy, non-operating treasury-gains line sitting below EBIT [`01_historical-financials.md` §3; `04_guidance-consensus.md` §6]. The synthesizer should weight the revenue pattern heavily as a base rate for the operating top line, and weight the EPS pattern lightly (or not at all) as a read on operating performance — it is closer to a coin-flip driven by treasury-book marks than a signal about the business.

## 8. Setup Verdict

**Setup is balanced.**

The single most important factor is that the two dominant near-term forces point in opposite directions and are roughly offsetting: paying-subscriber net adds have been negative in 3 of the last 4 quarters and management has given no timeline for a turn (a revenue-side drag), while EBITDA margin is currently "elevated" precisely because management has deliberately deferred customer-acquisition spend for "2, 3 more quarters" (a margin-side tailwind) [`02_revenue-drivers.md` §7; `03_margin-drivers.md` §8]. The single biggest risk that could flip this to a clear miss is if subscriber net adds deteriorate further and force management to resume acquisition spend earlier than planned to defend growth — that would turn both drivers negative at once, since the margin tailwind and the "hold off on spend" stance are the same policy choice.

## 9. Second-Quarter Look-Ahead

Visibility into the quarter after next (Q3 FY27, Oct–Dec 2026) is limited: `04_guidance-consensus.md` provides consensus only for the next quarter (FQ2 FY27) and the full FY2027 year, not FQ3 FY27 specifically [`04_guidance-consensus.md` §3-4]. The one directional signal available is that management's "2, 3 more quarters" churn-resolution and spend-pullback framing, given from the Q1 FY27 call (Jul-2026), would put the expected resolution window roughly around Q3-Q4 FY27 — meaning the margin tailwind identified in Section 8 could plausibly still be in place for Q2 FY27 but is more likely to be fading or reversing by Q3 FY27, a genuine change in setup rather than a continuation, though this is inference from management's own stated timeline, not from disclosed Q3 FY27-specific data. Inference, not from filings.

## 10. Pre-Mortem

If this balanced setup fails, the most likely reason is that the margin tailwind (deferred customer-acquisition spend) and the revenue drag (falling subscriber net adds) turned out not to be independent but to be the same underlying weakness expressing itself twice — management held spend back specifically because underlying demand was soft, and when that softness did not resolve in "2, 3 quarters," both margin and revenue missed together instead of offsetting. A secondary risk is treating a non-operating treasury-book swing as an operating EPS "beat" or "miss" when the real story sat entirely in EBIT/EBITDA.

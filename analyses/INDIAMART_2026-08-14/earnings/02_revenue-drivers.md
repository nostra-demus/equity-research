Segment decomposition applied — 2 segments from business-model module (`analyses/INDIAMART_2026-08-13/business-model/03_segment-map.md`).

The Web and related Services segment cleared the module's own >85% single-segment revenue threshold in FY26 (91.96% of consolidated revenue [FY26 Annual Report, Note 32]), and `03_segment-map.md` itself reads the company as "best read as a single-segment business... not a genuinely multi-segment company." This report keeps the second segment (Accounting Software Services, Busy Infotech + Livekeeping) broken out anyway, because although it is only 8.04% of FY26 revenue, it supplied roughly a third of the *incremental* growth in the most recent quarter (Section 6) — a revenue-share test alone would hide that. Both segments are covered in Section 5.

## 2. Revenue Driver Tree

| Business Type | Revenue Formula |
|---|---|
| Subscription (Web and related Services, ~92% of revenue) | Paying suppliers × ARPU (Average Revenue Per User) |
| Subscription / software licence (Accounting Software Services, ~8% of revenue) | Licences sold / billing accounts × realized price per licence |
| Multi-segment | Sum of the two segment formulas above |

**Company-specific formula:** Consolidated revenue ≈ (paying suppliers on the IndiaMART marketplace × ARPU) + (Busy Infotech / Livekeeping licence base × price per licence and add-on take-rate), where "ARPU" is the company's own defined metric — revenue from operations for the year ÷ paying subscription suppliers at year-end [FY26 Annual Report, p.82–83].

**Web segment, Q1 FY27 — volume component:**
```
Volume: 218,000 subscribers (Jun-26) ÷ 218,000 subscribers (Jun-25) − 1 = 0.0%
  [Q1 FY27 Earnings Call, Jul 21, 2026, basis: paying-supplier count at quarter-end, consolidated]
  [Q1 FY26 Earnings Call, Jul 18, 2025, basis: paying-supplier count at quarter-end, consolidated]
  → both figures are stated directly by management on their respective calls for the same defined
    metric (total paying suppliers at quarter-end) — same basis, both periods, no cross-basis error.
  = 0.0pp of the Web segment's 8.5% YoY revenue growth [`03_segment-map.md`, Section 4]
  → basis matches; the two counts are independently disclosed, not derived from each other
```

**Web segment, Q1 FY27 — price/ARPU component:**
```
Price: Web segment revenue ₹3,758.74m (Q1 FY27) ÷ implied Web segment revenue ₹3,465.2m (Q1 FY26) − 1
  where implied Q1 FY26 Web revenue = ₹3,758.74m ÷ 1.085 (the filing-stated +8.5% YoY segment growth rate)
  [IndiaMART_InterMESH_Limited_-_Form_Interim_Report(Jul-21-2026).pdf, Note 4/29 — Segment information]
  = +8.5% Web segment revenue growth, of which subscriber count contributed 0.0pp (above)
  → therefore price/realization accounts for the full +8.5% — this is arithmetic identity
    (revenue = subscribers × ARPU, subscribers unchanged), not an estimate requiring a separate
    sensitivity ratio, so there is no cross-basis risk to flag here.
  = 7.89pp of the 11.37pp consolidated growth (7.89 = 8.5% × [3,465.2m Q1 FY26 Web revenue ÷
    3,720.8m Q1 FY26 total revenue, i.e. the Web segment's 93.1% weight in the prior-year base])
  → basis matches: the weight used (93.1%) is the Web segment's own prior-period revenue share,
    not the current-period share and not a consolidated-level ratio applied to a segment.
```

**Accounting Software segment, Q1 FY27 — combined volume/price/mix:**
```
Asserted from disclosure, no separable ratio applied. The filing reports a single +49.4% YoY segment
revenue growth figure with no split between the licence-count driver (~+11% YoY) and the price/mix
driver (price increases + add-on take-rate, referenced qualitatively in the Q1 FY27 call but not

modelled split (CLAUDE.md §15: do not apply a ratio across a basis it was not measured on).
  = 3.48pp of the 11.37pp consolidated growth, reported as a single undifferentiated segment
    contribution [`03_segment-map.md`, Section 4]
```

**Reconciliation to Section 6's Total row (Q1 FY27):** 0.0pp (volume) + 7.89pp (Web price) + 3.48pp (Accounting Software, undifferentiated) = 11.37pp reconciled, **0.0pp residual** against the stated 11.37% total. This is an unusually clean reconciliation because the two segment revenue figures sum exactly to the reported consolidated total by construction (they are the filing's own segment note, not an estimate) — the only place an assumption enters is inside the Accounting Software segment's own internal split, which is explicitly left undifferentiated above rather than modelled.

**Reconciliation to Section 6's Total row (FY26 annual):** 8.84pp (Web, itself split 1.38pp volume + 7.81pp price via the multiplicative identity revenue = subscribers × ARPU, which the company's own ARPU definition makes an identity, not a modelled ratio) + 4.17pp (Accounting Software, undifferentiated) = 13.01pp reconciled, **0.01pp residual** (rounding only, from ARPU and subscriber counts being disclosed rounded to the nearest thousand).

Because the residual is ~0 in both periods, Section 7's single-biggest-driver claim below rests on a decomposition that is fully explained, not a bridge with a large unexplained gap.

## 7. The Single Biggest Revenue Driver

**Paying-subscriber net additions to the Web segment (volume)** is the single variable most likely to move revenue over the next 3–12 months — not ARPU, even though ARPU has done all the realized growth work in the last two reported periods (Section 6a). The reasoning: in the revenue identity (subscribers × ARPU), a 10–20% move in either factor produces an equivalent percentage move in Web segment revenue, so the two drivers have identical mechanical leverage. What separates them is volatility and current trajectory. ARPU has grown ~8% every year for four straight years with no reversal in the data [FY26 Annual Report, p.83] — it is the stable half of the equation. Subscriber net adds, by contrast, swung from +2,800 (Q2 FY26) to −1,850 (Q1 FY27) within the same fiscal year, have been negative in 3 of the last 4 quarters, and management has explicitly declined to guide on when net adds turn positive again: *"we'll continue to have no guidance on the net adds until we have multiple quarters of continual success"* [Q4 FY26 Earnings Call, Apr 30, 2026]. A continuation of negative net adds shrinks the base ARPU is multiplied against and, per management's own framing, risks the price increase becoming counter-productive if churn keeps rising in response — *"if the customers are not renewing, acquiring same customer with double the intensity will make the CAC go higher and the LTV go lower"* [Q1 FY27 Earnings Call, Jul 21, 2026]. The direction today is negative (flat YoY subscriber count, accelerating quarterly net losses through Q1 FY27), and the swing factors behind it — Silver-tier gross adds/churn, the war-related and OTP-verification-related buyer declines, and the still-unresolved SME "pain" management cites — are all outside the company's full control (`10_external-dependency.md`, Section 5, independently names "Consumer cycle" / SME sentiment as its own single biggest lever, which this agent's read agrees with).

**Cycle position (Cycle-Position Rule):** the latest reported quarter is **not** a demand peak. Evidence: consolidated revenue growth has decelerated every year from +30.78% (FY23) to +13.02% (FY26) to +11.37% (Q1 FY27, the softest of the last 8 quarters per `01_historical-financials.md` Section 3); consolidated collection-from-customers growth has decelerated from +17% YoY (Q1 FY26) to +8% YoY (Q1 FY27) [Q1 FY26 and Q1 FY27 Earnings Calls]; and net subscriber adds have been negative in 3 of the last 4 quarters. This reads as a soft-patch / decelerating phase, not a trough (the company remains profitable and cash-generative — see `01_historical-financials.md` Section 1) and not a peak. Two items must be treated as non-run-rate: (1) the FY25 EBITDA-margin snap-back (+1,048bps) reflected cost discipline, not a demand re-acceleration, per `01_historical-financials.md` Section 6, so FY25's margin should not be read as the operating baseline; (2) the government MSME/GST tailwind management cites in the FY26 MD&A is a stated, forward-looking claim not yet visible in the subscriber or inquiry data this quarter (Section 4) — it must not be treated as already embedded in the current run-rate.

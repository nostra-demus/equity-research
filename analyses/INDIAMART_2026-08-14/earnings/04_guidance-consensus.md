**The pattern that matters for the bar:** revenue surprises have been small and consistently positive (0% to +1.3%) across all four of the last quarters — the Street's revenue estimation has been tightly calibrated. EPS surprises over the same four quarters have swung wildly (roughly −35%, +37%–+56%, −52%, +28%–+33%) — and management's own Q1 FY27 disclosure that ₹107cr of other income came from treasury mark-to-market gains indicates the EPS volatility is substantially a below-the-line (non-operating) phenomenon, not a sign that the operating "bar" itself is mis-set.

## 7. Bar Assessment

**Bar is fair.**

The evidence points in different directions depending on the metric and horizon, and none dominates cleanly enough to call the bar low or high overall. Revenue: FY2027 consensus (₹1,720.19cr, +9.6% YoY) has been essentially flat over the last quarter (−0.9% vs 90 days ago) after a full year of cuts (−3.1% vs 12 months ago), and the historical beat/miss record shows the revenue line has been consistently, tightly in-line (0% to +1.3% surprise) for four straight quarters — a well-calibrated, not obviously low, bar. EBITDA/EBIT revision breadth is net positive (+28.6% over the last three months) even as the FY2027 EBITDA number sits 4.5% below where it was 12 months ago — margin expectations are being nudged back up from a lower base, roughly balancing out. EPS is the most mixed: the FY2027 number ticked up slightly in the last quarter (+0.8%) reversing part of a 12-month, −7.3% cut, but the *next-quarter* (FQ2 FY27) EPS estimate was cut −5.5% in the same window — notably, this cut came *after* Q1 FY27's large reported EPS beat, because the Street explicitly did not extrapolate the one-off treasury mark-to-market gain that drove that beat (Section 6). Revenue and EBITDA breadth for the next quarter both rose modestly post-print (+1.7% and an implied margin uptick), so the operating-line bar for the next quarter is, if anything, slightly higher, while the EPS-specific bar for the next quarter is lower — genuinely offsetting signals within the same reporting period, not noise. Layered on top: sell-side sentiment (target price −21% over six months, recommendation drifting from Outperform to Hold) has de-rated the stock alongside the estimate cuts, and management's own supplier-churn commentary points to continued near-term core-KPI softness for "2, 3 quarters" — a headwind the Street appears to already be pricing given the flattened (not rising) revenue estimates. No formal numeric guidance exists to anchor a guidance-vs-consensus gap (Section 3), so this call rests entirely on revision momentum and historical beat/miss behaviour.

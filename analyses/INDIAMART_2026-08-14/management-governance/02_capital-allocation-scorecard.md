**Reading the incremental-ROIC result plainly, in place of a fabricated number:** IndiaMART's core marketplace requires close to **zero, and structurally negative, operating capital** — customers pay a year or more of subscription upfront (Unearned Revenue, current + non-current, grew from ₹9,070.38mn FY22 to ₹19,652.6mn FY26), there is no inventory, and capex is trivial (₹41.3mn–₹172mn/year against ₹5,015.93mn of FY26 EBIT). This means growth **funds itself and releases cash** rather than consuming it — the standard incremental-ROIC formula is not the right tool here because the denominator is negative, not because the business is capital-inefficient. The correct read is the opposite of inefficiency: every rupee of revenue growth this business has added over the period has come with negative net new capital required, which is a genuinely strong organic-reinvestment characteristic — but it is a qualitative read, not a number that can be defensibly quoted as "X% incremental ROIC," and this report does not manufacture one.

Dividends are well covered in every year shown, including the tightest year (FY26 cash-paid basis, 2.3x FCF coverage); the payout ratio has risen from single digits (FY22–FY23) to a majority of net income (FY26 declared basis, 75.9%), reflecting the special dividend used to return the swollen treasury book rather than fresh reinvestment or a resumed buyback.

## 5. Capital-Allocation Read

Capital has been allocated with real per-share discipline in some respects and real drift in others — this is a **mixed** record, not a clean story either way. The best use of capital has been the core organic business itself: negative operating working capital and near-zero capex mean growth has been self-funding, throwing off cash (CFO rose every single year, FY22 ₹4,023.14mn to FY26 ₹6,942.19mn) without requiring net new capital — a genuinely strong, if not cleanly quantifiable, per-share characteristic. Dividends have been consistently well covered and have scaled up appropriately (from 15% of net income in FY22 to a 76% payout including a special dividend in FY26) as cash piled up. The worst use of capital is the acquisition/venture-investing program: the one control deal (Busy Infotech, $66.93mm) has grown revenue sharply but has posted a **negative segment EBITDA for three straight years**, and the ~19 minority venture checks have produced, on the record available, only a strike-off, a nominal-value divestiture, and two write-down provisions — real capital and real founder/management attention committed for an as-yet-unproven return. The single biggest capital-allocation signal is the combination of a swollen, growing treasury book (net cash on the broad basis nearly doubling from ₹22.8bn at FY24-end to ₹31.0bn at FY26-end) with **no buyback since September 2023** — capital is accumulating in mutual funds and bonds rather than being returned to shareholders or redeployed, even as the company continues writing small checks into unrelated MSME-tech startups. Verdict: **mixed**.

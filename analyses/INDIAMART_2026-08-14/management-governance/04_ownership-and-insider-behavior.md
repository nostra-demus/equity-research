# Ownership & Insider Behavior — INDIAMART

Jurisdiction: India (NSE: INDIAMART), SEBI-LODR regime. Ownership source is the shareholding-pattern table in the Corporate Governance Report (the Indian proxy-equivalent), cross-checked against the Capital IQ Public Ownership Summary export. Insider-transaction detail is limited: no dedicated SAST/PIT (Prohibition of Insider Trading) transaction-disclosure filing or exchange-intimation log is in the data pool, so per-transaction insider trading history rests on the CIQ ownership-flow table (a data-vendor export, not a filing) plus two Secretarial Audit compliance-lapse mentions. That gap is called out explicitly below and caps the "recent insider behavior" score component to low confidence.

It does not meet the evidence bar to fire RF-OWN-001 (promoter pledge above threshold) or RF-OWN-002 (pledge increased QoQ) because no pledge magnitude is disclosed to test against any threshold; `business-model/01_disqualifier-scan.md` independently reached the same conclusion and computed the disqualifier ratio as "not disclosed / not computable — treated as ~0%," well under the 50% hard-disqualifier threshold [`business-model/01_disqualifier-scan.md`, Section on disqualifier #2]. **Disclosure-completeness note:** SEBI shareholding-pattern filings generally carry a pledge/encumbrance column; its absence here from the reviewed pool means the analysis cannot independently confirm a true 0% promoter pledge — it can only confirm that no non-zero pledge magnitude is disclosed. This is flagged as a residual data gap, not treated as proof of a clean pledge position.

This is a materially different risk profile from a government-controlled or parent-subsidiary structure: the controlling owners' economic interest (49.08% of the equity) and their operating decisions are the same two people, which points toward alignment with per-share value creation rather than against it — subject to the execution-quality and succession caveats raised in `01_management-and-track-record.md` (27-year unbroken tenure with zero disclosed succession plan is a key-person risk, not a control-alignment problem).

## 5. Ownership / Insider Read

Skin in the game is real and large: the two founders hold 49.08% of the company directly, bought at founding rather than granted through options, and both still run it as full-time executive directors 27 years later — that is about as aligned an ownership structure as this kind of company gets. The insider-transaction signal is thin rather than alarming: the only data available (a data-vendor flow table, not a filing) shows a small, immaterial insider sale in the prior quarter and a larger, unidentified insider buy in the most recent quarter — reported here as labeled, low-confidence context because no SAST/PIT filing exists in the pool to verify who bought, at what price, or under what plan. The biggest risk to minority holders is not pledging (none is disclosed at the promoter level, only an unquantified individual pre-clearance lapse) or control structure (single-class equity, no dual-class trap) — it is succession: a 49%-owning, 27-year, two-person founder bloc with zero disclosed contingency plan, sitting on top of a 21.72%-against vote on a promoter-family related-party appointment, which is a governance-fairness watch item for the board-quality agent (`05`) to carry forward.

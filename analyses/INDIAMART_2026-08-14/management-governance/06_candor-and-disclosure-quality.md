## 3. Disclosure Transparency

Management discloses segment economics with real granularity: Busy Infotech's own collection, revenue, deferred revenue, cash flow from operations, and new-license counts are broken out every quarter, and the core marketplace's paying-supplier count, net additions/declines, and even tier-specific churn rates (silver-tier monthly churn quantified at "about 7%" on the Q1 FY27 call) are disclosed even when the numbers are unfavorable. The clearest positive signal is that the raw net-add/net-decline number kept being reported every quarter after the *forward guidance* on that number was withdrawn in Apr-2025 — management stopped forecasting the metric but never stopped reporting it, which is the correct distinction between withdrawing a promise and hiding a result. No disclosure identified in the reviewed pool got *less* transparent over time; if anything, disclosure granularity increased (buyer-count trends, silver-tier cohort churn, and buyer-monetization experiments were all added to the Q&A record only in the more recent FY26–FY27 calls, as the underlying issues became more material).

## 4. Tone in Bad Times

The worst recent period by the numbers is Q1 FY27 (Jul-2026 call): revenue growth slowed to 11%, the core paying-supplier base declined by 1,850 net, and management openly admitted the buyer-side funnel is "stagnating." The tone was candid, not promotional — the CEO led prepared remarks with the negative supplier number and its two named causes before any question was asked, and the Q&A that followed answered specific, pointed analyst questions (on buyer monetization, churn cohort analysis, silver-tier renewal-rate trajectory, and whether the churn problem might be structural rather than transient) with specific figures rather than deflection — e.g., breaking down the buyer decline into "monetizable buyer" growth (positive) versus overall buyer count (declining), and directly stating "we did cut down on some of the non-quality buyers" as a deliberate trade-off rather than an unexplained decline. The same pattern holds on the Q4 FY25 call (the sharpest miss in the dataset, where net-add guidance was withdrawn): the CEO's direct answer — "I'm really not confident to give you any answer as of now" — is the kind of blunt non-answer that a promotional management avoids giving; it costs credibility in the moment to say it, which is itself evidence of candor over spin.

## 5. Candor Read

Across five verifiable misses spanning FY24–FY27, management consistently led with the bad number in prepared remarks rather than waiting to be asked, paired every miss with a specific, checkable driver (percentage breakdowns, named causes, or a blunt "I don't know"), and did not stop reporting an unfavorable KPI once it stopped forecasting it. The strongest evidence for trust is the Q4 FY25 exchange where the CEO declined to fabricate a recovery number under direct analyst pressure — a promotional management manufactures reassurance in that moment; this one did not. The strongest evidence against a clean bill is the RF-DISC-002 nuance on "onetime" investment-gain labeling (individually defensible, collectively understates a genuinely recurring income category) and the still-unfilled guidance vacuum on the core net-add metric more than a year after it was withdrawn — a real, if narrow, transparency gap. The routed integrity signal from `business-model/01_disqualifier-scan` (Designated Person pledge/closed-window trade) was independently investigated by `01_management-and-track-record.md` at the primary source (FY26 Secretarial Audit Report) and found disclosed, self-reported, and immaterial — it does not compound against the trust read here, consistent with `01`'s conclusion that RF-MGT-005 does not apply. **Verdict: candid / high-trust**, with the RF-DISC-002 nuance the one item worth continued monitoring rather than dismissing.

## 3. Classification

**Partly externally driven.**

The balance-sheet-level external exposures (FX, commodity, freight, weather, interest rate on debt) are genuinely small or not applicable — this is a debt-free (Total Debt/EBITDA ~0.04x), 99.6%-India-revenue, asset-light subscription business with no commodity input and no hedging program because it needs none [Financials-1 Capital-Structure-Details.xls; Financials.xls Segments tab]. That is the "company-controlled" half of the picture: pricing, product mix (Silver/Gold/Platinum tiers), sales-force allocation, and the accounting-software bolt-on (Busy, Vyapar) are levers management pulls directly.

But the demand side is a different story. Paying-supplier additions and collection growth are visibly exposed to two forces management does not control: (1) the health of India's SME sector — management's own risk register names "Economic Risk" first among six standing risks, and on two consecutive earnings calls (Q4 FY26, Q1 FY27) leadership directly blamed weak net adds and buyer inquiries on SME financial pain, a geopolitical "war" event, and India-U.S. tension, rather than on execution; and (2) government MSME/GST policy, which the MD&A itself frames as the tailwind underpinning its addressable market (Udyam-registered enterprises grew from 7.9 million in FY22 to 79.4 million in FY26 largely on the back of formalization policy, not IndiaMART's own actions) [FY26 Integrated Annual Report, MD&A]. Because pricing power, retention programs, and product bundling are real, proven management levers that operate on top of — and can partly offset — these forces, this is not a pure pass-through name; it sits in the "material exposure with real levers" band.

## 4. External Dependency Risk Score

**52/100 — inverted scale: higher = worse (more dangerous external dependence).**

Drivers of the score: two High-dependency rows (Geopolitics, Consumer/SME cycle) and one Mid-dependency row with a real earnings swing (Interest rate/investment NAV, ~27% of FY26 EBITDA at a 5% NAV move) pull the score up from the "company-controlled" floor. It stays out of the 61+ band because FX, commodity, freight, and weather are genuinely not applicable or immaterial, debt-linked rate risk is negligible, and management demonstrably has and uses direct levers (pricing, subscriber tiering, product bundling) that the transcripts show working even through the SME-pain and geopolitical-shock quarters (paid subscriber base and revenue both kept growing through FY26 even as net adds slowed).

## 5. The Single Biggest Lever

A 20% adverse move in India SME/MSME sentiment and spending capacity — the "Consumer cycle" row — would do the most damage: it is the demand-side variable management has already named as the cause of decelerating collection growth and net-subscriber losses over FY26–FY27, it cannot be hedged or offset with a financial instrument the way currency or rate risk can, and it hits the core subscription-renewal engine directly rather than the smaller treasury-income line.

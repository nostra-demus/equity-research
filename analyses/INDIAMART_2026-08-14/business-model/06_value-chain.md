# Value Chain Position — INDIAMART

## 1. Stages Occupied

IndiaMART is a two-sided digital marketplace, not a physical-goods business — the standard raw-material/manufacturer/distributor ladder does not apply. Its "value chain" is really: (a) what it buys to run the platform (labor, outsourced telesales, cloud/hosting, third-party data verification), and (b) the two sides of its marketplace — paying suppliers (who pay to be found) and buyers (who search for free). A smaller, structurally separate value chain sits inside the group: Busy Infotech, a licensed accounting-software vendor to MSMEs.

## 2. Input Cost Pass-Through

There is no commodity or raw-material input to pass through — IndiaMART is an asset-light subscription business with no physical goods in its own cost base, and it states outright that it does not engage in commodity trading, hedging, or exchange-risk management [FY26 Annual Report (Ind AS), Corporate Governance Report — General Shareholder Information]. The dominant cost is labor: consolidated employee benefits expense was ₹6,928.25 Mn in FY26 vs ₹6,009.86 Mn in FY25 (+15% YoY), growing faster than the ~13% consolidated revenue growth over the same period [FY26 Annual Report, Note 21; MD&A p.83]. Outsourced sales cost (the telesales/channel-partner network that signs up new paying suppliers) was ₹800.45 Mn in FY26 vs ₹785.93 Mn in FY25 (+1.8%) [FY26 Annual Report, Note 24]. Because these are wage-linked and services costs, not indexed commodity contracts, there is no contractual escalator or pass-through clause disclosed — the company absorbs wage inflation directly into its P&L, which is the mechanism behind the 284bp standalone EBITDA-margin compression the business-identity read already flagged for FY26.

**Supplier / input concentration (quantify).** The CIQ Suppliers.xls export in the data pool lists ~40 large Indian and multinational industrial, healthcare, and consumer-goods companies (Ajax Engineering, Ashok Leyland, Blue Star, Canon, Cummins India, Tata Motors Passenger Vehicles, and similar) tagged as "Supplier" to IndiaMART — this is a relationship-graph artifact, not a cost-side vendor list: these are large corporates that reference IndiaMART's marketplace in their own filings, not companies IndiaMART sources material inputs from, and none of them appears in the cost notes above with a quantified spend. **Not proven from available data** that IndiaMART discloses a named vendor's share of COGS or purchases, or a single-source/sole-source dependency for any input — the Note 24 "Other expenses" breakdown shows category-level totals (Content development, Internet/hosting, Customer support, Outsourced sales) but no vendor-level concentration, and no risk-factor language in the reviewed filing names a specific third-party dependency (e.g., a named cloud provider or verification vendor) as material. The nearest quantifiable dependency is structural rather than vendor-specific: labor (44% of consolidated FY26 revenue via employee benefits expense alone) is the company's largest and least substitutable input, and it is bought in a competitive, non-concentrated Indian labor market rather than from a single counterparty.

## 3. Customer Pricing Power

IndiaMART can and does raise prices, but the last 24 months show the two customer tiers behave very differently. On the Gold/Platinum side (~50% of paying suppliers, >75% of revenue), pricing power is real: these suppliers buy value-added placement (search targeting, location targeting) that behaves less like a commodity subscription and more like a performance product, and retention stays high (Platinum monthly churn <1%, Gold ~1–1.5%) even as the company raises prices [Q4 FY26 Earnings Call, Apr 30, 2026, transcript]. On the entry-level Silver tier, the evidence points the other way: management implemented a price increase on the Silver subscription tier at the end of Q2 FY26, and by Q4 FY26 attributed roughly 1,000–1,500 of a ~3,000-supplier quarterly shortfall in net additions directly to that price increase (the rest to a geopolitical shock), with Silver monthly churn running ~7% versus ~1% for Platinum [Q4 FY26 Earnings Call, Apr 30, 2026, transcript: "out of that, I would say 1,000, 1,500 could be because of the war, 1,000, 1,500 could have been because of the price"]. Management's own framing — "we are not going back on pricing" despite acknowledging "SMEs are really going through a lot of pain right now" [Q4 FY26 Earnings Call, Apr 30, 2026, transcript] — confirms this was a deliberate price increase that traded near-term Silver-tier volume for higher realized price, not a case of customers simply absorbing it. FY26 ARPU grew 8% (11% for the top decile), but management itself attributes that growth mainly to mix-shift (suppliers upgrading Silver→Gold/Platinum) rather than a base-price increase across the board [FY26 Integrated Annual Report, MD&A, p.82-83], which is consistent with pricing power concentrated at the top of the customer base and weaker at the bottom.

## 4. Economic Control Verdict

**Mixed.**

IndiaMART controls its economics on the supply/input side almost completely — there is no commodity dependency, no disclosed single-vendor concentration, and its largest cost (labor) is bought in a competitive market it can flex up or down through its own sales-force sizing. On the customer side, the picture splits by tier: extreme fragmentation (no customer is 10%+ of revenue) gives IndiaMART structural leverage over any individual paying supplier, and Gold/Platinum customers absorb price increases with low churn — but the Silver tier, roughly half the paying-supplier base by count, pushed back hard enough on a mid-year price increase to cost the company net supplier additions for two consecutive quarters. This is not a squeezed business — no counterparty on either side can dictate terms to IndiaMART — but it is also not full pricing control across the base it serves.

## 5. The Single Biggest Bargaining Risk

The Silver-tier relationship: it is roughly half the paying-supplier base, the most price-sensitive, and the one segment where a proven pricing action has already cost net additions for two straight quarters — if that price resistance spreads to Gold/Platinum (currently insulated by value-added targeting features), the marketplace's core ARPU-growth engine loses its main lever.

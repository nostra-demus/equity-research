## 2. Aggregate Quality Score

**Band anchor:** the two lowest row scores are Customer stickiness (40) and Margin stability (40); the aggregate of 55 sits 15 points above the second-lowest row (40), inside the ~20-point ceiling the band anchor requires.

Weighting: Pricing power, Customer stickiness, and Margin stability are given the heaviest weight (roughly 45% combined) because they are the rows where the evidence is most negative and most central to whether this is a durable compounder — a subscription business with no contractual lock-in, rising customer concentration, and a five-year EBITDA-margin range of 26.5%–40.9% is structurally different from a "sticky SaaS" story despite the deferred-revenue growth. Capital intensity and Commodity dependence are weighted lightly (~15% combined) — both are near-ceiling scores, but being asset-light and commodity-free is a baseline feature of this business model, not a differentiator that should pull the aggregate up disproportionately. The remaining rows (recurring revenue, competitive intensity, rate-of-change, regulatory dependence, cyclicality, disclosure quality) sit in the 40–78 range and are weighted at their face value, pulling the blended read toward the middle band.

## 4. Read

IndiaMART is best read as a dominant, asset-light lead-generation marketplace for Indian MSMEs — not a durable, contractually-locked compounder. The subscription label and growing deferred-revenue book (+14% YoY, ahead of the 9% standalone revenue growth) are genuine positives, but they sit on top of annual/monthly renewals with no disclosed multi-year lock-in, a quantified retention metric that is simply absent from the filings, rising revenue concentration in the top decile of paying suppliers (41%→49% since FY21), and a five-year EBITDA margin range wide enough (26.5%–40.9%) to call "stable" only with real caveats. The single quality factor a buyer should watch over the next 24 months is **customer stickiness / renewal economics** — specifically, whether IndiaMART ever discloses a quantified retention or churn number, and whether the Silver-tier price-elasticity episode seen in Q4 FY26 (a price rise that turned net paying-supplier additions negative) recurs as management tries to keep pushing ARPU. On cyclicality: FY26's ROCE and margin sit closer to a multi-year high than a mid-cycle level — Return on Capital was 15.4% in FY25 and 13.5% in FY26 versus 7.5%–10.3% in FY22–FY24 [Capital IQ Financials Ratios export] — so the currently strong-looking returns are not the through-cycle level; the Cyclicality row (45/100, Mixed) is the more conservative anchor, and a reader should not extrapolate the FY25/FY26 peak margin and ROCE forward without discounting for the EBITDA volatility already visible in a merely decelerating (not recessionary) revenue environment. The industry rate-of-change row scored 45 — inside the Mixed band, not the ≤40 Weak-or-below threshold — so this does **not** trigger the §24 Filter-5 sector/technology-cycle-bet flag; the core subscription mechanic is mature, even though the discovery layer above it (AI search, WhatsApp-based buyer flows, ONDC) is genuinely still in flux and worth monitoring for a future re-score.

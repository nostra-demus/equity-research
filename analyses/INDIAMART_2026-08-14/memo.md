## 2. What the Company Does

IndiaMART runs India's largest online business-to-business marketplace. Buyers search the site for free. Suppliers — about 220,000 of them, nearly all small and mid-sized Indian firms — pay a subscription to be found by those buyers. The subscriptions come in tiers: Silver (entry level), Gold, Platinum. A smaller consolidated subsidiary, Busy Infotech, sells accounting software on a subscription too.

The revenue formula is simple:

> **Revenue ≈ (number of paying suppliers) × (average revenue per supplier, "ARPU")**

That is the whole engine, and it is why one number dominates this memo. In the latest quarter, revenue grew 11.37% year on year — and **0.0 percentage points of that came from supplier volume; +7.89pp came from ARPU alone** [earnings/02_revenue-drivers.md]. All the realised growth is now price and mix, none of it new customers.

The business is asset-light and generates real cash: positive operating cash flow every year FY22–FY26, with cash from operations exceeding EBITDA (earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation — a rough measure of operating profit before financing and accounting charges) every year for five years, at 121%–182% of it [earnings/06_earnings-quality.md]. Revenue is 99.6% India-concentrated. Web and related services are 91.96% of revenue and more than 100% of segment profit — Busy Infotech loses money at the segment line [business-model/03_segment-map.md].

## 3. The Variant Perception (§7)

**What everyone already knows.** Revenue growth has slowed every year since FY23: 30.8% → 13.02% in FY26 → 11.37% year on year last quarter, the softest of the last eight. Paying-supplier net additions have been negative in 3 of the last 4 quarters (Q3 FY26 −1,000, Q4 FY26 −1,200, Q1 FY27 −1,850). Management said on the Q1 FY27 call that buyer growth is stagnating. Sell-side target-price revisions run 16 down against 1 up out of 18 analysts over three months, and the average recommendation has drifted from Outperform to Hold [Capital IQ Estimates Consensus export, through 2026-08-12; Q1 FY27 earnings call, 2026-07-18].

**What is probably priced in.** A real slowdown, already. The reverse-DCF — working backwards from today's price to ask what growth the market must be assuming — says ₹1,784.60 requires only about **6.0% flat revenue growth a year for eight years**, below every single one of the last five years of actual growth [valuation/05_reverse-dcf.md §2–§3]. The market has marked this business down. What it has not marked down, judging by the sell-side mean target of ₹2,093.72 (+17.3% implied upside), is the specific mechanism below.

**What the engine thinks the market may be missing (the edge).** The 35.35% EBITDA margin in Q1 FY27 is not a new baseline. Management labelled it temporary — it comes from deliberately deferring customer-acquisition spend — and said it plans to hold that stance for roughly "2, 3 more quarters" [Q1 FY27 earnings call, 2026-07-18]. So the healthy margin and the shrinking customer count may not be two forces that cancel out. They may be **the same weak SME demand showing up twice**: once as fewer new suppliers, once as marketing spend the company chose not to make because there was nothing good to spend it on. If so, when spend resumes, revenue and margin miss together. The earnings module flags this **Critical** [earnings/08_earnings-red-flags.md §2.6].

**What evidence would prove the edge is real.** Over the next two prints (Q2 FY27 ~21-Oct-2026, Q3 FY27 ~Jan-2027): if paying-supplier net adds turn and stay positive for two consecutive quarters **while** the EBITDA margin holds at or above ~33–34% as acquisition spend resumes on schedule, this read is wrong and the Street's +17% target is closer to right. If net adds stay negative through that window while spend is forced to resume, the compounding-bear read is confirmed.

**Edge score: 55/100 (Moderate).** A genuine divergence — the engine's base and bear cases sit materially below the sell-side mean target — but it rests on management's own admissions in public filings and calls, not on a proprietary data source. It does not clear the bar for high conviction, and this memo does not claim it does.

## 4. Why It Could Work — Bull Case

Four drivers, ordered by how much they would move the thesis up [thesis §9A].

**1. The demand trough may already be behind us.** Q1 FY27's −1,850 net-add decline could be the bottom, not the start of a longer slide. Management has demand-side actions running: re-testing Google as an acquisition channel, cutting ad spend on low-quality buyers [earnings/04_guidance-consensus.md §2]. *Confirmation:* Q2 FY27 net adds at or above zero — which the engine puts at **35% probability** by 2026-11-05 (Unlikely, in the §10 bands).

**2. Deferred revenue is pointing up, and it leads.** Deferred revenue — cash already collected for subscription months not yet delivered — grew **14% in FY26, against 9% growth in recognised revenue** [business-model/02_business-identity.md]. That is the cleanest forward demand signal in the pool: prepaid demand may already be building ahead of what the net-add count shows.

**3. The market's bar is low.** Only ~6.0% annual revenue growth is priced in for eight years, against 11.37% delivered last quarter [valuation/05_reverse-dcf.md]. Clearing that on growth is near-certain; the real test is whether the market pays a higher multiple for the same earnings once the net-add trend visibly reverses. That re-rating is what the bull target of ₹2,298 (+28.8%) depends on.

**4. The balance sheet buys unlimited time.** Zero bank debt. Net cash on every measure: −₹573.11mn on the strict basis (total debt minus cash) and −₹30,971.63mn on the broad basis (which also nets in the liquid investment book) at FY26-end [balance-sheet-survival/01_capital-structure-and-leverage.md]. The stress test finds **no break point even past a 100% decline in EBITDA**. No forced seller, no covenant to breach, no need to raise equity and dilute holders — a levered competitor in the same demand cycle has none of that.

**The strongest single version of the bull case:** the market has over-punished a genuinely temporary soft patch, and this engine's own DCF is hostage to one unresolved input — the beta (how much the stock moves with the market, which sets the discount rate), where three vendors span 0.24 to 0.91. If beta resolves low, the fair value is understated, not overstated. The installed base is also genuinely healthy: ARPU compounding 8–9% a year for four years, 76% gross margin, and Platinum/Gold monthly churn under 1.5% through the same quarters Silver declined [business-model/04_unit-economics.md; 07_business-quality.md].

## 5. Why It Could Fail — Bear Case and the Killer Risk (§8)

**1. The core network is under strain on both sides at once.** Management confirmed buyer growth is stagnating on the Q1 FY27 call, in the same quarter a Silver-tier price rise pushed supplier net adds negative — the most severe finding in the business-model red-flag sweep (severity 55/100) [business-model/12_red-flags-sweep.md]. Revenue also keeps concentrating: the top decile of suppliers went from 41% to 49% of revenue, FY21 to FY26.

**2. The margin is a policy choice, not an improvement.** The 35.35% Q1 FY27 EBITDA margin comes from deferred acquisition spend management has guided will resume at roughly ₹10 crore a quarter. FY26's −385 basis point margin compression (a basis point is one hundredth of a percentage point) was about 78% driven by discretionary "other expenses" [earnings/03_margin-drivers.md]. The line moves both ways, on management's own timetable.

**3. No valuation cushion, and the peer check says the premium is not earned.** The base fair value of ₹1,699 sits below today's ₹1,784.60. On the one credible peer cross-section, IndiaMART trades at a **+87.9% P/E premium to Just Dial** (13.4x applied NTM P/E vs 10.29x) that the module finds only partly earned by its quality edge; peer-implied value is roughly ₹1,233 [valuation/03_relative-valuation-peers.md]. The moat is Narrow (55/100), and the economic-moat test (ROIC of 11.23% on a 5-year average vs a WACC of ~8.75% — profit per rupee invested against what that money costs to raise) **only passes on the low-beta assumption**. At a conventional small/mid-cap beta (WACC ~12.7–14.2%), ROIC would sit at or below the cost of capital [business-model/09_moat.md].

**4. Reported earnings lean on a non-operating line the company keeps calling one-off.** Roughly 30% of FY26 profit before tax and ~40% of diluted EPS came from a treasury mark-to-market gain — a paper gain on the investment book — recurring for five straight years while being labelled "onetime" on calls (₹1,038mn FY22 → ₹2,669mn FY25 → ₹1,907mn FY26; RF-DISC-002, High) [earnings/06_earnings-quality.md]. Consensus compounds it: FY27E EPS growth of +16.8% outruns FY27E EBITDA growth of +11.96% by 4.8pp, plausibly assuming that volatile line repeats. An EPS "miss" could happen even in a healthy operating quarter.

**5. Governance friction is real, though not disqualifying.** Incentive alignment scores **32/100 (Weak)**: the annual bonus "Balanced Scorecard" metrics are never itemised across FY22–FY26, and in the one checkable year CEO cash pay rose **+11.17% while diluted EPS fell 14.0% and the share price fell 9.75%** [FY26 Annual Report, Section 197(12) remuneration table]. A promoter-family related-party appointment drew **21.72% of votes against** [RF-SHR-001]. Against that: the two founders hold 49.08% directly, bought at incorporation in 1999 rather than granted, with no promoter pledge disclosed. Governance score 62/100, rating Watchlist, 0 Critical flags.

**The killer risk, in one sentence:** the margin help (deferred acquisition spend) and the revenue drag (falling supplier net adds) are not two independent forces that offset — they are the same SME-demand weakness expressed twice, so revenue and margin miss **together** at the first print that forces acquisition spend to resume.

**One caution on sizing:** earnings risk, valuation risk, execution risk, thesis-timing risk and India SME-sentiment risk are five readings of the same variable, not five independent risks — one adverse SME-demand shock moves all five at once. Do not treat that cluster as diversification [thesis §9].

## 14. Plain-English Glossary

Terms in order of first appearance; no new numbers or claims.

- **Net additions (net adds)** — new paying suppliers minus those who left, in a quarter. Negative means the customer base shrank.
- **Margin of safety** — the discount between the price paid and what the business is thought to be worth. Negative means paying *more* than the estimate.
- **Expected return (probability-weighted)** — each scenario's return times its probability, added up. **Risk/reward** sets that against the loss in the bad case; negative means the payoff does not compensate for the downside.
- **Data sufficiency** — how much solid primary evidence supports the rating; it caps how confident the engine may be. **Inverted score** — one where higher is worse.
- **ARPU** — average subscription revenue per paying supplier.
- **EBITDA** — earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation: rough operating profit before financing and accounting charges. **EBITDA margin** is it as a share of revenue; **EBIT** is operating profit after depreciation but before interest and tax.
- **Cash from operations (CFO) / free cash flow (FCF)** — cash the day-to-day business generated; FCF deducts capital spending, leaving what is genuinely spare.
- **Deferred revenue** — cash already collected for subscription months not yet delivered; a liability now, revenue later, so it signals demand early.
- **DCF (discounted cash flow)** — valuing a business by forecasting future cash and discounting it to today's money; **FCFF** uses cash available to all funders. **Reverse-DCF** runs it backwards: what growth must the current price already assume?
- **Beta** — how much a stock moves when the market moves; it feeds the discount rate, so a higher beta means a lower valuation. **WACC** — the blended annual cost of the company's money and the hurdle any investment must beat. **ROIC** — profit earned on each ₹100 put into the business; compare against WACC.
- **Moat** — a durable advantage stopping competitors copying the business; "Narrow" means real but shallow.
- **Basis point (bp)** — one hundredth of a percentage point (385bps = 3.85pp).
- **P/E (NTM)** — share price over expected earnings per share for the next twelve months. **EPS** is profit per share, **diluted** counting shares options could create. **EV/EBITDA** sets the whole business, debt included, against operating profit. **De-rating / re-rating** — the market paying a lower (or higher) multiple for the same earnings.
- **Net debt / net cash** — total debt minus cash (**strict basis**); the **broad basis** also nets liquid investments. Negative net debt is net cash. **Covenant** — a loan condition that, if breached, lets lenders demand repayment; there is none here, as nothing is borrowed. **Dilution** — new shares shrinking each holder's slice.
- **Treasury mark-to-market gain** — a paper gain from revaluing the investment portfolio, not operating profit. **Contingent liability** — a possible future obligation, such as a disputed tax demand under appeal. **Opex** — ongoing operating costs.
- **Consensus / sell-side** — the average forecasts and targets of analysts covering the stock.
- **Churn / NRR** — the rate paying customers cancel; net revenue retention measures revenue kept from existing customers, including upgrades.
- **RPT (related-party transaction)** — a deal with someone connected to the company, such as a founder's family member. **Promoter pledge** — founders borrowing against their own shares; none disclosed here. **AGM** — the annual meeting where shareholders vote.

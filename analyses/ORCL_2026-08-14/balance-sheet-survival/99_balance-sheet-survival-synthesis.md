# Balance-Sheet-Survival Module — ORCL (Synthesis)

## Abstract

Oracle's net leverage — net debt of $136,143mn against FY2026 EBITDA of $30,494mn, or 4.46x — is rising fast, from 3.83x two years ago, driven by debt-funded AI-infrastructure capex rather than acquisitions. The near-term maturity wall is small (5.5% of debt due within 12 months) and covered by cash plus the undrawn revolver. The headline ~20-month liquidity runway and the wide 3.0x minimum-coverage covenant headroom (+166%) both look reassuring on their own, but understate the real risk: once Oracle's guided ~$70bn FY2027 capex is folded in, committed liquidity is exhausted at an EBITDA decline of roughly 2-20%. Verdict: Stretched — solvent on debt service alone, but the capital program depends on open capital-market access while S&P holds Oracle one notch above junk.

## 1. Solvency Verdict

- **Verdict: Stretched**
- **Net leverage (net debt / EBITDA):** 4.46x strict, net debt $136,143mn (all-in gross debt $167,432mn incl. operating leases − cash $31,289mn) ÷ FY2026 reported/GAAP EBITDA $30,494mn [`01` §4, §5, §7]. Gross debt / EBITDA (all-in basis) = 5.49x; gross debt / EBITDA (interest-bearing-only, excl. operating leases) = 4.50x. Rising from 3.83x (FY2024) → 4.40x (FY2025) → 4.46x (FY2026) [`01` §6].
- **Liquidity runway:** ~20.1 months on the module-standard "hard obligations" basis (committed liquidity $41,894mn ÷ monthly burn of debt maturities + cash interest + maintenance-capex proxy + committed dividends) [`03` §3]. This collapses to ~5.8–6.9 months once Oracle's guided ~$70bn FY2027 net capex (or FY2026's actual $55,663mn) is substituted for the maintenance-capex proxy [`03` §3].
- **Maturity wall (% within 24 months):** 13.3% ($17,355mn of the $130,105mn notes-payable-and-term-loan base); 5.5% ($7,210mn) within 12 months [`02` §2].
- **Tightest covenant + headroom:** Revolving Credit Agreement — min. Consolidated EBITDA / Consolidated Net Interest Expense ≥ 3.0x, tested every fiscal quarter-end. Computed headroom +166.2% (net-interest basis) / +121.0% (gross-interest, conservative alternative) [`04` §2, §3] — the only maintenance covenant disclosed on any Oracle debt instrument, so tightest by default; quality is unconfirmed because the credit agreement's own EBITDA-addback schedule is not reproduced in the pool.
- **Stress break point (EBITDA decline that breaks it):** The real break is **liquidity, not the covenant** — once Oracle's actual capex spend (not the maintenance-capex proxy) is used, committed liquidity is exhausted at an EBITDA decline of roughly **2%–20%** (basis-dependent: ~2.2% using §15-normalised FY2026 FCF, ~19.6% using reported FY2026 FCF) [`06` §3]. Net leverage crosses an illustrative 6.0x refinancing-sensitive threshold at −25.6% (shallower than a standard −30% haircut). The disclosed covenant itself does not breach until −54.8% (gross-interest basis) to −62.4% (net-interest, literal basis) [`06` §3].
- Solvency strength /100: **38** — rising leverage, capex-funded debt issuance, S&P downgrade to BBB− (one notch above non-investment grade, 2026-07-09), CDS spreads near an 18-year high; no hard cap from missing data applies (see §4 below), but the trend and rating trajectory cap the score independent of data-completeness caps.
- Liquidity runway /100: **42** — the narrow, module-formula runway (~20 months) is solid, but the economically relevant runway (~6–7 months once real guided capex is included) is short; the score reflects the gap between the two readings, not just the headline number.
- Refinancing risk /100 *(higher = worse)*: **58** — the near-term wall itself is small and technically covered, but the ~$70bn FY2027 capex program depends on continued unsecured debt/equity market access at a moment of active rating deterioration and record-wide CDS spreads.
- Covenant headroom /100: **55** *(capped at max 60 — MODULE_RULES "covenant headroom relies on assumed covenant-EBITDA addbacks"; `04` §2 explicitly applies this cap because the credit agreement's own addback definition is not reproduced in the pool)*.
- Downside resilience /100: **45** — debt service and the disclosed covenant survive even a 12-month market-closure scenario, but the capital program (not a tail case — a normal 2–20% EBITDA decline) forces a capex retrenchment, fresh financing, or a covenant-basis dispute absent management action.
- Data quality /100: **92** — from `00`: sufficiency verdict "Sufficient," full debt note, maturity schedule, cash flow statement, revolver terms, and contingency disclosures all present; only the covenant-EBITDA addback definition (Exhibit 10.14 text) and a standalone rating-agency rationale report are missing.
- Overall usefulness /100: **88** — no hard MODULE_RULES cap is triggered (see §4); all six sections ran on primary filing data.
- **Biggest solvency risk (one line):** Oracle's ~$70bn FY2027 guided net capex is roughly 2.3x FY2026 EBITDA and depends on continued capital-market access at the exact moment S&P has cut Oracle to BBB− (one notch above junk) and CDS spreads sit near an 18-year high — a normal-recession-range 2%–20% EBITDA decline could exhaust committed liquidity within 12 months if capex is not cut back.

## 1A. Module Disconfirmation

- **Strongest bear point:** The guided ~$70bn FY2027 net capex program is nearly 2.3x FY2026 EBITDA ($30,494mn) and is funded mainly by continued debt/equity issuance, at the exact moment S&P has downgraded Oracle to BBB− and Oracle's 5-year CDS spread has widened to roughly its highest level in 18 years, above 2008-crisis levels [`01` §6; `02` §5].
- **Strongest bull point (steelman):** The one disclosed covenant does not breach until a 54.8%–62.4% EBITDA decline; debt service — not the capex program — survives even a 12-month "market closure" scenario using cash ($31.3bn) plus the fully available undrawn $10.0bn revolver [`06` §4]; and the EBITDA used throughout is genuinely cash-backed (CFO/EBITDA ~90%–105% in FY2026, per `04` §1 citing `earnings/06_earnings-quality.md`), so the coverage ratios are not an accounting artifact.
- **Single killer risk:** Committed liquidity is exhausted within 12 months at a shallow 2%–20% EBITDA decline if Oracle maintains its current guided capex pace without a spending cut or fresh financing [`06` §3] — a normal-recession-range shock, not a tail event.
- **Disconfirming evidence already visible:** The near-term maturity wall is small and self-funded in isolation (5.5% of debt due within 12 months, 13.3% within 24 months, both covered several times over by cash + the undrawn revolver) [`02` §2, §4], and management has already signaled capex flexibility as an available lever (Oracle's own guidance framing treats the ~$70bn figure as a plan, not a contractual commitment) [`06` §4] — meaning the liquidity "break" identified above is an avoidable, discretionary-spending outcome, not a forced default path.

## 3. Reconciliation

Three disagreements surfaced across the specialist outputs; the more conservative reading is used in every case:

1. **Covenant-headroom cap — soft flag (00) vs. hard cap (04).** `00`'s triage treated the undisclosed covenant-EBITDA addback definition as a soft flag not meeting the bar for MODULE_RULES' "assumed covenant-EBITDA addbacks" hard cap (Covenant headroom max 60), because the 3.0x threshold and a compliance attestation are both disclosed. `04`, computing the actual headroom, applied the cap anyway, reasoning that using reported EBITDA as a proxy for the credit agreement's own undefined "Consolidated EBITDA" is itself an assumed-addback substitution. **Reconciled view: the cap is applied** (covenant headroom capped at 60) — the more conservative reading, consistent with `04`'s own worked calculation and CLAUDE.md §4's default to the more conservative interpretation when evidence quality is genuinely ambiguous.

2. **EBITDA base for leverage — two Capital IQ tabs disagree.** `01` flags that Capital IQ's own Capital Structure Summary tab computes Total Debt/EBITDA of 5.03x (implying an EBITDA base near $33.3bn), roughly 9% above the $30,494mn reported-EBITDA figure this module uses throughout, which is cross-checked directly against the audited income statement and matches `earnings/01_historical-financials.md`'s independent calculation. **Reconciled view: use the filing-anchored $30,494mn figure** (the higher, filing-sourced number is preferred per §4/§5 — cite the number that actually appears in the source used) — this makes leverage ratios *higher*, i.e. more conservative, than the alternate CIQ tab would imply.

3. **−60% EBITDA haircut — covenant breach is basis-dependent.** `06`'s stress test shows the covenant survives at −60% EBITDA on the covenant's own literal net-interest basis (+6.5% headroom) but breaches on the conservative gross-interest alternative (−11.6%). **Reconciled view: treat the −60% scenario as a marginal breach**, not a clean pass, consistent with the conservative default.

No other material disagreements between specialists; all three canonical basis choices (net debt, gross debt, EBITDA) are used consistently from `01` through `06`.

## 5. Survival Summary

Oracle is more levered than at any point in its recent history and the trend is worsening, not stabilizing: strict net debt/EBITDA rose from 3.83x to 4.46x in two years, driven entirely by debt-funded AI-infrastructure capex rather than acquisitions or buybacks, and S&P has already cut the issuer rating to BBB− — one notch above non-investment grade — while Oracle's CDS spreads sit near an 18-year high. The near-term maturity wall is not a refinancing-dependent problem on its own: only 5.5% of the notes-payable book is due within 12 months and 13.3% within 24 months, and cash plus the fully available $10.0bn undrawn revolver would cover it several times over even under a 12-month market-closure scenario. The real vulnerability is liquidity, not the wall or the one disclosed covenant: the headline ~20-month runway and the wide (+166%/+121%) covenant headroom both apply only to a narrow "maintenance-level" spending assumption, and once Oracle's actual guided ~$70bn FY2027 capex is substituted in, the liquidity break point falls to an EBITDA decline of roughly 2%–20% — squarely inside normal-recession territory, not a tail scenario. A standard −30% to −40% recession-level EBITDA haircut is survivable for debt service and the covenant (which does not breach until −54.8% to −62.4%), but it is not survivable for the capital program as currently guided without one of: a capex cut, fresh debt/equity issuance (management has already signaled a ~$40bn FY2027 financing plan, including a $20bn at-the-market equity program), or, in the deepest case, a covenant-basis dispute if the tighter gross-interest reading is later applied.

## 6A. Survival Playbook (non-speculative levers)

- **Capex flexibility (management-signaled, not yet executed):** `06` §4 notes management has signaled capex retrenchment is the most plausible response to a funding shortfall; a portion of the disclosed $260,000mn of additional, not-yet-commenced data-center lease commitments (generally starting FY2027–FY2029) is the specific lever available, since these are signed but not yet on the balance sheet [`05` §1].
- **Committed, undrawn revolver:** $10.0bn, unsecured, non-borrowing-base, $0 drawn as of 2026-05-31, available through 2031-03-06 — a real, already-in-hand liquidity source [`03` §1].
- **Planned new financing:** management has guided a ~$40bn FY2027 financing plan including a previously announced $20bn at-the-market equity program [`03` §4, citing `earnings/04_guidance-consensus.md`] — disclosed and evidenced, though execution is not yet complete and depends on market access.
- **Dividend/buyback flexibility:** no covenant restricts dividends or buybacks in the disclosed debt terms (`04` §2 finds no such covenant); FY2026 buybacks were already minimal ($93mn) and not contractually committed, so a further pause would not represent a contractual breach — but no evidence in the pool shows Oracle has actually signaled an intent to cut the $5.7bn common dividend, so this is a theoretical, not evidenced, lever.
- No asset-sale program is disclosed or authorized in the data pool [`02` §4] — not listed as an available lever.

## 7. Note To The Final Synthesizer

- **Leverage — level and direction, gross and net:** Net debt/EBITDA 4.46x strict (net debt $136,143mn ÷ EBITDA $30,494mn), rising from 3.83x two years ago. Gross debt/EBITDA 5.49x (all-in, incl. operating leases) or 4.50x (interest-bearing debt only). The increase is entirely capex-funded (AI-infrastructure buildout), not M&A- or buyback-driven — total debt rose 54% in FY2026 alone on $42.7bn of new senior-note issuance plus $5.0bn of preferred stock.
- **Maturity wall:** Small and technically covered — 5.5% due within 12 months, 13.3% within 24 months, several times over-covered by cash + the undrawn $10.0bn revolver in isolation. Refinancing is not secured for the "thereafter" bucket (69.4% of the notes-payable book, mostly 20–40-year paper issued in FY2026), and current market refinancing rates run +180bp to +300bp above Oracle's existing 4.77% weighted-average coupon.
- **Liquidity runway:** ~20.1 months on the module-standard narrow-obligations basis, but this figure excludes Oracle's guided ~$70bn FY2027 net capex. Once actual capex is folded in, the runway collapses to ~5.8–6.9 months. The $31.3bn cash balance on the books is freshly-raised debt/preferred proceeds, not retained operating cash, and competes directly with the capex program for the same dollars.
- **Tightest covenant and headroom:** Only one maintenance covenant exists on any Oracle debt instrument — a 3.0x minimum EBITDA/net-interest-expense coverage test on the revolver. Computed headroom is wide (+166.2% net-interest basis / +121.0% gross-interest basis), but headroom quality is capped (max 60/100) because the credit agreement's own EBITDA-addback definition is not reproduced in the pool. No max-leverage covenant exists at all, so the rising leverage trend is not contractually constrained by anything in Oracle's own disclosed debt terms.
- **Largest off-balance-sheet / contingent exposure:** RF-OBS-001 (contingent-liability spike) — $260,000mn of additional, not-yet-on-balance-sheet data-center lease commitments (15–19-year terms, generally commencing Q1 FY2027 through FY2029), roughly 1.9x Oracle's entire current all-in gross debt stack ($167,432mn); total maximum contingent exposure across all categories is $307,165mn, or 7.13x total equity. Debt service specifically (not the capital program) survives even a 12-month market-closure scenario.

## 8. Simple Summary

- Total debt (all-in, including leases): $167,432mn gross; net debt $136,143mn after $31,289mn of cash. Net leverage: 4.46x EBITDA, up from 3.83x two years ago.
- Maturity wall: small near-term ($7,210mn, 5.5% of the notes-payable book, due within 12 months); covered by cash plus a fully available, undrawn $10.0bn revolver.
- Liquidity runway: ~20 months on the narrow module formula, but only ~6–7 months once Oracle's actual guided ~$70bn FY2027 capex is included instead of a maintenance-capex proxy.
- Tightest covenant (3.0x minimum EBITDA/interest coverage on the revolver) has wide headroom on paper (+121% to +166%), but the credit agreement's own EBITDA-addback definition is undisclosed, so headroom quality is capped.
- Biggest off-balance-sheet exposure: $260,000mn of signed-but-not-yet-recorded data-center lease commitments — nearly double Oracle's entire current debt stack — starting to convert onto the balance sheet over the next one to three years.
- Survives a 30–60% EBITDA drop for debt service and the covenant (breaches only past −54.8% to −62.4%), but the ~$70bn capital program breaks at a much shallower 2%–20% EBITDA decline if capex is not cut back — this is the real fragility, not a covenant or default risk.

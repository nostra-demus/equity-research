# Valuation Module — ORCL (Synthesis)

## Abstract

Oracle's base-case fair value of $133.77 a share sits roughly 13% below the current $153.94 price — a modest overvaluation driven by a terminal-value-heavy discounted cash flow (DCF, $68.92) pulling the blended base down from the ~$150 level where the own-history and peer multiple methods independently cluster. Bull is $212.67; the headline bear is a 24–36 month structural-reset value of $31.44, against a milder 12-month cyclical-trough bear of $94.62. The price already implies a 51% FY2027 revenue beat against management's own just-issued guidance and a 62% terminal profit margin with no peer precedent — an aggressive, largely unproven bet. There is no cushion (margin of safety −15.1%) and the loss to the bear case is large (80%), so the call is Modestly overvalued, with real value-trap risk if the AI-infrastructure bet does not convert to cash quickly.

## 1A. Module Disconfirmation

- **Strongest bear point:** The reverse-DCF finds today's price requires a FY2027 revenue print of +51.3% YoY — 18 points above management's own +33.6% guidance issued weeks ago — and a 61.9% terminal EBIT margin that exceeds every named peer's best margin (Microsoft 46.8%) by ~15 points, with no historical or peer precedent for either [`05_reverse-dcf.md` §2–3].
- **Strongest bull point (steelman):** Own-history (`02`, $151.27) and peer (`03`, $148.70) multiples — built from completely independent data — land within two points of each other and close to today's price, and the raw SOTP peer-parity ceiling ($212.01) is nearly identical to the independently-derived Bull case ($212.67), suggesting the market's current multiple is not obviously irrational if the RPO backlog (+363% YoY to $638bn) converts on schedule [`03_relative-valuation-peers.md` §5; `06_sum-of-the-parts.md` §4–5].
- **Single killer risk:** Terminal value is 80.7% of the DCF's enterprise value (EV) — the fair-value read is dominated by a single, largely unobserved assumption (whether Oracle's return on capital stabilizes at its own cost of capital or keeps eroding), not by anything in the explicit 8-year forecast [`04_intrinsic-dcf.md` §5a].
- **Disconfirming evidence already visible:** `business-model/09_moat.md` independently verdicts the moat trajectory "eroding" (return on capital 12.35%→8.22% over four straight years, now at/below the ~11.2% estimated cost of capital) — a finding that arrived from a different module using different evidence and still points the same direction as the DCF and reverse-DCF, which is why the DCF is weighted at 20% (not zero) despite the Multiples-First policy.

## 3. Reconciliation

The high-to-low spread across all methods is 207.7% ($68.92 to $212.01), and the spread between the two weighted multiples methods and the weighted DCF alone is 119.5% — both far above the 40% Reconciliation-Gate tolerance, so this disagreement leads, not trails.

`02` (own-history, $151.27) and `03` (peers, $148.70) are trusted for the central tendency: they use forward/trailing metrics the market actually prices off, and they independently land within two points of each other from completely different data (Oracle's own 5-year multiple history vs a 10-name peer set). `04`'s DCF ($68.92) is not dismissed as a stale-methodology outlier, because two other independent modules corroborate its caution: `05`'s reverse-DCF finds the price already requires a 61.9% terminal margin with no peer precedent, and `business-model/09_moat.md` independently verdicts the moat trajectory eroding (return on capital now at/below cost of capital). `07` weighted `04` at 20% (not zero, not equal) for exactly this reason, pulling the blended base ($133.77) below both multiples methods individually — a disclosed, reasoned departure from a pure 02/03 average (~$150.0), not a silent drag. `06`'s raw SOTP ($212.01) is excluded from the base-point blend (0% weight) because it is a peer-parity ceiling that prices Oracle's leverage (net debt/EBITDA 4.46x) as if it were MSFT's (0.6x) or SAP's (0.8x) — `03`'s leverage-adjusted read on the identical EBITDA base ($148.70) is the more defensible number per Core Principle 3 (warranted vs observed).

**Overall: methods disagree materially and the disagreement is reconciled by explicit, evidence-cited weighting — not averaged away.**

## 5. Fair-Value Summary

Oracle's bull/base/bear levels — $212.67 / $133.77 / $94.62 (12-month cyclical) / $31.44 (24–36 month structural, the headline bear) — are driven by which of two forces dominates: the own-history and peer multiples (80% combined weight, clustering near $150) versus a terminal-dominated DCF (20% weight, $68.92) corroborated by the reverse-DCF and the moat module's independent "eroding" verdict. The current $153.94 price is above even the base case, implying the market already expects a 51% FY2027 revenue beat against Oracle's own +33.6% guidance and a 62% terminal margin no named peer has ever posted (best peer: Microsoft's 46.8%) — earnings-module and moat-module evidence does not support that being achievable on the model's own terms. The margin of safety is negative (−15.1%: price sits above, not below, base fair value — there is no cushion), and the downside to the headline bear case is large (79.6% versus 38.5% for the milder cyclical trough), a wide gap that itself signals how much rides on whether the AI-infrastructure bet stabilizes or entrenches Oracle's moat erosion. This is not a classic value-trap setup (no misaligned controlling owner, no persistent below-fair-value cheapness to explain away) — it is closer to the opposite: a growth-and-margin story priced in that the evidence base does not yet support, with the balance sheet (net debt/EBITDA 4.46x, FCF −$23.7bn in FY26) leaving little room for the bet to go wrong before the structural-reset case becomes live.

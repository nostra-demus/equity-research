## 4. Read

Oracle is concentrated on both axes, but the two concentrations are asymmetric in how well they are evidenced. Geography is concentrated and rising: the United States alone is 59.1% of FY2026 revenue and climbing (46.8% → 55.9% → 59.1% over three years) [FY26 10-K, Note 13], and U.S. long-lived assets are now $102.7 billion versus $24.8 billion three years ago, so the capital is following the revenue into one country. Customer concentration on *recognized revenue* is genuinely low and filing-proven (no customer above 10% for three straight years) [FY26 10-K, Note 1], but the company's own risk factors flag that "in certain OCI offerings, we are more concentrated among a number of large customers" [FY26 10-K, Item 1A] — and the $638 billion RPO backlog that management is using to justify a $70 billion FY2027 capital-spending program is dominated by "certain significant cloud contracts" the 10-K does not name or size [FY26 10-K, Item 7 MD&A]. The single biggest dependency the synthesizer should know about: a very large share of Oracle's forward growth story rests on a small number of AI-infrastructure customers — press and company statements point to OpenAI, Meta, NVIDIA, AMD, TikTok and xAI, with OpenAI's reported ~$300 billion Stargate commitment alone approaching half of the total RPO balance — and that concentration is not quantified anywhere in the audited filing itself, which only clears the much weaker "no customer over 10% of *revenue*" bar.

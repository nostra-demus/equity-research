## 2. Classification

**Capital allocation concerns.**

The table is not dominated by governance red flags in the classic sense — there is no fraud signal, no auditor qualification, no restatement, and the controlling insider's stake has been stable for years. The concern is capital allocation: capex has been allowed to outrun operating cash flow so completely that FY26 levered free cash flow (cash from operations of $32.0bn minus total capex of $55.7bn) was -$24.5bn [Oracle Corporation NYSE ORCL Financials_Annual.xls, Cash Flow tab], the dividend ($5.8bn paid FY26) is now funded from debt and preferred-stock proceeds rather than from free cash flow, total debt rose 54% in a single year to $167.4bn, and S&P responded with a downgrade to BBB- on Jul-09-2026 [Oracle Corporation NYSE ORCL Key Developments.xls]. Buybacks have effectively stopped and the historical multi-year share-count reduction has reversed into renewed dilution. None of this is a proven value-destruction event yet — management frames it as demand-backed growth investment tied to a $638bn remaining-performance-obligation (RPO) book [Q4 FY26 earnings call, Jun-10-2026] — but the scale and speed of the debt-funded buildout, on top of the CEO and CFO both changing within the same fiscal year, is a material capital-allocation and balance-sheet risk that this agent flags for the synthesis and for the balance-sheet-survival module.

## 3. Most Material Signal

The debt-level-and-trajectory row is the one that would most change this classification if it worsens further. Total debt already rose 54% in a single fiscal year, net debt/(EBITDA-capex) is no longer a computable ratio because capex now exceeds all of EBITDA, and S&P has already cut the rating to BBB- — one downgrade notch from losing investment-grade status. Management has guided FY27 net capex to roughly $70bn, higher again than FY26's $48bn net outlay [Q4 FY26 earnings call, Jun-10-2026], which means the debt trajectory is not a one-off — it is scheduled to continue. If a further downgrade below investment grade occurs, or if the AI-cloud demand behind the $638bn RPO does not convert into cash on the schedule management expects, this moves from "capital allocation concerns" to a balance-sheet-survival question, which is the sharper test this agent's evidence points toward but does not itself adjudicate.

No rejector-filter cap applies: the acquisition-pattern row scored 30/100, well below the 70 severity threshold for the Filter 4 serial-acquirer cap (CLAUDE.md §24, MODULE_RULES.md), since Oracle's M&A has been two large, spaced-out deals over a decade with no goodwill impairment recorded — not a pattern of continuous, debt-funded serial acquisitions.

This score should be read together with the balance-sheet-survival module's leverage and refinancing-risk findings, which this agent's debt-trajectory evidence directly feeds.

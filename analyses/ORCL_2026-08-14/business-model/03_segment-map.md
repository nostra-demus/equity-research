# Segment Map — ORCL

## 1. Segment Table

Oracle reports three operating segments under ASC 280 ("Segment Reporting"): Cloud and Software (renamed in FY26 from "Cloud and License" — no dollar figures restated), Hardware, and Services [FY26 10-K, Note 13 (Segment Information), p.99–100]. Figures below are fiscal year ended May 31, 2026 (FY26), with FY25 shown for comparison. Segment "Margin" is a company-defined measure — see Section 3 for what it excludes.

Revenue shares sum to 100.0% ($58,530M + $3,084M + $5,743M = $67,357M) [FY26 10-K, Note 13, p.100]. There is no "Other"/unallocated bucket at the revenue line (see Section 3 for the unallocated bucket that does exist at the profit line).

**Sub-segment detail that matters for "which one drives the value" (disclosed in MD&A, not the audited ASC 280 note):** within Cloud and Software, revenue by offering in FY26 vs FY25 was: Cloud applications (SaaS) $15,888M (+11% actual / +10% constant currency) vs $14,272M; Cloud infrastructure (OCI, IaaS) $18,101M (+77% actual / +75% constant currency) vs $10,234M; Software license $4,737M (-9% / -10%) vs $5,201M; Software support $19,804M (+1% / -1%) vs $19,523M [FY26 10-K, Item 7 MD&A, "Revenues by Offerings," p.45]. Cloud revenue (applications + infrastructure) was 51% of total company revenue in FY26, up from 43% in FY25 [FY26 10-K, Item 7 MD&A, "Cloud and Software Business," p.38]. In the most recent quarter (Q4 FY26) cloud infrastructure alone grew 93% y/y to $5.8B versus cloud applications' 10% growth to $4.1B, and total remaining performance obligations (RPO, i.e. contracted-but-not-yet-recognized revenue) reached $638B, up 363% y/y in USD [Q4 FY26 investor deck (Jun-10-2026), slide 5; slide 8]. FY27 guidance is for $90B total revenue (+34% constant currency) [Q4 FY26 investor deck (Jun-10-2026), slide 16]. These offering-level figures are corroborating MD&A disclosure, not part of the audited segment note, and are shown here because they explain what inside the dominant segment is actually creating the growth.

## 2. Dominant Segment

Cloud and Software dominates on both counts: 86.9% of FY26 revenue and 90.7% of FY26 total segment margin [FY26 10-K, Note 13, p.100]. That clears the >85%-from-one-segment bar in both directions, so Oracle is, in substance, a single reportable-segment company — Hardware and Services are non-core, low-growth-to-shrinking adjuncts. Within Cloud and Software itself, the profit pool is shifting fast: cloud infrastructure (OCI) grew 77% in FY26 to $18.1B and is now nearly as large as software support ($19.8B, +1%), while software license revenue is shrinking (-9%) [FY26 10-K, Item 7 MD&A, "Revenues by Offerings," p.45]. The value driver going forward is not "cloud and software" as a blended label — it is specifically cloud infrastructure/OCI, which is also the only piece of the business carrying meaningful capital intensity ($48B net capex in FY26, ~$70B guided for FY27) [Q4 FY26 investor deck (Jun-10-2026), slide 15].

## 3. Segment Disclosure Quality

The three segments — Cloud and Software, Hardware, Services — have been reported on a consistent basis for at least FY2017–FY2026 per the ten-year CIQ segment history, with the only change being a name swap (Cloud and License → Cloud and Software) that both the FY26 10-K and the Mar-2026 10-Q describe as "formerly referred to as" — i.e., no dollar restatement, just relabeling [FY26 10-K, Note 13, p.99; FY26 10-Q (filed Mar-11-2026), p. (MD&A segment discussion); CIQ Financials_Annual.xls, Segments tab, FY2017–FY2026]. Revenue is fully allocated across the three segments with no "Other"/Corporate revenue bucket, so there is no revenue-side disclosure gap. Profit disclosure is a different story: segment "Margin" is explicitly defined to include only "the direct controllable costs of each line of business" and specifically excludes research and development, general and administrative expenses, stock-based compensation, amortization of intangible assets, restructuring, interest expense and other non-operating items [FY26 10-K, Note 13, p.100–101, footnote 1]. In FY26 those unallocated items totaled at least $13.9B (R&D $10,272M + G&A $1,618M + segment-level stock-based comp $1,618M + other allocations $395M), equal to 36.6% of the $38,018M total segment margin, before further deductions for amortization of intangibles and restructuring bring the total down to $24,115M of consolidated operating profit before tax [FY26 10-K, Note 13, p.101 (reconciliation table); CIQ Financials_Annual.xls, Segments tab, FY2026]. That means the segment margin rates in the table above (e.g., Hardware's 65.4%) are cost-allocation constructs, not GAAP operating margins, and cannot be compared directly to a peer's reported segment operating margin without the same adjustment. Oracle also does not disclose segment-level assets or capital expenditure ("We do not track our assets for each business" [FY26 10-K, Note 13, p.100]), which is a real gap given how capital-intensive the cloud-infrastructure buildout inside Cloud and Software has become — downstream capital-allocation and unit-economics work will need to rely on company-wide capex and RPO disclosures rather than a segment-level split.

# Competitive Map — ORCL

Both required upstream inputs are present: `02_business-identity.md` and `03_segment-map.md`.

## 1. Dominant Segment

Cloud and Software is the dominant segment — 86.9% of FY2026 revenue ($58,530M of $67,357M) and 90.7% of FY2026 total segment margin — and within it, cloud infrastructure (OCI) is the fastest-growing and most capital-intensive piece, up 77% to $18.1 billion in FY2026 [FY26 10-K, Note 13, p.100; segment-map upstream, Section 2].

## 2. Named Competitors

Oracle's own FY26 10-K Risk Factors name its competitive set directly: "Adobe Systems Incorporated, Alphabet Inc., Amazon.com, Inc., Cisco Systems, Inc., Intel Corporation, International Business Machines Corporation, Microsoft Corporation, Salesforce, Inc. and SAP SE, as well as other companies like Hewlett-Packard Enterprise and Workday, Inc." [FY26 10-K, Item 1A Risk Factors, p.~4]. A separate risk-factor passage on Oracle's multicloud strategy names the three hyperscale infrastructure rivals specifically: "OCI's multicloud services work with a number of our competitors' products, including Microsoft Azure, Amazon Web Services and Google Cloud" [FY26 10-K, Item 1A Risk Factors, p.~5 area]. From this named set, the three profiled below were selected because they are of comparable or larger scale, are named directly in the 10-K, and span the two halves of the dominant segment that matter most (cloud infrastructure and cloud/on-premise applications): Microsoft (both infrastructure and applications), Amazon (pure infrastructure, AWS), and SAP (applications/ERP, the closest direct rival to Oracle Fusion and NetSuite).

### Competitor A — Microsoft Corporation

- **Ticker / listing:** NasdaqGS:MSFT
- **Where they compete:** Cloud infrastructure via Azure (direct OCI rival, explicitly named alongside AWS and Google Cloud in Oracle's multicloud risk factor) and cloud/on-premise applications via Dynamics 365 (rival to Oracle Fusion/NetSuite) [FY26 10-K, Item 1A Risk Factors, p.~4-5].
- **Scale:** Total company revenue $331.8 billion for fiscal year ended June 30, 2026, up 18% [Web: Microsoft FY26 Q4 press release/10-K coverage, 2026-07-29 (unverified, dated)]. Intelligent Cloud segment (which houses Azure) revenue was $39.3 billion in the June-2026 quarter alone, up 32% (31% constant currency), with Azure and other cloud services revenue up 43% [Web: Microsoft FY26 Q4 investor materials, 2026-07-29 (unverified, dated)]. This is roughly 4.9x Oracle's FY2026 total revenue of $67.4 billion [Q4FY26 Earnings Press Release, p.1].
- **Profitability / return on capital:** LTM EBIT margin 46.8%, LTM EBITDA margin 58.5%, LTM net income margin 40.3% [Capital IQ Comparable Analysis, Operating Statistics tab, as of 2026-08-13] — versus Oracle's own LTM EBIT margin of 33.2% and LTM net income margin of 25.4% in the same comp set [Capital IQ Comparable Analysis, Operating Statistics tab, as of 2026-08-13]. Company-wide operating margin (not Azure-specific) was reported at roughly 45% in the June-2026 quarter [Web: Microsoft FY26 Q4 coverage, 2026-07-29 (unverified, dated)]. ROIC/ROE not independently computed here — not in the CIQ comp export pulled; flagged as a gap for the `moat` agent to fill if needed.
- **Source named in:** [FY26 10-K, Item 1A Risk Factors, p.~4-5 (both the broad competitor list and the Azure-specific multicloud passage)]
- **One-line read:** Larger, more profitable, and diversified across infrastructure and applications — Microsoft is the single most direct, comparable-scale rival across both halves of Oracle's dominant segment.

### Competitor B — Amazon.com, Inc. (AWS segment)

- **Ticker / listing:** NasdaqGS:AMZN
- **Where they compete:** Cloud infrastructure only, via Amazon Web Services (AWS) — the direct rival to OCI, explicitly named in Oracle's multicloud risk factor [FY26 10-K, Item 1A Risk Factors, p.~5 area].
- **Scale:** AWS segment sales were $128.7 billion for full-year 2025, up 20% year over year [Web: Amazon FY2025 10-K/annual report coverage, 2026 (unverified, dated)] — about 7.1x Oracle's FY2026 cloud infrastructure revenue of $18.1 billion [FY26 10-K, Item 7 MD&A, "Revenues by Offerings," p.45]. In the most recent reported quarter (Q2 2026, calendar), AWS revenue was $42.2 billion, up 36.7% year over year [Web: Amazon Q2 2026 earnings coverage, 2026-07-30 (unverified, dated)].
- **Profitability / return on capital:** AWS segment operating income was $45.6 billion on $128.7 billion of revenue in full-year 2025, an operating margin of about 35.4% [Web: Amazon FY2025 10-K/annual report coverage, 2026 (unverified, dated) — margin is this agent's calculation from the two disclosed figures]. In Q2 2026 the AWS operating margin expanded to 39.4% (operating income $16.6 billion on $42.2 billion revenue), up roughly 650 basis points year over year [Web: Amazon Q2 2026 earnings coverage, 2026-07-30 (unverified, dated)]. Amazon-consolidated ROIC/ROE not pulled — not evaluated here; AWS-specific return on capital is not separately disclosed by Amazon.
- **Source named in:** [FY26 10-K, Item 1A Risk Factors, p.~4-5]
- **One-line read:** AWS is the largest hyperscaler by revenue and runs a materially higher, and improving, operating margin than Oracle's consolidated business — a pure infrastructure comparison that is the toughest read on OCI's unit economics once OCI reaches AWS's scale.

### Competitor C — SAP SE

- **Ticker / listing:** XTRA:SAP (ADR: NYSE:SAP)
- **Where they compete:** Cloud and on-premise enterprise applications (ERP, finance, supply chain) — the direct rival to Oracle Fusion and NetSuite within the applications half of the Cloud and Software segment [FY26 10-K, Item 1A Risk Factors, p.~4].
- **Scale:** Cloud revenue of €6.3 billion in Q2 2026, up 22% (24% constant currency); current cloud backlog €22.9 billion, up 27% [Web: SAP Q2 2026 quarterly statement, 2026-07-23 (unverified, dated)]. Company-wide LTM revenue growth of 6.4% per the Capital IQ comp set [Capital IQ Comparable Analysis, Operating Statistics tab, as of 2026-08-13] — a smaller and slower-growing company than Oracle at the consolidated level, though its cloud applications line competes head-on with Oracle's SaaS applications revenue of $15.9 billion (+11%) [FY26 10-K, Item 7 MD&A, "Revenues by Offerings," p.45].
- **Profitability / return on capital:** LTM EBIT margin 28.8%, LTM EBITDA margin 30.8%, LTM net income margin 20.4% [Capital IQ Comparable Analysis, Operating Statistics tab, as of 2026-08-13] — below Oracle's LTM EBIT margin of 33.2% in the same comp set. Non-IFRS cloud gross margin was 74.6% in Q2 2026, down 0.7 points year over year on AI-investment and acquisition costs [Web: SAP Q2 2026 quarterly statement, 2026-07-23 (unverified, dated)]. ROIC/ROE not pulled from this data set — flagged as a gap.
- **Source named in:** [FY26 10-K, Item 1A Risk Factors, p.~4]
- **One-line read:** SAP is the applications-layer peer where Oracle currently earns a higher company-level margin, but SAP's cloud backlog growth (27%) is outpacing Oracle's total cloud applications growth (11%), suggesting SAP is not losing ground in ERP despite its lower overall margin.

## 3. Competitive Position

Oracle's position varies sharply by sub-market. In cloud infrastructure, Oracle holds roughly 3% of global cloud infrastructure spending versus AWS's 28%, Azure's 21%, and Google Cloud's 14% [Web: Synergy Research Group, cloud market share commentary, 2026 (unverified, dated)] — a small base, but Oracle's reported infrastructure growth (77% FY2026, 93% in Q4 alone) and its 363% RPO surge to $638 billion outpace AWS's 20-37% and sit closer to Google Cloud's fastest-in-class 63% [Q4FY26 Earnings Press Release, p.1-2; Web: Synergy Research Group, 2026 (unverified, dated)]. This is best read as **gaining share off a small base**, not holding or losing — but the RPO figure itself is a proxy for future, not realized, revenue, and $75 billion of the RPO increase is prepaid or customer-supplied-hardware contracts, a different risk profile from steady-state infrastructure revenue [FY26 10-K, Note 1 area; business-identity upstream, Section 3a]. In cloud applications, Oracle's 11% SaaS growth trails SAP's 22-27% cloud/backlog growth, which reads as **holding at best, more likely losing relative share** in the applications sub-market, though no head-count or seat-share data is disclosed by either company to confirm this directly. No official market-share disclosure exists from Oracle itself in this pool; the reads above rely on the third-party Synergy Research proxy and on relative growth-rate comparisons, both labelled and dated.

## 4. Competitive Shape

Cloud infrastructure (OCI's market) is an **oligopoly**: the "Big Three" (AWS, Azure, Google Cloud) together hold 63% of a $129 billion-per-quarter global market, with Oracle and a set of smaller "neoclouds" splitting a low-single-digit-percent remainder [Web: Synergy Research Group, cloud market share commentary, 2026 (unverified, dated)]. Cloud/on-premise enterprise applications (Oracle Fusion, NetSuite vs. SAP, Salesforce, Workday, Microsoft Dynamics) is more **fragmented at the margin but consolidated at the top** — a handful of large vendors (SAP, Oracle, Microsoft, Salesforce, Workday) dominate large-enterprise ERP and CRM, each named directly as a competitor in Oracle's own risk factors, with lower barriers to entry in narrower point-solution niches, per Oracle's own description: "due to the low barriers to entry in many of our market segments, new technologies and new and growing competitors frequently emerge" [FY26 10-K, Item 1A Risk Factors, p.~4].

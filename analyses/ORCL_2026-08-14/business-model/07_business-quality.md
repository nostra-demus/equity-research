It is also inside the module's own rate-of-change cap (Business Quality aggregate max 65 when the disruption-risk row scores ≤40 [`MODULE_RULES.md`, Rejector-Filter table]) — the more restrictive of the two caps (47) governs.

The weighting is explicitly **not** a simple average. Capital intensity, margin stability, competitive intensity and rate-of-change together carry roughly 55% of the judgment weight because they describe the part of the business now generating essentially all of the incremental revenue and all of the capital spend (OCI/AI infrastructure) — a segment that is capital-heavy, margin-dilutive, contested by the best-funded competitors on earth, and technologically unsettled. Recurring revenue and customer stickiness carry about 25% of the weight, reflecting the genuine, filing-proven annuity quality of the legacy software-support base. Pricing power, regulatory dependence, commodity dependence and disclosure quality carry the remaining 20% as secondary factors. The result is a business quality profile pulled down hard by what is now the dominant, not the peripheral, part of the company.

## 4. Read

Oracle today is two businesses inside one income statement: a durable, high-margin, low-capital-intensity software-support annuity (the historical compounder) bolted onto a fast-growing, capital-devouring, competitively contested AI-infrastructure business (OCI) that is now the entire growth story. The legacy piece still scores well on recurring revenue and stickiness, but it is shrinking as a share of the whole (software license revenue -9% y/y), and the piece actually driving results — OCI, at +77% for the year and +93% in Q4 — is the one dragging gross margin down five straight years to 65.8% [CIQ Financials_Annual.xls, Ratios tab] and consuming capex equal to roughly three-quarters of total revenue. Return on capital is not sitting at a cyclical peak that a buyer should discount for mean reversion — it has actually been declining (Return on Capital % fell from 12.3% in FY22 to 8.2% in FY26 [CIQ Financials_Annual.xls, Ratios tab]) even as revenue growth accelerated, because the capex base is growing faster than the earnings it has not yet produced; the two facts (headline revenue acceleration and declining returns on capital) reconcile as the same underlying story — a business in the early, unproven-payback phase of an infrastructure build, not a business enjoying a temporary margin high. The single quality factor a buyer should watch over the next 24 months is **capital intensity converting into free-cash-flow recovery** — specifically whether the ~$638B RPO book actually converts to recognized, cash-generative revenue on schedule as the FY27 ~$70B capex program comes online, or whether demand growth decelerates while the depreciation and lease commitments remain fixed.

Because the industry rate-of-change / disruption-risk row scored 33 (≤40), this reads closer to a sector / technology-cycle bet on AI infrastructure demand than a durable software compounder for the portion of the business now driving results (CLAUDE.md §24, Filter 5) — the legacy database/ERP annuity is durable, but it is no longer what determines the outcome of an Oracle investment over the next two years.

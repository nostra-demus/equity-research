# Moat — ORCL

## 1. Named Competitors

(Inherited from `08_competitive-map.md` §2 — all three drawn directly from Oracle's own FY26 10-K Risk Factors competitor list.)

- **Microsoft Corporation (NasdaqGS:MSFT)** — competes via Azure (cloud infrastructure, direct OCI rival) and Dynamics 365 (applications, rival to Fusion/NetSuite).
- **Amazon.com, Inc. (NasdaqGS:AMZN)** — competes via AWS, cloud infrastructure only, direct OCI rival.
- **SAP SE (XTRA:SAP)** — competes in cloud/on-premise enterprise applications (ERP), direct rival to Oracle Fusion and NetSuite.

The two moat sources with real evidence are **switching costs** (65/100) in the legacy database/ERP annuity, and, more weakly, **distribution** via multicloud embedding (45/100) and **brand** (40/100) in the support base. The other seven are not proven, or are net negatives (cost advantage, scale, natural-resource access) in the segment now driving results.

They are shown for context only; the decisive test below runs on Oracle's own filing-sourced numbers.

**Oracle's own computed ROIC (this agent's calculation, standard base).** NOPAT = EBIT × (1 − normalized tax rate). EBIT (GAAP operating income) FY26 = $22,385M [CIQ Financials_Annual.xls, Income Statement tab]. The FY26 **reported** effective tax rate was 12.6% ($2,467M tax / $19,554M EBT incl. unusual items) [CIQ Financials_Annual.xls, Income Statement & Ratios tabs] — but Oracle's own 10-K discloses this rate is depressed by one-off items: excluding stock-based compensation, intangible amortization, restructuring, a deferred-tax benefit from a prior legal-entity realignment, and the one-time remeasurement impact of the U.S. "One, Big, Beautiful Bill Act," **the company's own disclosed normalized effective tax rate is 19.9% for FY26 (19.7% for FY25), versus the 12.6% (12.1%) reported rate** [FY26 10-K, Note 12 Income Taxes, p.~12558]. This agent uses Oracle's own 19.9% normalized rate, per the same normalization discipline the DCF orb applies to its own NOPAT — **stripped items: stock-based compensation, intangible amortization, restructuring, the FY2021 legal-entity-realignment deferred-tax benefit, and the FY26 One Big Beautiful Bill Act deferred-tax remeasurement.**

- NOPAT (normalized) = $22,385M × (1 − 0.199) = **$17,930M**.
- Invested capital (Total Debt + Total Equity, CIQ basis): FY25 ending $129,921M; FY26 ending $210,488M [CIQ Financials_Annual.xls, Balance Sheet tab]. Note: CIQ's "Total Debt" ($167,432M FY26) includes finance/operating lease liabilities beyond the $129,541M of "notes payable and other borrowings" disclosed in the 10-K balance sheet itself [FY26 10-K, Note 6, p.~58017] — both bases are shown because they diverge materially and should not be conflated.
- **Ending-capital ROIC (FY26, normalized tax):** $17,930M / $210,488M = **8.52%** — close to, and cross-checking, CIQ's own headline 8.22% (CIQ's figure implicitly uses the lower 12.6% reported tax rate on a similar capital base: $22,385M × 0.874 = $19,565M / $210,488M ≈ 9.30%, still landing in the same 8–9% band; the exact few points of divergence from CIQ's 8.22% likely reflect a different capital-base definition CIQ does not disclose).
- **Average-capital ROIC (FY26, normalized tax, capital base averaged FY25–FY26 to smooth the year's near-doubling of the balance sheet):** $17,930M / [($129,921M+$210,488M)/2] = $17,930M / $170,205M = **10.53%**.

Both computed figures (8.5% ending-capital, 10.5% average-capital) **cross-check against, and materially confirm, the CIQ headline (8.22%)** rather than diverging from it — no red flag of misread.

**The economic moat test (required).**

> Return on capital **at or below** cost of capital: **~8.5–10.5% ROIC** (Oracle, FY26, own-computed, ending-to-average capital range) vs **~11.2% WACC** (CAPM-based estimate: risk-free rate 4.7% [Web: 10-year US Treasury yield, 2026-08-12 (dated, unverified)] + 5-year beta 1.72 [Capital IQ Comparable Analysis, Operating Statistics tab; NYSE:ORCL Public Company Profile, "Beta 5Y"] × 5.0% equity-risk-premium estimate → cost of equity ~13.3%; after-tax cost of debt ~4.25% from FY26 senior-note coupons averaging ~5.3% [FY26 10-K, Note 6, p.~22247-22389] × (1−19.9%); market-value weights ~77% equity / ~23% debt using $441,519M market cap [NYSE:ORCL Public Company Profile] and $129,541M notes payable [FY26 10-K, Note 6, p.~58017]) — **Inference, not from filings; no company-disclosed WACC or hurdle rate exists in this data pool.** Gap: roughly **−70 to −270 basis points** below the estimated cost of capital, depending on which capital basis is used.

No company-disclosed cost of capital exists (checked FY26 10-K, transcripts, investor deck — no hurdle rate or WACC stated). The CAPM estimate above is clearly labeled inference. Because the gap is close (a few tens to a couple hundred basis points, well inside the uncertainty band of a CAPM estimate whose beta, ERP and debt-cost inputs are all approximations), the honest read is that Oracle's return on capital sits **at, or modestly below, its estimated cost of capital** — not comfortably above it.

**Through-cycle read.** CIQ's own "Return on Capital %" series has declined every year for four straight years: FY22 12.35% → FY23 10.54% → FY24 10.07% → FY25 9.62% → FY26 8.22% [CIQ Financials_Annual.xls, Ratios tab]. The 5-year average is **~10.2%** — itself close to or below the ~11.2% WACC estimate, so this is not a case of a single depressed peak year distorting the read; the trend has been declining for four years running, and FY26 (the year with by far the largest capital deployment — capex 82.6% of revenue [`07_business-quality.md` §1]) is the low point of that trend, not an outlier peak. Per `07_business-quality.md` §4, this decline coincides with, and is caused by, the OCI capital build-out outrunning the earnings it has not yet produced.

## 4. Where The Company Sits

1. **Relative to peers:** Oracle sits at the **bottom of named peers** on both margin and computed return on capital. LTM EBIT margin (33.2%) trails Microsoft (46.8%) and is roughly in line with SAP (28.8%, Oracle ahead) [`08_competitive-map.md` §2]; on return on capital, Oracle's own-computed ~8.5–10.5% trails Microsoft's third-party-estimated ~21% and SAP's ~12–14%, and is roughly comparable to or below Amazon's consolidated ~11–12% (noting Amazon's AWS-segment margin of 35.4–39.4% is itself well above Oracle's consolidated margin) [§3 above].
2. **Absolute (the economic moat test):** the company earns a return on capital **at or modestly below** its estimated cost of capital (~8.5–10.5% vs ~11.2% WACC, Inference, not from filings) — the decisive test, and it is not comfortably cleared.

## 5. Moat Verdict

**Narrow moat**, capped there by the hard rule in §5 of this report's instructions: Oracle's own-computed and CIQ-cross-checked return on capital (~8.5–10.5%, through-cycle average ~10.2%) sits at or modestly below its estimated ~11.2% cost of capital, so a "Strong moat" verdict is foreclosed regardless of the genuine peer-relative weakness that already argues against it. This is close to the **"moat in structure, not economics"** case: **switching costs** are real and evidenced (support revenue held flat at $19,804M even as license revenue fell 9%, the signature of customers locked into a legacy database/ERP annuity they are not expanding but cannot leave [FY26 10-K, Item 7 MD&A, p.45]), but that structural advantage sits inside a consolidated business whose capital-weighted return does not clear its cost of capital, because the dominant, capital-hungry part of the business (OCI) is being built at a pace and cost that has pulled the whole company's return on capital down for four straight years. The durability test over the next five years is whether the ~$638 billion RPO order book converts to recognized, cash-generative revenue fast enough for the return on capital to durably clear its cost of capital — or whether the legacy switching-cost annuity keeps shrinking as a share of the business while the low-return infrastructure buildout keeps growing as a share of it. Because the industry rate-of-change/disruption row in `07_business-quality.md` scored 33 (≤40, Filter 5), this durability test must be discounted further: a moat judgment made mid-AI-infrastructure-cycle is inherently harder to underwrite than the same judgment in a stable, slow-moving industry (CLAUDE.md §24, Filter 5).

**Moat trajectory: eroding.** Return on capital vs. cost of capital has moved the wrong way for four consecutive years (CIQ Return on Capital % 12.35% FY22 → 8.22% FY26, a period during which the estimated ~11.2% WACC did not move nearly as much) [CIQ Financials_Annual.xls, Ratios tab; §3 above]. Market-share evidence points the same direction in the segment that increasingly defines the company: Oracle holds ~3% of cloud infrastructure spend against a "Big Three" holding 63% [`08_competitive-map.md` §3-4], and in applications, SAP's cloud/backlog growth (27%) is outpacing Oracle's cloud-applications growth (11%) [`08_competitive-map.md` §2-3]. Pricing power is explicitly contested by the 10-K's own language on competitor discounting [FY26 10-K, Item 1A Risk Factors, p.~5994]. The one countervailing, evidenced fact — the support-renewal annuity holding flat despite a shrinking license base — is a sign the legacy switching-cost moat itself is stable, not eroding on its own; it is the consolidated economics that are eroding because the legacy annuity is a shrinking share of a business now dominated by a low-return capital build-out. This eroding trajectory is the early-warning signal that should feed the permanent-impairment / declining-perpetuity checks in the valuation module's DCF (CLAUDE.md §24, Filter 5).

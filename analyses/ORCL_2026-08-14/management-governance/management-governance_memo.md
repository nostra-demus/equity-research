## 2. What This Module Found

Oracle's stewardship record is mixed and tilts weak. For six years it was genuinely good at capital allocation: buybacks cut the diluted share count (the count used for per-share figures, including shares that would come from options) by 34.4% from FY2017 to the FY2023 trough [`02`, finding 02-005]. That reversed. Over FY2022–FY2026, the total cash Oracle spent exceeded the cash its operations produced by $130.5 billion, and the gap was closed entirely with new borrowing and share issuance, not operating cash [`02`, finding 02-001].

The most important driver of the downgrade is that the AI buildout is being paid for with borrowed money. FY2026 free cash flow — operating cash minus capital spending — was **−$23.7 billion**, the $5.8 billion dividend was not covered by cash flow for a second straight year, buybacks fell to $93 million (1.9% of the stock handed to employees as pay), the diluted share count rose 5.3% off its FY2023 low, and total debt rose 54% to $167.4 billion, followed by an S&P downgrade to BBB- on 9 July 2026 [Synthesis §1; RF-CAP-002, RF-CAP-003].

The most important risk is an unresolved securities class action filed 3 February 2026, alleging Oracle overstated its AI-deal growth outlook between June and December 2025. It names the CTO, both co-CEOs, and one director [FY26 10-K, Note 15; RF-MGT-005]. Nothing in the source pool contains a ruling, settlement, or admission, so it stays unproven — but it caps this module's conviction.

Two things pull the other way. The remaining performance obligations backlog (contracted revenue not yet booked) genuinely grew 363% to $638 billion — real, verifiable demand, not a story [`01`, finding 01-012]. And the CFO volunteered the less flattering earnings figure unprompted on a good-news call: $6.83 per share excluding one-off gains, against the $7.63 headline [`06`, finding 06-002].

Pay cannot be judged properly. The 2026 proxy statement has not been filed (due around 28 September 2026), so co-CEO pay is invisible. The one metric disclosed anywhere — the new CFO's performance stock options — pays on revenue, a size measure, not on per-share value [`03`, finding 03-004].

Founder and Executive Chair Larry Ellison holds 40.21% (1,158,232,353 shares), built almost entirely by exercising options and keeping the stock rather than buying with cash; total insiders hold 40.45%. He has pledged 346,000,000 shares as loan collateral — 29.9% of his own holding, about 12.0% of all shares outstanding [FY25 DEF 14A, "Review of Pledging Arrangements", p.16031, via `business-model/01`]. Every dollar-material insider sale filing in the last twelve months is a sale, with zero open-market buying (RF-MKT-001).

**The disagreement that matters.** `04` reported zero pledged shares and scored that item a full 20/20; the disqualifier scan, reading the FY25 proxy directly, showed 346,000,000 shares pledged. The synthesis resolved this against `04`: its "zero pledging" rested on vendor ownership workbooks that carry no pledge field at all — an absent field, not evidence of no pledge. The pledge is treated as real (finding 99-001, RF-OWN-001), and shareholder friendliness was reconciled from 57 down to 53 [Synthesis §3(a)].

A second, same-run miss: `05` reported no current-period related-party dealings, but the same FY25 proxy itemizes aircraft charter from Ellison-owned firms (~$2.5m and ~$0.5m), a SailGP sponsorship of a league he owns ($7.5m of a roughly $10m/year, three-year deal), and pay to family members of insiders — together 0.02% of revenue and 0.04% of operating expenses, all reviewed independently by the board's Governance Committee under a written price-protection agreement. Because the amounts sit far below the module's 1%-of-revenue red-flag bar, this is corrected as finding 99-002 but carries **no** red-flag ID and does not cut the score further; it lowered the module's confidence score from 60 to 57 instead [Synthesis §3(c), §5A].

`02` (40/100, negative) and `04` (73/100, positive) point opposite ways. The synthesis kept both: a large founder stake is a real alignment signal, but it does not offset a capital-allocation record that has destroyed per-share value in the last two years. Separate questions, neither averaged away [Synthesis §3(b)].

## 4. What Would Change This Read

**Would strengthen it**
- The 2026 proxy shows CEO and CFO pay gated on per-share or returns measures, not just revenue.
- FY2027 free cash flow turns positive and the dividend is again covered by cash flow.
- The securities class action is dismissed or settled with no finding of misstatement.
- Buybacks resume at a meaningful scale once capital spending peaks.

**Would weaken it**
- The class action survives a motion to dismiss or moves toward a proven-fraud finding — this would trigger the Disqualifier Deference lock to "Serious governance concerns."
- A third consecutive "restructuring" plan is named in FY2027, confirming the recurring-one-off pattern (RF-DISC-002).
- The Ellison pledge grows, or a margin call forces him to sell.
- FY2027 capital spending overshoots guidance mid-year again.
- Related-party dealings with Ellison-linked entities scale well beyond the current 0.02–0.04% of revenue, or the independent review turns out to be a formality.

**Data needed:** the 2026 Proxy Statement (DEF 14A, due ~28 Sept 2026); the FQ2 FY2026 earnings-call transcript; FY2027 quarterly cash-flow and dividend-coverage figures; the motion-to-dismiss ruling [Synthesis §6].

## 5. Bottom Line

- **Verdict: misaligned or weak stewardship.** Governance Score 47/100, confidence-adjusted 27/100, rating Weak. Two Critical red flags (RF-MGT-005, RF-CAP-003) out of ten total.
- **Why it could be better than it looks:** no hard disqualifier exists — clean unqualified audit since 2002, one share class, no serial-acquirer pattern (two deals in ten years), and a real 34.4% share-count cut from FY2017 to FY2023. The $638bn backlog is verified demand, and the related-party dealings are tiny and independently reviewed.
- **Why it could be worse:** if the AI-deal allegation is validated in court, the verdict locks at "Serious governance concerns." Meanwhile the dividend and buyback are being funded with debt into a 54% one-year debt increase and a BBB- downgrade.
- **What is missing:** the 2026 proxy. It would resolve CEO pay, the board-independence and committee gap, and current-period related-party detail in one document — and should be used to re-verify both corrected findings directly. That is the single highest-value next data request.
- **Watch next:** the motion-to-dismiss ruling on the class action (company response deadline 16 Sept 2026), and FY2027 free cash flow against the $5.8bn dividend.
- **Handling note:** this module supersedes the `business-model/11_capital-allocation-governance` quick-read and is the primary governance view. Its own confidence was cut to 57 because two specialists each missed a real fact sitting in the same proxy — do not take any single downstream "no pledge / no related-party dealing" claim at face value without checking the proxy [Synthesis §7].

## 6. Plain-English Glossary

- **Free cash flow** — operating cash minus capital spending; the cash left after keeping and growing the asset base. Oracle's was −$23.7bn in FY2026.
- **Diluted share count** — the share count used for per-share figures, counting shares that options and awards would create. Oracle's rose 5.3% off its FY2023 low.
- **Capital allocation** — how management chooses to use the company's cash: reinvest, buy back stock, pay dividends, buy other companies, or pay down debt.
- **Incremental ROIC** — the profit earned on each new dollar of capital put to work; 5.3–7.0% on recent years' new capital, thin against the scale of the AI spending [Synthesis §7].
- **Say-on-pay** — the shareholder vote on executive pay; 18% voted against at the FY2025 annual meeting, ~22% the year before.
- **Pledged shares** — shares posted as collateral for a personal loan; if the stock falls far enough, the lender can force a sale.
- **Related-party transaction** — business the company does with an insider or an insider-owned entity, where the price may not be arm's length.
- **Non-GAAP adjustment** — a company-defined figure that strips out items management calls one-off; Oracle excluded restructuring charges in both FY2025 ($374mm) and FY2026 ($1,838mm).
- **Remaining performance obligations (RPO)** — contracted revenue signed but not yet recognised; $638bn, up 363%.

# Candor & Disclosure Quality — ORCL

All figures in USD unless stated otherwise. Fiscal year ends May 31 ("FY2026" = year ended May-31-2026). Reporting standard: US GAAP. This report draws on `01_management-and-track-record.md`, `earnings/06_earnings-quality.md`, and `earnings/04_guidance-consensus.md`, plus its own read of the FY2026 10-K and two verbatim earnings-call transcripts in the data pool.

**Transcript coverage caveat (governs Sections 1 and 4).** The data pool holds two verbatim earnings-call transcripts: FQ3 FY2026 (Mar 10, 2026) and FQ4 FY2026 (Jun 10, 2026) [Oracle Corporation, Q3 2026 Earnings Call, Mar 10, 2026.rtf; Oracle Corporation, Q4 2026 Earnings Call, Jun 10, 2026.rtf]. Both are quarters Oracle beat on revenue and EPS. The pool does **not** contain the FQ2 FY2026 (Dec 10, 2025) call transcript — the single worst quarter in the review period, where Oracle missed revenue, disclosed a $15 billion capex-guidance overshoot, and the stock fell 10.8% in a day [`01_management-and-track-record.md`, Section 4; Oracle Corporation NYSE ORCL Key Developments.xls, 2026-02-05 entry]. A law-firm press release quoting the Dec 10, 2025 earnings release ("now expect[ed] fiscal 2026 CapEx will be about $15 billion higher than [it] forecasted after Q1") is in the pool and is used below strictly for **what was disclosed** (the quantified fact), never for **how it was said** — no paraphrase or press release substitutes for a verbatim transcript on tone or Q&A-evasiveness (per this module's partial-data rule). Tone and Q&A directness in the actual crisis quarter are therefore marked **Insufficient Data** below, and the two available transcripts are used only to test general Q&A style in favorable quarters — a lower and different bar than "candor in bad times," and the two are not conflated.

## 2. Non-GAAP / Adjustment Aggressiveness

| Signal | Detail | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Recurring "one-off" addbacks | Restructuring & other charges excluded from non-GAAP results recurred in consecutive fiscal years under successive named plans: 2024 Restructuring Plan ($374M, FY2025) and a newly initiated 2026 Restructuring Plan ($1,838M, FY2026). A charge labelled "restructuring" that recurs every year under a new plan name is the textbook recurring "one-off" — RF-DISC-002 triggered | earnings/06, Sections 4, 5, 8 |
| GAAP-to-adjusted gap (magnitude / trend) | Operating income gap: 40.4% of reported (FY2026) vs 41.6% (FY2025) — consistently large, not a one-year anomaly. Net income gap: 30.7% (FY2026) vs 38.9% (FY2025). Stock-based compensation alone ($4,811M, 28.2% of GAAP net income) drives over half of the total addback in both years, and is excluded from non-GAAP results in full every year | earnings/06, Section 7 |
| Does adjusted framing flatter reality? | Yes, on two specific dimensions the module's own scan already caught: (1) the GAAP effective tax rate (12.6%) sits well below the company's own non-GAAP rate (19.9%), a gap driven mainly by stock-based-compensation-related tax benefits that scale with the share price, not operating performance; (2) a $2,493M pre-tax Ampere Computing sale gain plus Bloom Energy warrant gains drove roughly a third of FY2026's reported 36% net-income-available-to-common growth. Management itself backs the investment gains out of its own forward growth math (18% vs. 34% headline non-GAAP EPS growth) — a genuine mitigating factor, since the flattering treatment is disclosed and self-corrected, not hidden | earnings/06, Sections 8, 10 |

**New metric introduced the same year capex ballooned.** Oracle introduced a company-defined "net cash outlay for capital expenditures" metric on the Q4 FY2026 call — $70bn guided for FY2027, versus a gross capex figure of $90–95bn once ~$20–25bn of customer prepayments and timing impacts are added back [Q4 FY26 transcript, CFO Maxson prepared remarks]. The netting is disclosed with a reconciliation (not hidden), and it tracks a real cash mechanic (large customer prepayments, which `earnings/06` independently verified are cash-backed, not fabricated). But introducing a new, smaller-looking headline capex number in the exact year gross capex nearly tripled ($21.2bn → $55.7bn) is a framing choice worth flagging, not a neutral change in convention.

## 3. Disclosure Transparency

Segment and operating disclosure is genuinely specific, not boilerplate: management discloses global GPU utilization (97.5%), a stated RPO-conversion schedule (12% recognized in the next 12 months, 34% in months 13–36), and site-by-site capacity-delivery percentages for its five largest data centers [Q4 FY26 transcript, CEO Magouyrk]. Results are filed 10 days after quarter-end — management calls this out itself as the fastest in the S&P 500 — and no delayed-filing or delayed-disclosure pattern was found in the pool (RF-REG-002 not triggered). Disclosure got LESS transparent in one specific respect worth watching: capex maintenance-vs-growth spend is not broken out (unchanged, a standing gap, not new), and the new "net cash outlay for capex" metric (Section 2) replaces gross capex as the headline figure precisely when gross capex became the least flattering number to lead with.

## 4. Tone in Bad Times

**Not directly assessable for the actual worst quarter** — the Dec 10, 2025 (FQ2 FY26) transcript, the quarter that missed revenue, disclosed the $15bn capex overshoot, and cost the stock 10.8%, is absent from the pool. What is assessable: the two available transcripts (both strong-beat quarters) show management answering hard, specific questions directly rather than deflecting — e.g., on the Q4 FY26 call, when analyst John DiFucci noted Oracle "spent a little bit more this quarter on CapEx than we expected" and asked about rising component costs, CEO Magouyrk gave a specific, technical answer (fixed-price vs. floating-price contract mechanics tied to cost certainty) rather than a generic reassurance. That is evidence of a generally non-evasive Q&A style, but it is evidence from favorable quarters and cannot stand in for how the team handled the actual bad print — "anyone is candid when results are good" is exactly the gap this report cannot close from the available pool.

## 5. Candor Read

Oracle's disclosure is specific and numbers-heavy where it counts — the $15bn capex miss, the ex-one-off EPS figure, and the gross-margin mechanism were each given a dated, quantified explanation rather than vague language, and the CFO volunteered the least flattering number ($6.83 ex-gains vs. $7.63 headline) unprompted on the Q4 call. Set against that: restructuring charges have recurred as "one-offs" under successive named plans for two straight years, the non-GAAP-to-GAAP gap has stayed above 40% of operating income for two years running, a new company-defined capex metric appeared the same quarter gross capex nearly tripled, and prepared-remarks language ("tremendous quarter," "overdelivering... across the board") describes a beat streak that `earnings/04_guidance-consensus.md` shows is closer to a coin flip (2 of 4 quarters clean on both revenue and EPS once one-off gains are stripped) — a real gap between the verbal framing and the underlying numbers. The single biggest candor signal is a genuine gap in the evidence, not a finding: the pool has no verbatim record of how management spoke in the one quarter that actually went badly, so this report cannot confirm candor was maintained under real pressure — only that the numbers, when disclosed, were disclosed specifically. Combined with the unresolved securities-fraud allegation (RF-MGT-005, routed from `01`) that management has not substantively addressed beyond legal boilerplate, this compounds rather than nets out against the positive specificity evidence. Verdict: **mixed**.

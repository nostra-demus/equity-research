## 4. Incentive Alignment Read

Oracle's own pay disclosure for this fiscal year is almost entirely absent — the 2026 Proxy Statement that would show both co-CEOs' bonus and LTIP metrics, weights, and dollar totals has not been filed, so alignment cannot be judged at the CEO level at all. The one hard number the pool does give — the new CFO's performance-based stock options, 20% of a $26 million target equity grant, vesting on "certain revenue metrics" rather than any per-share, margin, cash-flow, or returns test — is a size-based metric, and it sits inside a year in which Oracle's levered free cash flow turned sharply negative (−$24.5bn) and total debt rose 54% while capex ran roughly $15bn ahead of what management had guided one quarter earlier [`01_management-and-track-record.md`, Section 4]; paying even part of one senior executive's package on revenue growth, in that specific context, points the same direction as the spending pattern already visible in the numbers rather than against it. Given the near-total absence of proxy disclosure, the honest classification is **"mixed"** — not because the visible evidence is balanced, but because there is too little of it to call outright misaligned, and what little exists (a revenue-based PSO metric, a large equity-heavy CFO package with no per-share performance gate, no say-on-pay data, no CEO disclosure at all) leans toward size rather than per-share value without proving a pattern.

# Board & Shareholder Rights — ORCL

## 0. Data Availability Note

Oracle's FY26 10-K incorporates Part III (Items 10–14: directors, executive compensation, security ownership, related-party transactions, and principal accountant fees) **entirely by reference** to the "2026 Proxy Statement," which the company states it will file with the SEC within 120 days of its May 31, 2026 fiscal year end [FY26 10-K, Part III, Items 10–14]. As of this report's date (2026-08-14), that 2026 proxy has not yet been filed, and no DEF 14A / proxy document of any vintage is in `data/ORCL/`. This is a genuine primary-source gap, not a language issue (CLAUDE.md §27) — the document simply does not exist yet for the current fiscal year.

Per the Partial-Data Rule, this agent used the pool's FY26 10-K signature page (which names the sitting board) and labeled, dated **web sources** — principally Oracle's most recently filed proxy (FY2025 DEF 14A, filed 2025-09-26, covering the board elected at the November 18, 2025 annual meeting) sourced via SEC EDGAR, Oracle's own bylaws/governance-guidelines filings, and the FY2025 annual-meeting voting-results 8-K — to fill governance facts not in the pool. These are CLAUDE.md §4 tier-7 web sources (search-engine syntheses of primary SEC filings, not a direct read of the filing text, since direct fetch of sec.gov returned HTTP 403). They are dated, labeled "Web (unverified)" throughout, and confidence is capped at 2–3 accordingly. Where a fact could not be found in any source, it is marked "Insufficient Data." The 2026 proxy, once filed, should be used to confirm or update every web-sourced fact below.

**Historical precedent — the channel is not hypothetical.** Oracle's $9.3bn acquisition of NetSuite Inc. (closed FY17) was litigated for eight years as a related-party self-dealing claim: two derivative suits (filed May and July 2017, consolidated) alleged Ellison and Catz caused Oracle to overpay for NetSuite — a company in which Ellison personally held a large stake — effectively bailing out Ellison's own investment at Oracle shareholders' expense. The Delaware Court of Chancery's post-trial opinion (May 12, 2023) found the evidence fell "well short of proving" Ellison was a conflicted controller in the deal, and the Delaware Supreme Court affirmed that ruling on January 21, 2025 [Web: Dechert LLP case summary and Sidley "Enhanced Scrutiny" case note, both citing the Delaware Court of Chancery post-trial opinion and Delaware Supreme Court affirmance, 2026-08-14 (unverified, but citing primary court opinions)]. This is not a currently open red flag — it was fully litigated and resolved in the company's favor — but it is direct evidence that the founder/board self-dealing channel this section tests for is a real, previously-tested risk at Oracle, not a theoretical one, and that Ellison's ~40% stake plus board presence creates recurring exposure to this class of claim in future large acquisitions.

**Follow-up:** Once the 2026 proxy is filed, check Item 13 for any current insider-linked commercial contracts, family-member employment disclosures, or transactions with entities in which Ellison, Catz, or Henley hold an interest.

## 5. Minority-Shareholder Protection Read

Board mechanics look reasonable on paper — single class of stock, annual (not staggered) director elections, a nominal 61.5% independent majority, and fully independent Audit and Compensation Committees — but the substance underneath is weaker: the board's own leadership seats concentrate insiders (Ellison as Executive Chair *and* CTO with ~40% of the vote, plus two former CEOs — Catz and Henley — retained as Executive Vice Chairs, plus both sitting co-CEOs), the director-election standard is plurality-with-discretionary-resignation rather than true majority voting, and two of the eight "independent" directors have sat on the board for 29–32 years alongside the founder they are meant to oversee. Say-on-pay opposition of 18–22% in each of the last two annual meetings is a real, recurring minority-shareholder signal that a meaningful bloc of outside investors is not satisfied with pay governance, even though it has never crossed the threshold needed to force a binding change. The single biggest self-dealing risk to minority holders is the founder-insider concentration on the board itself (Ellison/Catz/Henley/Magouyrk/Sicilia hold 5 of 13 seats, and Ellison alone controls ~40% of the vote) combined with a director-election standard that gives a dissatisfied minority no reliable mechanism to remove an entrenched nominee — a structure already tested once, in the NetSuite litigation, and resolved in the company's favor only after eight years of court proceedings. Governance and ownership disclosure for the *current* period is also incomplete because the 2026 proxy (which would carry Item 13's related-party detail and updated committee/voting facts) had not yet been filed as of this report's date. Verdict: **weak / entrenched**, primarily on structure (insider board seats + weak voting standard + long-tenured independents around the founder) rather than on any proven current-period self-dealing.

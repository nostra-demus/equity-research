## 2. What This Module Found

Oracle's calendar is made of real dates, not themes. Seven of fourteen identified events have proven dates, and five of those sit within six weeks of this report [`01_catalyst-calendar.md` §1].

The nearest is the Annual General Meeting on 2026-08-25. It tests whether opposition to the executive pay vote (say-on-pay — a shareholder vote on how management is paid) falls below the 18–22% dissent seen at each of the last two AGMs [`management-governance/05` §4A, RF-SHR-001]. That is a governance signal, not a stock-moving event on its own.

The single most important catalyst is the FQ1 FY2027 earnings release on 2026-09-04. A buyer can genuinely time it: the date is fixed and the bar is published — revenue +27–29% and non-GAAP earnings per share (EPS) of $1.72–$1.76 [`earnings/04_guidance-consensus.md` §2-3]. The catch is that clearing that bar is not the same as proving what the price assumes. At $153.94, the stock already requires 51.3% year-over-year FY2027 revenue growth — about 18 points above management's own 33.6% guide — plus a 22.6% annual growth rate to FY2034 and a 61.9% terminal operating margin (EBIT margin) with no peer precedent [`valuation/05_reverse-dcf.md` §2, §5]. So an in-line quarter is a real risk to the current price, not merely a miss.

The most important risk on the calendar is the Netherlands Supreme Court GDPR ruling. It was scheduled for 2026-06-28 and is still pending as of 2026-08-14. A non-binding Advocate General opinion from 30-Jan-2026 already recommended against Oracle, and the per-Dutch-internet-user damages formulas (€500 + €100) have an undisclosed total size [`balance-sheet-survival/05` §3; FY26 10-K, Note 15]. This is not a "soon" catalyst — it is already late and can land any day.

Past the FQ1 print, the dated events tilt bearish: $2,250mn of 2.80% senior notes due Apr-2027 and a ~$4,645mn term loan due 2027-08-16 (part of $17,355mn due within 24 months), against FY2026 levered free cash flow of −$23,686mn, with S&P at BBB− since 2026-07-09 and 5-year CDS spreads near an 18-year high [`balance-sheet-survival/02` §1, §3-§5]. A $3.3bn Oracle guarantee of a data-center lessor's borrowing also matures in September 2026 [FY26 10-K, Note 9].

Timing quality splits. It is proven for the near-term governance, earnings and legal-deadline cluster. It is vague exactly where the multi-year growth story is decided — the FERC pipeline order, the lease-commencement window, the financing mix, and the FQ2 FY2027 date. That split is the module's honest read, not a uniform one.

## 5. Bottom Line

- **Verdict: dated, evidenced near-term catalysts.** Catalyst strength 52/100 under a §24 cap of 55; timing visibility 65/100; catalyst risk 62/100 (inverted); data quality 82/100; overall usefulness 78/100.
- **Could be better than it looks:** the 2026-09-04 print is the one catalyst that is material, dated, and falsifiable against a published number — revenue +27–29%, non-GAAP EPS $1.72–$1.76 — so a buyer can actually time it.
- **Could be worse than it looks:** the price already assumes 51.3% FY2027 revenue growth against a 33.6% guide, so a merely in-line quarter hurts. Past that print the dated events are mostly funding and legal stress — a maturity wall against −$23,686mn FY2026 levered free cash flow, a $3.3bn guarantee maturing in September, and an overdue court ruling with unsized damages.
- **Missing evidence:** the 2026 DEF 14A proxy is not yet filed (expected ~2026-09-28 under the fixed SEC 120-day rule). Until it lands, the AGM's actual ballot items and any new related-party disclosure are unconfirmed from a primary source. Two calendar dates remain inference, not filings.
- **Do not over-read one beat.** A single strong FQ1 print does not validate the multi-year path, in an industry the module scores 33/100 for disruption risk. That is the error §24 Filter 5 exists to prevent.
- **Watch next:** the Dutch Supreme Court ruling — already overdue since 2026-06-28, adverse-leaning, and able to land on any day.

## 6. Plain-English Glossary

- **Say-on-pay** — the shareholder vote on how top executives are paid; a high "against" share signals unhappy owners.
- **Guidance / guide** — the company's own published forecast for the coming quarter or year.
- **EPS (earnings per share)** — profit divided by the number of shares; "non-GAAP" means company-adjusted rather than the standard accounting figure.
- **Gross margin** — what is left of each sales dollar after the direct cost of delivering the service.
- **Reverse-DCF / "priced in"** — working backwards from today's share price to find the growth the buyer is implicitly paying for.
- **EBIT margin** — operating profit as a share of revenue, before interest and tax.
- **RPO (remaining performance obligations)** — contracted work already signed but not yet booked as revenue; a backlog.
- **OCI (Oracle Cloud Infrastructure)** — Oracle's cloud computing business.
- **Free cash flow (FCF) / levered free cash flow** — the cash left after running the business and paying for equipment and buildings; "levered" is after debt service. FY2026 was −$23,686mn, i.e. cash went out, not in.
- **Payout ratio** — the share of profit paid out as dividends; here 33.4% on a $2.00/share dividend.
- **Maturity wall** — a cluster of debt coming due in a short window that must be repaid or rolled over.
- **Coupon / basis points (bps)** — the interest rate on a bond; 100bps = 1 percentage point.
- **Revolver** — a pre-agreed credit line the company can draw on; $10bn is undrawn here.
- **BBB− / ratings action** — a credit rating one notch above "junk"; a further cut raises borrowing costs.
- **CDS spread** — the cost of insuring against Oracle defaulting; near an 18-year high means lenders see more risk.
- **Contingent guarantee** — a promise to pay someone else's debt if they cannot; $3.3bn here, real cash only if drawn.
- **Motion to dismiss** — a defendant's request to throw a lawsuit out before evidence-gathering begins.
- **ATM equity issuance / dilution** — selling new shares into the market over time; each existing share then owns a smaller slice.
- **Gross debt** — all borrowings added up, without subtracting cash; $167,432mn here.
- **Net capex** — spending on property and equipment, net of disposals; guided at ~$70bn for FY2027.
- **§24 Filter 5** — the house rule that a fast-changing industry, where winners cannot be picked in advance, caps how much conviction any single event can add.

## 2. The Nearest Dated Catalyst

The nearest proven-dated catalyst is the **2026-08-25 Annual General Meeting**, roughly 11 days from this report's date. Its two-sided outcome is narrower than a typical AGM: with the 2026 proxy statement not yet filed, the specific ballot items are not independently confirmed from a primary source [`management-governance/05_board-and-shareholder-rights.md` §0], so the meeting's main visible signal will be the say-on-pay vote tally against the last two years' 18–22% opposition [`management-governance/05` §4A, RF-SHR-001]. A result below 18% would read as improving minority-shareholder confidence in pay governance; a result at or above the recent range would extend a persistent dissent pattern that has never blocked a resolution but has also never been resolved. The next hard date after that is **2026-09-04**, Oracle's FQ1 FY2027 earnings release, discussed in Section 3 below.

## 3. The Single Most Important Catalyst

The single most important catalyst is the **FQ1 FY2027 earnings release on 2026-09-04** — not because of its own magnitude in isolation, but because it is the first live test of the assumption the market has already priced into the stock. `valuation/05_reverse-dcf.md` finds that at the current $153.94 price, the market requires FY2027 revenue to grow +51.3% year-over-year — 18 points above management's own freshly issued +33.6% guidance — sustained into a 22.6% revenue CAGR through FY2034 and a 61.9% terminal EBIT margin with no peer precedent (best peer: Microsoft at 46.8%) [`valuation/05_reverse-dcf.md` §2, §5]. The bullish path is that OCI capacity conversion from the $638bn RPO backlog runs at or above the guided pace and the flagged gross-margin "step down" lands no worse than management has already signaled — supporting a re-rating toward the Bull case of $212.67/share [`valuation/07_scenario-and-fair-value.md` §3]. The bearish path is that any one of the four named >$8bn AI-infrastructure customers (AMD, Meta, NVIDIA, OpenAI, TikTok, xAI) pulls back, or the margin lag between data-center capex and revenue recognition runs longer than guided, pointing toward the cyclical-trough Bear ($94.62) or, if the moat-erosion trend `business-model/09_moat.md` already confirms does not stabilize, the headline structural-reset Bear ($31.44) [`valuation/07` §3]. A buyer can time this: the date is fixed and the guidance bar is published, so the print is directly falsifiable against a known number — but this is also a Filter 5 (fast-changing industry, CLAUDE.md §24) situation: the outcome hinges on an AI-infrastructure capex race where `business-model/07_business-quality.md` scores disruption risk at 33/100, and a single strong print does not, on its own, prove the multi-year growth path the price already assumes.

## 4. Negative / Bearish Catalysts

- **Netherlands Privacy Class Action — overdue Dutch Supreme Court ruling.** Originally scheduled 2026-06-28, still unresolved as of 2026-08-14, with a non-binding Advocate General opinion (30-Jan-2026) already recommending against Oracle. Could land at any time with an unquantified per-Dutch-internet-user damages exposure [`balance-sheet-survival/05` §3].
- **OCI securities class action deadlines (2026-09-16 defendants' response; ~2026-12-18 if fully briefed).** Names the Executive Chair/CTO, both co-CEOs, and one board member individually; early-stage but building toward a dismissal ruling [`management-governance/05` finding 05-014].
- **$3.3bn lessor-borrowing guarantee maturing September 2026.** A contingent liability that could convert to a real cash outflow if the third-party lessor cannot refinance independently, at a moment Oracle's own credit is one notch above junk [`balance-sheet-survival/05` §2].
- **FY2027 debt maturities (Apr-2027 $2,250mn senior notes; 2027-08-16 Term Loan final maturity) against negative free cash flow.** FY2026 levered FCF was −$23,686mn; refinancing today's maturities would add an estimated +180 to +300bps versus the existing 4.77% weighted-average coupon [`balance-sheet-survival/02` §3, §5].
- **~$40bn FY2027 capital financing plan amid a downgraded, CDS-stressed credit profile.** S&P cut Oracle to BBB− on 2026-07-09; Oracle's 5-year CDS spread has widened to roughly an 18-year high, above 2008-crisis levels — both signal the market is pricing further-downgrade risk into whatever Oracle raises next [`balance-sheet-survival/02` §4].
- **Next ex-dividend date (~early October 2026, inferred).** The $2.00/share common dividend has not been covered by free cash flow for two straight fiscal years (RF-CAP-003); a cut or pause is a live, if unconfirmed, risk at the next declaration [`management-governance/02` §4].
- **$260bn of additional data-center lease commitments commencing Q1 FY2027–FY2029.** As these convert from off-balance-sheet commitments to recognized liabilities, they layer roughly 1.9x today's entire gross-debt stack onto a business already showing rising leverage [`balance-sheet-survival/05` §5].

## 5. Calendar Read

Of the 14 catalysts identified, 7 carry proven dates (AGM 2026-08-25, FQ1 FY2027 earnings 2026-09-04, the 2026-09-16 litigation-response deadline, the ~2026-09-28 SEC-mandated proxy-filing deadline, the September-2026 lessor-guarantee maturity, the Apr-2027 senior-note maturity, and the 2027-08-16 Term Loan maturity); the remaining 7 are windowed or fully vague, including two items — the next ex-dividend date and the Netherlands GDPR ruling — that are inferred or overdue rather than freshly scheduled. The next six weeks (2026-08-25 through ~2026-09-28) carry unusually dense, proven-dated event risk in both directions: a governance vote, an earnings print the market has already priced beyond guidance, a litigation deadline, a proxy filing that should close a real primary-source gap, and a contingent-guarantee maturity, all landing inside one month. The single most important timing caveat is that the calendar's biggest catalyst — the FQ1 FY2027 print — is not being asked to simply beat a normal bar; per the reverse-DCF, it is being asked to validate a growth and margin path that has no precedent in Oracle's own history or its peers', so a merely in-line quarter, not just a miss, is a real risk to the current price.

## What This Module Found

CEO Andy Jassy and CFO Brian Olsavsky have delivered what they promised: revenue and operating income beat guidance every quarter from Q2 2025 through Q1 2026 [Q2 2025–Q1 2026 transcripts]. Return on invested capital — the profit earned on each $100 invested — rose from 6.8% in FY2023 to 10.7% in FY2024 before a slight pullback to 9.7% in FY2025 [FY2023/2024/2025 10-K]. Normalised earnings per share grew 241%, from $1.38 to $4.70, between FY2021 and FY2025 [FY2021–FY2025 10-K].

The single biggest driver of the "aligned" read is candour: FCF (free cash flow — cash left after paying for capex) compression was pre-signalled in FY2023 and FY2024 shareholder letters a full year before it showed up in the numbers, and the Q3 2025 $4.3B of FTC + severance charges were disclosed in the first sentence of the CEO's prepared remarks [Q3 2025 transcript].

The single biggest risk is on the capital-allocation side: the $6.1B buyback authorisation has not been used in three straight fiscal years (FY2023–FY2025), while share-based compensation added 348 million net new shares (+3.4% share count) with zero offset [FY2023/2024/2025 10-K]. Underneath that sits a $131.8B FY2025 capex spend that missed the implied ~$125B guide by $6.8B, with management now committing to roughly $200B in FY2026 [FY2025 10-K; Q4 2024 transcript]. Five-year capex is 90.5% of operating cash flow, so strict free-cash-flow per share is now LTM negative [FY2024/2025 10-K; Q1 2026 10-Q].

## Bottom Line

- **Verdict is Aligned & competent, rating capped at Watchlist** — the Watchlist call is a data-quality artefact of the missing proxy, not a governance failure; the uncapped weighted score would be roughly 73 (Good).
- **Reason it may be better than it looks:** management has told shareholders what to expect and then delivered it for five years, and pre-flagged every major negative — the strongest single positive in the file.
- **Reason it may be worse than it looks:** RSUs vest on time with no performance hurdles, and $6.1B of authorised buybacks has sat unused for three years while share-based comp diluted holders by 3.4%. If the $200B FY2026 capex bet on AI infrastructure returns less than expected, there is no pay-side mechanism forcing a course correction.
- **What is missing:** the 2026 DEF 14A (Amazon's proxy statement) — the beneficial-ownership table and the compensation-committee detail. Filing it to the pool would lift all three caps and could shift the rating from Watchlist to Good.
- **One thing to watch next:** FY2026 quarterly capex run-rate against the ~$200B commitment, and whether any of the $6.1B buyback authorisation gets activated.

## Plain-English Glossary

- **ROIC (return on invested capital)** — the profit the company earns on each $100 of capital put into the business. Amazon's ROIC ran 6.8% → 10.7% → 9.7% across FY2023–FY2025.
- **EPS (earnings per share)** — profit divided by the share count; the per-share slice of earnings.
- **FCF (free cash flow)** — cash from operations minus capex; the recurring cash the business actually throws off. Amazon's strict FCF per share went from $3.12 to $0.71 to LTM negative.
- **Capex** — cash spent on long-lived assets (data centres, servers, warehouses). $131.8B in FY2025, ~$200B guided for FY2026.
- **SBC (share-based compensation)** — employees paid in shares rather than cash; it dilutes existing holders unless the company buys back an offsetting amount. $24.0B → $22.0B → $19.5B FY2023–FY2025.
- **10b5-1 plan** — a pre-set insider-sales schedule filed in advance, so the timing is not driven by inside information.
- **DEF 14A / proxy statement** — the annual disclosure of executive pay, board detail, and beneficial ownership. The 2026 one is not in the pool — this is what drives the caps.
- **RSU (restricted stock unit)** — an equity grant that turns into shares as time passes; here it vests on time only, not on hitting a performance target.
- **Say-on-pay** — the annual non-binding shareholder vote on executive pay. Opposition fell 44% → 6% from 2022 to 2026.
- **Poison pill** — a defence that makes a hostile takeover far more expensive; Amazon does not have one.

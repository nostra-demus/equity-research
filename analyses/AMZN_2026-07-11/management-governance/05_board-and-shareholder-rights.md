## 5. Minority-Shareholder Protection Read

The board provides real if imperfect protection for minority holders. The structural features are genuinely shareholder-friendly: no classified board (annual director elections), no dual-class shares, majority voting standards for director elections, a low 25% threshold to call special meetings, written-consent rights, no supermajority charter-amendment requirements, no poison pill, no golden parachutes. The absence of anti-takeover devices means the company is more susceptible to hostile bids than peers, but it also means the board cannot easily entrench itself against shareholder will. The Lead Independent Director (Gorelick, 14-year tenure) and the split Chair/CEO structure provide formal independence architecture.

The biggest entrenchment risk to minority holders is not structural — it is the very large SBC expense ($19.5B in FY2025, roughly $1.70 per share per year) running through an unused $10B buyback program that has not offset a single share of dilution in 2023, 2024, or 2025. Minority shareholders are experiencing net dilution of approximately 1.3% per year while management's equity compensation continues to grow. This is a capital-allocation concern (owned by agent 02) rather than a governance-structure concern, but it is the clearest direct cost to minority holders from the current governance configuration. The disqualifier scan returned all 8 checks clear; the RF-OWN-004 filter does not apply (no structurally misaligned controlling owner); Bezos as a non-controlling founding shareholder has interests broadly aligned with per-share value creation, as both his wealth and his 10b5-1 sell-plan price depend on the stock.

Verdict: **Adequate** — board structure is sound and minority-friendly features are above the large-cap norm, but ongoing SBC dilution with no buyback offset is the most direct minority-holder cost and the audit committee's independence and financial expertise require continued monitoring (proxy-sourced data, unverified).

## 3. Stated Strategy & Targets

Management's stated strategy rests on three pillars. First, AWS dominance in cloud and AI infrastructure: spend roughly $200 billion in capital expenditure in 2026 (up from $131.8 billion in FY2025 and $83.0 billion in FY2024) to build data-center and chip capacity ahead of demand, backed by $364 billion in AWS revenue backlog (as of Q1 2026) and specific enterprise and government commitments. The core argument is that faster AWS growth requires more front-loaded capex, but the return on these assets over their 5–30 year lives will be high, replicating the first AWS growth cycle on a much larger base. Second, retail reinvention: extend grocery to become the second-largest US grocer by growing Whole Foods to 650+ stores and adding same-day perishable delivery to 2,300+ US cities; use robotics and the Same-Day Fulfillment Center network to compress delivery further; expand Amazon Now (20-minute delivery) globally. Third, AI across all customer experiences: Alexa+, Rufus (agentic shopping), Kiro (coding agents), and Amazon's own custom silicon (Trainium, Graviton) to compound value across every layer.

Explicit checkable targets management has set in the pool period:
- **Capex FY2026: ~$200B** (stated Q4 2025 call, Feb 5, 2026; reiterated Q1 2026 call, Apr 29, 2026).
- **AWS capacity to double by end of 2027** (FY2025 Shareholder Letter, p.4; Q3 2025 call, Oct 30, 2025; Q4 2025 call).
- **Amazon to be second-largest US grocer** (stated as achieved in FY2025 — "over $150 billion in gross sales in 2025," Q4 2025 call and FY2025 Shareholder Letter).
- **Quarterly revenue guidance, one quarter forward** (the only formal numerical guidance Amazon issues).
- **Q2 2026 revenue: $194B–$199B; Q2 2026 operating income: $20B–$24B** (Q1 2026 call, Apr 29, 2026).

## 5. Management Read

Jassy and Olsavsky have been in their roles for 5 and 11 years respectively, and the leadership bench (Garman at AWS, Herrington at Stores) is similarly long-tenured and built from within. The stability signal is the cleanest in the observable data. On delivery: the record shows revenue and operating income guidance beaten or met in every period available, with the one near-miss (FY2025 capex slightly above $125B guidance) reflecting faster capital deployment rather than cost failure. The single biggest signal in management's record is the explicit, multi-year framing of the AWS capex cycle — management told investors in FY2023 and FY2024 that FCF would fall before recovering, then FCF fell exactly as described ($38B → $11B), and AWS growth re-accelerated to 28% in Q1 2026 with $364B in backlog. Whether that FCF recovery materializes on the 2027–2028 timeline management projects is unproven, but the accuracy of the downward forecast strengthens credibility. The one persistent concern for this agent is that the Q1 2026 "beat" included a very large Anthropic mark-up that inflated reported EPS; management disclosed this clearly but the scale ($2.47/share in FY2025, likely even larger in Q1 2026) means reported earnings frequently overstate operating performance — a recurring feature of the investment portfolio that reduces the value of headline EPS as a scorekeeping tool.

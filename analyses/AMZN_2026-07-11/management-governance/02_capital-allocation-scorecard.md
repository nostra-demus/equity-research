**Net share count assessment:** From December 2023 to December 2025, shares outstanding grew from 10,383M to 10,731M — a net increase of 348M shares (+3.4%), driven entirely by RSU vesting. SBC shares issued were approximately 209M (FY2023), 210M (FY2024), and 138M (FY2025). Buybacks in those three years were zero. **Intrinsic-value discipline not assessable from this module.** This module runs before the valuation module. Judgments on whether $0 in buybacks at AMZN's current share price is disciplined or a missed opportunity require a fair-value comparison that belongs to the valuation module.

**RF-CAP-002 assessment:** Buybacks are not reducing the share count — the share count rose 3.4% over two years. However, the cause is clear (SBC vesting from a large tech workforce) and management explicitly states its philosophy: "We seek to efficiently manage shareholder dilution while maintaining the flexibility to issue shares for strategic purposes." [FY2025 10-K, p.19] The dilution is not fraudulent, but it is real and unmitigated. RF-CAP-002 is triggered at **Medium severity** — buybacks are not reducing share count and SBC dilution is absorbing capital that could be returned. The severity is Medium rather than High because the primary capital deployment (AWS capex) has a credible return thesis backed by $364B backlog; the non-execution of buybacks reflects a deliberate choice to reinvest, not governance failure. However, the $6.1B of unused authorization sitting dormant while CFO runs at $139.5B warrants attention.

**NOPAT and Invested Capital methodology:** NOPAT = EBIT × (1 − 0.25) using an approximate 25% effective tax rate (a conservative estimate; Amazon's actual effective rates vary but averaged roughly 25–28% over the period). Invested Capital = Common Equity + Total Debt (broad basis, including lease liabilities), per CIQ Capital Structure Summary. FY2023 Total Debt (broad) is estimated as Total Debt/EBITDA ratio (1.6449 per CIQ Ratios) × FY2023 EBITDA ($85,515M) = ~$140,665M; Invested Capital FY2023 ≈ $201,875M + $140,665M = $342,540M. FY2024: $285,970M + $155,401M = $441,371M. FY2025: $411,065M + $178,547M = $589,612M. All inputs from CIQ workbooks [Capital IQ Financials export].

**Incremental ROIC read:** The FY2023→FY2024 incremental ROIC of 24.1% is strong — it shows that the $98.8B of additional invested capital in FY2024 generated $23.8B of additional NOPAT, a very high return on the marginal dollar. The FY2024→FY2025 incremental ROIC of 5.8% is low — the $148.2B of additional invested capital in FY2025 generated only $8.5B of additional NOPAT. This step-down is expected and pre-disclosed: the massive FY2025 capex surge ($131.8B gross, vs $83.0B in FY2024) puts large amounts of capacity into service that is not yet billing customers at scale. The 3-year cumulative incremental ROIC of 13.1% is above what appears to be Amazon's approximate cost of capital (9–11% range, inference from comparable infrastructure-heavy tech businesses, not formally computed here as this belongs to the valuation module). This suggests the aggregate investment is returning above its cost over the period reviewed.

**No dividends — reinvestment philosophy:** Amazon has never paid a dividend. Management's stated philosophy is to reinvest all available cash into long-term growth opportunities, principally AWS infrastructure and AI. This is consistent across every filing in the pool. It is a coherent philosophy rather than an absence of shareholder return — CFO per share grew from $8.24 (FY2023) to $12.88 (FY2025) (+56% over two years). The question of whether the company should begin returning capital is left to the valuation module.

## 5. Capital-Allocation Read

Amazon's capital allocation over FY2021–FY2025 is the story of one dominant choice: reinvest operating cash flow into AWS and AI infrastructure at an increasing rate, fund no dividends, execute minimal M&A, and leave buybacks dormant. Capex consumed 90.5% of cumulative CFO over the five-year period and is accelerating — gross capex of $131.8B in FY2025 (94.5% of FY2025 CFO) represents the largest single capital commitment in the company's history, and management has guided to approximately $200B in FY2026. Per-share value creation has been strong on the operating metrics that matter: normalized EPS rose from $1.38 (FY2021) to $4.70 (FY2025), CFO per diluted share from $8.24 (FY2023) to $12.88 (FY2025), and ROIC improved from 6.8% (FY2023) to 10.7% (FY2024) before the capex surge compressed it slightly to 9.7% (FY2025). The best use of capital was organic reinvestment in AWS: AWS operating income grew from $24.6B (FY2023) to $45.6B (FY2025) on $24.8B of segment capex in FY2023 rising to $96.5B in FY2025 — the incremental return on that investment, while compressing short-term FCF, is tracking above cost of capital over the 3-year cumulative period. The worst element is the dormant buyback program: $6.1B of authorization has sat unused for three consecutive years while 348M shares were added through RSU vesting — a 3.4% dilution absorbed entirely by shareholders. Shareholder dilution from SBC is real and unmitigated, and the $6.1B authorization being ignored while CFO hit $139.5B reflects management's prioritization of reinvestment over per-share shrinkage. The single biggest capital-allocation signal is the FY2025 capex step-up to $131.8B (94.5% of CFO) and the implied $200B FY2026 commitment — this is the most consequential capital decision in Amazon's history, made on the credibility of $364B in AWS backlog and management's explicit commitment to a 6–24 month lag between capex deployment and revenue recognition.

**Verdict:** Capital allocation is **value-creative / disciplined** in the organic reinvestment component (AWS capex generating above-cost returns on a cumulative basis), **mixed** on shareholder returns (no dividends, no buybacks, dilutive SBC unmitigated), and **clean** on M&A (bolt-on only, no serial-acquirer pattern, no impairment). Overall verdict: **mixed** — the reinvestment thesis is compelling and credible but the per-share dilution from SBC and the dormant buyback authorization represent a genuine cost to minority shareholders that management acknowledges but does not actively offset.

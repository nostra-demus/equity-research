# Off-Balance-Sheet & Contingencies — EMAR

Reporting currency: AED (UAE Dirham). Reporting standard: IFRS. Fiscal year end: 31 December. All figures in AED millions unless stated.

**Partial-data caveat:** The full Contingencies & Commitments note for Emaar's FY2024 and FY2025 annual reports is only available in Arabic in this data pool. The English-language sources accessible are: (a) the FY2025 investor / preliminary presentation (Feb 2026), (b) the FY2024 balance sheet line items (Arabic, amounts read from the consolidated statement), and (c) the FY2019 English annual report (dated; included for structural comparison only). No English-language contingencies note, litigation schedule, or guarantees disclosure has been extracted. The disclosures below reflect what can be confirmed from available English sources plus Arabic balance sheet amounts. Undisclosed exposures cannot be ruled out; however, this is not a highly-levered or litigious name and the FY2025 net cash position (broad basis: AED 17.3 bn) provides a substantial buffer.

## 5. Contingency Read

The single largest confirmed off-balance-sheet item is the project completion commitment of approximately AED 2,265 mn (FY2024, from Arabic annual report notes), representing Emaar's contractual obligation to complete construction on units already sold but not yet delivered. This is a recurring, structural commitment for a master developer with an AED 154.8 bn backlog — it will replenish as new launches are made and is funded by the AED 42.9 bn held in buyer escrow accounts, so it does not represent a net solvency exposure. No guarantees, financial indemnities, or litigation provisions are quantifiable from available English sources; the contingencies note is Arabic-only, which limits confirmation but also means rating agencies (S&P BBB+, Moody's Baa1, both stable) are the primary English-language signal that no material undisclosed exposure exists. Even if a substantial undisclosed contingency were to crystallize — for example, AED 5–7 bn from contractor disputes, regulatory claims, or tax assessments — the AED 17.3 bn broad-basis net cash position (FY2025) and AED 33.5 bn annual operating cash flow would absorb it without threatening solvency or requiring external financing.

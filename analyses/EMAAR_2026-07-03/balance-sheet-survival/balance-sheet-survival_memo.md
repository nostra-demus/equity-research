## What This Module Found

Emaar's balance sheet is genuinely fortress-grade, and the read is unambiguous. Gross debt of AED 10.6 bn is 0.44x FY2025 EBITDA (very low), and on the canonical broad net-debt basis (gross debt minus unrestricted cash minus liquid short-term investments — the strict basis nets only cash), Emaar holds net cash of AED 17.3 bn at FY2025, rising to AED 24.6 bn by Q1-2026 [Capital IQ Capital Structure Summary, FY2025]. The trend is clean: gross debt fell from AED 19.0 bn (FY2021) to AED 10.6 bn (FY2025) while EBITDA grew 3.1x, entirely organically — no asset sales, no equity raised.

The one near-term event is Sukuk 3 (a US$750 mn / ~AED 2.75 bn Islamic bond) maturing September 2026. Unrestricted cash alone covers it 4.4x at Q1-2026; specialists expect it repaid from cash, not refinanced. In-hand committed liquidity (cash plus liquid short-term investments plus trading securities, AED 28.3 bn) covers all next-twelve-month obligations (AED 16.2 bn) 1.75x without a dirham of new operating cash. Include the AED 7.3 bn undrawn revolver (a committed bank credit line maturing 2030) and total committed liquidity is AED 35.6 bn — 2.2x twelve-month uses. Normalised free cash flow (operating cash minus total capex, net of one-offs) throws off an AED 8.1 bn surplus per year above obligations.

The single largest risk the module names is a prolonged Dubai property-cycle reversal that simultaneously drains the AED ~43 bn of buyer escrow (customer money held in trust that must be refunded on project cancellations), eliminates pre-sale cash inflows, and continues the AED 8.9 bn declared dividend — slowly depleting the liquid buffer over multiple years. It is the only pathway to financial pressure, and it would take several consecutive years of simultaneous earnings collapse and full dividend maintenance to become acute.

An important correction the module surfaces: the company's own headline "AED 61.7 bn net cash" includes AED 42.9 bn of restricted buyer escrow that cannot be used for corporate purposes. An investor relying on the company's own figure would overstate freely usable liquidity by roughly 5x on the strict basis. Specialist 01 strips this; always use the AED 17.3 bn broad-basis figure or the AED 861 mn strict-basis figure, never the company's inflated number.

## What Would Change This Read

**Would strengthen the Fortress verdict:**
- Disclosure of the credit agreement confirming covenant headroom matches or exceeds the labeled 3.0x interest-coverage assumption.
- Formal confirmation that the AED 7.3 bn revolver has no borrowing-base or springing-covenant constraints.
- Evidence Sukuk 3 is formally defeased or escrowed for September 2026 repayment.
- Gross debt reduction below AED 5 bn.

**Would weaken it:**
- A large undisclosed contingency (guarantee, litigation, regulatory claim) materialising above AED 5 bn.
- Simultaneous collapse in Dubai off-plan pre-sales alongside a cycle of large buyer cancellations forcing refunds from restricted escrow.
- A sustained declared dividend above AED 8.9 bn per year that outpaces deteriorating operating cash flow.
- Discovery that the covenant EBITDA definition includes narrow addbacks (management-friendly adjustments to reported EBITDA) that materially shrink effective headroom.

**Highest-value missing document:** the credit agreement (sukuk trust deed and bank facility terms). It would unlock actual covenant thresholds, addback caps, cross-default provisions, change-of-control puts (lender rights to demand repayment on ownership change), and revolver availability mechanics. None of these are expected to change the Fortress verdict — but confirming them is standard diligence.

## Bottom Line

- **Verdict:** Fortress balance sheet. Overall usefulness 75/100 (capped for missing covenant disclosure); the Fortress verdict itself is unambiguous and does not depend on the missing covenant data at these leverage and coverage levels.
- **Could be better than it looks:** Emaar's AED 28.3 bn of in-hand liquid assets already exceeds its AED 10.1 bn entire gross debt stack — this is not leverage management, it is a structurally near-debt-free business that happens to carry some fixed-rate sukuk. Broad-basis net cash of AED 17.3 bn is a strategic asset that funds counter-cyclical action.
- **Could be worse than it looks:** The company's own AED 61.7 bn "net cash" headline includes AED 42.9 bn of restricted buyer escrow; strip that out or usable liquidity is overstated roughly 5x on the strict basis. A historically unprecedented cancellation wave across the AED 154.8 bn off-plan backlog is the one pathway to real stress.
- **Evidence missing:** The credit agreement (covenants, addbacks, cross-default, revolver mechanics); English-language contingencies note from the FY2025 annual report; the DMTT tax charge quantum for FY2025. Overall usefulness is capped at 75 for this reason.
- **Watch next:** Sukuk 3 September 2026 repayment. Cash repayment is expected — watch whether it is announced as defeased/escrowed or refinanced. A surprise refinancing would not weaken solvency but would be the first signal worth reading closely.

## Plain-English Glossary

- **EBITDA** — a rough proxy for annual operating profit before interest, tax, and non-cash charges; used here as the yardstick for how much debt Emaar carries relative to what the business earns.
- **Net debt (broad basis)** — gross debt minus unrestricted cash minus liquid short-term investments; the canonical figure this module uses. When it is negative, the company has more cash-like assets than debt (net cash).
- **Net debt (strict basis)** — gross debt minus unrestricted cash only; a tighter definition that ignores investments.
- **Gross leverage** — gross debt divided by EBITDA; how many years of operating profit it would take to repay all debt.
- **ICR / EBITDA-to-interest coverage** — EBITDA divided by interest expense; how many times over operating profit covers the interest bill. 49x means interest is a rounding error.
- **Sukuk** — an Islamic-finance bond; here, three series of publicly issued debt from Emaar's parent company.
- **Revolver** — a committed bank credit line the company can draw on as needed; Emaar's is AED 7.3 bn, nearly fully undrawn, and does not expire until 2030.
- **Restricted / buyer escrow** — customer money held in trust for specific off-plan projects; legally cannot be used for corporate debt service and must be refunded if projects are cancelled.
- **Off-plan pre-sales** — sales of unbuilt apartments where buyers pay in instalments during construction; the cash engine that funds Emaar's operations.
- **Covenant / covenant headroom** — a lender-imposed financial test in a credit agreement (e.g. "keep interest coverage above 3x"); headroom is the gap between the actual number and the threshold.
- **FCF (free cash flow)** — operating cash flow minus total capex; the recurring cash the operations throw off after keeping the assets running. "Normalised" strips one-offs.
- **BBB+ / Baa1** — investment-grade credit ratings from S&P and Moody's respectively; both agencies have Emaar on "stable" outlook.

**WAC note:** The overall 5.9% WAC is significantly elevated above the USD sukuk coupon (3.72%) because the EGP subsidiary facilities (AED 578 + AED 25 mn) carry rates of 11–20.5% and the PKR facility (AED 431 mn) carries ~10.85% (3M KIBOR − 0.15%). Floating-rate risk is not a material concern at current exposure levels.

**Commentary on the next-12–24 month wall:**

The 2026 maturity is real but is already covered several times over by existing liquid assets alone, without any reliance on new market access or operating cash flow. At Dec-31-2025, Emaar held AED 27,902 mn in unrestricted cash and liquid investments against AED 5,317 mn due within 12 months — a coverage ratio of 5.2x. Even on the strict basis (cash only, AED 9,754 mn), coverage of the 12-month wall is 1.83x. At Q1-2026 (Mar-31-2026), cash and STI had grown to AED 35,034 mn, further widening the buffer. The Sukuk 3 repayment of USD $750 mn (~AED 2,753 mn) in September 2026 is the largest single event; at current run-rate FCF of AED 24–32 bn per annum, the group generates the equivalent of Sukuk 3's face value in operating cash every 5–7 weeks.

The international subsidiary facilities (EGP, PKR, AED working capital lines) described in the investor presentation as "rolled over on annual basis" represent a structural revolving obligation rather than a true maturity event. Their non-renewal would be the main operational risk, but these are local-currency facilities supported by local operating cash flows in Egypt, Pakistan, and India.

The rating posture is supportive. S&P upgraded Emaar to BBB+ (stable) and Moody's to Baa1 (stable) in 2025 (FY2025 Investor Presentation, p.8; confirmed by web-sourced credit commentary). At this rating, the GCC primary debt market — which issued AED ~55 bn in bond and sukuk in Q1-2026 alone (web-sourced) — is fully open to Emaar. No new issuance has been publicly announced or committed; the plan appears to be cash repayment of Sukuk 3, consistent with the scale of the liquid balance sheet.

**Floating-rate share and rate repricing:** The 10.1% floating-rate exposure (EIBOR + KIBOR-linked) reprices with market rates. At a +200 bps shock, annual incremental interest is AED ~21 mn — trivial against AED 24,132 mn EBITDA. This is not a material risk.

**Verdict:** The near-term wall (2026) is **self-funded / low refi risk**. Emaar does not need market access to retire Sukuk 3 in September 2026. Its liquid assets alone (AED 27,902 mn at Dec-31-2025) exceed the entire 2026 maturity by 5.2x. Remaining post-2026 maturities are small (2027–2028: AED 947 mn combined) until the Sukuk 4 bullet of AED 1,837 mn in 2029 — which, at FY2025 normalised FCF of AED 24 bn per annum, is generated in approximately three weeks of operations.

## 5. Refinancing Read

The only true maturity event in the next 24 months is Sukuk 3 (USD $750 mn, AED ~2,753 mn), maturing September 2026. It will almost certainly be repaid from cash on hand rather than refinanced: Emaar's unrestricted cash and liquid investments of AED 27.9 bn at Dec-31-2025 (rising to AED 35.0 bn by Q1-2026) make a new issuance unnecessary, and no replacement sukuk has been publicly announced. If Emaar were to refinance rather than repay, the cost step-up is approximately 109 bps above the existing 3.72% weighted sukuk coupon (to an estimated ~4.81%, based on the 5-yr US Treasury yield of 4.14% as of Jun-29-2026 plus a GCC BBB+ spread of ~67 bps — both web-sourced and indicative) — a moderate increase that is completely immaterial given interest coverage of 45–49x EBIT/EBITDA to interest.

The single biggest refinancing risk is not the 2026 sukuk but rather a scenario where Dubai's property cycle reverses sharply after the sukuk is repaid, liquidity is drawn down to fund project delivery obligations, and Sukuk 4 ($500 mn, 2029) comes due in a closed market at the same time as a working-capital crunch from stalled pre-sales. Even that scenario is remote: the AED 154.8 bn revenue backlog (FY2025 Investor Presentation, p.6) provides 3–4 years of forward cash conversion, the revolver (AED 7.3 bn undrawn, matures 2030) provides an emergency buffer, and the group would need to lose over 90% of its liquid assets before facing any maturity gap.

Under a "market closure" scenario (no new unsecured issuance for 12 months from Jul-2026): Emaar survives comfortably. Cash and liquid investments at Q1-2026 of AED 35.0 bn exceed total gross debt of AED 10.1 bn by 3.5x. The company could repay every single instrument in the stack out of its own balance sheet without touching operating cash flow. Market closure for 12 months is irrelevant to survival.

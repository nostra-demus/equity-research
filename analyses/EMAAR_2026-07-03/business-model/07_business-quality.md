Emaar is not a pure REIT (it is a developer-first business with income-producing assets attached), but the REIT / real estate overlay from `frameworks/SECTOR_OVERLAYS.md` is the closest match. Factor interpretations are adjusted accordingly: "Capital Intensity" = asset-heaviness and LTV of the income-producing portfolio plus development land bank, not pure fixed-asset capex/sales; "Recurring Revenue" = weighted-average lease length (WALT), mall occupancy, and AFFO visibility, not SaaS ARR; "Cyclicality" = property cycle severity in Dubai plus hotel and retail cycle; "Margin Stability" = same-store NOI margin stability for the recurring businesses, and development margin stability for the dominant development segment.

## 2. Aggregate Quality Score

**Aggregate Quality Score: 51 / 100 (Mixed/Average)**

The two factors that dominate this read are Cyclicality (30) and Recurring Revenue (45), and they both pull hard toward the lower half of the scale. Together they account for roughly 40% of the judgement weighting because they define what kind of business Emaar actually is: predominantly a property developer (not a recurring-income compounder), exposed to a single-city property cycle at a moment when that cycle is near peak. Margin Stability (48), Customer Stickiness (50), and Competitive Intensity (55) are secondary factors that compound the same directional read. The strong factors — Commodity Dependence (65), Capital Intensity (60, reflecting near-zero net debt and low LTV), and Disclosure Quality (68) — are real positives but cannot rescue a business that is 73% exposed to a deeply cyclical and geographically concentrated development revenue stream. The aggregate is the weighted average of these reads, with Cyclicality and Recurring Revenue weighted at ~25% each, Pricing Power and Margin Stability at ~15% each, and the remaining factors at ~5–8% each. The anchor check: the second-lowest row score (Recurring Revenue = 45); the aggregate of 51 is within ~6 points above it, well within the ~20-point band permitted.

## 4. Read

Emaar is a cyclical property developer with a high-quality embedded recurring income business — not a durable compounder. The development segment (73% of revenue) is structurally tied to Dubai's property cycle: volume, pricing, and margins all move with the cycle, and the current FY2025 EBITDA of AED 25.6 Bn is almost certainly not a through-cycle number — it reflects a peak in Dubai residential demand, ASPs, and completion timelines simultaneously. The recurring income portfolio (Emaar Malls, Hospitality, Entertainment — 21% of revenue, 32% of EBITDA) is a genuine, high-quality business with 86% EBITDA margins in malls, >98% occupancy, and a growing footfall base; but at 21% of revenues it is not large enough to anchor the group's quality rating. The one factor a buyer must keep their eye on over the next 24 months is **Cyclicality**: specifically, whether new property sales in Dubai (a leading indicator for recognised revenue 3–5 years later) slow materially as the current upcycle matures — the backlog of AED 134.3 Bn in the UAE provides a 5–6 year revenue bridge but does not prevent a sharp drop in forward bookings from hitting the stock. The current headline ROCE and margins are peak-cycle figures; the moat and DCF must anchor to through-cycle levels, which are substantially lower.

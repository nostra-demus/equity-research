# Unit Economics — EMAR

## 1. Natural Unit

The natural economic unit for UAE Real Estate Development (the dominant segment) is a residential or commercial property unit sold and progressively delivered to a buyer.

Secondary unit types exist: square foot of gross leasable area (GLA) for the Emaar Malls segment, and RevPAR (revenue per available room per night) for the Hospitality segment. The table below covers the dominant development unit only.

## 3. Value Creation Read

Each new UAE development unit creates value at current reported margins, but those margins reflect a peak in the Dubai property cycle and should not be treated as permanent.

The math: a unit sold in FY2025 was booked at ~AED 3.73 Mn and carries a 47% gross margin on the portfolio under development (slide 19). That implies ~AED 1.75 Mn gross profit per unit sold. The 50,800 units under construction are 89% sold by value, which means Emaar collects payment from buyers as construction progresses — the bulk of capital deployed (land) is recovered well before delivery. The customer default rate of 0.5% is low, and the revenue backlog of AED 134.3 Bn (UAE only) locks in approximately 3.7 years of FY2025-equivalent UAE development revenue, providing strong near-term visibility. At a 46–50% gross margin and ~46% EBITDA margin, there is a wide spread between revenue and cost per unit, indicating genuine value creation at the unit level.

The risk in the read: the EBITDA/homebuilding-sales ratio has swung from 22% in FY2020 (Covid trough) to 51% in FY2025 — a peak-cycle level. The through-cycle average over FY2020–FY2025 was roughly 38% (Capital IQ Industry-Specific data, EBITDA/Homebuilding Sales). At a 38% EBITDA margin rather than the current 46–51%, per-unit EBITDA would fall from AED 1.38 Mn to approximately AED 1.14 Mn — still positive but 17% lower. Value creation persists through the cycle; the margin of safety is thinner if pricing corrects.

The single most valuable disclosure that is missing is a per-unit land cost and per-unit SG&A allocation, which would allow a direct calculation of unit-level return on invested capital rather than requiring use of group ROCE as a proxy.

**Verdict: Creates value — at current cycle margins. Creates value (more modestly) at normalised through-cycle margins. Does not appear to destroy value at any point in the cycle observed since FY2020.**

---

## 4. Sensitivity

The per-unit value creation read is most sensitive to the average selling price (ASP) per unit. A 20% decline in ASP — holding cost-to-build constant at AED 1.52 Mn — would cut UAE Dev revenue per delivered unit from AED 3.01 Mn to AED 2.41 Mn and collapse gross profit per unit from AED 1.49 Mn to AED 0.89 Mn (a 40% decline), pushing gross margin from 50% to 37%. At those margins the unit still creates value, but the spread is narrow and EBITDA margin would converge toward the FY2020 trough level of ~22%. Historically, the most volatile input has been unit volume (units sold and units delivered move sharply with the Dubai cycle: units sold fell from 7,987 in FY2018 to 2,305 in FY2020, a 71% drop), while ASP has been more stable. A volume collapse compresses EBITDA because a portion of SG&A is fixed. A 20% drop in units delivered — all else equal — would reduce UAE Dev EBITDA by roughly AED 2.8 Bn (20% of AED 16,710 Mn) but leave per-unit economics intact, whereas a concurrent ASP decline of 20% would destroy both aggregate and per-unit economics simultaneously. The combination (volume down and price down) is the scenario that has historically marked the worst outcomes for Dubai property developers.

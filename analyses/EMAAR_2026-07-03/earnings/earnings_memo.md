## 2. What This Module Found

Revenue grew +39.6% in FY2025 to AED 49.6 Bn, and EPS compounded at 39.7% a year over four years. That growth is mechanical: it is the conversion of a large 2021–2024 wave of off-plan property sales into recognised revenue under IFRS 15 percentage-of-completion accounting (revenue is booked as construction milestones are certified, not when the flat is sold). The AED 134.3 Bn UAE revenue backlog — up 31% in one year — locks in the next three to four years of recognised revenue.

The single biggest earnings driver is the pace at which construction milestones get certified against that backlog. A ±15% shift in delivery pace moves EBITDA (a rough measure of operating profit) by about AED 2,500 Mn — roughly five to six times any other variable. Q4 2026 alone carries 32–33% of annual revenue, so a few weeks of milestone slippage can flip a full-year beat into a miss.

The single biggest earnings risk is that the leading indicators are already softening while the trailing numbers look strong. New units sold fell 27% in FY2025 (13,905 vs 19,058). Value growth is purely price-driven, which historically precedes a volume plateau. Emaar Misr's net income in Egyptian pounds fell 64% on flat local-currency revenue — that is operational damage, not just a currency translation effect, and it puts the margins on AED 20.6 Bn of international backlog in question.

The consensus bar was cut from AED 2.32 to AED 1.95 EPS in three months, largely on one analyst's 22% cut on Jun-11-2026. Emaar has beaten by 11–23% for three straight years, so the bar looks beatable — but revision breadth is 0 up / 3 down, and FY2027 estimates were also cut 14%, which argues the cut may be a multi-year cycle call, not a one-off overshoot.

## 6. Plain-English Glossary

- **Backlog** — sales already made but not yet recognised as revenue; the pipeline of already-locked-in future revenue.
- **IFRS 15 percentage-of-completion** — accounting rule that spreads revenue from a property sale across the years it takes to build the unit, not the day it is sold. Revenue prints as construction milestones are certified.
- **EBITDA** — a rough measure of operating profit before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation.
- **EBITDA margin** — EBITDA as a percentage of revenue; how much of each AED of revenue drops through to operating profit.
- **EPS** — earnings per share; profit divided by the share count.
- **FCF (free cash flow)** — cash from operations minus capital spending; the recurring cash the business throws off after keeping itself running.
- **CFO/EBITDA** — cash from operations divided by EBITDA; how much of the accounting profit turns into actual cash. The synthesis flagged that Emaar's 135–139% headline figure normalises to 97–104% once obligated buyer deposits are removed.
- **Net cash / net debt (strict basis)** — cash minus total debt. The synthesis flagged that Emaar's headline "Net Cash AED 61.7 Bn" includes about AED 43 Bn of escrow cash ring-fenced for buyer projects — not freely available.
- **DMTT (Domestic Minimum Top-up Tax)** — UAE's minimum corporate tax; permanently pushed Emaar's effective tax rate from ~2% to ~13%.
- **Sukuk** — an Islamic bond; Emaar has a USD 500 Mn (AED ~2.75 Bn) sukuk maturing Sept-15-2026.
- **POC delivery pace** — the rate at which construction milestones are certified, which drives when revenue prints under IFRS 15.
- **Revision breadth** — the ratio of analysts raising estimates to those cutting them; 0 up / 3 down is decisively negative.

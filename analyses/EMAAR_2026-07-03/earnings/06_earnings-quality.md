**Key observations on the bridge:**

1. **FY2022 CFO/EBITDA of 203%** is anomalous. A large positive swing in other working capital items (AED 3,339 Mn ex-unearned) combined with record unearned revenue inflow (AED 4,138 Mn) pushed CFO far above EBITDA. This is not a recurring pattern.

2. **FY2021 and FY2024 "Other WC change"** swings are wide. FY2021 saw a AED −4,385 Mn drain; FY2024 saw a AED +11,399 Mn inflow. These reflect lumpiness in contract asset recognition (IFRS 15 percentage-of-completion timing) and payables. The underlying pattern is structural, not a quality red flag — it reflects the property developer model.

3. **Normalised FCF was roughly flat FY2022–FY2024** (AED 13.8 Bn → AED 15.5 Bn) despite strong EBITDA growth, because buyer advance payments are partly netted out. FY2025 showed re-acceleration to AED 24.3 Bn as EBITDA growth at scale outpaced the advance-payment component.

4. **Tax paid entered the bridge in FY2024 (AED 224 Mn) and rose sharply in FY2025 (AED 844 Mn)**. The effective tax rate moved from 1.5% (FY2023) → 7.7% (FY2024) → 13.0% (FY2025). This is a permanent structural change that will grow the CFO-to-EBITDA gap going forward.

## 2. Cash Conversion Assessment

CFO/EBITDA has held at 135%–139% in four of the five years shown (203% in FY2022 is the outlier). At first glance, CFO significantly above EBITDA appears extraordinary, but the mechanism is clear and disclosed: advance cash payments from off-plan property buyers inflate CFO before revenue is earned. This is not an earnings-quality concern; it is the natural consequence of Emaar's off-plan pre-sale model under IFRS 15.

On a normalised basis (removing unearned revenue inflows), CFO/EBITDA drops to approximately 97%–104% — a range consistent with a well-run developer with modest capex needs and low working capital intensity outside of the advance-payment cycle. This is healthy. There is no pattern of reported EBITDA failing to convert to cash; if anything, cash lands before earnings are recognised.

The trajectory of normalised FCF (AED 7.2 Bn → AED 24.3 Bn over five years) is broadly tracking EBITDA growth with a lag, consistent with the delivery cycle on the revenue backlog. No concern here.

## 3. Working Capital Trends

**Important context for DSO:** Emaar's accounts receivable under IFRS 15 consist primarily of contract assets (unbilled revenue recognised as construction milestones are completed, but not yet invoiced to buyers). These are structural and are settled over a 5–6 year delivery cycle — not traditional trade receivables collected in 30–90 days. DSO in absolute terms is very high (400–700 days) but this is the normal arithmetic for a property developer with a multi-year backlog, and the direction is the right one: DSO is falling as revenue accelerates faster than the AR balance.

| Metric | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | Direction | Risk |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|---|
| Receivable days (DSO — short-term AR only) | 675 | 576 | 427 | Improving | Low (structural model; AR growing slower than revenue) |
| Inventory days (DIO) | 2 | 1 | 1 | Stable/declining | Low (negligible inventory for a developer) |
| Payable days (DPO) | 71 | 35 | 29 | Declining | Low-medium (payables being settled faster; no supplier-finance concern apparent) |
| Cash conversion cycle (DSO + DIO − DPO) | 607 | 542 | 399 | Improving | Low (improving trend driven by revenue outgrowing AR) |

*[Source: CIQ Annual Balance Sheet and Income Statement, FY2023–FY2025. DSO = short-term accounts receivable / (revenue ÷ 365). DIO = inventory / (COGS ÷ 365). DPO = accounts payable / (COGS ÷ 365).]*

**Flag assessments:**
- **DSO rising >10% YoY:** Not triggered. DSO is declining sharply (FY2023: 675 → FY2025: 427 days, −37%). Revenue is growing faster than the AR balance. AR grew +3.4% in FY2025 while revenue grew +39.6%. This is the opposite of a revenue-recognition concern.
- **DIO rising >15% YoY:** Not triggered. Inventory is negligible and declining.
- **DPO rising sharply:** Not triggered. DPO is declining (payables are being paid faster), which is a mild liquidity signal but not distress.

## 9. Earnings Quality Score

**Score: 72 / 100**

*Band: 61–80 — Mostly clean, but some working capital or adjustment noise.*

**Primary reason for this score:** Emaar's GAAP earnings are substantially backed by real cash flows that arrive before income is recognised (the off-plan advance-payment model). The single biggest mechanical feature — CFO consistently far above EBITDA — is explained by structural buyer deposits, not by accounting manipulation. Receivables are improving relative to revenue. The EBITDA adjustment between CIQ and company figures is modest, mechanically sound, and disclosed. Deductions from a higher score: (1) the FY2021–FY2023 recurring "one-off" writedowns totalling AED 3.6 Bn, (2) growing non-operating income (interest on escrow + affiliate income) now contributing ~14% of reported net income and giving a structural flattery to EPS growth, (3) deferred contract costs growing faster than revenue, and (4) the new tax regime permanently reduces after-tax free cash flow and EPS growth relative to EBIT growth going forward — analysts using pre-FY2024 normalised multiples will overstate the quality-adjusted earnings.

## 10. The Single Biggest Quality Concern

The single most important risk that reported earnings overstate economic reality is the **inflation of reported CFO and headline FCF by off-plan buyer advance payments**. In FY2025, reported FCF was AED 32,524 Mn but normalised operating FCF — the cash the business generates before receiving buyer deposits for future-delivery projects — was AED 24,295 Mn, a gap of AED 8,229 Mn. Over FY2021–FY2025, cumulative advance payments from buyers that inflated reported FCF totalled AED 26,206 Mn. This cash is real but is not free: it is legally ring-fenced in project escrow accounts (AED 42,879 Mn restricted cash at FY2025 year-end) and must be used for construction. An analyst who values Emaar on a headline FCF yield basis without removing unearned-revenue inflows will significantly overstate the true recurring cash generation of the business. Similarly, the AED 2,505 Mn of interest income in FY2025 — earned on the large escrow and deposit balances — is non-recurring in the sense that it will shrink as projects are delivered and escrow balances are drawn down. These two items together (advance payments + escrow interest) mean that reported earnings at peak delivery and peak escrow will look artificially elevated versus the economic steady-state once the current backlog is delivered.

That said, this is a disclosure and model-understanding risk, not a fraud or manipulation risk. Emaar discloses the unearned revenue balance, the restricted cash balance, and the adjustment between reported and normalised EBITDA clearly in its investor presentations. The earnings are real and the cash conversion is genuine — the quality concern is one of investor interpretation, not accounting dishonesty.

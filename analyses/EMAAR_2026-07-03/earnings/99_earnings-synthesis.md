# Earnings Module — EMAR (Synthesis)

## Abstract

Emaar's revenues have grown at an exceptional pace — FY2025 revenue of AED 49.6 Bn was +40% year-on-year and +39.7% EPS CAGR over four years — driven almost entirely by the conversion of a massive 2021–2024 vintage off-plan sales wave into IFRS 15 percentage-of-completion revenue through the AED 134.3 Bn UAE backlog. The consensus bar has been cut sharply: FY2026 EPS fell from AED 2.32 to AED 1.95 in three months, largely on one analyst's single 22% cut in June 2026, leaving a bar that looks beatable given three consecutive years of double-digit EPS beats. The biggest earnings risk, however, is not the next quarter — it is that unit volumes sold fell 27% in FY2025, Emaar Misr's EGP-denominated net income collapsed 64% despite flat local-currency revenue (operational deterioration, not FX alone), and the escrow-inflated FCF figure overstates recurring cash generation by approximately AED 8.2 Bn annually. The verdict is mixed: near-term execution on an already-locked backlog is credible, but the leading demand indicators are deteriorating and the earnings quality has structural caveats that the headline numbers obscure. No earnings transcripts were available; earnings clarity is capped at 70.

## 1A. Module Disconfirmation

**Strongest bear point:** The Emaar Misr EGP-denominated net income collapse of 64% in FY2025 (EGP 5,544.9 Mn vs EGP 15,404.8 Mn) with flat EGP revenue is the most dangerous finding in the data pool — it signals operational margin deterioration inside the international subsidiary that is not attributable to FX, putting AED 20.6 Bn of international revenue backlog margin assumptions in doubt and flagging a quality problem in a segment that contributed AED 30.7 Bn of international assets to the consolidated balance sheet. [08_earnings-red-flags.md §2.9; business-model/12_red-flags-sweep.md §2]

**Strongest bull point:** The AED 134.3 Bn UAE revenue backlog at December 31, 2025 — up 31% in one year — provides a 3–4 year floor of locked-in revenue that is largely immune to near-term demand softness; even if new UAE property sales fell 30% in FY2026, recognised revenue would not compress until FY2028–2029, and the Q1 2026 EPS of AED 0.57 already beat pre-quarter estimates by ~9%. [02_revenue-drivers.md §8; 05_beat-miss-setup.md §8]

**Single killer risk specific to earnings quality and beat/miss setup:** The entire beat thesis rests on a single-variable bet — construction milestone certification in Q4 2026, which alone carries 32–33% of annual revenue; a few weeks' delay in Q4 milestone certification shifts 2–3% of annual EBITDA into FY2027 with no change in underlying business value, turning a full-year beat into a miss. [07_earnings-sensitivity.md §6; 08_earnings-red-flags.md #9]

**Disconfirming evidence already visible:** Unit volumes sold fell 27% in FY2025 (13,905 vs 19,058 in FY2024); EPS revision breadth was 0 up / 3 down over three months, with FY2027 estimates also cut 14% — a multi-year, not one-quarter, concern; Emaar Misr's operational collapse is already in filed subsidiary financial data; reported FCF of AED 32,524 Mn overstates recurring cash generation by AED 8,229 Mn annually; and interest income on ring-fenced escrow (AED 2,505 Mn, ~14% of reported net income) will structurally decline as the backlog delivers. [04_guidance-consensus.md §5; 06_earnings-quality.md §1, §10; 08_earnings-red-flags.md §1]

## 3. Reconciliation

**Disagreement 1: Emaar Misr margin collapse — FX vs operational deterioration.**
`03_margin-drivers.md §6` attributed Emaar Misr's AED-reported EBITDA collapse (from AED 1,559 Mn at 96% margin in FY2024 to AED 479 Mn at 31% margin in FY2025) primarily to EGP/AED depreciation of 10.7%. `business-model/12_red-flags-sweep.md §2`, using EGP-denominated subsidiary-level data (CASE:EMFD, Capital IQ Key Developments, Feb-23-2026), found that EGP-denominated net income fell 64% (EGP 5,544.9 Mn vs EGP 15,404.8 Mn) despite flat EGP revenue — a result that FX cannot explain.
**Reconciled view:** The business-model agent used local-currency subsidiary data that was not available to the margin-drivers agent, which relied solely on AED-translated investor deck figures. The local-currency result (64% EGP income decline with flat EGP revenue) is the more credible signal because it strips out the translation effect. This synthesis adopts the operational deterioration reading: Emaar Misr has a real earnings-quality problem beyond FX, and the AED 20.6 Bn international revenue backlog's achievable margins carry more uncertainty than the investor presentation implies.

**Disagreement 2: Construction cost inflation magnitude — biggest risk vs. modest near-term impact.**
`03_margin-drivers.md §8` called construction cost inflation "the single biggest margin driver going forward." `07_earnings-sensitivity.md §6` ranked it sixth of seven variables by near-term EBITDA impact (only ~AED 175 Mn per year per 10% cost move), assigned Low confidence.
**Reconciled view:** Both are correct for their respective time horizons. The sensitivity agent correctly quantified the annual income-statement flow-through as modest — costs are spread over a 5–6 year delivery cycle. The margin-drivers agent correctly identified it as the biggest structural forward risk in aggregate over the delivery horizon (total exposure AED 700–1,000 Mn). This synthesis uses the near-term (AED 175 Mn/year) figure for FY2026 earnings impact and flags the cumulative risk as a structural concern for FY2027–2030.

**Disagreement 3: CFO/EBITDA "healthy" vs "inflated."**
`06_earnings-quality.md §2` described CFO/EBITDA of 135%–139% as healthy. `06_earnings-quality.md §10` immediately contradicted this by noting that normalised CFO/EBITDA is 97%–104% once advance buyer payments are removed.
**Reconciled view:** The §10 framing is the one that matters for investors. The 135%–139% figure is mechanically accurate but misleading — it includes AED 8,229 Mn of obligated buyer deposits. This synthesis leads with normalised CFO/EBITDA of ~97%–104%, consistent with an average-quality developer, not a high-quality cash compounder.

**Disagreement 4: Single-analyst revision — "idiosyncratic overcut" vs. "prescient cycle call."**
`04_guidance-consensus.md §7` concluded the bar is "low" because the sharply cut consensus is beatable given the beat history. `08_earnings-red-flags.md #5` flagged that the single analyst who cut on Jun-11-2026 may be reading a structural demand deceleration visible in unit volume data (units down 27%) that other analysts have not yet fully embedded.
**Reconciled view:** This synthesis cannot resolve the question from available data alone. The conservative reading — consistent with CLAUDE.md §4's preference for the more cautious interpretation when evidence quality is equal — is that the Jun-11-2026 revision could be prescient. The consensus setup score is therefore set at 62 (in the lower end of the "strong" band) rather than a higher number, to reflect this uncertainty. If FY2026 Q2–Q3 new-sales data confirms structural demand deceleration, the consensus will be cut further.

**Disagreement 5: Red-flags verdict vs beat-miss setup.**
`08_earnings-red-flags.md §5` issued a "Critical concerns" severity verdict. `05_beat-miss-setup.md §8` concluded "Setup favors beat." Both verdicts are simultaneously true at different time horizons and for different questions. The beat-setup verdict is for the next quarter and near-term period (Q2–Q4 2026), where the lowered bar and backlog visibility tilt toward outperformance. The red-flags verdict addresses the structural earnings quality and the risk that "accelerating earnings" is a trailing indicator. This synthesis preserves both findings and surfaces the conflict explicitly: the next-quarter setup is conditionally positive; the 12–36 month structural picture carries material downside risk that Critical concern flags correctly capture.

## 5. Earnings Setup Summary

### Revenue Setup

Emaar's reported revenue acceleration — +40% in FY2025 and +23% year-on-year in Q1 2026 — is real but mechanically driven by the conversion of 2021–2024 vintage off-plan sales into IFRS 15 percentage-of-completion revenue, not by new organic demand growth. The AED 134.3 Bn UAE backlog provides genuine near-term revenue insulation: even a 30% drop in new sales in FY2026 would not compress recognised revenue until FY2028–2029 at the earliest. The trajectory is therefore sustainable for at least two to three more years at the level of revenue recognised, but the underlying demand flow that replenishes the backlog is already weakening — unit volumes sold fell 27% in FY2025, and value growth is purely ASP-driven, a pattern that historically precedes volume plateaus. The single factor that would flip revenue direction in the near term is a sustained construction slowdown in Q4 2026 that delays IFRS 15 milestone certification, since Q4 alone carries 32–33% of annual revenue. Reported revenue and underlying forward-looking backlog accretion tell different stories: revenue is printing strongly because old sales are delivering, while the pipeline of new sales feeding future backlog is growing more slowly — an AED ~9 Bn gap in annual accretion rate versus the FY2023–FY2024 pace.

### Margin Setup

The current EBITDA margin corridor of 47%–51% has held for eight consecutive quarters, which is genuine stability — but it reflects a business that has settled post the FY2023 POC anomaly (53.8% EBITDA margin), not one with structural margin expansion ahead. The margin is unlikely to be at a cyclical peak in the absolute sense, but it is at or near the peak that the current product mix (62.7% apartments, 36.5% villas in UAE Development deliveries) and tax regime (13.0% effective rate with DMTT now fully embedded) will allow. The segment or driver that, if it moved adversely by 10–20%, would take the largest consolidated bite is UAE Development gross margin: a 200 bps further compression from apartment-heavy deliveries would reduce consolidated EBITDA by approximately AED 500–700 Mn annually. Emaar's structural margin protection in the malls and hospitality arms (86% and 48% EBITDA margins respectively, backed by contractual lease escalation clauses) acts as a partial floor, but these segments together are only 20% of group revenue and cannot offset a development-segment margin move. Emaar is predominantly a price-taker on construction costs (no pass-through after units are pre-sold), meaning any contractor cost inflation in the Dubai building boom hits the locked-in backlog margins directly with a 2–4 year lag.

### Quality Check

The largest gap between reported and economic earnings is the AED 8,229 Mn of off-plan buyer advance payments that inflate reported FCF (AED 32,524 Mn) above normalised recurring FCF (AED 24,295 Mn) in FY2025 — a 25% overstatement that is structural, not cyclical, and actually widening as the backlog grows. The second largest gap is interest income on ring-fenced project escrow (AED 2,505 Mn, ~14% of reported net income in FY2025), which will mechanically shrink as the escrow is drawn down over the 5–6 year delivery cycle. These two items together mean peak-year EPS embeds declining income streams that are presented alongside genuinely earned operating income without separation. Management's GAAP-to-non-GAAP adjustment (the IFRS 9/15 discount unwinding add-back, worth AED 1,429 Mn) is mechanically sound and recurs every period — it is not genuinely one-time. Modelling normalised earnings for FY2027 would require starting from GAAP earnings, removing the escrow interest contribution as a non-recurring decline, and using the CIQ-reported net income (AED 17,599 Mn attributable to EMAR equity holders) rather than the company's group figure (AED 22,326 Mn, which includes minority interests).

### Consensus Bar

For consensus to be beaten by a material margin in FY2026, the construction delivery pace across 50,800+ UAE units under development would need to hold at or above the FY2025 pace (6,129 units delivered, +44% year-on-year) and Q4 2026 milestone certifications would need to arrive on schedule. Given that the consensus bar was set at AED 53.1 Bn revenue (only ~7% growth over FY2025 actual AED 49.6 Bn) and AED 1.95 EPS, the bar looks undemanding against a backlog that is releasing revenue at a run-rate that already exceeded AED 51.9 Bn on a TTM basis as of Q1 2026. The bar is most likely set too low on revenue (the TTM run-rate already implies ~AED 50–54 Bn before seasonal weighting) and roughly right-to-slightly-low on EPS (which depends on tax timing and escrow interest). The biggest uncertainty is whether the Jun-11-2026 analyst cut was the start of a multi-step revision cycle anchored to structural demand deceleration, in which case the current bar of AED 1.95 could itself be cut further by Q3 2026 results. The consensus embeds no explicit buffer for the Emaar Misr operational deterioration, which is already in filed subsidiary data but not yet fully reflected in group estimates.

## 5b. Leverage & Capital Structure

**Trigger B fires** (material leverage change is not applicable based on the data — the company's strict net debt / EBITDA is near zero at 0.04x FY2025 and -0.72x on the broad basis). However, a **near-term refinancing event of material size is present** that warrants disclosure regardless of the trigger thresholds:

A USD 500 Mn sukuk (Islamic bond) — equivalent to approximately AED 2.75 Bn — matures on September 15, 2026, roughly ten weeks from the date of this analysis. [Capital IQ Capital Structure Details FY2025; 08_earnings-red-flags.md §1, bearish evidence row 4] This maturity falls during the same period as Q2 2026 results (expected around August 12, 2026) and within the delivery window for Q3 2026.

At the most recent period-end (Q1 2026, March 31, 2026): Total Debt = AED 10,064 Mn; Cash & Equivalents (strict, unrestricted per Capital IQ) = AED 12,180 Mn; Short-Term Investments = AED 22,503 Mn. Strict net cash (unrestricted cash minus total debt) = AED 2,115 Mn. Broad net cash (including STI) = AED 24,619 Mn. [Capital IQ Quarterly Financials, Q1-2026 Balance Sheet tab]

The AED 2.75 Bn sukuk is well within the broad net cash position of AED 24.6 Bn, and even within the strict net cash of AED 2.1 Bn when the sukuk is considered against the unrestricted cash balance — the company is not in any distress. However, the company's reported "Net Cash AED 61.7 Bn" (FY2025) includes approximately AED 43 Bn of project escrow cash that is ring-fenced for buyer projects and is not freely available for general corporate use. An investor reading AED 61.7 Bn as freely deployable capital and concluding this sukuk maturity is trivially manageable would be making an error — the actual unrestricted liquidity cushion above the sukuk is AED 2.1 Bn strict or AED 24.6 Bn broad, not AED 61.7 Bn. The company's adjusted EBITDA basis (AED 25,561 Mn) is used in its own credit analysis; on the strict CIQ EBITDA basis (AED 24,132 Mn), the ratio is 0.04x net debt at FY2025. Debt maturity profile beyond the September 2026 sukuk is not fully disclosed in the preliminary results filing; the full FY2025 audited Annual Report (not yet available in English) would contain the maturity schedule. No management commentary or rating-agency action has been noted in the available data pool indicating capital-allocation constraints or the sukuk refinancing is at risk.

## 8. Note To The Final Synthesizer

- **Dominant earnings trend:** Earnings are executing on a locked-in 2021–2024 vintage backlog — revenue is printing as construction milestones are certified, not because new demand is accelerating. This is an important distinction: the trailing indicator looks exceptional, the leading indicator (unit volumes down 27%, value growth purely ASP-driven) is already softening. Do not conflate the two.

- **Earnings clean and cash-backed:** Partially — GAAP earnings are real and substantiated, but the headline CFO figure (AED 32,524 Mn reported FCF in FY2025) overstates recurring cash generation by approximately AED 8.2 Bn of advance buyer payments that are obligated for project delivery. Interest income on ring-fenced escrow (AED 2,505 Mn, ~14% of net income) will structurally decline as the AED 43 Bn escrow is drawn down. Use normalised FCF (AED 24,295 Mn) and CIQ-attributable net income (AED 17,599 Mn), not company group figures, for valuation.

- **Consensus bar:** The bar looks beatable in the near term: EPS cut to AED 1.95, only 7% revenue growth required, and Emaar has a three-year streak of 11%–23% EPS beats. But the bar's quality depends on whether one analyst's large June 2026 cut is an idiosyncratic overreaction or a prescient structural call — this cannot be resolved from available data. The negative revision breadth (0 up / 3 down on EPS in three months) argues the cut may not be over.

- **Next-quarter setup:** Conditionally favors a Q2 2026 beat — the lowered bar and backlog visibility support this. The bigger risk is Q3 and Q4, which together carry ~58% of annual revenue. The full-year FY2026 beat thesis depends on Q4 milestone timing, which is the single most consequential and most uncertain variable.

- **Top sensitivity variable:** UAE Development backlog POC delivery pace — AED 2,500 Mn EBITDA sensitivity for a ±15% delivery shift. Current direction is positive (units delivered up 44% in FY2025). The delivery and cost inflation risks compound from the same root cause (contractor capacity) and should not be treated as independent bear scenarios.

- **Partial-data cap applied:** Earnings clarity capped at max 70 (no earnings transcripts for FY2025 or Q1 2026 calls). This means management's own characterisation of delivery pace, Egypt, the AED 200 Bn masterplan commitment, and the May 2026 CFO change is unavailable. All management commentary sourced from Level 3–7 documents only.

- **Biggest missing data point:** The English-language FY2025 audited Annual Report is the single highest-value next data request — it would provide IFRS 7 sensitivity tables, the English RPT note, Emaar Misr segment disclosures in a language accessible for full verification, and the full debt maturity schedule including all sukuk and bank facilities beyond the September 2026 maturity.

- **What would change the verdict:** A Q2 or Q3 2026 UAE new-property-sales disclosure showing unit volume stabilisation, combined with an Emaar Misr quarterly EBITDA recovering in EGP terms, would upgrade the verdict from mixed to "Earnings stable" or "Earnings inflecting — positive (backlog-driven)." A second large EPS cut following Q2 2026 results, or confirmed Q4 milestone delays, would downgrade to "Earnings decelerating."

- **Red-flag agent verdict (mandatory propagation):** The `08_earnings-red-flags` agent issued an overall severity verdict of **Critical concerns** — 1 Critical flag, 16 High flags, 6 Medium flags. The single Critical flag is the Emaar Misr EGP-denominated income collapse of 64% with flat EGP revenue, which is already in filed subsidiary data and indicates operational margin deterioration beyond FX. Every High-severity flag from the red-flag agent has been incorporated in Section 1A, Section 3, Section 5b, and the current and downgrade rows of Section 7 above. None have been silently omitted.

- **Conflict between red-flags verdict and beat-miss setup:** The beat-miss agent concluded "Setup favors beat" while the red-flags agent issued "Critical concerns." Both verdicts are correct for their own question: the next-quarter setup is conditionally positive given the lowered bar and backlog mechanics; the structural earnings quality and cycle-position assessment carries Critical concerns. The master synthesizer should treat these as operating on different time horizons rather than treating one as wrong.

## 9. Simple Summary

- Revenue is printing at +40% year-on-year — but this is backlog conversion of 2021–2024 off-plan sales, not new organic demand. The leading indicator (unit volumes sold) is already declining 27%.
- Margins are stable at 47%–51% EBITDA, with no near-term catalyst for expansion; development gross margin declining two years in a row; DMTT permanently shifted the effective tax rate from ~2% to ~13%, which is fully in run-rate.
- Earnings are partially clean — GAAP income is real, but reported FCF overstates recurring cash by ~AED 8 Bn annually and ~14% of net income comes from non-operational escrow interest that will decline as projects complete.
- The consensus bar looks beatable in the near term (EPS cut from AED 2.32 to AED 1.95 in three months, with a strong three-year beat history), but the Jun-11-2026 revision that drove it could be a structural cycle call, not a temporary overcut.
- Next-quarter setup conditionally favors a Q2 2026 beat. The full-year FY2026 thesis hinges on Q3 and Q4, which together carry 58% of annual revenue and depend on milestone timing.
- Biggest sensitivity variable: POC delivery pace — AED 2,500 Mn EBITDA swing for ±15% delivery change. Delivery and cost inflation risks compound from the same contractor-tightness root cause.
- Earnings volatility is moderate (38/100, where higher = worse) — the backlog provides structural insulation, but Q4 concentration creates asymmetric downside if milestones slip.
- This module is useful for the master synthesizer but should be paired with the business-model module's cycle-position and external-dependency assessments to correctly frame the distinction between the strong trailing earnings signal and the softening leading indicators.

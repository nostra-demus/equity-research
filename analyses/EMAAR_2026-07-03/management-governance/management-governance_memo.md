## 2. What This Module Found

Amit Jain has been CEO for nine years and the numbers he has delivered on the UAE business are unusually good. Earnings per share (the profit attributed to each share) grew 39.7% per year for four years on a share count that did not move, the dividend grew 6.7×, and the company moved from AED 16 bn net debt (borrowings minus cash) to AED 17 bn broad net cash (cash-and-investments comfortably above debt) while cutting total debt by half. The incremental return on invested capital (the profit earned on each new dirham put into the business) was 35.4% — high, and driven by organic UAE development rather than acquisitions. M&A was two coherent bolt-ons (Dubai Creek Harbour, Emaar Malls privatisation) plus one clean disposal (Namshi at a AED 700 mn gain); there is no serial-acquirer pattern.

The single most important driver of the "misaligned or weak" verdict is not any of that — it is the ownership architecture. The Dubai government bloc (Dubai Holding group) holds 29.73% and the wider government-linked block is 37.2% combined. Its objective is city-building alongside financial return, which structurally diverges from per-share value for minority holders. The AED 200 bn masterplan announced 11 June 2026 — at a probable cycle peak, with no phased targets, no pre-sale gates, and no capital-commitment schedule — is the clearest current expression of that conflict. Founder Mohamed Alabbar (Managing Director) simultaneously chairs Eagle Hills Properties LLC, a directly competing developer, and there is zero English-language related-party disclosure addressing it. Executive compensation is not publicly disclosed in English, so it cannot be confirmed whether the long-term plan rewards returns or size. Individual insiders hold 0.03% of shares — essentially no economic skin in the game.

The single most important risk is RF-OWN-004: structurally unaligned controlling owner. It is a High-severity flag (not Critical) because no non-arm's-length related-party transaction has been proven yet. If the English-language related-party note reveals problem transactions, it would escalate.

## 6. Plain-English Glossary

- **Earnings per share (EPS):** the profit attributed to each share outstanding.
- **Net debt (broad basis):** the company's cash and short-term investments minus its total borrowings. A negative figure means it has more cash-and-investments than debt.
- **Incremental ROIC:** the extra profit earned on each new dirham of capital put into the business — a measure of whether reinvestment creates value.
- **Long-term incentive plan (LTIP) / phantom share:** a management pay plan that pays out over multiple years and tracks the share price without giving actual shares.
- **Related-party transaction (RPT):** a deal between the company and someone connected to it (a controlling owner, a director's other company). Minority holders need these disclosed to check they were done at fair prices.
- **Confidence-adjusted score:** the composite governance score scaled by how good the underlying data is — 54 × 60/100 = 32.

**Key observation:** The LTIP is structured as phantom shares (not actual equity grants). Vesting is tied to unnamed "3-year forward financial matrices" — this language is opaque. The payout is linked to the company's share price, which introduces a partial per-share value signal (share price appreciation benefits the recipient). The language "financial matrices" is consistent with either approach. This is the single largest gap in this analysis.

Emaar does not use actual equity grants (RSUs, performance shares with real dilution). Phantom shares settled in cash sidestep dilution concerns but also mean management does not hold locked-up equity alongside minority shareholders in the same way actual share awards would require.

## 4. Incentive Alignment Read

The CEO of Emaar is paid a total of at least AED 14.4 million in salary and related compensation (FY2023) with an LTIP built on phantom shares that vest over three years against undisclosed "3-year forward financial matrices" and are settled at the prevailing share price. The phantom-share mechanism introduces a share-price link — which is a partial per-share value signal — but the exact performance conditions driving vesting are opaque. If the underlying metrics are revenue or absolute net profit targets, the plan rewards size; if they are EPS, ROIC, or TSR, it rewards per-share value. The data pool does not allow this distinction to be drawn. What can be said: (1) board director fees are modest and not performance-linked; (2) management holds negligible personal shareholding (the founder at 0.03%, the CEO's own stake not disclosed but not material based on available data); (3) no equity dilution is used, meaning management does not have sustained locked-up stock alongside minority holders; and (4) the absence of confirmed returns-based metrics (ROIC, EPS, FCF/share, TSR) in the LTIP documentation is itself a cautionary signal — companies with strong returns-based incentives generally disclose them.

**Verdict: Mixed.** The phantom-share LTIP has a share-price link that partially aligns management with market-price performance, but the core performance conditions are undisclosed in English, the economic ownership of senior management is thin, and the absolute pay level appears moderate (which reduces empire-building incentive). Without confirmed returns-based metric weights, alignment cannot be rated well-aligned. The single biggest incentive signal is the share-price linkage on the LTIP, which is better than a pure size metric — but the opacity of the underlying conditions is a material governance gap.

**Classification: Mixed (partial per-share via share price; underlying performance conditions not confirmed as returns-based).**

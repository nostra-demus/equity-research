## 5. Earnings Setup Summary

### Revenue Setup

The next 12 months still have support from a larger credit book and higher revenue per active customer, rather than a disclosed acquisition or divestiture contribution, but Q2's 50.3% reported pace is not proven as a normal run rate. Management says originations are normalising, and Q4 rather than Q3 carries the identified purchase-volume seasonality. [Q2 2026 Earnings Call, Q&A, p.9] The single factor that would flip revenue direction is weaker conversion of credit-card and loan balances into interest income, whether from slower book growth, lower realization or weaker Brazilian consumer credit. Reported IFRS revenue growth of 50.29% differs by 11.29 percentage points from management's 39% FX-neutral gross-revenue growth, but the numerator definitions differ; the gap cannot be called organic growth or FX. [H1 FY2026 Interim Financial Statements, Statement of Income, p.5]; [Q2 2026 Earnings Presentation, slide 21]

### Margin Setup

Q2's 12.4% company-defined risk-adjusted NIM is an expansion-period result, not a proven cycle peak, floor or normalized margin; management itself said the level was not a floor. [Q2 2026 Earnings Call, Q&A, pp.10–11] Product and country profit are absent, and the filing supplies no earnings coefficient for a 10–20% adverse driver move, so such a stress cannot be quantified without invention. The largest disclosed adverse margin variable is credit cost: a 100% downside macro weighting raises ECL by US$504.700m before tax, while the same risk expansion that helps yield has already raised early delinquency. [H1 FY2026 Interim Financial Statements, Note 3(a), p.15]; [Q2 2026 Earnings Presentation, slide 18] Nu has no disclosed automatic cost pass-through or measured realized recovery rate; it can change loan and deposit offers, but that is not proof of broad customer pass-through. [Nu Holdings Ltd., Form 20-F, FY2025, Item 3.D, pp.111–112 and 136]

### Quality Check

The largest current gap between reported and economic earnings is the US$991.0m deferred-tax benefit, equal to 51.3% of H1 net income; it does not prove improper accounting, but it makes the period less cash-backed. [H1 FY2026 Interim Financial Statements, Statement of Income, p.5; Note 30, pp.36–37] Management's adjusted net income also adds back recurring share-based compensation: US$359.0m in FY2025, after US$408.2m in FY2024 and US$289.3m in FY2023. Normalized next-year earnings should therefore start from reported IFRS income and adjust explicit tax timing and credit performance, not start from management's adjusted figure. [Nu Holdings Ltd., Form 20-F, FY2025, Item 5, “Adjusted Net Income (Loss)” reconciliation]

### Consensus Bar

A material beat requires the existing credit book to keep converting into interest income while credit cost and operating spending stay controlled enough to exceed US$6.11484bn of revenue and US$0.23272 of reported EPS, 3% and 5% above consensus. [Beat / Miss Setup — NU, §4, 2026-08-30] The bar looks more likely high than low: revenue consensus rose 7.0% in 90 days, last-month revisions were 8 up / 0 down for FY2026 EPS and 5 up / 2 down for revenue, and management provides no numeric revenue or EPS guide. [Capital IQ Estimates — Trends and Revisions, FQ3/FY2026] The export does not isolate how much of consensus assumes Q2's one-time Desenrola benefit; management said it was a minority of the 115-basis-point sequential credit-cost help, so its exact consensus effect is not proven. [Q2 2026 Earnings Call, Q&A, pp.10–12]

## 4. Margin Walk — Which Margin Level Matters Most?

Risk-adjusted NIM matters most. NIM alone rises when Nu adds higher-yield unsecured credit, but it does not deduct the associated credit cost. Risk-adjusted NIM therefore shows the trade between yield, funding cost, and defaults more directly; Q2's 12.4% result followed a 22.9% NIM but a 10.5% implied credit-cost rate. The efficiency ratio is a useful secondary gauge, but it is not a standard cost-to-income measure. [Q2 2026 Earnings Deck, slides 15–16, 22]

**Cycle position: credit expansion, not proven peak.** The Q2 credit portfolio was US$39.4bn (+37% YoY), with unsecured lending up 45% to US$10.3bn; risk-adjusted NIM reached a company-reported high. This is an expansionary credit backdrop, but the available data does not establish a prior comparable peak, so it should not be treated as a normalized peak or trough. The Desenrola debt-renegotiation program was a one-time policy-related tailwind: management said it affected about 5% of Q2 cost of credit and that it was a minority of the 115bp contribution. It is not a run-rate margin source. [Q2 2026 earnings-call transcript, prepared remarks and Q&A, pp.7–12]

  → basis matches: company-defined, annualized consolidated risk-adjusted NIM

  = +115bp of the +294bp observed change
  → basis matches

  = −5bp of the +294bp observed change

Reconciliation: **178 + 115 + 5 − 5 + 1 = 294bp reconciled, 1bp residual.** Credit income explains 178 / 294 = **60.5%** of the movement, so it clears the threshold for the single-biggest-driver read in Section 8. The adverse counterpart is material: management's intentional risk expansion added 24bp to early delinquency and US$170m to the allowance build, so the same mix that raises credit income can later raise ECL. ## 6. Seasonality Read

Seasonality neither helps nor hurts the FQ3 setup on the available evidence: the historical-financials review found insufficient like-for-like primary quarterly history for a three-year seasonality table. Management has described Q4 as seasonally higher for purchase volume and revenue, but that is a Q4 observation, not evidence that Q3 carries a seasonal advantage. [Historical Financials — NU, §5, 2026-08-30; Q2 2026 earnings-call transcript, Q&A, p. 9]

## 7. Historical Pattern

The last four reported quarters show no stable beat signal. Revenue beat in Q3 2025 (+3.3%), Q4 2025 (+3.0%) and Q2 2026 (+0.6%), but missed in Q1 2026 (−1.8%); EPS beat in Q3 and Q2 but missed in Q4 and Q1. This is a four-observation descriptive record, so it should receive little weight and is not an empirical probability of an FQ3 beat or miss. [Capital IQ Estimates — Surprise, Fiscal Quarters, Q3 2025–Q2 2026]

## 8. Setup Verdict

**Setup favors miss.** The single most important factor is the raised revenue bar: it requires 42.3% YoY growth after the FQ3 estimate increased 7.0% in 90 days, without a numeric company guide, while management says originations are normalising. The biggest risk to this conclusion is that earlier credit growth and the unsecured mix continue to mature into interest income fast enough to exceed the bar despite that normalisation. [Capital IQ Estimates — Consensus and Trends, FQ3 2026; Q2 2026 earnings-call transcript, prepared remarks and Q&A, pp. 6, 9]

## 9. Second-Quarter Look-Ahead

The FQ4 2026 setup may differ because management describes Q4 purchase volume and revenue as seasonally higher; that could help card-related activity, but it is not a quantified FQ4 earnings forecast. Visibility is otherwise limited: no FQ4 consensus bar is carried in the guidance-consensus output, and the durability of risk-adjusted NIM, credit cost and the approximately 20% FY2026 efficiency objective remains the key issue. [Q2 2026 earnings-call transcript, Q&A and prepared remarks, pp. 8–11]

## 10. Pre-Mortem

If this setup fails, the most likely error is treating normalising originations as an immediate slowdown in reported revenue when the existing credit book can still produce a delayed Q3 interest-income lift. The opposite error is also possible: reading the Q2 risk-adjusted-NIM bridge as durable despite the disclosed early-delinquency and allowance effects from intentional risk expansion. [Q2 2026 earnings-call transcript, prepared remarks and Q&A, pp. 6–9; Q2 2026 Earnings Presentation, slides 16, 18]

## 5. Interaction Effects

Credit-book growth, unsecured mix, yield and credit cost are not independent. The Q2 bridge assigns +178bp to credit income and +115bp to lower credit cost, while the same risk expansion raised early delinquency and the allowance; adding those two bps effects to the macro ECL range would double count related credit-cycle outcomes. Higher rates can also lift interest income while increasing funding cost, reducing credit demand and worsening defaults, so the disclosed DV01 must not be added to the credit-income or credit-cost rows. [Q2 2026 Earnings Presentation, slides 15–18; H1 FY2026 Unaudited Interim Condensed Consolidated Financial Statements, Note 32, p.42]

## 6. Non-Linear Or Asymmetric Risks

The credit-loss range is asymmetric around the current weighted allowance: the downside state adds US$504.700m while the upside state releases US$418.759m. That asymmetry follows the disclosed weighted starting point, whose scenario weights are not provided; it should not be extrapolated linearly. There is also operating leverage in the wrong direction for a credit shock: the revenue benefit from higher-yield unsecured lending arrives before the associated losses are fully known, while management already reported a 24bp rise in early delinquency from risk expansion. Transaction costs are also not a pure price shock: the US$49.505m historical movement can be accompanied by activity and fee-revenue changes, so its cost-only bounds cannot be added to a revenue case. [H1 FY2026 Unaudited Interim Condensed Consolidated Financial Statements, Note 3(a), p.15; Q2 2026 Earnings Presentation, slides 16 and 18; H1 FY2026 Unaudited Interim Condensed Consolidated Financial Statements, Note 6(d), p.15]

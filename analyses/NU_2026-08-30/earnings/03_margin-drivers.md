# Margin Drivers — NU

NU reports under IFRS Accounting Standards in U.S. dollars. Amounts below are US$ millions unless noted. The primary margin read uses Nu's lender metrics: net interest margin (NIM), risk-adjusted NIM (NIM after management's cost-of-credit measure), funding cost, and operating efficiency. NIM and risk-adjusted NIM are annualized management measures, not IFRS line items.

## 1. Segment Decomposition Status

**Sector overlay applied: bank / lender — margin analysis uses NIM, risk-adjusted NIM, credit cost, and efficiency ratio.** Nu has one reportable segment, Nu Group, representing 100% of disclosed revenue and profit. It does not disclose country, product, or segment profit; the analysis is therefore consolidated. Brazil is economically concentrated but is not a reportable profit segment. [FY2025 Form 20-F, Note 34, F-97–F-98]

The strict cost-to-income ratio and a CASA split are not disclosed. Nu instead reports an efficiency ratio: total operating expenses divided by NII plus fee income net of transactional cost and revenue-based taxes. [Q2 2026 Earnings Deck, slides 6 and 22]

Capital IQ's FY2025 banking line is US$6,991m (100% of its vendor-defined segment total), not the IFRS US$15,774.7m total revenue. The difference is the vendor's reclassification after US$4,578.7m of interest and other financial expenses and US$4,204.9m of expected credit loss: US$15,774.7m − US$4,578.7m − US$4,204.9m = US$6,991.1m. It is a presentation difference, not a second segment. [Capital IQ Financials→Segments, revenues, 12 months ended 2025-12-31 — vendor export; FY2025 Form 20-F, Consolidated Statements of Income, F-6–F-7]

The management efficiency ratio and the IFRS operating-expense/revenue ratio are not interchangeable: the former uses a net-revenue denominator and an adjusted operating-expense scope, while the latter is US$1,105.6m / US$5,513.2m = 20.1% in Q2 2026 versus 18.2% a year earlier. Nu has no disclosed automatic cost pass-through and no measured realized recovery rate for a network, cloud, inflation, or other input-cost shock. ## 4. Margin Walk — Which Margin Level Matters Most?

Risk-adjusted NIM matters most. NIM alone rises when Nu adds higher-yield unsecured credit, but it does not deduct the associated credit cost. Risk-adjusted NIM therefore shows the trade between yield, funding cost, and defaults more directly; Q2's 12.4% result followed a 22.9% NIM but a 10.5% implied credit-cost rate. The efficiency ratio is a useful secondary gauge, but it is not a standard cost-to-income measure. [Q2 2026 Earnings Deck, slides 15–16, 22]

**Cycle position: credit expansion, not proven peak.** The Q2 credit portfolio was US$39.4bn (+37% YoY), with unsecured lending up 45% to US$10.3bn; risk-adjusted NIM reached a company-reported high. This is an expansionary credit backdrop, but the available data does not establish a prior comparable peak, so it should not be treated as a normalized peak or trough. The Desenrola debt-renegotiation program was a one-time policy-related tailwind: management said it affected about 5% of Q2 cost of credit and that it was a minority of the 115bp contribution. It is not a run-rate margin source. [Q2 2026 earnings-call transcript, prepared remarks and Q&A, pp.7–12]

## 7a. Bridge Attribution and Residual (MODULE_RULES "Driver Attribution" / §15)

All four named components are **asserted from Nu's disclosed management bridge, no sensitivity applied**. Their basis is the same company-defined, annualized risk-adjusted NIM for Q1 and Q2 2026; they are not applied to IFRS revenue, a different period, or a country/product subsegment.

```text
Credit income: asserted +178bp from management's Q1-to-Q2 risk-adjusted-NIM bridge; no sensitivity applied
  = +178bp of the +294bp observed change
  → basis matches: company-defined, annualized consolidated risk-adjusted NIM

Cost of credit: asserted +115bp from the same bridge; no sensitivity applied
  = +115bp of the +294bp observed change
  → basis matches

Float income: asserted +5bp from the same bridge; no sensitivity applied
  = +5bp of the +294bp observed change
  → basis matches

Funding cost: asserted −5bp from the same bridge; no sensitivity applied
  = −5bp of the +294bp observed change
  → basis matches
```

Reconciliation: **178 + 115 + 5 − 5 + 1 = 294bp reconciled, 1bp residual.** Credit income explains 178 / 294 = **60.5%** of the movement, so it clears the threshold for the single-biggest-driver read in Section 8. Lower cost of credit explains 39.1%. [Q2 2026 Earnings Deck, slide 16; analyst calculation]

## 8. The Single Biggest Margin Driver

**Credit income from prior loan growth and the move toward unsecured lending is the single biggest current margin driver.** It added 178bp, or 60.5%, of the 294bp QoQ rise in risk-adjusted NIM, compared with 115bp from lower credit cost. Its current direction is a tailwind. The adverse counterpart is material: management's intentional risk expansion added 24bp to early delinquency and US$170m to the allowance build, so the same mix that raises credit income can later raise ECL. The Q2 bridge is well reconciled, but it is a single-quarter management measure, not proof that this spread is permanent. [Q2 2026 Earnings Deck, slides 16, 18; Q2 2026 earnings-call transcript, prepared remarks, pp.7–8]

**Current read:** the future-cost reading has the direct evidence, although D&A remains only 0.8% of Q2 IFRS revenue; a positive demand read is not proven from available data. The observable that would flip this read is a disclosed link showing a specific technology or capacity investment generating incremental credit income or net revenue faster than its associated D&A.

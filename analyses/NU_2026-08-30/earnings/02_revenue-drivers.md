# Revenue Drivers — NU

Nu Holdings reports under IFRS Accounting Standards in U.S. dollars and has a 31 December year-end. Unless stated otherwise, figures below are US$ and relate to the reported IFRS revenue line. Management's portfolio, ARPAC, NIM and gross-revenue measures are separately labelled as management measures or non-IFRS.

## 1. Segment Decomposition Status

Single-segment business (>85% from one segment) — consolidated analysis. Nu reports one reportable segment, the Nu Group, which was 100% of FY2025 audited IFRS revenue of US$15.775bn. Product and country profit are not disclosed, so this report does not invent them. [Nu Holdings Ltd., Form 20-F, FY2025, Note 34, F-97–F-98; business-model segment map, 2026-08-30]

Capital IQ's present sidecar read is `Banking US$6.991bn (100%) of Total US$6.991bn` [CIQ Financials→Segments (Revenues, latest annual column), 12 months ended 31 December 2025 — vendor export]. This is not a second segment or audited IFRS revenue: it reconciles to US$15.775bn IFRS revenue less US$4.579bn interest and other financial expenses less US$4.205bn expected credit loss = US$6.991bn. The vendor's bank-template measure is therefore not used as revenue in this report. [Nu Holdings Ltd., Form 20-F, FY2025, Statement of Income, F-7; Note 34, F-97]

## 2. Revenue Driver Tree

| Business Type | Revenue Formula |

| Lender / digital bank | Loan and card receivables × effective yield, plus interest on other earning assets and financial-instrument gains/losses, plus card purchase volume × interchange and other fees |

Company-specific formula: **IFRS total revenue = interest and gains on credit-card receivables, loans and other financial assets + card/prepaid interchange income, late fees, insurance commissions and other fees.** Credit-card interchange is linked to transaction value and merchant-segment rates; interest on revolving/instalment card balances and loans uses the effective-interest method. [Nu Holdings Ltd., Form 20-F, FY2025, Item 5, pp. 157–158; Note 6, F-39]

The bank/lender overlay is applied. NIM, loan growth, deposit growth, NPLs, credit cost, coverage, CAR and ROE are available; standard CASA/funding mix and group ROA are not disclosed. Deposits and their cost are not a substitute for CASA. [business-model/02_business-identity.md, §3a, 2026-08-30]

## 6a. Decomposition Attribution and Residual (MODULE_RULES "Driver Attribution" / §15)

All pp contributions use the same reported Q2 2025 IFRS revenue denominator, US$3,668.470m. They are **asserted from disclosure, no quoted volume/price/mix ratio applied**:

```text
Credit-card interest: (US$1,805.581m − US$1,091.598m) ÷ US$3,668.470m [H1 FY2026 Interim Financial Statements, Note 6, p. 14]
  = 19.46pp of the 50.29pp observed growth
  → no volume/price/mix ratio applied

Loan interest: (US$1,734.365m − US$1,128.020m) ÷ US$3,668.470m [H1 FY2026 Interim Financial Statements, Note 6, p. 14]
  = 16.53pp of the 50.29pp observed growth
  → no volume/price/mix ratio applied

Other interest and gains: US$311.502m net reported change ÷ US$3,668.470m [H1 FY2026 Interim Financial Statements, Note 6, p. 14]
  = 8.49pp of the 50.29pp observed growth
  → no volume/price/mix ratio applied

Credit/prepaid-card fees: (US$534.970m − US$392.568m) ÷ US$3,668.470m [H1 FY2026 Interim Financial Statements, Note 6, p. 15]
  = 3.88pp of the 50.29pp observed growth
  → no volume/price/mix ratio applied

Late fees: (US$136.801m − US$90.193m) ÷ US$3,668.470m [H1 FY2026 Interim Financial Statements, Note 6, p. 15]
  = 1.27pp of the 50.29pp observed growth
  → no volume/price/mix ratio applied

Insurance and other fees: US$23.898m net reported change ÷ US$3,668.470m [H1 FY2026 Interim Financial Statements, Note 6, p. 15]
  = 0.65pp of the 50.29pp observed growth
  → no volume/price/mix ratio applied
```

Reconciliation: 19.46 + 16.53 + 8.49 + 3.88 + 1.27 + 0.65 = **50.29pp reconciled, 0.00pp residual** (rounding to two decimals). The resulting attribution is a reported revenue-stream bridge, not a causal price/volume decomposition.

## 7. The Single Biggest Revenue Driver

**Interest income from the credit-card and loan book is the single biggest revenue driver, and it is improving.** Its US$1.320bn year-on-year increase equals 35.99pp, or 71.6%, of Q2's 50.29% IFRS revenue growth, so it clears the "main driver" test on the reported bridge. The underlying management portfolio grew 37% YoY FX-neutral to US$39.4bn, led by cards and unsecured lending; management says Q1 balance growth matured into Q2 income. That leaves the revenue driver exposed to Brazilian consumer-credit conditions, rates, underwriting and mix: Brazil represented 91.4% of the filing's narrower FY2025 geographic-revenue table, and the standard CASA mix is still not disclosed. Q4's holiday purchase-volume seasonality and the recognised lag from originations mean the current Q2 pace should not be treated as a normalised run rate.

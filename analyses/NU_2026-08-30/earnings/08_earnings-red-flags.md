## 5. Red-Flag Severity Verdict

**Material concerns**

The reported Q2 improvement is real, but its persistence is fragile: the FQ3 bar is higher after revisions, while higher-yield credit mix and deliberate risk expansion are already increasing early-delinquency and allowance indicators. The single most dangerous flag is a reversal in credit cost after the unsecured-credit expansion; a Q3 disclosure showing stable or improving early delinquency, ECL coverage, and cost of credit on the expanded book would resolve it. The deferred-tax benefit and negative lender cash flow add quality concerns but do not prove improper accounting or fictitious income.

## 7. Pre-Mortem — If The Earnings Setup Fails

The most likely failure mode is that the Q2 boost from earlier credit-book growth and higher-yield unsecured lending is treated as a lasting earnings step-up, but the same risk expansion later raises ECL faster than interest income and leaves the raised FQ3 consensus unmet. The warning is already visible in the 24bp early-delinquency effect and US$170m allowance-bridge contribution; Q3 like-for-like credit-cost and ECL coverage are the decisive checks.

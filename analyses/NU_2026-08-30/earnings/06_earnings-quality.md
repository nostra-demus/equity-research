# Earnings Quality — NU

Nu Holdings Ltd. reports under IFRS Accounting Standards in U.S. dollars and has a 31 December year-end. Amounts below are US$ millions unless stated otherwise. NU is a deposit-taking consumer lender, not an industrial company: changes in loans, credit-card receivables, securities, deposits and network payables drive cash from operating activities (CFO). Therefore, ordinary working-capital days and free cash flow (FCF, CFO less total capex) do not measure distributable cash in the usual way. NU does not report EBITDA (earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortization), so a direct CFO/EBITDA cash-conversion test is not assessable. [Nu Holdings Ltd., Form 20-F, FY2025, Consolidated Statements of Income and Cash Flows, F-6–F-7 and F-14–F-15]

## 2. Cash Conversion Assessment

The usual CFO/EBITDA threshold cannot be applied because NU reports neither EBITDA nor an EBITDA reconciliation. Annual CFO nevertheless rose from US$0.8bn in FY2022 to US$3.5bn in FY2025 while net income also rose, which is better evidence than a non-GAAP proxy. [Nu Holdings Ltd., Form 20-F, FY2025, Consolidated Statements of Income and Cash Flows, F-6–F-7 and F-14–F-15]

The latest period is materially weaker: TTM CFO and FCF are negative on the filing-built basis. The H1 FY2026 deficit was driven by US$8.1bn of credit-card-receivable cash outflow, US$6.6bn of loan cash outflow and US$2.9bn of securities cash outflow, partly funded by US$3.4bn of deposits, US$1.9bn of network payables and US$2.0bn of other liabilities. This is a growth-and-funding mismatch within a lender’s cash-flow statement, not proof that reported income is fictitious; it does mean cash support for the current earnings run-rate is not established. [Nu Holdings Ltd., H1 FY2026 Unaudited Interim Condensed Consolidated Financial Statements, Statements of Cash Flows, p.12]

The lender-specific balance-sheet read is more useful. Deferred income also increased from US$68.4m to US$71.6m and US$77.5m; it is therefore not a declining contract-liability signal. [Nu Holdings Ltd., Form 20-F, FY2024, Statement of Financial Position Data, “Credit card receivables,” “Loans to customers,” “Deposits” and “Deferred income”; Nu Holdings Ltd., Form 20-F, FY2025, Consolidated Statements of Financial Position, F-8–F-9; Note 26, “Deferred income,” F-76]

Credit-loss coverage is the key accrual monitor. On a matched point-in-time basis, total expected credit loss (ECL) was US$5,022.0m on US$32,666.7m gross card receivables plus loans at FY2025, or 15.37%. At 30 June 2026 it was US$6,642.3m on US$39,398.4m gross card receivables plus loans, or 16.86%: US$4,522.5m card ECL + US$2,119.8m loan ECL divided by US$25,961.1m gross card receivables + US$13,437.3m gross loans. The 149bp increase is a credit-risk warning, but it does not by itself show inadequate provisioning; the allowance has risen rather than been released. [Nu Holdings Ltd., Form 20-F, FY2025, Note 13, “Credit card receivables”; Note 14, “Loans to customers”; Nu Holdings Ltd., H1 FY2026 Unaudited Interim Condensed Consolidated Financial Statements, Note 13, “Credit card receivables”; Note 14, “Loans to customers”]

## 9. Earnings Quality Score

**54/100 — Material concerns.** The main reasons are the filing-built TTM CFO/FCF reversal, the two-row lender-specific accrual flag, the US$991.0m H1 FY2026 deferred-tax benefit that suppresses book tax expense, and a recurring share-based-compensation add-back in Adjusted Net Income. Offsetting evidence is that annual CFO was positive and rising from FY2022 through FY2025, the FY2025 Adjusted Net Income uplift was 7.1% rather than above the 15% threshold, and no repeated restructuring charge or conventional working-capital anomaly is evidenced. Cash-flow data is available, so no partial-data score cap applies; the missing reported EBITDA prevents a direct CFO/EBITDA assessment.

## 10. The Single Biggest Quality Concern

The central risk is credit growth consuming cash while the credit-loss estimate rises. At 30 June 2026, gross card receivables plus loans were US$39.4bn and total ECL was US$6.64bn (16.86%), up from US$32.7bn and US$5.02bn (15.37%) at FY2025. In the same H1 period, CFO was US$(1.24)bn despite US$1.93bn of net income because cash outflows into credit-card receivables, loans and securities exceeded funding inflows. This does not prove earnings are overstated: NU records ECL before losses are realized and the higher allowance may be prudent. It does mean the durability of earnings depends on collections and credit performance rather than the FY2025 positive FCF headline. [Nu Holdings Ltd., Form 20-F, FY2025, Notes 13–14 and Consolidated Statements of Cash Flows, F-14–F-15; Nu Holdings Ltd., H1 FY2026 Unaudited Interim Condensed Consolidated Financial Statements, Statements of Cash Flows, p.12; Notes 13–14]

## 2. Classification

**Standard professional management** — the evidence shows a clean audit history, limited disclosed related-party exposure, modest net dilution now offset by buybacks, and no proven serial-acquirer pattern; these positives do not remove the governance constraint from one founder’s voting control or the need to monitor the new CFO and large unused credit limits.

## 3. Most Material Signal

The most material signal is founder control. David Vélez has 74.4% of voting power through super-voting Class B shares, is also CEO and chair, and retains consent rights over material actions including dividends, equity issuance, mergers and acquisitions, and asset transactions above 20% of net equity while the shareholder agreement remains in force [FY2025 Form 20-F, 2025, pp.193 and 203]. This is not evidence of misconduct or of a controller-minority conflict today, but it limits outside holders’ ability to block one if capital allocation or related-party conduct deteriorates; any use of those powers that disadvantages Class A holders would change this classification.

## 4. Capital Allocation Score /100

**65 / 100 — higher = better.** This is the inverse of the weighted severity profile, with the greatest weight on controller power, off-balance-sheet credit commitments, debt funding and dilution. No serial-acquirer cap applies: the available evidence does not show multiple material, debt-funded acquisitions near the company’s own value, and the Banco Porto Real consideration and integration outcome are not disclosed.

## 2. Input Cost Pass-Through

**Answer this on two separate axes, and never let one stand in for the other (CLAUDE.md §9).**

| Axis | What it asks | Verdict | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Contractual pass-through** | Is there an escalator, indexed-pricing, or cost-plus clause that moves price automatically? | **No disclosed automatic pass-through.** No indexed, cost-plus, or automatic customer-price clause was located in the available filings; this is not proof that none exists. The filing says network-rule changes and inflation-related costs may not be passed to customers in every circumstance. | [FY2025 Form 20-F, Item 3.D — Risk Factors: Mastercard payment scheme, pp.111–112; Item 3.D — Risk Factors: inflation, p.136] |
| **Realised recovery** | Of a given input-cost increase, how much did the company actually recover — through price, mix, hedging, re-sourcing, or a cost programme? | **Not computable from the pool.** Nu does not disclose a matched gross pre-mitigation input-cost shock and the associated reported margin impact, so the required recovery calculation cannot be made. | [FY2025 Form 20-F, Item 3.D — Risk Factors: Mastercard payment scheme and inflation; Q2 2026 earnings-call transcript, prepared remarks] |

Nu has some economic tools, but the pool does not measure their recovery rate. In Q2 2026, management said it was optimizing Mexico deposits, lowering the cost of deposits to 88% of the local interbank rate, three percentage points below a year earlier, while Mexican deposits declined modestly. It also described individualized loan and deposit pricing. These are funding-cost and product-pricing levers, not evidence that Nu passed a specific network, cloud, or inflation cost to customers. In 2025, Nu said changes to Brazilian deposit remuneration and Money Boxes had no material impact on deposit inflows; that is cost absorption through funding-price management, not customer price pass-through. [Q2 2026 earnings-call transcript, prepared remarks and Q&A; FY2025 Form 20-F, Item 5 — Key Business Metrics: Deposits]

**Supplier / input concentration (quantify).** The filing does not disclose the largest supplier's share of purchases or COGS, a top-three supplier share, or the cost share of any input. It does, however, identify Mastercard and certain other third-party services as sole-source or limited-source, and says replacement could be difficult and disruptive. Mastercard authorizes Nu's credit and prepaid-card transactions, making it a material operational dependency even though its spend share is not disclosed. [FY2025 Form 20-F, Item 3.D — Risk Factors: third-party service providers]

## 3. Customer Pricing Power

Nu has partial, product-level pricing control rather than proven broad customer pricing power. Management says it can set a personalized price for each loan, credit product or deposit offer. The observable 2025–Q2 2026 actions are mainly **upstream funding** decisions: Nu changed deposit remuneration and Money Boxes in Brazil without a material reported effect on deposit inflows in 2025, then optimized Mexican deposits in Q2 2026, with a modest deposit decline and a cost of deposits 3 percentage points lower year on year. Those results do not show that borrowers or merchants accepted a higher Nu price. [FY2025 Form 20-F, Item 5 — Key Business Metrics: Deposits; Q2 2026 earnings-call transcript, prepared remarks and Q&A]

On the downstream side, no specific loan-rate or fee increase and matching volume reaction were located in the last 24 months. The filing instead says Nu could be forced to raise deposit rates, cut loan rates or cut fees when customers receive better competing offers; customers do not have long-term contracts, and open-finance rules make switching easier. Brazilian regulation also caps prepaid-card interchange at 0.7% and debit-card interchange at 0.5%, constraining a key card-payment price. **Not proven from available data** that Nu can raise customer prices without losing volume. [FY2025 Form 20-F, Item 3.D — Risk Factors: competition and open finance; Item 3.D — Risk Factors: customers; Item 4.B — Brazilian payments regulation]

## 4. Economic Control Verdict

**Mixed.** Nu has meaningful control over product and deposit offers and benefits from a large retail deposit base, but its funding price is set in a contest with customers and market rates. Its card-payment economics are less controlled: Mastercard authorizes transactions, may raise per-transaction fees, and controls rules that Nu may not always pass on; Brazilian interchange caps further restrict price. [FY2025 Form 20-F, Item 3.D — Risk Factors: liquidity, competition, third-party providers and Mastercard payment scheme, pp.111–112; Item 4.B — Brazilian payments regulation]

## 5. The Single Biggest Bargaining Risk

**Mastercard payment-scheme terms and continuity:** Nu relies on Mastercard for card authorization and a significant source of card-related revenue; fee or rule changes, suspension, or loss of registration could hurt both service continuity and margins, with no disclosed timely substitute. [FY2025 Form 20-F, Item 3.D — Risk Factors: third-party service providers and Mastercard payment scheme, pp.111–112]

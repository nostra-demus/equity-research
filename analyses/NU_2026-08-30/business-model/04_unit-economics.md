# Unit Economics — NU

## 1. Natural Unit

The natural economic unit for Nu Group (the single reportable segment) is an individual monthly active customer. Nu reports under IFRS Accounting Standards in U.S. dollars. [Nu Holdings Ltd., Form 20-F, FY2025, Note 34, F-97; Presentation of Financial and Other Information, p.1]

Secondary units are credit-card and loan exposures and card-payment transactions, but Nu does not disclose product-level contribution economics. [Nu Holdings Ltd., Form 20-F, FY2025, Note 6, F-39]

## 3. Value Creation Read

**Verdict: Unclear.** The disclosed operating spread is favorable but incomplete: Q2 2026 ARPAC of US$17.1 less the US$1.0 cost to serve equals US$16.1 per active customer per month. That is a revenue-minus-partial-service-cost spread, not contribution margin, because the US$1.0 measure excludes financial-service costs and expected credit losses. [Nu Holdings Ltd., Q2 2026 Earnings Presentation, slide 28; Nu Holdings Ltd., Form 20-F, FY2025, Gross Profit, p.171]

CAC of US$7.4 per customer is below one month of the current disclosed ARPAC, but the two metrics are from different periods and ARPAC is not profit. Nu therefore has not disclosed the cohort revenue, funding cost, expected credit loss, full variable cost, retention and lifetime contribution needed to test whether contribution margin × lifetime exceeds CAC. The single most valuable missing disclosure is a new-customer cohort table with those inputs and a stated payback period. [Nu Holdings Ltd., Form 20-F, FY2025, Overview—Low Cost to Acquire, p.157; Q2 2026 Earnings Presentation, slide 28]

## 4. Sensitivity

Expected credit loss is the input most likely to change this read, because it is not included in cost to serve. FY2025 expected credit loss was US$4.2049 billion; a mechanical 20% increase is US$0.8410 billion and, holding revenue and every other cost unchanged, would reduce Group gross profit from US$6.6250 billion to US$5.7840 billion—a 12.7% reduction. This is a zero-mitigation bound, not a forecast or a per-customer result. Expected credit loss rose 32.7% in FY2025 while the reported monthly cost to serve stayed US$0.8 in FY2023–FY2025; the series use different bases, so this does not establish a unit-level causal attribution, but it shows why credit loss matters more than the reported servicing-cost line alone. [Nu Holdings Ltd., Form 20-F, FY2025, Gross Profit, p.171; Monthly Average Cost to Serve per Active Customer, p.161]

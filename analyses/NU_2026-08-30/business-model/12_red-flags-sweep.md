## 3. Most Severe New Flag

The most severe new flag is deferred-tax asset dependence in H1 2026 earnings. The US$991.046m deferred-tax benefit accounts for 51.3% of the reported US$1.933bn H1 net income, while the US$3.649bn net deferred-tax asset equals 27.5% of equity. The filing supports the asset with technical-feasibility studies and each subsidiary's profitability history, so this is not a finding of aggressive accounting. It does mean that the headline tax rate and net income should be read alongside current tax expense and the future realization of credit-loss tax deductions. [Q2 2026 Unaudited Interim Condensed Consolidated Financial Statements, Note 30, pp. 36–37; Statements of Income, p. 7; Statements of Financial Position, p. 8]

## 4. Cross-Cutting Patterns

Inference, not from filings: H1 IFRS net income contains two large estimate-driven movements in opposite directions — a US$991.046m deferred-tax benefit raised income while US$25.588m of provisions and contingent liabilities was charged versus US$2.122m a year earlier. This is not an allegation of improper accounting; it makes current-tax expense, deferred-tax-asset realization, and the legal-provision roll-forward more informative than a simple read of headline net income alone. [Q2 2026 Unaudited Interim Condensed Consolidated Financial Statements, Note 25, pp. 32–33; Note 30, pp. 36–37; Statements of Cash Flows, p. 12]

# Business Identity — NU

## 1. What The Company Actually Does

Nu is a mobile-first financial-services group that offers accounts, credit and prepaid cards, consumer and small-business lending, payments, savings and investment products, and insurance distribution through one app for individual consumers and small businesses in Brazil, Mexico, and Colombia. [FY2025 Form 20-F, Item 4.B, pp.13–15]
Customers use Nu to pay, hold and move money, borrow, save, invest, and buy protection without using a branch; merchants and financial-product partners are also part of the payment and distribution flows. [FY2025 Form 20-F, Item 4.B, pp.14, 32–35; p.158]
*Inference, not from filings:* its economic engine is balance-sheet-led rather than a software subscription model: interest income and gains on financial instruments were 85.2% of FY2025 revenue, generated chiefly from card receivables, loans, and cash/investment assets. [FY2025 Form 20-F, Item 5, p.157]
Customer deposits support its funding needs; *inference, not from filings:* this makes deposit pricing, credit quality, and the size and mix of the credit book important alongside customer growth. [FY2025 Form 20-F, Item 5, p.157]
Nu also makes fee income when customers use its cards and through loyalty subscriptions, late fees, and partner commissions for investments, insurance, remittances, and marketplace sales. [FY2025 Form 20-F, Item 5, p.158]
At Q2 2026, management reported 139 million customers, an 83.5% activity rate, and US$17 monthly ARPAC (average revenue per active customer); these are management measures, with ARPAC defined as monthly revenue divided by average monthly active customers. [Q2 2026 earnings-call transcript, prepared remarks, pp.4–5; Q2’26 earnings deck, slide 6]

## 2. How The Company Makes Money

**Core financial income:**

`Interest and gains = (interest-bearing credit-card receivables + customer loans + cash/investment assets) × yield, plus/minus financial-instrument gains and losses`

Nu charges interest on revolving and refinanced card balances, purchased card receivables, and customer loans; it also earns interest and records gains or losses on its financial assets. [FY2025 Form 20-F, Item 5, pp.157, 169]

**Fee income:**

`Fee and commission income = card purchase volume × interchange rate (net of rewards) + subscription fees + late fees + partner and marketplace commissions`

Most fee income comes from credit and prepaid-card transactions. Mastercard-set interchange varies by merchant segment and is paid by accepting merchants, while Nu also earns the other fee streams shown in the formula. [FY2025 Form 20-F, Item 5, pp.158, 169–170]

*Inference, grounded in the disclosed revenue streams:* volume is driven by customer acquisition, active use, card purchase volume, lending balances, deposits, and adoption of more products by existing customers. [FY2025 Form 20-F, Item 5, pp.157–158, 175]
*Inference, grounded in the disclosed revenue streams:* price is driven mainly by lending yields, the market rates earned on cash and investment assets, interchange, and paid tiers; interest-rate changes can affect both earned yields and funding costs. [FY2025 Form 20-F, Item 5, pp.157–158]
*Inference, grounded in the disclosed cost structure:* margin depends on the spread after deposit and other funding costs, expected credit loss (ECL), payment-network and chargeback costs, rewards, and customer-service/operating costs. [FY2025 Form 20-F, Item 5, pp.157, 170]

## 3. Business Type Classification

*Inference, not from filings:* Latin American digital retail bank/lender, using customer deposits to support funding needs and earning card-payment and distribution fees alongside interest income. [FY2025 Form 20-F, Item 5, pp.157–158]

# Unit Economics — NU

## 1. Natural Unit

The natural economic unit for Nu Group (the single reportable segment) is an individual monthly active customer. Nu reports under IFRS Accounting Standards in U.S. dollars. [Nu Holdings Ltd., Form 20-F, FY2025, Note 34, F-97; Presentation of Financial and Other Information, p.1]

Secondary units are credit-card and loan exposures and card-payment transactions, but Nu does not disclose product-level contribution economics. [Nu Holdings Ltd., Form 20-F, FY2025, Note 6, F-39]

## 2. Unit Economics Table

| Unit Economic | Value | Period | Direction vs Prior Year (Improving / Stable / Deteriorating / Unknown) | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue per unit | US$17.1 monthly ARPAC (average monthly revenue per average individual monthly active customer) | Q2 2026 | Improving: US$13.8 in Q2 2025 and US$15.9 in Q1 2026 | [Nu Holdings Ltd., Q2 2026 Earnings Presentation, slide 28] |
| Gross margin per unit | Not disclosed. The reported FY2025 gross margin was 42.0% at Group level, not a per-active-customer margin. | FY2025 | Unknown | [Nu Holdings Ltd., Form 20-F, FY2025, Gross Profit, p.171] |
| Contribution margin per unit (after variable costs) | Not disclosed. The US$1.0 monthly cost to serve is not a contribution-cost measure: it covers transaction, customer-support and operations costs, while financial-service costs and expected credit losses sit outside it. | Q2 2026 | Unknown | [Nu Holdings Ltd., Q2 2026 Earnings Presentation, slide 28; Nu Holdings Ltd., Form 20-F, FY2025, Monthly Average Cost to Serve per Active Customer, p.161; Gross Profit, p.171] |
| Cost to acquire / build the unit | US$7.4 CAC per customer; paid marketing was about 32% of CAC. | FY2025 | Deteriorating: US$6.4 in FY2024 | [Nu Holdings Ltd., Form 20-F, FY2025, Overview—Low Cost to Acquire, p.157] |
| Payback period or unit lifetime | Not disclosed. Net churn was 0.1% per month, but Nu does not translate that figure into a lifetime or a CAC payback calculation. | FY2025 | Improving: net churn was 0.2% per month in FY2024 | [Nu Holdings Ltd., Form 20-F, FY2025, Net Churn, p.163] |

## 3. Value Creation Read

**Verdict: Unclear.** The disclosed operating spread is favorable but incomplete: Q2 2026 ARPAC of US$17.1 less the US$1.0 cost to serve equals US$16.1 per active customer per month. That is a revenue-minus-partial-service-cost spread, not contribution margin, because the US$1.0 measure excludes financial-service costs and expected credit losses. [Nu Holdings Ltd., Q2 2026 Earnings Presentation, slide 28; Nu Holdings Ltd., Form 20-F, FY2025, Gross Profit, p.171]

CAC of US$7.4 per customer is below one month of the current disclosed ARPAC, but the two metrics are from different periods and ARPAC is not profit. Nu therefore has not disclosed the cohort revenue, funding cost, expected credit loss, full variable cost, retention and lifetime contribution needed to test whether contribution margin × lifetime exceeds CAC. The single most valuable missing disclosure is a new-customer cohort table with those inputs and a stated payback period. [Nu Holdings Ltd., Form 20-F, FY2025, Overview—Low Cost to Acquire, p.157; Q2 2026 Earnings Presentation, slide 28]

## 4. Sensitivity

Expected credit loss is the input most likely to change this read, because it is not included in cost to serve. FY2025 expected credit loss was US$4.2049 billion; a mechanical 20% increase is US$0.8410 billion and, holding revenue and every other cost unchanged, would reduce Group gross profit from US$6.6250 billion to US$5.7840 billion—a 12.7% reduction. This is a zero-mitigation bound, not a forecast or a per-customer result. Expected credit loss rose 32.7% in FY2025 while the reported monthly cost to serve stayed US$0.8 in FY2023–FY2025; the series use different bases, so this does not establish a unit-level causal attribution, but it shows why credit loss matters more than the reported servicing-cost line alone. [Nu Holdings Ltd., Form 20-F, FY2025, Gross Profit, p.171; Monthly Average Cost to Serve per Active Customer, p.161]

## 3. Customer Pricing Power

Nu has partial, product-level pricing control rather than proven broad customer pricing power. Management says it can set a personalized price for each loan, credit product or deposit offer. The observable 2025–Q2 2026 actions are mainly **upstream funding** decisions: Nu changed deposit remuneration and Money Boxes in Brazil without a material reported effect on deposit inflows in 2025, then optimized Mexican deposits in Q2 2026, with a modest deposit decline and a cost of deposits 3 percentage points lower year on year. Those results do not show that borrowers or merchants accepted a higher Nu price. [FY2025 Form 20-F, Item 5 — Key Business Metrics: Deposits; Q2 2026 earnings-call transcript, prepared remarks and Q&A]

On the downstream side, no specific loan-rate or fee increase and matching volume reaction were located in the last 24 months. The filing instead says Nu could be forced to raise deposit rates, cut loan rates or cut fees when customers receive better competing offers; customers do not have long-term contracts, and open-finance rules make switching easier. Brazilian regulation also caps prepaid-card interchange at 0.7% and debit-card interchange at 0.5%, constraining a key card-payment price. **Not proven from available data** that Nu can raise customer prices without losing volume. [FY2025 Form 20-F, Item 3.D — Risk Factors: competition and open finance; Item 3.D — Risk Factors: customers; Item 4.B — Brazilian payments regulation]

## 4. Economic Control Verdict

**Mixed.** Nu has meaningful control over product and deposit offers and benefits from a large retail deposit base, but its funding price is set in a contest with customers and market rates. Its card-payment economics are less controlled: Mastercard authorizes transactions, may raise per-transaction fees, and controls rules that Nu may not always pass on; Brazilian interchange caps further restrict price. [FY2025 Form 20-F, Item 3.D — Risk Factors: liquidity, competition, third-party providers and Mastercard payment scheme, pp.111–112; Item 4.B — Brazilian payments regulation]

## 5. The Single Biggest Bargaining Risk

**Mastercard payment-scheme terms and continuity:** Nu relies on Mastercard for card authorization and a significant source of card-related revenue; fee or rule changes, suspension, or loss of registration could hurt both service continuity and margins, with no disclosed timely substitute. [FY2025 Form 20-F, Item 3.D — Risk Factors: third-party service providers and Mastercard payment scheme, pp.111–112]

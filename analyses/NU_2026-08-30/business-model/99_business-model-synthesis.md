# Business Model Reality Check — NU (Synthesis)

## Abstract

Nu is a digital bank for consumers and small businesses in Brazil, Mexico, and Colombia. Its sole reportable segment earns from cards, loans, cash, and investments; interest and gains supplied 85.2% of FY2025 revenue. [FY2025 Form 20-F, Item 4.B, pp.13–15; Item 5, pp.157–158; Note 34, F-97] Reach is the clearest positive: Q2 2026 had 139 million customers and 83.5% activity. [Q2 2026 earnings presentation, slide 6] The main weakness is concentration and incomplete economics: Brazil supplied 91.3% of FY2025 geographic revenue, while cohort contribution and payback are undisclosed. [FY2025 Form 20-F, Note 34, F-97; Item 5, p.157] No disqualifier fired. [Disqualifier Scan — NU, Verdict-Lock Signal, 2026-08-30] This average business merits deeper work only with a clear price buffer.

### Module Disconfirmation

- **Strongest bear point:** Brazil supplied 91.3% of FY2025 geographic revenue, leaving the group exposed to one country's consumer-credit, policy, rate, and currency conditions. [FY2025 Form 20-F, Note 34, F-97; Item 3.D, country-risk factors]
- **Strongest bull point:** NU had 139 million customers, an 83.5% activity rate, and US$17 monthly ARPAC—average revenue per active customer—in Q2 2026, evidence of reach and repeat use even though revenue is not contracted. [Q2 2026 earnings presentation, slide 6]
- **Single killer risk:** A weaker Brazilian credit cycle that raises expected credit loss while funding costs stay high would hit the dominant revenue base; the filing does not quantify that combined scenario. [FY2025 Form 20-F, pp.131–132, 166–171]
- **Disconfirming evidence already visible:** Q2 2026 NIM—net interest income relative to interest-earning assets—rose to 22.9% from 18.8% a year earlier, risk-adjusted NIM rose to 12.4% from 9.9%, and both delinquency measures improved sequentially. These recent readings argue against an immediate deterioration call, but they do not establish through-cycle economics. [Q2 2026 earnings presentation, slides 15, 17–19]

## 3. Reconciliation

- **Revenue basis:** Segment Map uses US$15.775bn of consolidated IFRS revenue, while Customer and Geography Map uses the US$12.084bn geographic-revenue subtotal defined in Note 34. The latter excludes financial expenses and expected credit loss from the consolidated presentation, so geographic shares apply only to that narrower subtotal; they are not segment shares. [FY2025 Form 20-F, Consolidated Income Statement, F-7; Note 34, F-97–F-98]
- **Brazil share:** Customer and Geography Map reports 91.3%, External Dependency rounds the same filing inputs to 91.4%, and CIQ displays 91%. The synthesis uses the filing's exact US$11,038.313m divided by US$12,083.766m, or 91.3% to one decimal; CIQ's whole-number display is a reconciled vendor rounding, not a separate fact. [FY2025 Form 20-F, Note 34, F-97; CIQ Financials→Segments, Geographic Segment → Revenues, FY2025 — vendor export]
- **Moat trajectory:** Moat calls the trajectory provisionally widening, while its own table shows gross margin down 150 basis points. The reconciled read keeps the qualifier: operating leverage and ARPAC improved, but pricing power and market-share widening are not proven; Business Quality's mixed verdict therefore stands. [FY2025 Form 20-F, p.155; Item 5.A, pp.158–159; Moat — NU, Moat Trajectory, 2026-08-30]
- **Operating cash flow:** The audited FY2025 filing reports positive US$3.500bn cash from operations, while CIQ reports negative US$10.305bn for LTM June 2026. Different periods and vendor classification make them unsuitable for direct comparison. The audited annual series governs the four-year disqualifier test; the later LTM vendor read remains unreconciled and must not be relabelled as a filing figure. [FY2025 Form 20-F, Cash Flows, F-14; CIQ Financials→Cash Flow, “Cash from Ops.”, LTM June 2026 — vendor export]

No material disagreement remains after preserving these period, scope, source, and definition differences.

## 4. Note To The Final Synthesizer

- **Strongest business-model positive:** Scale is paired with frequent use: Q2 2026 had 139 million customers, 83.5% activity, and US$17 monthly ARPAC. [Q2 2026 earnings presentation, slide 6]
- **Strongest business-model negative:** Brazil supplied 91.3% of FY2025 geographic revenue, so country-specific credit, rate, policy, regulatory, and FX shocks can affect most of the measured revenue base at once. [FY2025 Form 20-F, Note 34, F-97; Item 3.D, country-risk factors]
- **Most important segment:** Nu Group is the sole reportable segment and supplies 100% of disclosed segment revenue and profit; Brazil is the economic concentration, not a separately reported segment. [FY2025 Form 20-F, Note 34, F-97–F-98]
- **Cleanest unit-economics read:** US$17.1 monthly ARPAC less US$1.0 cost to serve equals US$16.1 per active customer, but this is revenue minus partial service cost, not contribution margin, because financial-service costs and expected credit loss are excluded. [Q2 2026 earnings presentation, slide 28; FY2025 Form 20-F, p.171]
- **Versus named peers:** NU's four-year IFRS ROE—net income earned on average shareholder capital—was 17.2%, between Itaú's 24.3% and Santander Brasil's 12.5% and near Bradesco's 16.2%. The peer figures are current management-defined measures, so no precise ranking is supported; matched gross and EBIT margins are unavailable. [FY2025 Form 20-F, pp.155, 157; Itaú 2Q26 Form 6-K; Bradesco 2Q26 Form 6-K, p.7; Santander Brasil 2Q26 Form 6-K, p.3]
- **Main external dependency:** Brazilian consumer-credit conditions, followed by interest rates and regulation; specified hedges do not remove household-demand or default risk. [FY2025 Form 20-F, pp.53, 131–132, 166–167; H1 2026 interim financial statements, Notes 20 and 32]
- **Capital allocation and governance:** Audit history is clean and no serial-acquirer pattern is proven, but David Vélez's 74.4% voting power gives the founder/CEO/chair decisive control over outside Class A holders. [FY2025 Form 20-F, pp.193, 203]
- **Contingent credit exposure:** US$38.164bn of unused card limits was off balance sheet but included in expected-credit-loss measurement; it is a disclosed lending commitment, not an undisclosed financing vehicle. [Q2 2026 interim financial statements, Note 32, p.40]
- **Automatic disqualifier:** None triggered. Pledged shares and related-party sales/purchases remain not assessable rather than zero. [Disqualifier Scan — NU, Disqualifier Check, 2026-08-30]
- **Rejector filters and caps:** Crooks/integrity, serial acquirers, and fast-changing industry did not trip. No rejector-filter cap or capital-structure transaction cap was applied. [Disqualifier Scan — NU, 2026-08-30; Capital Allocation & Governance — NU, 2026-08-30; Business Quality — NU, 2026-08-30]
- **Deferred-tax flag:** The US$991.046m H1 2026 deferred-tax benefit was 51.3% of US$1.933bn net income, and the US$3.649bn net deferred-tax asset was 27.5% of equity. Future taxable profit must support realization; the filing's support means this is estimate dependence, not evidence of improper accounting. [Q2 2026 interim financial statements, pp.7–8; Note 30, pp.36–37]
- **Legal and tax provision flag:** The provision balance rose US$13.423m, or 43.4%, in six months to US$44.343m, with H1 additions of US$27.370m versus US$14.474m a year earlier. The balance is small beside group earnings, but the direction is adverse and cash timing is uncertain. [Q2 2026 interim financial statements, Note 25, pp.32–33; Cash Flows, p.12]
- **Biggest missing data point:** A new-customer cohort table showing revenue, funding cost, expected credit loss, full variable cost, retention, lifetime contribution, and payback. Without it, NU's low CAC and high ARPAC do not prove that each new customer creates value. [FY2025 Form 20-F, p.157; Q2 2026 earnings presentation, slide 28]
- **Deeper-work decision:** Yes, but only as a valuation-gated average business. A full cohort-profit disclosure and IFRS ROE staying above a group-relevant cost of equity through a weaker credit period would move the answer toward high quality; FY2026 IFRS ROE below that hurdle, failed deferred-tax-asset realization, or credit losses overwhelming the improved spread would move it toward low quality. [Moat — NU, Economic Moat Test and Moat Verdict, 2026-08-30; Q2 2026 interim financial statements, Note 30, pp.36–37]

## 5. Simple Summary

- Nu is a mobile-first retail bank for consumers and small businesses in Brazil, Mexico, and Colombia. [FY2025 Form 20-F, Item 4.B, pp.13–15]
- Interest and financial-instrument gains supplied 85.2% of FY2025 revenue; card and partner fees supplied the rest. [FY2025 Form 20-F, Item 5, pp.157–158]
- NU has not shown whether each new customer creates value: ARPAC is revenue, and cost to serve excludes funding cost and credit loss. [Q2 2026 earnings presentation, slide 28; FY2025 Form 20-F, p.171]
- The group reports one segment. Brazil supplied 91.3% of the narrower FY2025 geographic-revenue subtotal. [FY2025 Form 20-F, Note 34, F-97–F-98]
- The moat is narrow and only provisionally widening, built mainly on low-cost digital distribution and scale rather than network effects; Itaú, Bradesco, and Santander Brasil remain powerful named competitors. [FY2025 Form 20-F, Item 3.D; Item 5.A, p.157]
- Rates, regulation, and the Brazilian consumer-credit cycle matter more than commodities or industrial demand. [FY2025 Form 20-F, pp.53, 131–132, 166–167]
- Capital allocation has no proven serial-acquirer pattern, but the founder/CEO/chair controls 74.4% of the vote. [FY2025 Form 20-F, pp.193, 203]
- No automatic disqualifier fired. NU deserves deeper work only if valuation compensates for Brazil concentration, incomplete cohort economics, and estimate-heavy H1 tax benefits.

# Business Identity — NU

## 1. What The Company Actually Does

Nu is a mobile-first financial-services group that offers accounts, credit and prepaid cards, consumer and small-business lending, payments, savings and investment products, and insurance distribution through one app for individual consumers and small businesses in Brazil, Mexico, and Colombia. [FY2025 Form 20-F, Item 4.B, pp.13–15]
Customers use Nu to pay, hold and move money, borrow, save, invest, and buy protection without using a branch; merchants and financial-product partners are also part of the payment and distribution flows. [FY2025 Form 20-F, Item 4.B, pp.14, 32–35; p.158]
*Inference, not from filings:* its economic engine is balance-sheet-led rather than a software subscription model: interest income and gains on financial instruments were 85.2% of FY2025 revenue, generated chiefly from card receivables, loans, and cash/investment assets. [FY2025 Form 20-F, Item 5, p.157]
Customer deposits support its funding needs; *inference, not from filings:* this makes deposit pricing, credit quality, and the size and mix of the credit book important alongside customer growth. [FY2025 Form 20-F, Item 5, p.157]
Nu also makes fee income when customers use its cards and through loyalty subscriptions, late fees, and partner commissions for investments, insurance, remittances, and marketplace sales. [FY2025 Form 20-F, Item 5, p.158]
At Q2 2026, management reported 139 million customers, an 83.5% activity rate, and US$17 monthly ARPAC (average revenue per active customer); these are management measures, with ARPAC defined as monthly revenue divided by average monthly active customers. [Q2 2026 earnings-call transcript, prepared remarks, pp.4–5; Q2’26 earnings deck, slide 6]

## 2. How The Company Makes Money

**Core financial income:**

`Interest and gains = (interest-bearing credit-card receivables + customer loans + cash/investment assets) × yield, plus/minus financial-instrument gains and losses`

Nu charges interest on revolving and refinanced card balances, purchased card receivables, and customer loans; it also earns interest and records gains or losses on its financial assets. [FY2025 Form 20-F, Item 5, pp.157, 169]

**Fee income:**

`Fee and commission income = card purchase volume × interchange rate (net of rewards) + subscription fees + late fees + partner and marketplace commissions`

Most fee income comes from credit and prepaid-card transactions. Mastercard-set interchange varies by merchant segment and is paid by accepting merchants, while Nu also earns the other fee streams shown in the formula. [FY2025 Form 20-F, Item 5, pp.158, 169–170]

*Inference, grounded in the disclosed revenue streams:* volume is driven by customer acquisition, active use, card purchase volume, lending balances, deposits, and adoption of more products by existing customers. [FY2025 Form 20-F, Item 5, pp.157–158, 175]
*Inference, grounded in the disclosed revenue streams:* price is driven mainly by lending yields, the market rates earned on cash and investment assets, interchange, and paid tiers; interest-rate changes can affect both earned yields and funding costs. [FY2025 Form 20-F, Item 5, pp.157–158]
*Inference, grounded in the disclosed cost structure:* margin depends on the spread after deposit and other funding costs, expected credit loss (ECL), payment-network and chargeback costs, rewards, and customer-service/operating costs. [FY2025 Form 20-F, Item 5, pp.157, 170]

## 3. Business Type Classification

*Inference, not from filings:* Latin American digital retail bank/lender, using customer deposits to support funding needs and earning card-payment and distribution fees alongside interest income. [FY2025 Form 20-F, Item 5, pp.157–158]

## 4. What Drives Variance

*Inference, grounded in the disclosed revenue and cost definitions:* revenue normally moves with customer activity, card purchase volume, credit balances, loan growth, deposits and the yield earned on assets; in FY2025 the company attributed revenue growth chiefly to card and loan interest income and to card-income growth with purchase volume. [FY2025 Form 20-F, Item 5, p.175]
*Inference, grounded in disclosed revenue and cost definitions:* interest rates and credit/product mix change both lending and float income, while interchange and paid tiers affect fees; the same rate environment can raise deposit funding costs and affect borrowers’ ability to repay. [FY2025 Form 20-F, Item 5, pp.157–158]
*Inference, grounded in the disclosed cost definitions:* margins move with funding expense, ECL, payment-network and chargeback costs, rewards, and operating cost; FX can also change the U.S.-dollar presentation, so the Q2 deck’s FX-neutral comparisons should not be confused with reported IFRS results. [FY2025 Form 20-F, Item 5, p.170; Q2 2026 earnings-call transcript, prepared remarks, p.4]

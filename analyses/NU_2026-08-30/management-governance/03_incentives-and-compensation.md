# Incentives & Compensation — NU

**Reporting currency and period:** USD; fiscal year ended December 31, 2025 (FY2025), unless stated otherwise. Nu Holdings Ltd. is Cayman-incorporated and a NYSE foreign private issuer. Cayman law does not require individual management-pay disclosure, so the filing reports directors and key-management compensation only in aggregate. [FY2025 Form 20-F, Item 6B — Compensation of Directors and Management Team, p.194]

The deterministic CIQ read reports 4,830.7m shares outstanding as of August 29, 2026, 25.0m (0.51%) below the FY2025 filing's 4,855.674m year-end denominator. This is a date-basis difference, not a material extraction conflict; the annual burn and overhang calculations above deliberately use the filing's FY2025 denominator. [CIQ Comps → Financial Data, Shares Outstanding Latest, as of 2026-08-29 — vendor export; FY2025 Form 20-F, Description of Share Capital — issued and outstanding ordinary shares, p.210]

One small within-filing reconciliation also does not change the conclusion: Item 7 says 53.104m RSUs outstanding while Note 10's audited roll-forward says 53.088m, a 15,874-share (0.03% of RSUs) difference. The calculation uses Note 10 because it provides the full grant roll-forward.

## 4. Incentive Alignment Read

**Mixed.** Nu pays through salary, a short-term profit-sharing program and mostly equity-based long-term awards, so management has share-price exposure and the employee plan is broad. But the disclosed RSU program is mainly time-based and the 20-F withholds individual pay, performance targets, weights, and outcomes; it therefore does not prove that executives are paid for per-share value rather than growth or other company goals.

The single biggest signal is that Nu's long-term incentive is retention-oriented, with a 3.8-year weighted-average vesting period, rather than a published ROIC, EPS, FCF/share, or TSR scorecard. That makes the structure better than cash-only pay but leaves the actual performance incentive mixed. [FY2025 Form 20-F, Note 10 — Share-based payments, F-45–F-47; Item 6B — Share-Based Compensation, p.195]

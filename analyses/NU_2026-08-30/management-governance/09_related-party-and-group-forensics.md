# Related-Party & Group Forensics — NU

## 1. RPT Intensity & the Applicable Regime (A5-01)

Nu is a Cayman Islands foreign private issuer listed on the NYSE and reports under IFRS. The applicable disclosure read is Form 20-F Item 7.B and IAS 24 Note 28, not the India LODR Reg. 23 slabs or the domestic-U.S. Item 404 $120,000 rule. For Nu's regulated Brazilian entities, CMN Resolution 4,693 requires related-party credit transactions to be market-compatible and without extra benefits, subject to specified exceptions; it also requires an internal related-party policy. [FY25 Form 20-F, Item 4.B — “Transactions with Related Parties”]

The filing does not disclose an annual aggregate of all related-party transaction flows. It separately reports key-management compensation and, for the unnamed director-linked company, a closing liability rather than an annual transaction amount. These are different bases and are not added together. The $0.926m closing liability is only 0.0059% of FY25 revenue, 0.0323% of FY25 net income, and 0.0012% of FY25 total assets; it is not evidence of a $0.926m annual outflow. ## 14. Read

No annual amount of value moving to a promoter or promoter-linked entity is proven from the available filings. The only quantified current non-management other-RPT item is a $0.926m closing liability tied to a conditional cash incentive received from an unnamed company led by a Nu director — 0.0323% of FY25 net income, and a balance rather than an annual cash outflow. [FY25 Form 20-F, Note 28(a), F-76; earnings/01, FY25 net income]

The main risk is disclosure granularity, not a proved leakage channel: the counterparty name, commercial terms, annual flow, director/KMP lending balances, and parent/subsidiary cash location are absent. The deepest observed group path is three layers and the disclosed-versus-vendor entity counts differ (45 subsidiaries in Item 4.C versus 34 current operating units in CIQ); this could make a small flow harder to trace, but does not itself establish hidden value transfer. The highest-value next disclosure is a named, annual, transaction-level RPT schedule covering the director-linked company and all director/KMP credit balances, rates, approvals, and repayments.

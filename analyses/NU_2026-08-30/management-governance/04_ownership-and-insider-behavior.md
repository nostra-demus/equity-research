# Ownership & Insider Behavior — NU

**Scope and basis.** NU is a Cayman Islands company whose investable primary line is Class A ordinary shares on the NYSE. Its annual ownership disclosure is the FY2025 Form 20-F, not an Indian promoter filing. Amounts below are shares unless stated otherwise. The filing's ownership table is as of December 31, 2025, with the stated share-count denominator as of March 1, 2026; Capital IQ holdings are later vendor snapshots and are not substituted for the filing.

| Consent / veto rights | Above 10% voting power, David's prior written approval is required for specified matters including major debt, related-party actions, strategy, dividends, equity issuance, M&A, change of control, assets above 20% of net equity, and officer/director compensation. | [FY2025 Form 20-F, Item 6A, Shareholder's Agreement, p.193] |
| Controlling-owner objective alignment *(Filter 6)* | NU is not evidenced to be government-controlled, a listed subsidiary of a value-maximizing parent, or a sprawling unrelated conglomerate; RF-OWN-004 does not apply. David's commercial founder stake supplies real exposure, but the dual-class and contractual rights leave Class A holders unable to block his decisions. | [FY2025 Form 20-F, Item 6A, pp.187, 193 and 201; Item 7A, p.203] |

The direct control risk is not an allegation of misconduct. It is a durable minority-rights constraint: a holder of Class A has one vote per share while David can elect a majority of the board and approve specified actions alone under the agreement. It should lower the control-structure-fairness component; it is not the separate government/parent/conglomerate conflict required for RF-OWN-004. [FY2025 Form 20-F, Item 6A, p.193; Item 7A, pp.202–204]

## 5. Ownership / Insider Read

NU has meaningful founder economic ownership — 22.80% for all insiders in the latest vendor classification — but the record does not establish how much founder stock was cash-bought rather than granted before listing. The behavioral signal is net open-market selling: four sales and no buys, while the apparent all-type net acquisition is mostly awards and an exercise. The largest minority-holder risk is David Vélez's 74.4% voting control from an 18.6% economic stake, reinforced by board-nomination and consent rights; pledging remains unassessable rather than clean.

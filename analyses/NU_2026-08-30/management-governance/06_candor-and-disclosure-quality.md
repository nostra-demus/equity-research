## 3. Disclosure Transparency

NU has kept its main customer, monthly-active-customer, activity-rate, Monthly ARPAC and monthly-cost-to-serve KPIs through the FY2021–FY2025 annual filings, with definitions and cautions stated; the FY2025 Managerial P&L was disclosed as a complementary framework that preserves IFRS net income, cash flow and capital, not as a KPI rebase. [FY2021 Form 20-F, Presentation of Financial Information — Customers and Active Customers; FY2025 Form 20-F, Item 5A, Managerial P&L]

The limitation is material: the CEO/CODM reports the group as one IFRS operating segment, so investors do not receive a country- or product-level profit-and-loss account even though the filing supplies product information and geographical revenue/assets. [FY2025 Form 20-F, Note 34, Segment information, F-97] The H1 2026 statements disclose both the US$991.0m deferred-tax benefit and negative CFO, but the reviewed Q2 prepared remarks and Q&A did not address either item; this is a presentation gap, not a contradiction with the filed numbers. [H1 2026 Unaudited Interim Condensed Consolidated Financial Statements, Statements of Income, p.5; Note 30, p.37; Q2 2026 earnings call, prepared remarks and Q&A]

## 4. Tone in Bad Times

Q1 was a Street miss but still a profitable quarter. The CEO’s language was promotional — calling it “another clean expression” of the model and saying higher provisions showed no deterioration — but the same call gave the three stated provision drivers and the CFO gave a numeric timing bridge for the unusually low efficiency ratio. [Q1 2026 earnings call, prepared remarks and Q&A]

The follow-through supports part of that explanation: Q2 operating expenses rose 20% sequentially as the delayed expenses landed, the ratio moved to 19.5%, and management restated the roughly 20% FY2026 target. [Q2 2026 earnings call, prepared remarks] In the reviewed Q2 Q&A, the CFO answered the Desenrola contribution directly (about 5% of cost of credit, a minority of the 115bp cost-of-credit contribution), while management declined to give an invented 2027 growth number; no Q&A deflection was evidenced in those exchanges. [Q2 2026 earnings call, Q&A — Goldman Sachs questions]

## 5. Candor Read

**Verdict: mixed.** Management’s clearest positive signal is its contemporaneous disclosure that two-thirds of Q1’s unusually low efficiency ratio was timing, followed by the expected Q2 reversal. [Q1 2026 earnings call, Q&A — efficiency-ratio question; Q2 2026 earnings call, prepared remarks]

The counterweight is recurring share-based-compensation exclusion from Adjusted Net Income and an earnings-call narrative that did not address the material H1 cash-flow and deferred-tax bridge; detailed IFRS notes prevent this from being an opaque filing record, but it does not support a high-trust conclusion. [FY2025 Form 20-F, Item 5A, Adjusted Net Income reconciliation; H1 2026 Unaudited Interim Condensed Consolidated Financial Statements, Statements of Income, p.5; Note 30, p.37]

The single biggest candor signal is the recurring US$359.0m FY2025 stock-compensation addback (US$203.7m net of tax/hedge effects), because it turns a normal recurring cost into “adjusted” profit despite an otherwise transparent reconciliation. [FY2025 Form 20-F, Item 5A, Adjusted Net Income reconciliation]

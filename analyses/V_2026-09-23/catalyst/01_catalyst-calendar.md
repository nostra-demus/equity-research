## 2. The Nearest Dated Catalyst

The nearest scheduled event is the **27 October 2026** FQ4/FY26 release, but its timing is only Capital IQ-derived and Visa has not confirmed it. It can help if matched-basis FQ4 revenue meets the $12.097bn vendor bar and the next outlook does not validate the temporary-demand and incentive-cost concerns; it can hurt if activity normalizes and guidance shows that those pressures persist. The specific date should not be treated as firm until issuer confirmation. [data/V/Visa Inc NYSE V Events Calendar.xls, 2026 Events Calendar, Oct-27-2026—CIQ Derived]; [analyses/V_2026-09-23/earnings/05_beat-miss-setup.md, §§1–5]

## 3. The Single Most Important Catalyst

The FQ4/FY26 release is the largest potential mover because it resolves the open full-year guide and updates the evidence behind a valuation that, conditional on a 9.04% WACC, requires roughly 19.67% five-year FCF growth. The bullish path is continued payment activity, pricing/value-added-services mix and contained incentive/marketing pressure; the bearish path is normalization of temporary cross-border activity with incentives and marketing taking more of net revenue. A buyer can plan around the vendor's 27 October estimate, but cannot precisely time it until Visa confirms the date. [analyses/V_2026-09-23/valuation/05_reverse-dcf.md, §§2–5]; [Visa Q3 FY26 Earnings Call, CFO prepared remarks and Q&A, 2026-07-28]

## 4. Negative / Bearish Catalysts

- The 27 October expected results release can skew down if the FQ4 revenue result misses the $12.097bn vendor bar and the guide confirms normalization in cross-border activity or higher incentive and marketing costs. The $11.855bn material-miss screen is 2% below the vendor bar and remains a matched-basis comparison against FQ4 FY25 revenue of $10.724bn. [analyses/V_2026-09-23/earnings/05_beat-miss-setup.md, §§1, 4–5]

- The April, August and September 2027 note maturities are scheduled funding events, not an identified solvency cliff: $1.500bn + $0.500bn + $0.750bn = $2.750bn of named principal. The bearish version would require declining liquidity or an exceptional settlement/legal cash requirement alongside continued discretionary cash returns; it is not proven by the current maturity schedule. [Visa Q3 FY26 Form 10-Q, Note 8 (Debt), pp.17–18; analyses/V_2026-09-23/balance-sheet-survival/99_balance-sheet-survival-synthesis.md, §§3A, 5]

- Two material risks do **not** qualify as calendar catalysts. The injunctive-relief class plaintiffs filed their final-approval motion on 15 July 2026, but no future hearing or decision date is disclosed. Visa also says Brazil's central-bank approval of enhanced operating rules would raise settlement exposure, but gives no decision date. These are unbounded downside risks, not timing support. [Visa Q3 FY26 Form 10-Q, Note 16—Legal Matters; MD&A—Liquidity and Capital Resources]

## 5. Calendar Read

There are four evidenced forward events: one vendor-estimated earnings date and three contractual debt-maturity months. The only potentially material operating event is not issuer-confirmed, while the three proven funding windows are currently self-funded on the reported cash balance. The key timing caveat is that the material litigation and Brazil settlement-rule risks have no disclosed decision date, so the thesis cannot treat their resolution as a scheduled event.

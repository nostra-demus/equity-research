# Balance-Sheet-Survival Module — V (Synthesis)

## Abstract

Visa’s strict net leverage—debt after cash divided by EBITDA, an operating-earnings measure—is low at 0.41x but rose from 0.32x at FY25 as cash fell faster than debt. Debt due is $3.0bn in year one and $2.678bn in year two; $12.359bn cash covers both. Liquidity is $12.359bn cash plus $1.433bn current investments; operating cash after capital spending exceeds committed uses, but credit-line availability and covenant headroom—the distance to a lender breach—are not assessable. The zero-mitigation scheduled-use bound survives a 60% EBITDA fall; unchanged buybacks and dividends create a 22.9% cash-policy bound. The verdict is Solid, not Fortress, because settlement or legal cash calls remain unquantified. [Visa Q3 FY2026 Form 10-Q, pp.4, 10, 14–19, 24–27; calculations] [Visa FY2025 Form 10-K, p.65; calculations]

## 1. Solvency Verdict

- **Verdict:** Solid
- **Reporting basis:** USD; U.S. GAAP; September fiscal year-end; latest balance-sheet date June 30, 2026. [Visa Q3 FY2026 Form 10-Q, Cover Page, Basis of Presentation and Consolidated Balance Sheets]
- **Net leverage (net debt / EBITDA):** 0.41x on the strict basis: $11.499bn net debt = $23.858bn carrying-value gross debt − $12.359bn cash, where gross debt equals $24.131bn principal − $165m discounts/issuance costs − $108m hedge-accounting adjustments. The denominator is $28.338bn LTM GAAP-derived EBITDA = $6.464bn FY25 Q4 + $21.874bn FY26 nine-month EBITDA. Gross leverage is 0.84x. Canonical debt excludes the $913m operating-lease liability measured at September 2025; no finance-lease balance is separately disclosed. Strict net leverage rose from 0.32x at FY25 even though gross leverage fell from 1.00x, because cash fell faster than debt. [Visa Q3 FY2026 Form 10-Q, Consolidated Balance Sheets and Note 8 (Debt), pp.4, 17–18; Visa FY2025 Form 10-K, pp.60, 65 and Note 9 (Leases), p.82; calculation]
- **Liquidity runway:** No finite runway on the committed-use base because $21.013bn of LTM free cash flow = $22.580bn cash from operations − $1.567bn total capital spending, exceeding the $3.0bn next-12-month maturity plus the roughly $1.273bn declared-dividend proxy. Usable liquidity is $13.792bn = $12.359bn cash + $1.433bn current investment securities; the $7.0bn revolving credit line (revolver) is excluded because current availability is unknown. The cash-return sensitivity instead has $8.623bn annual burn = $3.0bn maturities + $4.998bn ordinary dividends + $21.638bn repurchases − $21.013bn free cash flow, giving a 19.2-month runway. [Visa FY2025 Form 10-K, Consolidated Statements of Cash Flows, p.65; Visa Q3 FY2026 Form 10-Q, pp.4, 10, 14–18; calculation]
- **Maturity wall (% within 24 months):** 23.5%, or $5.678bn = $3.0bn due in the first 12 months + $2.678bn in year two. Total principal is $24.131bn = $22.631bn senior notes + $1.5bn commercial paper; 12.4% is due within 12 months. Cash alone covers the 24-month wall 2.18x. Principal-weighted average maturity is 9.3 years, with commercial paper assumed to mature in six months solely for that calculation; the largest single maturity year is 2045 at $3.5bn, or 14.5% of principal. [Visa Q3 FY2026 Form 10-Q, Note 8 (Debt), pp.17–18; calculation]
- **Tightest covenant + headroom:** Not assessable. Visa disclosed compliance but not the maintenance thresholds, lender-defined EBITDA, addback caps, springing trigger or cure rights. The 89.9% headroom to an assumed 4.0x maximum leverage test is illustrative only and is not a lender test. [Visa Q3 FY2026 Form 10-Q, Note 8 (Debt), p.18; Visa FY2025 Form 10-K, Note 10 and Exhibit Index, p.84]
- **Stress break point (EBITDA decline that breaks it):** The zero-mitigation scheduled-debt and declared-dividend bound does not break from an EBITDA decline within the 0–100% range; its liquidity solve is 135.2% = ($13.792bn starting liquidity + $21.013bn free cash flow − $4.273bn uses) ÷ (79.7% cash conversion × $28.338bn EBITDA), where $4.273bn uses = $3.0bn maturities + roughly $1.273bn declared dividend. The first quantified failure is a voluntary cash-policy bound at a 22.9% EBITDA decline if $29.636bn of uses continue: $3.0bn maturities + $4.998bn ordinary dividends + $21.638bn repurchases. Contractual covenant failure remains not assessable. [Visa FY2025 Form 10-K, p.65; Visa Q3 FY2026 Form 10-Q, pp.4, 10, 14–18; calculation]
- **Solvency strength /100:** 78 — very low gross and strict net leverage and cash-funded near-term maturities support the score; undisclosed covenant terms and exceptional contingent cash calls prevent a very-strong reading. [Visa Q3 FY2026 Form 10-Q, Notes 8, 9 and 16, pp.17–19, 24–27]
- **Liquidity runway /100:** 60 — the hard cap applies because the $7.0bn revolver’s current availability is unknown, even though cash, liquid securities and LTM free cash flow cover the stated uses. [Visa FY2025 Form 10-K, Note 10, p.84; Visa Q3 FY2026 Form 10-Q, pp.4, 10, 14–18]
- **Refinancing risk /100 (higher = worse; inverted):** 18 — the $3.0bn first-year wall and the additional $2.678bn due in year two are covered by cash, although a like-for-like USD refinancing proxy implied a roughly 207-basis-point, or 2.07-percentage-point, cost increase. [Visa Q3 FY2026 Form 10-Q, Note 8, pp.17–18; Web: finanzen.net, Visa 4.700% notes due 2036, quote dated 2026-09-10 — indicative, unverified; calculation]
- **Covenant headroom /100:** Not assessable — numerical terms and covenant definitions are absent. [Visa Q3 FY2026 Form 10-Q, Note 8, p.18; Visa FY2025 Form 10-K, Note 10 and Exhibit Index, p.84]
- **Downside resilience /100:** 80 — the zero-mitigation scheduled-use bounds survive every modeled EBITDA haircut through 60%, but the cash-return-continuation bound and unquantified settlement/legal calls limit the score. [Visa Q3 FY2026 Form 10-Q, Notes 4, 8, 9 and 16, pp.14, 17–19, 24–27; calculation]
- **Data quality /100:** 72 — recent primary statements, debt details, maturities, cash flow and contingencies are available, but facility availability, covenant terms, a current rating-agency report and a complete acceleration-trigger scan are missing. [Visa Q3 FY2026 Form 10-Q, Notes 7–9, 14 and 16; Visa FY2025 Form 10-K, Note 10 and Exhibit Index]

- **Biggest solvency risk:** *Inference, not from filings:* a client-settlement failure or legal cash call could consume liquidity on a scale the model cannot quantify. The disclosed $168.6bn is a historical maximum daily gross unsettled-payment exposure, not debt or expected loss; Visa held $9.5bn of collateral and did not estimate the future guarantee obligation or litigation-loss range. [Visa Q3 FY2026 Form 10-Q, Notes 9 and 16, pp.19, 24–27]

## 1A. Module Disconfirmation

- **Strongest bear point:** The $168.6bn historical maximum daily settlement exposure and active litigation have no disclosed loss range, so the modeled debt-service cushion does not prove protection from an exceptional cash call. The $9.5bn collateral figure is a risk mitigant, not a matched loss-coverage ratio. [Visa Q3 FY2026 Form 10-Q, Notes 9 and 16, pp.19, 24–27]
- **Strongest bull point:** In the zero-mitigation 60% EBITDA-fall bound, strict net leverage is only 1.01x, EBITDA covers gross interest 14.61x, and the 12-month scheduled-use test remains cash-positive without new unsecured debt or a revolver draw. [Visa Q3 FY2026 Form 10-Q, pp.4, 10, 14–18; calculation]
- **Single killer risk:** *Inference, not from filings:* an exceptional client-settlement failure that converts part of the guarantee into a cash demand could exhaust available funds faster than the scheduled-debt model shows. The future obligation is not determinable from the filing. [Visa Q3 FY2026 Form 10-Q, Note 9, p.19]
- **Disconfirming evidence already visible:** Strict net debt rose from $8.007bn at FY25 ($25.171bn debt − $17.164bn cash) to $11.499bn at June 2026 ($23.858bn debt − $12.359bn cash), and maintaining the latest repurchase and dividend pace makes liquidity fail at a 22.9% EBITDA decline in the zero-mitigation cash-policy bound. [Visa Q3 FY2026 Form 10-Q, Consolidated Balance Sheets and Cash Flows, pp.4, 10; Visa FY2025 Form 10-K, p.65; calculation]

## 5. Survival Summary

Visa carries $23.858bn of carrying-value gross debt = $24.131bn principal − $165m discounts/issuance costs − $108m hedge-accounting adjustments, equal to 0.84x LTM GAAP-derived EBITDA, and $11.499bn of strict net debt = $23.858bn debt − $12.359bn cash, equal to 0.41x; gross leverage improved from 1.00x at FY25, but strict net leverage worsened from 0.32x because cash fell faster than debt. The canonical debt figures exclude a $913m September 2025 operating-lease liability. [Visa Q3 FY2026 Form 10-Q, pp.4, 17–18; Visa FY2025 Form 10-K, pp.60, 65 and Note 9 (Leases), p.82; calculation] The $5.678bn 24-month principal wall = $3.0bn first-year maturities + $2.678bn second-year maturities and is covered by cash alone, so it is not refinancing-dependent on the current numbers; replacing USD debt at the cited market proxy would cost roughly 207 basis points, or 2.07 percentage points, more. [Visa Q3 FY2026 Form 10-Q, pp.4, 17–18; Web: finanzen.net, Visa 4.700% notes due 2036, quote dated 2026-09-10 — indicative, unverified; calculation] Usable liquidity is $13.792bn = $12.359bn cash + $1.433bn current investments, and free cash flow exceeds scheduled debt plus the declared dividend, but the revolver is excluded and the tightest contractual covenant is Not assessable. [Visa FY2025 Form 10-K, p.84; Visa Q3 FY2026 Form 10-Q, pp.4, 10, 14–18] In the zero-mitigation 60% EBITDA-fall bound, strict net leverage reaches 1.01x, interest coverage remains 14.61x and the $16.981bn 12-month surplus = $13.792bn starting liquidity + $7.462bn stressed free cash flow − $4.273bn uses ($3.0bn debt maturities + roughly $1.273bn declared dividend); no new debt, asset sale, equity or waiver is required. [Visa Q3 FY2026 Form 10-Q, pp.4, 10, 14–18; calculation] The module’s zero-mitigation 30% and 40% recession bounds are therefore survivable if discretionary repurchases can stop; keeping the latest repurchase and dividend pace instead exhausts liquidity at a 22.9% EBITDA decline in the cash-policy bound, while an unquantified settlement or legal call could overturn every modeled case. [Visa FY2025 Form 10-K, p.65; Visa Q3 FY2026 Form 10-Q, pp.10, 19, 24–27; calculation] These are survival bounds, not forecasts: no price, cost, hedge, capital-spending or payout mitigation is credited, and the earnings realized-offset case was unavailable.

## 6A. Survival Playbook (non-speculative levers)

- **Refinancing already completed:** Visa repaid $5.565bn of senior notes in the first nine months of FY2026 and issued $2.995bn of fixed-rate notes in February 2026. This proves past access, not a future commitment. [Visa Q3 FY2026 Form 10-Q, Consolidated Statements of Cash Flows and Note 8, pp.10, 18]
- **Discretionary repurchases can be paused:** The $28.4bn authorization has multi-year flexibility and no expiry. Stopping repurchases removes the modeled 22.9% cash-policy bound without requiring equity, an asset sale or a waiver. [Visa Q3 FY2026 Form 10-Q, Note 11, pp.22–23; calculation]
- **No other evidenced lever:** No announced asset-sale program, proven capex-cut plan or prior covenant-amendment pattern appears in the specialist outputs, so none is credited.

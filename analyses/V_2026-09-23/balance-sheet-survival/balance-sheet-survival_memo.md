## What This Module Found

Visa had $23.858bn of carrying-value gross debt and $11.499bn of strict net debt at June 30, 2026. Strict net leverage—debt after cash divided by EBITDA, an operating-earnings measure—was 0.41x, while gross leverage was 0.84x on $28.338bn of LTM GAAP-derived EBITDA. Gross leverage improved from FY25, but strict net leverage rose from 0.32x because cash fell faster than debt. [Visa Q3 FY2026 Form 10-Q, pp.4, 17–18; Visa FY2025 Form 10-K, pp.60, 65; calculation]

The $5.678bn due within 24 months, made up of $3.0bn in the first year and $2.678bn in the second, is covered 2.18x by $12.359bn of cash. Usable liquidity is $13.792bn = $12.359bn cash + $1.433bn current investments, and $21.013bn of LTM free cash flow—cash from operations less total capital spending—exceeds committed uses. [Visa FY2025 Form 10-K, p.65; Visa Q3 FY2026 Form 10-Q, pp.4, 10, 14–18; calculation]

The scheduled-use stress bound remains cash-positive even after a 60% EBITDA fall: strict net leverage reaches 1.01x and interest coverage is 14.61x. This is a zero-mitigation survival bound, not a forecast; it gives no credit for a management response or an earnings realized-offset case. [Visa Q3 FY2026 Form 10-Q, pp.4, 10, 14–18; calculation]

The most important weakness is not the stated debt wall. *Inference, not from filings:* a client-settlement failure or legal cash call could consume liquidity faster than the debt model shows; the $168.6bn historical maximum daily gross unsettled-payment exposure is not debt or an expected loss, and Visa did not estimate the future guarantee obligation or litigation-loss range. [Visa Q3 FY2026 Form 10-Q, Notes 9 and 16, pp.19, 24–27]

## Bottom Line

- **Solid:** Visa’s low leverage, cash-funded near-term maturities and free-cash-flow surplus support the solvency verdict.
- It could be better than it looks if the missing facility certificate shows current revolver availability and wide contractual covenant headroom.
- It could be worse if strict net debt keeps rising, repurchases and dividends continue through an earnings fall, or a settlement or legal cash call draws on liquidity.
- Contractual covenant headroom, current revolver availability, a current rating-agency report and a complete acceleration-trigger scan are missing.
- Watch the latest revolving-credit-facility compliance certificate or lender availability report next.

## Plain-English Glossary

- **EBITDA:** an operating-earnings measure, before interest, tax, depreciation and amortization.
- **Strict net leverage:** debt after cash divided by EBITDA; it shows debt relative to operating earnings.
- **Free cash flow:** cash from operations less total capital spending.
- **Covenant headroom:** the distance to a lender breach.
- **Revolver:** a revolving credit line that can be borrowed, repaid and borrowed again up to its available limit.

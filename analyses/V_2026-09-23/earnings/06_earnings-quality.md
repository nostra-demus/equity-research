# Earnings Quality — V

Basis: Visa Inc. (NYSE: V) reports in USD under U.S. GAAP and has a September fiscal year-end. Amounts are USD millions unless stated otherwise. EBITDA is **GAAP-derived** operating income plus depreciation and amortization because Visa does not report EBITDA. Free cash flow (FCF) is cash from operations (CFO) less total capex—the cash left after property, equipment and technology spending.

## 1. EBITDA → CFO → FCF Bridge (3–5 years)

The FY23–FY25 bridge uses audited figures. The broad working-capital row is the sum of every operating asset/liability cash-flow movement disclosed in the statement (settlement receivable, accounts receivable, client incentives, other assets, accounts payable, settlement payable, accrued and other liabilities, and accrued litigation); it is deliberately broader than the conventional receivables/inventory/payables cycle.

Source for the bridge: [Visa FY25 Form 10-K, Consolidated Statements of Operations, p.60; Consolidated Statements of Cash Flows, p.65]. EBITDA is calculated as reported operating income plus reported depreciation and amortization: FY25 $23,994 + $1,220 = $25,214. FCF is CFO less purchases of property, equipment and technology: FY25 $23,059 − $1,482 = $21,577.

*The residual is mechanical, not a separately disclosed operating driver: `CFO − EBITDA − broad working-capital change + cash tax paid + interest paid`. It includes the remaining non-cash and timing items, such as client-incentive amortization, share-based compensation, equity-investment movements and accrual-versus-cash differences. For FY25, `$23,059 − $25,214 − (−$15,172) + $4,541 + $587 = $8,145`.

*Capex split not disclosed — total capex used. FCF may understate true recurring free cash flow.*

No FCF normalisation is imposed. FY25 cash tax paid included $1.9bn for federal transferable tax credits, but the filing does not establish that payment as non-recurring; calling it a one-off would be inference, not from filings. Reported FY25 FCF is therefore the lead measure. [Visa FY25 Form 10-K, p.65]

## 2. Cash Conversion Assessment

Cash conversion was healthy over FY23–FY25: CFO covered 81.0%–94.6% of GAAP-derived EBITDA, comfortably above the 70% healthy reference point. The latest read is weaker: nine-month FY26 CFO was $16,342 on $21,874 of GAAP-derived EBITDA, or 74.7%, versus 89.7% in the comparable FY25 period; it remains above 70% but is a meaningful deterioration. [Visa Q3 FY26 Form 10-Q, Consolidated Statements of Operations, p.4; Consolidated Statements of Cash Flows, p.10]

The frozen facts sidecar reports LTM CFO of $22,580m, which matches the vendor workbook's authoritative read. Its LTM EBITDA is $31,094m, $2,756m above the $28,338m GAAP-derived LTM EBITDA. Inference, not from filings: the vendor series excludes certain special operating costs; it is therefore not mixed into the cash-conversion ratio. The primary-filing LTM cash-conversion calculation is $22,580 / $28,338 = 79.7%. [CIQ Financials→Cash Flow “Cash from Ops.”, LTM Jun-30-2026 — vendor basis; CIQ Financials→Income Statement “EBITDA”, LTM Jun-30-2026 — vendor basis; Visa FY25 Form 10-K, p.65; Visa Q3 FY26 Form 10-Q, p.10]

CFO/EBITDA was not below 50% in any of FY23–FY25; no cash-conversion-breakdown tag is triggered.

Settlement balances generally remain outstanding only one to two business days and are not a normal credit-sales collection balance. Calculating DIO or DPO against revenue or a mismatched expense line would be misleading, so the cash conversion cycle is not assessable. The broad cash-flow bridge nevertheless shows large client-incentive and settlement/liability movements. [Visa FY25 Form 10-K, p.65]

## 9. Earnings Quality Score

**73 / 100 — Mostly clean but some working-capital or adjustment noise.** The main support is three years of 81%–95% annual CFO/GAAP-derived EBITDA and FY25 FCF of $21.577bn. The deduction is for the latest cash-conversion decline, FY25 receivables growing faster than revenue, and a non-GAAP series that excludes litigation every year, including a $2.533bn FY25 provision. [Visa FY25 Form 10-K, pp.42, 60, 65; Visa Q3 FY26 Form 10-Q, p.10]

## 10. The Single Biggest Quality Concern

The key concern is not a lack of cash generation; it is the risk that recurring litigation exclusions and operating-balance movements make adjusted earnings look steadier than cash results. In the nine months to June 2026, CFO fell $479m year on year ($16,342m versus $16,821m) while net income rose $2,534m. The arithmetic is visible in the cash-flow statement: operating asset/liability movements worsened by $3,621m, partly offset by a $3,142m improvement in net income and other non-cash items (`−$3,621m + $3,142m = −$479m`). The largest adverse changes included accrued litigation (`−$1,758m` versus `+$622m`) and settlement payables (`−$1,436m` versus `+$619m`). That does not prove earnings are overstated, but it means the next reports must show whether cash conversion returns toward the FY23–FY25 range rather than relying on adjusted profit alone. [Visa Q3 FY26 Form 10-Q, Consolidated Statements of Cash Flows, p.10]

Visa is expected to release FQ4 FY2026 results for the standalone three months ending September 30, 2026, alongside FY2026 results, on October 27, 2026; the later Form 10-K is annual rather than a standalone-quarter filing. [Capital IQ Estimates → Consensus, FQ4 FY2026 header] **Consensus restated onto filing basis: No restatement is needed for FQ4: the direct standalone bar is $12,096.64m revenue and $3.42986 normalized EPS. The direct FY2026 bar remains $45,743.58m and $13.21574 rather than a constructed sum.** [Capital IQ Estimates → Consensus, FQ4 FY2026 / FY2026]

FQ4 is Visa's seasonally largest quarter, at a 26.6% average share of annual revenue across FY23–FY25, although seasonality is modest rather than decisive. **Required period-basis adjudication:** the 18.9% growth figure in `04_guidance-consensus` divides the FQ4 consensus by $10.172bn, but that is Q3 FY25 revenue in the historical quarterly table; reported FQ4 FY25 revenue is $10.724bn. On the matched FQ4/FQ4 basis, the same $12,096.64m consensus bar implies **12.8%** growth: `($12,096.64m / $10,724m) − 1`. The consensus dollar bar is unchanged, but 12.8% is within management's qualitative low-double-digit to low-teens GAAP nominal outlook rather than above it. ## 6. Seasonality Read

Seasonality helps the absolute FQ4 revenue level but is not a separate reason to expect an earnings surprise. FQ4 represented 26.4%, 26.8% and 26.8% of FY23–FY25 revenue, respectively, a 26.6% average, versus 25.0% for Q3; the FQ4 consensus share of direct FY2026 consensus is 26.4%. That fit supports the quarter's usual weight in the year, while the matched-basis 12.8% growth bar is within management's low-double-digit to low-teens GAAP outlook; what matters is whether the temporary Q3 demand and cost effects persist or reverse. [Capital IQ Financials Quarterly workbook, Visa, FY23–FY25 fiscal quarters — vendor export; Capital IQ Estimates → Consensus, FQ4 FY2026 / FY2026; Visa Q3 FY26 Financial Results Presentation, p.20]

## 7. Historical Pattern

Visa beat revenue and normalized EPS in each of the last four reported quarters, with revenue surprises of +1.0%, +2.0%, +4.5% and +2.0% and normalized-EPS surprises of +0.3%, +1.0%, +6.8% and +2.8%. This is a useful counterweight to temporary-demand and cost risks, but it is not a statistical prediction: the sample is four quarters, and GAAP EPS beat only once because the outcome changes with the earnings definition. The synthesizer should give the pattern modest weight and refresh the 43-day-old consensus before using it to draw a trading conclusion. [Capital IQ Estimates → Surprise and Recent Changes, FQ4 FY2025–FQ3 FY2026; 2026-08-10 to 2026-08-11]

## 8. Setup Verdict

**Setup is balanced.** The single most important factor is the matched-period revenue bar: $12.097bn implies 12.8% FQ4 growth, broadly consistent with management's low-double-digit to low-teens GAAP outlook rather than the 18.9% implied by the wrong Q3 FY25 denominator. The biggest risk that could flip the setup toward a miss is the normalization of promotional/FIFA-linked cross-border activity together with faster client incentives and deferred marketing expense; the main upside counterweight is the four-quarter revenue and normalized-EPS beat record. [Visa Q3 FY26 Financial Results Presentation, p.20; Capital IQ Estimates → Consensus and Surprise, FQ4 FY2026 / FQ4 FY2025–FQ3 FY2026; Capital IQ Financials Quarterly workbook, Visa, Q4 FY25 — vendor export; Visa Q3 FY26 earnings call, CFO prepared remarks and Q&A, 2026-07-28]

## 9. Second-Quarter Look-Ahead

The following reporting period is FQ1 FY2027, but the admitted pool contains no FQ1 FY2027 consensus bar or company guidance, so the setup is not assessable with the same precision. The direction could differ because the Q3 $563m severance and FIFA-related campaign effects are not normal ongoing items, while the Q4 renewal/incentive pressure and normalization of temporary cross-border activity could continue; their timing and net effect are not proven from available data. [Visa Q3 FY26 Form 10-Q, pp.34–35; Visa Q3 FY26 earnings call, CFO prepared remarks and Q&A, 2026-07-28]

## 10. Pre-Mortem

If this setup fails, the most likely error is bad period matching: using Q3 FY25 revenue as the FQ4 comparator would manufacture an 18.9% hurdle instead of the matched-basis 12.8% hurdle. A second risk is stale-data and causal-inference error—the August 10–11 consensus export may have changed before the result, while the filing does not quantify how much June/July promotional and event activity contributed; the falsifier is an updated consensus plus reported FQ4 payment-volume, processed-transaction, cross-border, incentive and guidance data.

## What the Company Does

Visa runs a global payment network. It connects cardholders, banks, merchants, and payment processors. It authorizes, clears, and settles transactions, but it does not lend money to cardholders. That means its core economics come from payment activity and network services rather than consumer interest income or consumer credit losses. [data/V/Visa-Fiscal-2025-Annual-Report.pdf, pp.8–13]

The revenue model in simple form is:

**Service and network fees + data-processing fees + cross-border fees + value-added-service fees − client incentives = net revenue.**

Client incentives reduce reported net revenue, so their growth matters. In Q3 FY2026, payment volume and processed transactions each grew 10% on the stated comparable basis. Service revenue grew 14%, data-processing revenue grew 17%, and value-added-services revenue grew 34% in constant currency. Client incentives, however, grew 18% while net revenue grew 14%. [data/V/Visa_Inc_-_Form_10-Q(Jul-29-2026).doc, pp.31–35; data/V/Visa Inc., Q3 2026 Earnings Call, Jul 28, 2026.rtf, prepared remarks]

Visa reported one Payment Services segment with FY2025 revenue of $40.000bn, of which 61% was generated internationally. Its network advantage is assessed as strong and stable, not proven to be widening. [`analyses/V_2026-09-23/business-model/99_business-model-synthesis.md`, §§1–3]

At $364.15, a reverse-DCF—working backward from the share price to see what the market assumes—requires roughly 19.67% annual free-cash-flow growth for five years when the weighted average cost of capital, or WACC, is 9.04%. WACC is the estimated return demanded by the providers of debt and equity. The same modeled cash path can be reconciled to the price using a lower 7.15% implied WACC. That conflict makes the market hurdle demanding, but it does not prove the market is wrong. [`analyses/V_2026-09-23/valuation/05_reverse-dcf.md`, §§2–5]

### What the engine thinks the market may be missing

The possible edge is an earnings-quality gap. Q3 client incentives grew 18% against 14% net-revenue growth. GAAP-derived EBITDA margin—operating cash-like profit before interest, tax, depreciation, and amortization, divided by revenue—fell 157 basis points to 62.3%. One basis point is one-hundredth of a percentage point. Over the latest trailing 12 months, cash from operations, or CFO, fell 3.9% while revenue rose 14.4%. 1. **The network can turn activity growth into faster fee growth.** In Q3, service revenue grew 14% on 11% nominal payment-volume growth; data-processing revenue grew 17% on 10% processed-transaction growth; and value-added-services revenue grew 34% in constant currency. Scale, acceptance, pricing, mix, and services could keep revenue growing faster than basic transaction counts. Confirmation would be FQ4 revenue of at least $12.339bn, margin of at least 60.3%, and better cash conversion. [data/V/Visa_Inc_-_Form_10-Q(Jul-29-2026).doc, p.33; data/V/Visa Inc., Q3 2026 Earnings Call, Jul 28, 2026.rtf, prepared remarks]

2. **Recent margin pressure may not be the continuing run-rate.** Q3 included $563m of severance and marketing timing shifted some spending into FQ4. A lower litigation provision also affected the comparison. The expense bridge fully reconciles the 161-basis-point fall in EBIT margin, where EBIT means profit before interest and tax, but the filing does not reveal the exact causal split among price, volume, incentives, and mix. The bull case needs the next two matched quarters to show revenue growing faster than recurring expense and incentive growth after named one-offs are removed. [data/V/Visa_Inc_-_Form_10-Q(Jul-29-2026).doc, pp.4, 34–35; `analyses/V_2026-09-23/earnings/03_margin-drivers.md`, §§3, 5]

## Plain-English Glossary

- **Expected return** — the probability-weighted gain or loss the scenario model estimates over the stated 12-month period.
- **Downside risk** — the estimated loss from the current price to the bear-case value.
- **Risk/reward** — expected return divided by the estimated downside; a negative figure means the modeled reward is negative.
- **Conviction** — how strongly the evidence supports taking the stated investment stance; here it is used for position sizing.
- **Data sufficiency** — how complete and decision-useful the admitted evidence is.
- **Rating cap** — a ceiling that prevents a more positive rating even if other evidence looks favorable.
- **Client incentives** — amounts deducted when Visa calculates net revenue; rising incentives can reduce how much gross activity reaches reported revenue.
- **Net revenue** — revenue after client incentives and similar reductions.
- **Normalized EPS** — vendor-adjusted earnings per share; it is not proven identical to Visa's non-GAAP or GAAP EPS.
- **Free cash flow** — cash from operations less total capital expenditure.
- **Reverse-DCF** — a model that starts with the share price and works backward to the growth or discount-rate assumptions needed to justify it.
- **WACC** — weighted average cost of capital, or the estimated return required by the providers of debt and equity.
- **GAAP** — the formal U.S. accounting rules used in filed financial statements.
- **EBITDA** — earnings before interest, tax, depreciation, and amortization; a rough operating-profit measure before those items.
- **Basis point** — one-hundredth of one percentage point.
- **CFO** — cash from operations, the cash generated by normal business activity before investing and financing flows.
- **EBIT** — earnings before interest and tax, often called operating profit.
- **Moat** — a durable advantage that helps protect a company's profits from competitors.
- **Strict net debt** — total debt minus cash equivalents, without also subtracting investments.
- **Net leverage** — net debt divided by EBITDA; it shows debt relative to the business's operating earnings capacity.
- **Liquidity** — cash and other readily usable funding available to meet obligations.
- **Covenant headroom** — the distance between current financial measures and limits set in debt agreements.
- **Margin of safety** — the gap between estimated fair value and the market price; a negative value means the market price is higher.
- **Fair value** — the model's estimate of what a share could reasonably be worth under stated assumptions.
- **EV/EBITDA** — enterprise value, meaning the value of the operating business, divided by EBITDA.
- **Valuation multiple** — the amount investors pay for each unit of an operating or earnings measure.
- **Consensus** — the collected forecasts of outside analysts as supplied by the data vendor.
- **Diluted shares** — the share count after including potential shares from options and other instruments.
- **Capital expenditure** — cash spent on long-lived assets and systems; often shortened to capex.

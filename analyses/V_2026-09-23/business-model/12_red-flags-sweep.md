## 3. Most Severe New Flag

Tax-rate normalisation and incentive-expiry exposure is the most material incremental flag. The $351m FY26 benefit is explicitly one-time and non-cash, while the $453m FY25 Singapore incentive is conditional and ends under the disclosed terms in September 2028. Neither fact establishes that Visa will lose the Singapore incentive, but both mean reported tax rates and after-tax earnings should not be extended mechanically. [FY25 Form 10-K, Note 19—Income Taxes, p.98; Visa Q3 FY26 Earnings Release, 2026-07-28, pp.10–11]

## 4. Cross-Cutting Patterns

Near-term GAAP earnings contain disclosed items moving in opposite directions: the $351m deferred-tax benefit lifts nine-month net income, while $563m of severance and $1.255bn of litigation provision reduce it. They are separately disclosed and should be retained as separate normalisation items rather than netted into a single view of operating momentum. This is not an aggressive-accounting allegation: the company identifies the tax benefit as one-time and the severance cost as non-representative in its official reconciliation. [Visa Q3 FY26 Earnings Release, 2026-07-28, pp.10–11]

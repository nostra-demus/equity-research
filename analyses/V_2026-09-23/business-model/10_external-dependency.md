## 3. Classification

**Partly externally driven.** Visa Inc. (NYSE: V) reports under U.S. GAAP in USD. Its transaction-based revenue model gives it material exposure to household and business spending, cross-border travel and commerce, foreign exchange and government rules. The June 2026 quarter shows the upside of those factors: nominal payments volume grew 11% for the quarter and 10% for the nine months, while exchange rates added about one percentage point to reported net-revenue growth. Management retains real levers in pricing, client incentives and FX hedging, which keeps this below “Mostly externally driven”; those levers do not remove the high exposure to regulatory and demand conditions. [Q3 FY26 Form 10-Q, MD&A, p.32; FY25 Form 10-K, Item 7A]

## 4. External Dependency Risk Score

**54 / 100 — INVERTED: higher = worse.** Consumer spending and regulation are high dependencies; FX, rates, geopolitics and business-cycle conditions are material but partly mitigated. The score is below the “Mostly externally driven” band because Visa can price, manage client incentives and hedge transactional FX exposure, while its disclosed direct interest-rate sensitivity is immaterial. [FY25 Form 10-K, Item 1A, pp.23–25, 30; Item 7A]

## 5. The Single Biggest Lever

**Global consumer spending, especially travel-linked cross-border commerce:** a 20% adverse move would do the most damage because payments volume drives service revenue and Visa identifies consumer spending and global macro conditions as volume determinants; no filing quantifies the resulting revenue or earnings change. [Q3 FY26 Form 10-Q, MD&A, p.32; FY25 Form 10-K, Item 1A, p.30]

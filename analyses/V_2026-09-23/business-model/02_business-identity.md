# Business Identity — V

## 1. What The Company Actually Does

Visa is a U.S. GAAP company reporting in U.S. dollars that runs a global payments technology network. [FY25 Form 10-K, Cover; Item 8] In its four-party model, the network authorizes, clears and settles a payment between the person or business paying, the card issuer, the merchant’s acquirer and the seller; Visa sends the authorization request between acquirer and issuer rather than funding the purchase itself. [FY25 Form 10-K, pp.4–5] Its direct customers are primarily issuing and acquiring financial institutions, while sellers, consumers, payment processors, fintechs and governments participate in the wider network. [FY25 Form 10-K, Note 1, p.69] These customers buy network access and related processing, issuer, acceptance, risk-and-security, and advisory services so they can issue payment credentials, accept payments and move money without building a global network themselves. [FY25 Form 10-K, p.4; Note 1, p.69] Visa does not issue cards, lend money, set cardholder rates or earn interchange reimbursement fees or merchant discount rates. [FY25 Form 10-K, p.6] In FY25, Visa processed 258 billion of 329 billion Visa-branded payments and cash transactions; it had nearly 5 billion payment credentials available at more than 175 million merchant locations in over 200 countries and territories. [FY25 Form 10-K, p.4] For financial reporting, Visa has one reportable segment: Payment Services. [FY25 Form 10-K, Note 14, p.87]

## 2. How The Company Makes Money

`Net revenue = (payments volume × service-fee rate) + (processed transactions × data-processing/network-access fee) + (cross-border volume × cross-border processing and currency-conversion fee) + (value-added-service, licence, certification and account-holder fees) − client incentives.` Service revenue is mainly calculated by applying current-quarter pricing to prior-quarter payments volume; the other activity-based revenue lines are recognized in the current period. [FY25 Form 10-K, Note 1, p.69]

These are Visa’s network and service fees, not the interchange reimbursement fees paid by acquirers to issuers or the merchant discount rate charged by acquirers to sellers. [FY25 Form 10-K, p.6] Usage drives the first three terms: in Q3 FY26, payments volume grew 10%, processed transactions grew 10% to 71.7 billion, and cross-border volume excluding intra-Europe grew 12%, all on a constant-dollar basis. [Visa Q3 FY26 Earnings Release, 2026-07-28, p.2] Price and mix matter because cross-border revenue includes currency conversion, while value-added services can carry fixed or transaction-based fees. [FY25 Form 10-K, Note 1, p.69] Operating margin—the portion of revenue left after operating costs—also depends on client incentives and costs: in Q3 FY26, client incentives rose 18% and GAAP operating expenses rose 19%, versus net-revenue growth of 14%. [Visa Q3 FY26 Earnings Release, 2026-07-28, pp.2, 4]

## 3. Business Type Classification

Global payments-network and transaction processor: payments volume, transaction count and cross-border commerce drive fees, while value-added services add fixed and transaction-based revenue. [FY25 Form 10-K, Note 1, p.69]

## 3a. Sector Overlay & Required-KPI Checklist

No sector overlay for global payments-network and transaction processor — generic read. The generic usage read has payment volume, processed transactions and cross-border volume present in the pool; a single reported network take-rate KPI is not proven from available data. [Visa Q3 FY26 Earnings Release, 2026-07-28, p.2; FY25 Form 10-K, Note 1, p.69]

## 4. What Drives Variance

Revenue moves first with payments volume and transaction count, while cross-border activity adds a separate fee stream that includes currency conversion. [FY25 Form 10-K, Note 1, p.69] Timing matters: Q3 FY26 service revenue used the payments volume of the quarter ended March 31, 2026, whereas the other revenue categories reflected current-quarter activity. [Visa Q3 FY26 Earnings Release, 2026-07-28, p.2] In that quarter, service revenue grew 14%, data-processing revenue 17%, international-transaction revenue 6%, other revenue 45% and client incentives 18%; this shows that revenue mix and client-contract economics can move differently from aggregate payment volume. [Visa Q3 FY26 Earnings Release, 2026-07-28, pp.2, 4]

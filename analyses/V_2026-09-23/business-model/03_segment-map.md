## 2. Dominant Segment

Payment Services is unambiguously dominant: it is Visa’s only reportable segment and represented all $40.0bn of FY25 net revenue. The CEO assesses performance and allocates resources using consolidated net income, so the filing provides no separate profit pool beneath Payment Services. [FY25 Form 10-K, Note 14, p. 87] Visa is therefore effectively a single-segment business; U.S./international geography and service/data-processing/international-transaction/other revenue are views of that one business, not substitute segments. [FY25 Form 10-K, Item 1, pp. 5–6; Note 14, pp. 87–88]

## 3. Segment Disclosure Quality

Disclosure is clear at the reportable-segment level but shallow below it. FY25 reports one Payment Services segment, and the Q3 FY26 Form 10-Q repeats the same definition, so no recent definition change is disclosed. [FY25 Form 10-K, Note 14, p. 87; Q3 FY26 Form 10-Q, Note 10, p. 19] The CIQ annual segment export likewise shows a single Payment Services row and total row for FY23–FY25; this is a vendor cross-check, not evidence that supersedes the filings. [CIQ Financials→Segments, FY23–FY25 — vendor export] There is no reportable “Other” or Corporate segment. “Other revenue” was $4.053bn in FY25, but it is a revenue category within Payment Services, not a segment. [FY25 Form 10-K, Item 7, p. 45; Item 1, pp. 5–6] The CEO receives consolidated net income and does not use segment asset information; Visa therefore does not disclose product-, revenue-category- or geography-level profit and capital allocation. [FY25 Form 10-K, Note 14, p. 87] That limits any attempt to rank the economics of individual revenue categories.

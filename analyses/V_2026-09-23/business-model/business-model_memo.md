## What This Module Found

Visa runs a global payments network: it authorizes, clears and settles payments but does not lend, issue cards, set cardholder rates or earn interchange reimbursement fees. [FY25 Form 10-K, pp.4–6] Payment Services is its only reportable segment and generated all $40.0bn of FY25 net revenue; volume, processed transactions and cross-border commerce drive fees, while value-added services add fixed and transaction-based revenue. [FY25 Form 10-K, Note 1, p.69; Note 14, pp.87–88]

Scale is the core advantage. Visa connects roughly 12 billion cards, bank accounts and wallets with more than 175 million merchant locations; this supports a strong moat, but the evidence supports stable rather than confirmed widening. [FY25 Form 10-K, pp.4, 17]

The main operating risk is the cost of defending routes and volume. Client incentives rose 18% in Q3 FY26, faster than 14% net-revenue growth and 10% processed-transaction growth, while some large clients can use competing products or leave on relatively short notice. [Q3 FY26 Earnings Release, pp.2, 4; FY25 Form 10-K, pp.27–29]

Regulation can separately change routing, interchange-linked economics, domestic processing and market access. Consumer spending and travel-linked cross-border commerce also drive payment volume; no filing sensitivity quantifies a 20% demand or regulatory shock. [Q3 FY26 Form 10-Q, p.32; FY25 Form 10-K, pp.23–25, 30]

The stable-moat read must also account for GAAP operating margin—the share of revenue left after operating costs—falling 559 basis points from FY21 to FY25 and cash conversion—the extent to which profit becomes operating cash—falling 940 basis points, even though gross margin, EBITDA margin and return on capital improved. The $2.562bn FY25 litigation provision explains part, but not all, of that caution. [FY25 Form 10-K, p.60; CIQ Financials→Income Statement/Cash Flow/Ratios, FY21–FY25 — vendor export]

## The Specialists, Briefly

- **data-triage:** Sufficient — the frozen pool contains recent FY25 and Q3 FY26 filings, with all 23 raw sources and 44 workbook tabs extracted without failures. [Frozen-generation manifest, 2026-09-23; FY25 Form 10-K, cover; Q3 FY26 Form 10-Q, cover]
- **disqualifier-scan:** No disqualifier triggered — operating cash flow was positive in FY22–FY25 and no hard audit, restatement, pledge, financial-reporting enforcement or customer-concentration trigger was established. [CIQ Financials→Cash Flow, FY22–FY25 — vendor export; FY25 Form 10-K, cover, pp.56, 99]
- **business-identity:** Global payments network and transaction processor — Visa moves payment instructions but does not lend or issue cards. [FY25 Form 10-K, pp.4–6]
- **segment-map:** Payment Services is unambiguously dominant — it is the sole reportable segment and generated all $40.0bn of FY25 net revenue; no product- or geography-level profit pool is disclosed. [FY25 Form 10-K, Note 14, pp.87–88]
- **unit-economics:** Unclear — derived net revenue per processed transaction rose 1.1% to $0.155 in FY25, but contribution margin, retention, payback and unit lifetime are not disclosed. [FY25 Form 10-K, pp.45–46; FY25 Annual Report, p.2]
- **customer-geography:** A disclosed large client and aggregate international exposure, but no single-country concentration — one unnamed client supplied 11% of revenue; International supplied 61%, while no non-U.S. country reached 10%. [FY25 Form 10-K, Item 7; Note 14]
- **value-chain:** Mixed economic control — Visa controls the processing platform and can change pricing, but large clients can use competing products or leave and incentives rose 18% in Q3 FY26. [FY25 Form 10-K, pp.4–6, 28–29; Q3 FY26 Earnings Release, pp.2, 4]
- **business-quality:** 66/100 — capital intensity is the clearest positive: FY25 capex was 3.7% of revenue; regulatory dependence is the weakest factor. [FY25 Form 10-K, pp.18–25, 63, 75]
- **competitive-map:** Position-versus-peers trend is not disclosed; branded-card networks are an oligopoly — Visa and Mastercard represented 90.2% of the five-network CY24 payment-volume chart, but one snapshot cannot establish share direction. [FY25 Form 10-K, p.17]
- **moat:** Strong moat; trajectory stable, not confirmed widening — scale and returns support durability, but GAAP operating margin and cash conversion weakened while no comparable market-share series exists. [FY25 Form 10-K, pp.4, 17, 60; CIQ Financials→Income Statement/Cash Flow/Ratios, FY21–FY25 — vendor export]
- **external-dependency:** Partly externally driven; 54/100 risk, higher is worse — consumer spending and regulation are high dependencies, despite pricing, incentives and FX hedging. [FY25 Form 10-K, pp.23–25, 30; Item 7A]
- **capital-allocation-governance:** Standard professional management; 80/100 — litigation and settlement guarantees are the main constraint: $3.142bn was paid for legal matters in the first nine months of FY26, with $1.274bn accrued and $0.888bn in restricted litigation escrow. [Q3 FY26 Form 10-Q, Note 16 and Liquidity and Capital Resources, pp.24–25, 39]
- **red-flags-sweep:** Tax-rate normalisation and incentive expiry is the most severe new flag — the FY25 Singapore incentive reduced tax by $453m and expires under disclosed terms in September 2028; Q3 FY26 results include a separate $351m one-time non-cash tax benefit. [FY25 Form 10-K, Note 19, p.98; Q3 FY26 Form 10-Q, Note 15; Q3 FY26 Earnings Release, pp.10–11]

The main reconciliation is that a strong existing moat does not mean high business quality or widening economics. Scale, distribution and return spread support the moat, while client bargaining power, regulation and new rails limit retained value; the synthesis therefore retains both the improving gross-margin, EBITDA-margin and return series and the 559-basis-point GAAP operating-margin and 940-basis-point cash-conversion declines. [FY25 Form 10-K, pp.4, 17, 23–29, 60; CIQ Financials→Income Statement/Cash Flow/Ratios, FY21–FY25 — vendor export]

## What Would Change This Read

| Would strengthen the deeper-work decision | Would weaken it | Biggest missing data point |
|---|---|---|
| A recovery to at least FY24's 65.7% GAAP operating margin without weaker cash conversion would strengthen the moat trajectory. [FY25 Form 10-K, p.60; CIQ Financials→Cash Flow, FY24–FY25 — vendor export] | Adverse routing or fee rules causing meaningful volume loss; incentives continuing to outpace revenue and transactions; or FY26 GAAP operating margin remaining below FY25's 60.0% while cash conversion deteriorates further. [Q3 FY26 Earnings Release, pp.2, 4; FY25 Form 10-K, pp.23–29, 60; CIQ Financials→Cash Flow, FY24–FY25 — vendor export] | Cohort-level incremental contribution after client incentives, with retention, contract duration and payback, to prove whether added clients or transactions create value rather than merely add volume. [FY25 Form 10-K, Note 1, p.69] |

Do not extend the current tax rate mechanically. The Singapore operating-hub incentive reduced FY25 tax by $453m and expires under disclosed terms in September 2028, while nine-month FY26 GAAP net income includes a separate $351m one-time non-cash deferred-tax benefit; renewal or post-2028 treatment is not established. [FY25 Form 10-K, Note 19, p.98; Q3 FY26 Form 10-Q, Note 15; Q3 FY26 Earnings Release, pp.10–11]

## Bottom Line

- **High-quality business — worth deeper work:** Visa’s global network scale and transaction-processing model are clear.
- It could be better than it looks if margins recover without weaker cash conversion and per-client economics prove value creation after incentives.
- It could be worse if routing or fee rules shift volume away, incentives keep rising faster than revenue and transactions, or the tax incentive is not renewed after September 2028.
- Per-unit contribution, retention, contract duration, payback, product-, geography- and revenue-category profit and capital, and current policy status are not proven from the available data.
- Watch incentive growth against net revenue and processed-transaction growth, as well as GAAP operating margin and cash conversion.

## Plain-English Glossary

- **Payment network:** the system that moves payment instructions between banks and sellers.
- **Operating margin:** the share of revenue left after operating costs.
- **Cash conversion:** the extent to which profit becomes operating cash.
- **Capital intensity:** capital spending relative to revenue.
- **Return on capital:** the profit earned on money invested in the business.

# Moat — V

## 1. Named Competitors

- **Mastercard Incorporated (NYSE: MA):** the closest direct rival: a global, multi-regional branded card network that authorizes, clears and settles transactions. [FY25 Form 10-K, Competition, p. 17; Mastercard FY25 Form 10-K, Item 1]
- **American Express Company (NYSE: AXP):** a global branded-network rival, but its issuer-and-lender model is not the same as Visa’s stand-alone network model. [FY25 Form 10-K, Competition, p. 17; American Express FY25 Form 10-K, Table 1, p. 42]

## 3. Competitive Economics

Visa is an operating company, so the relevant test is return on invested capital (ROIC), not return on equity (ROE). Its FY25 GAAP operating margin includes a $2.562bn litigation provision; Capital IQ’s normalized FY25/LTM operating presentation places legal settlements below operating income. They are not interchangeable. [data/V/Visa-Fiscal-2025-Annual-Report.pdf, FY25, p. 60; data/V/Visa-Inc-NYSE-V-Financials_Annual.xls, FY25, Income Statement]

**Return on capital above cost of capital: 22.4% standardized ROIC vs 7.8% WACC (1,460 bps) — [data/V/Visa-Inc-NYSE-V-Financials_Annual.xls, FY21–FY25, Ratios; WACC calculation below].** The 22.4% is the conservative five-year average of CIQ’s annual return-on-capital series (16.5%, 20.8%, 23.1%, 24.8%, 26.9%). CIQ reports 30.3% for LTM June 2026, but that latest result is not the through-cycle decision measure. [data/V/Visa-Inc-NYSE-V-Financials_Annual.xls, FY17–LTM Jun-2026, Ratios; CIQ facts sidecar, `multi_year_trajectory`, present]

Visa does not disclose a group ROIC. As a cross-check, calculated GAAP ROIC is 28.4% for FY21–FY25: NOPAT (after-tax operating profit) ÷ average gross invested capital. NOPAT uses a 17.6% normalized tax rate, the average of FY22–FY24 effective tax rates (17.53%, 17.89%, 17.45%); the FY25 litigation provision remains in GAAP EBIT because the filing does not disclose its tax treatment, so no unsupported tax add-back is made. The annual build is FY21 21.7% ($13.0bn NOPAT ÷ $60.0bn average gross capital), FY22 27.5% ($16.2bn ÷ $59.0bn), FY23 29.1% ($17.3bn ÷ $59.5bn), FY24 32.2% ($19.4bn ÷ $60.4bn), and FY25 31.7% ($19.8bn ÷ $62.4bn). Gross invested capital is total debt plus total equity, before cash netting; its components are shown in the cited annual balance-sheet export. [data/V/Visa-Inc-NYSE-V-Financials_Annual.xls, FY21–FY25, Income Statement/Balance Sheet/Capital Structure Summary; data/V/Visa-Fiscal-2025-Annual-Report.pdf, FY25, pp. 59–60]

The 600 bps difference between the calculated 28.4% and CIQ’s 22.4% average is material and the vendor workbook does not state a full return-on-capital definition. The economic test therefore uses the lower 22.4% figure rather than silently choosing the higher calculation.

**WACC basis — Inference, not from filings:** no Visa disclosure supplies a group WACC with the same scope as group ROIC. The estimate is 7.8%: 96.6% equity weight × [4.2% risk-free rate + 0.76 five-year beta × 5.0% judgmental U.S. equity-risk premium] + 3.4% debt weight × [3.25% LTM interest cost × (1 − 17.6% normalized tax rate)]. The $668.4bn equity value uses 1,835.6m shares × $364.15 on August 17, 2026; debt is $23.858bn at June 30, 2026. The 4.2% risk-free input is Visa’s FY25 option-valuation Treasury-curve assumption, so the estimate has a timing mismatch and is a test, not a company-disclosed WACC. [data/V/Visa-Fiscal-2025-Annual-Report.pdf, FY25, p. 93; data/V/Visa-Inc-NYSE-V-Public-Company-Profile.txt, CIQ vendor export, data as of 2026-08-14; CIQ facts sidecar, `current_price` and `total_debt_m`, present]

## 4. Where The Company Sits

1. **Relative to peers:** Visa sits at the top of the available direct operating-margin comparison (60.0% GAAP versus Mastercard’s 57.6%); named-peer gross-margin and clean ROIC data are insufficient, and American Express’s 33.9% ROE includes lending economics. [data/V/Visa-Fiscal-2025-Annual-Report.pdf, FY25, p. 60; Mastercard FY25 Form 10-K, p. 68; American Express FY25 Form 10-K, Table 1, p. 42]
2. **Absolute (the economic moat test):** Visa earns a return on capital above its cost of capital (22.4% vs 7.8%; 1,460 bps) on the conservative, five-year CIQ return series and the disclosed-input CAPM WACC estimate. [data/V/Visa-Inc-NYSE-V-Financials_Annual.xls, FY21–FY25, Ratios; data/V/Visa-Fiscal-2025-Annual-Report.pdf, FY25, p. 93]

## 5. Moat Verdict

**Strong moat.** Visa’s strongest advantage is the two-sided network: its roughly 12bn credentials and more than 175m merchant locations create a distribution and acceptance system that Mastercard and American Express also have, but at smaller reported payment-volume scale in Visa’s CY24 chart. The return test clears the estimated cost of capital by 1,460 bps even on the lower standardized return measure. Over the next five years, durability requires Visa to preserve payment volume and merchant acceptance while routing mandates, real-time payments, wallets and client incentives do not weaken its take rate or force excessive rebates. [data/V/Visa-Fiscal-2025-Annual-Report.pdf, FY25, pp. 4, 17–18, 23–25]

**Moat trajectory: stable, not confirmed widening.** The payments industry is changing quickly enough to limit durability: Visa names real-time payments, wallets, blockchain, stablecoins and agentic commerce as sources of new entry, while regulation can require domestic processing or change routing. [data/V/Visa-Fiscal-2025-Annual-Report.pdf, FY25, pp. 16–18, 23–25; analyses/V_2026-09-23/business-model/07_business-quality.md, industry rate-of-change row]

|---|---:|---:|---|---|

| Market share (or the closest disclosed volume/position proxy) | Not disclosed | CY24 Visa payment volume $13.433tn; no comparable earlier chart | Not assessable | Neither supports nor contradicts: one point cannot show share direction. |
| Cash conversion (CFO ÷ EBITDA) | 91.7% FY21 ($15.227bn ÷ $16.611bn) | 82.3% FY25 ($23.059bn ÷ $28.028bn) | −940 bps | Contradicts widening and weakens the stable read. |
| Pricing / realised price or mix | Not disclosed | Q3 FY26 benefited from select pricing modifications | Not assessable | No three-to-five-year realised-price or mix series; does not establish pricing-power direction. |

Gross margin rose 80 bps and CIQ EBITDA margin rose 116 bps, while the CIQ return-on-capital series rose from 16.5% to 26.9%; these rows are consistent with a durable network. However, GAAP operating margin fell 559 bps from 65.6% to 60.0% and cash conversion fell 940 bps from 91.7% to 82.3%. The margin decline includes the $2.562bn FY25 litigation provision, but it still belongs in the reported record; the cash-conversion decline is not explained away by that non-cash presentation. Market-share and realised-pricing trends are not disclosed on a comparable basis. The evidence is therefore mixed: a stable moat is supported, but widening is not confirmed. [data/V/Visa-Inc-NYSE-V-Financials_Annual.xls, FY21–FY25, Income Statement/Cash Flow/Ratios; data/V/Visa-Fiscal-2025-Annual-Report.pdf, FY25, p. 60; data/V/Visa_Inc_-_Form_10-Q-Jul-29-2026.txt, Q3 FY26, MD&A—Net Revenue]

The single next data point that would flip this trajectory read is **FY26 GAAP operating margin** in the next Form 10-K, for the year ending September 30, 2026 (exact filing date is not established in the frozen evidence). A recovery to at least FY24’s 65.7% would support widening if cash conversion does not deteriorate further; a second year below FY25’s 60.0% would support erosion. [data/V/Visa-Fiscal-2025-Annual-Report.pdf, FY24–FY25, p. 60]

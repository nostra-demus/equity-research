# Unit Economics — V

## 1. Natural Unit

The natural economic unit for Payment Services is a processed payment or cash transaction. Visa also earns service revenue mainly from payment volume and international-transaction revenue from cross-border activity, so a dollar of payment volume and a cross-border transaction are necessary secondary units; these different pricing bases cannot be reduced to one fully disclosed contribution calculation. [FY25 Form 10-K, Item 7, pp. 45–46]

## 3. Value Creation Read

**Unit economics: Unclear.** The FY25 revenue-minus-network-processing spread proxy was about $0.152 per processed transaction, but it does not prove incremental value creation because Visa does not split fixed from variable costs and its revenue is earned from several bases, not only transaction count. Aggregate FY25 GAAP operating income was $23.994bn, or 60.0% of net revenue, but that is a company-wide outcome rather than contribution margin per transaction. [FY25 Form 10-K, Item 7, pp. 45–47] The single most valuable missing disclosure is cohort-level incremental contribution after client incentives, together with contract retention and payback; without it, the test of contribution margin × unit lifetime exceeding acquisition cost cannot be completed.

## 4. Sensitivity

Client incentives are the disclosed input most likely to change this read. Incentives grew from $12.297bn in FY23 to $15.751bn in FY25; per processed transaction they rose from $0.058 to $0.061 (5.8%), while net revenue per processed transaction increased only 1.1% over the same two years. [FY25 Form 10-K, Item 7, pp. 45–46; FY25 Annual Report, p. 2] The latest quarter points the same way: Q3 FY26 client incentives rose 18%, compared with 14% net-revenue growth and 10% processed-transaction growth. [Q3 FY26 earnings release, Financial Highlights and Operational Performance Data] A 20% increase in FY25 incentives is a **zero-mitigation bound, not a forecast**: $15.751bn × 20% = $3.150bn, or $0.0122 per FY25 processed transaction; holding pre-incentive revenue and transaction count fixed would reduce net revenue from $40.000bn to $36.850bn (−7.9%) and revenue per transaction from $0.1553 to $0.1431. Pricing, volume, mix, and client-contract responses could offset part of that result, but the filing does not quantify the offset.

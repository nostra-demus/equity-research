# Regulatory, Legal & Compliance — V

**As of:** 2026-09-23  
**Entity and scope:** Visa Inc. (NYSE: V; CIK 0001403161), a U.S. SEC registrant. Five-year direct-entity lookback: 2021-09-23 to 2026-09-23. This report uses the frozen evidence inventory cited logically as data/V/. “No result” means only that the named source search did not produce an attributed item; it is not a clearance unless the sweep log says coverage is complete.

**Bottom line:** **hygiene lapses, watch.** Visa discloses a live DOJ civil antitrust case, an EC preliminary inquiry and large, continuing interchange litigation. The evidence reviewed does not show a material public matter absent from Visa’s own disclosures, but the external sweep is coverage-limited and key hygiene evidence is missing.

**Legal & regulatory risk: 52/100 (INVERTED — higher means worse); confidence 3/5.** This is a risk score, not an investment rating. The main drivers are two unresolved government matters, rather than a proven undisclosed-liability or messenger-retaliation finding.

## 4. Whistleblower Machinery and Messenger Response

Visa discloses several reporting routes: businessconduct@visa.com, a confidential hotline and web reporting channel at visa.alertline.com, mail, and Board communications. It states that communications are handled under its Whistleblower Policy. The proxy does not disclose received, closed, substantiated or pending complaint statistics, and the reviewed governance material does not identify an ombuds function. [Visa 2026 DEF 14A, filed 2025-12-08, Stockholder Communications with the Board; Code of Business Conduct and Ethics]

A shareholder proposal asked Visa to report on AI-driven online sexual exploitation and cited reporting about adult-platform-related risks. Visa opposed the proposal and described payment-integrity controls, its High Integrity Risk Program, and Board oversight. The reviewed record does not show an independent forensic review of that underlying allegation, nor does it show Visa suing or otherwise attacking the messenger. This is not proof that no investigation occurred; it is a disclosure-limit finding. [Visa 2026 DEF 14A, filed 2025-12-08, Stockholder Proposal—Report on AI-Driven Online Sexual Exploitation, pp.102–105]

## 9. Read

The public record reviewed corroborates Visa’s filings rather than contradicting them: the live DOJ case, the EC preliminary inquiry and the major interchange matters are disclosed. The concern is the scale and duration of those unresolved matters, not a proven disclosure omission.

The remaining evidence gap matters. Eight-quarter exchange-fine history, twelve-quarter timeliness, current rating-action history, lender identity/quality, and a complete sanctions/court sweep are not established. Visa has accessible whistleblower channels, but it does not provide complaint outcomes or an identified ombuds, and its response to the published adult-content concern does not show an independent forensic review.

**Conclusion: hygiene lapses, watch.** No hard A9-10 candor gate or critical misconduct finding is triggered on the evidence reviewed. The incomplete sweep sets a legal-regulatory risk floor of 40 and a confidence ceiling of 70 under the module rules; this report applies a 52/100 inverted risk score and 3/5 confidence.

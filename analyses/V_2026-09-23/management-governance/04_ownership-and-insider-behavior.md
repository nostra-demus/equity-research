This report uses the FY25 proxy for legal beneficial ownership and the frozen Capital IQ (CIQ) ownership workbooks for later transaction and holder snapshots. The two are not interchangeable: the proxy includes shares obtainable within 60 days, while the vendor export reports a later ownership/trading view. The source-bound facts sidecar is the authoritative read of the CIQ workbook summaries used below.

## 4. Control Structure

| Feature | Detail | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Dual-class / super-voting | Not a promoter super-voting structure. Class A votes on ordinary shareholder matters. | Controlled-company status | No controlling shareholder is identified in the proxy. Vanguard (8.18%) and BlackRock (7.32%) were the only disclosed 5%+ Class A holders; no director, named executive officer, or the 17-person group exceeded 1%. | [FY25 DEF 14A, Beneficial Ownership of Equity Securities, pp.110–111] |
| Shareholder bloc with board rights | No shareholder bloc with board-nomination or veto rights was identified. The B/C financial-institution holders have limited class protections on specified transactions, not a stated right to nominate directors. | [FY25 Form 10-K, Risk Factors—Capital Structure, p.34; Note 15—Stockholders’ Equity, pp.90–91] |
| Controlling-owner objective alignment *(Filter 6)* | Filter 6 does not apply on the available evidence: Visa is neither state-controlled nor a listed subsidiary of a value-maximizing parent, and no controlling conglomerate is identified. The legacy B/C classes nevertheless create a bounded conflict risk because current and former financial-institution clients may have different interests from Class A holders in significant transactions. | [FY25 Form 10-K, Risk Factors—Capital Structure, p.34] |

The control concern is limited, not absent. The B/C classes do not control routine elections or grant a shareholder group general super-votes, but the company itself says their financial-institution holders can have divergent interests on the specified major transactions. Separately, the charter’s 15% ownership cap (5% for competitors) can make a takeover harder; that is an entrenchment feature, not evidence that management controls the vote. [FY25 Form 10-K, Risk Factors—Capital Structure, p.34]

## 5. Ownership / Insider Read

Visa has dispersed institutional ownership, not owner-operator alignment: the 17-person director/officer group beneficially held 0.125% of Class A at 2025-12-01, of which 1.378m of 2.103m shares were merely obtainable within 60 days. The clean 12-month open-market signal is net selling of 32,180 shares with no purchases; it is a weak negative signal, not evidence of misconduct. The biggest minority-holder issue is the legacy B/C class protection in specified major transactions, not a controller, pledge, or super-voting promoter.

## 3. Disclosure Transparency

Visa discloses the core economic drivers of its single Payment Services segment: payments volume, processed transactions, revenue components, client incentives, and the relationship between those drivers and service/data-processing revenue. The same core KPI framework is present in FY23–FY25 reporting and the Q3 FY26 10-Q, with the filing noting that prior-period volume updates were not material; this is useful consistency but does not prove the five-year A7-03 Green standard from the supplied record. [Visa FY25 Form 10-K, Item 7—Payments Volume and Processed Transactions; Visa Q3 FY26 Form 10-Q, Item 2—Payments Volume and Processed Transactions]

The disclosure is less conservative in adjusted presentation: the reconciliations are detailed, but a recurring litigation exclusion makes non-GAAP profit more flattering than GAAP profit. No KPI was found to have been dropped or redefined immediately after deterioration in the available FY23–Q3 FY26 materials. [Visa FY25 Form 10-K, Item 7—Non-GAAP Financial Results, pp.41–44; earnings/06_earnings-quality, 2026-09-23, §§4–5]

## 4. Tone in Bad Times

The supplied Q1–Q3 FY26 calls are verbatim transcripts, but none contains a reported management-guidance miss or weak operating quarter. The closest adverse financial event is FY25's $2,533m litigation provision: Visa disclosed that GAAP operating expenses rose 30%, primarily because of litigation and personnel costs, and supplied the reconciliation; it then excluded the provision from non-GAAP results. [Visa FY25 Form 10-K, Item 7—Results of Operations / Non-GAAP Financial Results, pp.41–44]

Accordingly, bad-times tone and Q&A evasiveness are not assessable from this snapshot. The Q3 call's warning that unusually high June–July cross-border e-commerce should settle toward a more typical travel relationship is a bounded example of adverse qualification, but it is not a test of responding to a miss. [Visa Q3 FY26 Earnings Call, CFO prepared remarks and Q&A, 2026-07-28; earnings/04_guidance-consensus, 2026-09-23, §2]

## 5. Candor Read

**Mixed.** Visa is transparent about the numbers behind its non-GAAP measures and has not been shown to withdraw or redefine guidance quietly in the two completed FY26 comparisons. The strongest negative signal is the recurring litigation provision excluded every year from FY23 through FY25, culminating in $2,533m in FY25; it materially improves the adjusted series even though the reconciliation is visible. [Visa FY25 Form 10-K, Item 7—Non-GAAP Financial Results, pp.41–44]

There is no evidence in this pool of an actual guidance miss or weak operating period, so high-trust candor is not earned. The single biggest candor signal is the tension between transparent GAAP reconciliation and recurring costs labelled outside continuing performance. [01_management-and-track-record, 2026-09-23, §4; earnings/06_earnings-quality, 2026-09-23, §§4–5]

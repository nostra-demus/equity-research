## What This Module Found

Visa is professionally run: it has no identified controller, an independent chair and 10 of 11 independent directors. Six current executive officers have median Visa tenure of 9.7 years, while two resolved FY26 guidance comparisons were met or exceeded; FY26 remains open. [FY26 DEF 14A, Executive Officers, pp.47–48; Director Nominees / Board Leadership; Management & Track Record — V, §§2–4]

Capital allocation created visible per-share results. Visa returned $59.732bn through FY23–FY25 buybacks and dividends—$47.130bn plus $12.602bn—and the as-converted share count fell 2.7% in FY25; however, 9M FY26 cash uses of $26.552bn exceeded CFO, operating cash generated by the business, of $16.342bn by $10.210bn, and strict net debt rose $3.492bn to $11.499bn. [Capital-Allocation Scorecard — V, §§1–4; Q3 FY26 Form 10-Q, Consolidated Cash Flows and Balance Sheet]

Pay is heavily equity-based, but alignment is incomplete. CEO pay was $31.561m, 77.3% equity, and CFO pay was $11.247m, 72.0% equity; annual-bonus weights are undisclosed, insider beneficial ownership is 0.125%, and the trailing-12-month record is 0 buys and 4 sells, net 32,180 shares sold. [Incentives & Compensation — V, §§1–5; FY26 DEF 14A, Beneficial Ownership, pp.97, 110–111; Capital IQ Insider Trading, latest filed 2026-07-31 — vendor export]

The largest governance risk is legal and regulatory pressure. The DOJ debit-network action survived Visa’s dismissal motion, the EC fee inquiry remains open, and recognised litigation plus estimated gross unrecognised tax benefits total $3.005bn, 8.5% of June 2026 equity; possible-loss tails are unquantified. [DOJ, *U.S. v. Visa Inc.*, case page updated 2025-07-07; FY25 Form 10-K, Note 20, pp.99–107; Q3 FY26 Form 10-Q, Notes 15–16, pp.23–28]

The evidence does not support a concealment claim. Visa disclosed the DOJ, EC, U.S. and European interchange matters, and the reconciliation found no material public case absent from its filings; that does not remove the economic risk or the incomplete docket sweep. [Q3 FY26 Form 10-Q, Note 16, pp.24–28; Regulatory, Legal & Compliance — V, §8]

## Bottom Line

- **Standard / mixed; Governance Rating: Watchlist.** The gate passes, with no hard disqualifier and no Critical flag.
- It could be better than it looks if the legal matters resolve without material economic remedy, cash uses return within CFO, and the legal/person sweep clears.
- It could be worse if the DOJ or EC matters change Visa’s core economics, distributions remain above internally generated cash, recurring exclusions widen, or a missing legal/audit/RPT fact proves adverse.
- The record is incomplete for legal databases, rating history, RPT amounts, voting results, audit-partner detail and some person/entity registers.
- Watch the DOJ docket and direct EC case status, then FY26 cash uses versus CFO and every recurring non-GAAP exclusion.

## Plain-English Glossary

- **CFO:** cash from operations, the cash generated by normal business activity.
- **Strict net debt:** debt minus cash equivalents.
- **Related-party transaction (RPT):** a transaction with a director, executive, controller or related entity that can create a conflict of interest.
- **Non-GAAP result:** a company performance measure that excludes some items from the standard accounting result.
- **Contingent liability:** a possible obligation whose final cost depends on a future event or outcome.
- **Beneish M-score:** a screen for patterns common in manipulated accounts.

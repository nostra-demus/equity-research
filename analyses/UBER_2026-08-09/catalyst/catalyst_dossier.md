## 2. The Nearest Dated Catalyst

The nearest proven-dated catalyst is Q3 FY2026 earnings, expected Nov-03-2026. Management has already guided EBITDA to $2,860M–$2,960M and EPS Normalized to $0.84–$0.88, and Street consensus sits within 0.4% of both midpoints — a fair bar, not a stretched one [`earnings/04_guidance-consensus.md` §3, §7]. The two-sided outcome is concrete: Uber has beaten its own EBITDA guidance in four straight quarters and cleared the guided high end in the last two by a near-identical ~2.5%, which argues for another beat; but revenue has missed consensus in the last two quarters and the freshest post-print revisions skew clearly negative (8 up vs. 19 down on next-quarter revenue in the last month), so a wider-than-usual revenue miss is the scenario most likely to dominate the market's reaction even if EBITDA/EPS clear their ranges again [`earnings/05_beat-miss-setup.md` §8].

## 3. The Single Most Important Catalyst

By potential financial impact, the single most important catalyst is the Delivery Hero SE tender offer, expected in the second half of 2027 — a ~$14.8bn deal that would roughly double Uber's gross debt via a new €14.2bn bridge facility and require divesting overlapping Uber Eats operations in 14 markets for $1.6bn [`management-governance/02_capital-allocation-scorecard.md` §2]. The bullish path is a clean close with contained financing terms and the accretion management has claimed; the bearish path is a deal that gets delayed, blocked, or renegotiated, or one that closes and pushes pro-forma net leverage toward ~5.5x in a downturn — a scenario the balance-sheet-survival module could not resolve because the actual covenant package is undisclosed [`balance-sheet-survival/99` §1, §7]. A buyer cannot time this precisely: the offer window is a half-year, not a date, it sits outside the 12-month band, and the module rules require reading it through the §24 Filter 4 serial-acquirer lens — Uber has done roughly a dozen deals since its 2019 IPO, and the two largest prior deals with observable post-close results (Transplace/Uber Freight, Drizly) both failed to deliver [`management-governance/02` §2]. The nearer, and arguably more decision-useful, proxy event is the Q3 2026 term loan/revolver refresh (row 3 above), which should reveal the actual permanent-financing terms for this deal roughly a year before the tender itself.

## 4. Negative / Bearish Catalysts

- **2026 Term Loan maturity (December 2026, $2,000M).** Bearish path: rolled into new debt at a step-up above the ~60–90bps estimated on plain notes, or funded via the revolver/FCF instead of cash on hand, signaling tighter liquidity than the standalone maturity math implies [`balance-sheet-survival/02` §3–4].
- **Q3 2026 bridge-facility term-out.** Bearish path: the €14.2bn Delivery Hero bridge converts to permanent debt near its full size with a tight, unfavorable covenant package — the balance-sheet-survival module's own named "single killer risk" [`balance-sheet-survival/99` §1A].
- **Delivery Hero tender offer (H2 2027).** Bearish path: deal delay, a blocked merger-control clearance, a triggered €700mm break fee, or a close that roughly doubles gross debt on undisclosed covenant terms — a §24 Filter 4 serial-acquirer pattern already capping the capital-allocation score at 50/100 [`management-governance/02` §2, §5].
- **Getir Turkey and Blacklane closes (H2 2026 / by end-2026).** Smaller individually, but each slipping or closing with no disclosed operating detail adds to the same serial-acquirer pattern (RF-CAP-004) ahead of the much larger Delivery Hero integration.
- **FY2027 AGM (pattern-based, ~May 2027).** Bearish path: Nikesh Arora's 20.31% against-vote or Chairman Ronald Sugar's 8.09% against-vote grow further, or a proposal tied to the derivative lawsuit or Delivery Hero draws material opposition — signaling the board's independence-on-paper is not translating into investor confidence [`management-governance/05` §4A, §7].

Buyers should weigh these alongside the undated risks in Section 1 (the UK VAT appeal, driver-classification litigation, and the board-wide derivative lawsuit) — none of which carry a date, but all of which are real and material.

## 5. Calendar Read

Two of the seven catalysts in this calendar carry a proven date (Q3 FY2026 earnings, the Term Loan maturity) — roughly 29% of the set — and the remaining five are bounded windows, one of them (Delivery Hero) an explicit management-stated half-year that also sits outside the strict 12-month horizon. The near-term proven events (the November earnings print, the December term loan) read close to neutral on the evidence: the earnings setup favors a beat on the metrics Uber guides but carries a live revenue-miss risk, and the term loan is fully pre-funded by cash on hand. The calendar's single largest item by dollar size — the Delivery Hero acquisition — is not a bullish catalyst on the evidence gathered here: it is the deal that tripped the §24 Filter 4 serial-acquirer flag, financed by debt that would roughly double the balance sheet, with two of Uber's two largest comparable prior deals having failed to deliver. The single biggest timing caveat for the whole calendar is that the event most likely to move the risk read — the actual permanent-financing and covenant terms for the €14.2bn bridge facility — has no confirmed date, only a management-stated "third quarter of 2026," and until that specific event resolves, both the balance-sheet-survival and this module's own read of Delivery Hero risk stay open questions.

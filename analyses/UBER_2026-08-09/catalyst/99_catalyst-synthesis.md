# Catalyst Module — UBER (Synthesis)

## Abstract

Uber's calendar carries real dated catalysts, not just a thematic story: a proven-date Q3 FY2026 earnings release on Nov-03-2026 and a proven-date $2,000M Term Loan maturity in December 2026 both sit inside six months and test concrete, evidenced outcomes. The nearest event, the November print, tests whether Uber clears its own guided EBITDA/EPS range a third straight quarter while a widening revenue-miss pattern continues. The single most important catalyst by size is the Delivery Hero SE tender offer, expected in the second half of 2027 — a roughly $14.8bn deal that would double Uber's gross debt, but its offer window is vague, sits outside the 12-month band, and is tripped by the §24 serial-acquirer filter. The biggest bearish, windowed item is the Q3 2026 bridge-facility term-out, whose covenant terms are undisclosed. Verdict: dated, evidenced near-term catalysts exist, but the largest item cannot be timed and should not be read as bullish.

## 1A. Module Disconfirmation

- **Strongest bear point:** the Q3 2026 bridge-facility term-out (management-stated quarter, Q2 FY26 10-Q, "Liquidity and Material Cash Requirements") is the calendar's single largest source of downside skew — a €14.2bn bridge with no disclosed covenant package, converting to permanent debt right as the Dec-2026 Term Loan is also due [`01_catalyst-calendar.md` row 3, row 2; `balance-sheet-survival/99` §1A].
- **Strongest bull point:** Q3 FY2026 earnings (Nov-03-2026, proven date) — Uber has cleared its own guided EBITDA high end in the last two quarters by a near-identical ~2.5%, a real, evidence-based four-quarter beat pattern against an already-issued guidance range [`01_catalyst-calendar.md` §2; `earnings/05_beat-miss-setup.md` §2, §8].
- **Single killer risk specific to the calendar:** the Delivery Hero tender offer is the largest dollar item on this calendar and reads, on a first pass, like the dominant bullish catalyst — it is not. It is vague-timed (H2 2027, outside the 12-month band), §24 Filter-4 flagged, and the specialist calendar explicitly concludes it "is not a bullish catalyst on the evidence gathered here" [`01_catalyst-calendar.md` §5]. Treating it as conviction-lifting would be the single biggest misread this module can produce.
- **Disconfirming evidence already visible:** Uber's two largest prior deals with observable post-close results — Transplace/Uber Freight and Drizly — both failed to deliver [`management-governance/02_capital-allocation-scorecard.md` §2], and the capital-allocation score is already capped at 50/100 on the pattern (RF-CAP-004).

## 4. Note To The Final Synthesizer

- The nearest dated, evidenced catalyst is Q3 FY2026 earnings (Nov-03-2026, proven). It tests a third straight EBITDA/EPS-guidance beat against a widening revenue-miss pattern — a genuinely two-sided, timeable event.
- The single most important catalyst by size — the Delivery Hero tender (H2 2027) — cannot be timed precisely. It is a half-year window, sits outside the 12-month horizon, and its financing terms (Q3 2026 bridge term-out) are the real near-term proxy a buyer should actually watch.
- The near-term dated calendar (earnings, Term Loan repayment) reads close to neutral, not clearly bullish: the earnings setup favors a metric beat but carries a live revenue-miss risk, and the Term Loan is already fully pre-funded by cash on hand rather than a source of upside.
- The biggest negative / bearish catalyst is the Q3 2026 bridge-facility term-out — undisclosed covenant terms on a €14.2bn facility, the balance-sheet-survival module's own named "single killer risk."
- The Delivery Hero acquisition is §24 Filter-4 flagged (serial acquirer, RF-CAP-004) and must NOT be read as conviction-lifting even though it is the largest catalyst on the calendar — Uber's two largest comparable prior deals both failed to deliver.
- Timing is mixed: proven for the two nearest, highest-quality catalysts (earnings, Term Loan), vague for everything else including the calendar's single largest item. Treat near-term timing as usable, but do not extend that confidence to the M&A-driven capital-structure outcome.
- Biggest missing scheduled-event data point: no numeric covenant package for the €14.2bn Delivery Hero bridge/permanent financing, and no scheduled regulatory-decision date for driver-classification litigation or the UK VAT appeal.
- **Explicit handoff:** the master synthesizer's "Catalyst Calendar" (§7) section should defer to this synthesis rather than re-deriving the calendar; its catalyst-timing confidence caps (CLAUDE.md §17) should be driven by the mixed proven/vague timing-visibility read here (45/100), not by the size of the Delivery Hero deal alone.

## 5. Simple Summary

- Yes, there are real dated catalysts — not just a story: a proven earnings date and a proven debt-maturity date, both inside six months.
- Nearest dated event: Q3 FY2026 earnings, Nov-03-2026.
- Most important catalyst by size: the Delivery Hero acquisition tender, expected H2 2027 — but its date is a soft half-year window, not a hard date.
- Biggest thing that could go wrong on the calendar: the €14.2bn bridge loan for Delivery Hero converts to permanent debt on undisclosed, possibly unfavorable terms around Q3 2026.
- The thesis can lean on timing for the next two quarters (earnings, Term Loan) but not for the M&A story that dominates headlines — that part is genuinely unproven on timing.
- This module is useful: the calendar is unusually complete for a first-year build, with proven dates in three categories and every upstream module feeding it.
- Do not treat the Delivery Hero deal as a bullish catalyst — it is a §24-flagged serial-acquisition pattern with a poor track record on the two largest comparable prior deals.

# Catalyst Module — UBER (Synthesis)

## Abstract

Uber's calendar is a mix, not a story: three catalysts carry proven dates — Q3 FY2026 earnings, the December-2026 Term Loan maturity, and the mechanical Q1 FY27 UK-VAT lap — while six more, including the deal that matters most, are windows, not dates. The nearest dated catalyst is Q3 FY2026 earnings, estimated November 3, 2026, which tests whether a four-quarter EBITDA-guidance-beat streak survives a revenue line that has missed consensus the last two quarters running. The single most important catalyst is the Delivery Hero acquisition and its bridge-to-term-loan financing, a $14.8 billion deal that would roughly triple pro-forma leverage if drawn in full, with a bullish clean-close path against a bearish path that repeats Uber's own Drizly and Careem pattern. The biggest bearish catalyst is that same bridge conversion, expected in Q3 2026 on undisclosed terms. Verdict: dated, evidenced near-term catalysts exist, but the calendar's largest catalyst cannot be cleanly timed and is flagged under the serial-acquirer rule.

Reading it as bullish rather than as a leverage and integration test is the single biggest way this module's calendar could mislead a reader.
- **Disconfirming evidence already visible:** Two of Uber's own prior deals (Drizly, Careem) already failed or reversed within 2.5–3 years; the Getir Turkey and Blacklane deals now closing in this window carry no disclosed standalone-return framework, extending rather than breaking that pattern [`01_catalyst-calendar.md` §4].

## 4. Note To The Final Synthesizer

- The nearest dated, evidenced catalyst is Q3 FY2026 earnings (est. Nov-03-2026); it tests whether the four-quarter EBITDA-guidance-beat streak survives a revenue line that has missed consensus the last two quarters. A buyer CAN time this one — the date is proven.
- The single most important catalyst is the Delivery Hero acquisition and financing. A buyer CANNOT cleanly time it — the bridge-conversion terms are undisclosed and the tender completion is a stated half-year window (H2 2027), not a date, sitting outside this calendar's primary 12-month horizon even though its financing is already live on today's balance sheet.
- The near-term calendar skews cautiously constructive operationally (the guidance-beat streak favors another beat at Q3 FY2026 earnings and the Dec-2026 Term Loan is well-covered by cash) but skews bearish on leverage/structure — the calendar's single largest-scale event is a serial-acquirer, debt-funded deal whose financing terms are the least disclosed item in the pool.
- The biggest bearish catalyst is the Delivery Hero bridge-to-term-loan conversion (Q3 2026, vague window): if priced above the ~5.2–5.7% indicative market range or if a first-ever maintenance covenant is disclosed, the illustrative debt-covenant break point collapses from ~73% to ~30% EBITDA decline.
- The Delivery Hero acquisition, and the smaller Getir Turkey and Blacklane closes, are §24 Filter-4-flagged and must NOT be read as conviction-lifting bullish catalysts — they are M&A events already capped by `management-governance/02`'s capital-allocation score (RF-CAP-004), and Uber's own record (Drizly full write-off, Careem reversed divestiture) is the base rate they should be judged against.
- Timing is mixed: three of nine catalysts (earnings, Term Loan maturity, UK-VAT lap) are proven-dated; six, including the single most important one, are vague windows. Timing visibility is scored 48/100 — this should drive the master synthesizer's §7 confidence caps toward the "vague window" side rather than the "proven near-term" side, notwithstanding that a proven-date catalyst exists.
- The biggest missing scheduled-event data point is the Delivery Hero bridge-to-term-loan conversion's actual terms — maturity, pricing, and covenants are undisclosed anywhere in the pool, despite the conversion being expected within the next two months of this report date (Q3 2026).
- **Explicit handoff:** the master synthesizer's "Catalyst Calendar" (§7) section should defer to this synthesis in full; its catalyst-timing confidence caps (CLAUDE.md §17) should be driven by the Timing-visibility score (48/100) and the one-third-proven-by-count read above, not by treating the calendar as uniformly dated.

## 5. Simple Summary

- Yes, there are real dated catalysts — not just a story: Q3 FY2026 earnings (Nov-03-2026), the December-2026 Term Loan maturity, and a mechanical UK-VAT anniversary lap in Q1 FY27 all have proven dates.
- The nearest dated event is Q3 FY2026 earnings, estimated November 3, 2026.
- The most important catalyst by far is the Delivery Hero acquisition — a $14.8 billion deal that would roughly triple leverage if its bridge loan is drawn in full, and its own timing (bridge terms, tender completion) is the least proven thing on the calendar.
- The biggest thing that could go wrong on the calendar is that same Delivery Hero bridge loan converting to term debt on worse-than-expected terms sometime in Q3 2026 — the pricing and covenants are not disclosed anywhere in this data pool.
- The thesis can lean on timing for the near-term operating story (earnings, the term loan) but not for the deal that actually decides the balance-sheet risk.
- This module is useful to the master synthesizer: it gives three proven dates, a clear bull/bear split on the nearest earnings print, and a specific warning not to treat the Delivery Hero deal as a clean bullish catalyst.
- Watch for: whether Q3 FY2026 EBITDA clears guidance a third straight quarter, and whether the Delivery Hero bridge terms get disclosed before or at the conversion.

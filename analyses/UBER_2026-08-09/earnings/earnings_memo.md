## What This Module Found

Uber's underlying demand is genuinely accelerating. Gross Bookings — the total dollar value of all rides and deliveries booked — grew 24% year-on-year in Q2 FY26 to $58.0B, the fourth straight quarter above 20% [`02_revenue-drivers.md` §4]. Trip volume is the single biggest earnings driver and still expanding.

But reported revenue growth cooled to 12.2% year-on-year, because a one-time UK rule change on how Mobility revenue is booked cut the quarter's revenue by $1.1B [`02_revenue-drivers.md` §6a]. That is an accounting reclassification, not weaker demand — organic ex-UK, ex-currency growth ran closer to 19.9pp.

The biggest risk sits in the metric behind the beat streak. Uber has beaten its own guided "EBITDA" (a profit measure, roughly earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation) in 4 of 4 quarters. But that guided metric's exact definition is not confirmed in the data pool, and it likely still includes a recurring "one-off" legal and regulatory reserve add-back worth up to 17.3% of Adjusted EBITDA in FY2024 and 6.5% in FY2025 [`06_earnings-quality.md` §4–5; `08_earnings-red-flags.md` §2.5, §2.7]. That same legal line already flipped once — from +$549M helping (FY2025) to −$138M hurting (Q2 FY26) [`03_margin-drivers.md` §7a].

Cash backing is strong: cash from operations exceeded unadjusted EBITDA every profitable year (160–202% of EBITDA, FY2023–FY2025) [`06_earnings-quality.md` §2]. But the two GAAP headline numbers most investors default to are flattered — net income and diluted EPS were inflated by one-time, non-cash tax benefits two years running ($6.4B FY2024, $5.0B FY2025) [`06_earnings-quality.md` §5, §10].

## What Would Change This Read

**Would upgrade it:**
- A third straight quarter of clean, ex-UK cost-of-revenue leverage without an offsetting legal-accrual reversal.
- Revenue consensus stabilizing or turning positive once the UK item laps in Q1 FY27.
- Resumption of quarterly Adjusted EBITDA disclosure with the legal-reserve line broken out.

**Would downgrade it:**
- A third straight revenue miss beyond the ±0.6% range of the last two quarters.
- EBITDA/EPS falling below the guided low end, breaking the 4-quarter streak.
- The legal-accrual line swinging unfavorably again (as it already did once).
- The first quantified cost figure for self-driving-car (AV) investment, which the CFO has deferred sizing.
- Any adverse driver-classification or labor-reclassification ruling in a large market.

**Data needed:** The Q4 FY26 guide (next print, expected early November 2026) — whether it shows the normal seasonal step-up, restates the guided metric's composition, and introduces a first AV cost figure.

## Bottom Line

- **Verdict:** Mixed earnings setup — three axes genuinely conflict (revenue, margins, quality/consensus), not one axis simply capped.
- **Could be better than it looks:** Underlying demand is accelerating (Gross Bookings +24%, four straight quarters above 20%) and cash conversion is strong (CFO 160–202% of EBITDA) — the reported revenue slowdown is mostly a one-time UK accounting change.
- **Could be worse than it looks:** The beat streak rests on a guided "EBITDA" whose definition is unconfirmed and likely still nets a recurring "one-off" legal reserve; if that line turns unfavorable again the streak can break with no change in demand.
- **What's missing:** The exact definition and composition of the guided "EBITDA" metric — CIQ labels it simply "EBITDA," and its relationship to the company's historically disclosed Adjusted EBITDA is not independently confirmed in this pool.
- **One thing to watch next:** The Q4 FY26 guide in early November — does it restate the guided metric and does the legal-accrual line reverse again?

## Plain-English Glossary

- **Gross Bookings** — the total dollar value of everything booked on Uber (rides plus deliveries) before Uber's own cut.
- **EBITDA** — a profit measure: earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation. Uber's guided "EBITDA" here is an adjusted, company-defined version whose exact makeup is unconfirmed.
- **Adjusted EBITDA** — EBITDA after the company strips out items it labels one-off; the concern is that a recurring legal reserve keeps getting stripped out.
- **GAAP net income / EPS** — profit and per-share profit under standard US accounting rules; here inflated by one-time non-cash tax benefits.
- **CFO (cash from operations) / EBITDA** — how much real operating cash the business generates against its reported profit; above 100% means strong cash backing.
- **Strict net debt** — total debt minus cash; the conservative way to measure borrowing.
- **Basis points (bps)** — hundredths of a percent; 100bps = 1 percentage point.

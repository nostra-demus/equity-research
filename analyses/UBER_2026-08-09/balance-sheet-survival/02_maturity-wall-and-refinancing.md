# Maturity Wall & Refinancing — UBER

Reporting currency: **USD** (millions unless stated). All dates below run from the report date, **August 9, 2026**. Figures are sourced from **Note 5 – Debt and Credit Arrangements** (Q2 FY26 10-Q, filed Aug-05-2026) and reconcile to `01_capital-structure-and-leverage.md`'s canonical gross debt of **$13,033M** (Note 5 bonds/notes/term loan, carrying value $12,723M, + finance leases $310M, Dec-31-2025 balance carried forward).

**Read this first — the maturity schedule below is INCOMPLETE for what actually matters most.** On July 16, 2026 — three weeks before this report — Uber executed a **€14.2 billion (≈$15.3bn) bridge credit agreement** for senior unsecured loans to help fund its pending acquisition of Delivery Hero (cash consideration €41.50/share, $14.8bn implied equity value, expected close H2 2027). The bridge is **undrawn** as of the Q2 FY26 10-Q filing date, postdates the June 30, 2026 balance sheet, and is not in any figure in Section 1 below. Uber states it "expect[s] to enter into term loan facilities that will reduce the commitments under the bridge credit agreement" in Q3 2026 — i.e., the bridge is meant to be taken out by permanent debt, which is itself a refinancing event, not a one-off draw. No maturity date, pricing, or covenant terms for the bridge facility were found in the extracted 10-Q text (Exhibit 10.2 is listed but its terms are not reproduced in the body text this agent could extract) [Q2 FY26 10-Q, p.21659–21694 (Pending Acquisition of Delivery Hero), Exhibit 10.2 (Bridge Credit Agreement, dated Jul-16-2026)]. This is flagged throughout and is the single largest forward refinancing exposure Uber carries — larger than its entire existing debt stack — even though it cannot be scheduled into a maturity table because its own maturity is not disclosed in this pool.

**WAM formula and inputs:** WAM = Σ(principal_i × years-to-maturity_i) / Σ(principal_i), computed from the report date (Aug-09-2026) using each instrument's disclosed maturity month (day-level precision is not disclosed for most instruments, so month-level rounding is used). Finance-lease principal is included at its current/non-current split ($136M at an assumed 0.5-year average maturity; $174M at an assumed 2.5-year average maturity, since the year-by-year lease amortization schedule is not disclosed — *Inference, not from filings* for the lease time-weighting only). Excluding finance leases entirely would move WAM only marginally (leases are 2.4% of the principal base). The $1,250M 2054 Senior Notes (28 years out) alone contribute roughly 2.7 years to the 6.1-year WAM — without that single tranche, the WAM of the rest of the stack would be closer to **3.4 years**, which is the more decision-relevant number for near-to-medium-term refinancing planning.

**Nearly half the debt stack (49.8%) matures within three years**, concentrated in three events: the Dec-2026 Term Loan, the May-2028 Exchangeable Notes, and the Dec-2028/Aug-2029 Convertible + Senior Notes cluster. None of this yet includes the pending Delivery Hero bridge facility.

**Why the blended coupon is misleading, and why it is shown anyway:** Uber's 3.81% blended weighted-average coupon looks unusually cheap because $2,850M (22% of the principal base) is in two equity-linked instruments priced far below straight-debt market rates in exchange for conversion/exchange optionality — the 2028 Convertible Notes (0.875%) and the 2028 Exchangeable Senior Notes (0.00% — no regular cash interest at all, secured instead by a pledge of Aurora Innovation shares). These are not comparable to a plain refinancing: if Uber has to replace them with straight unsecured debt at maturity (rather than converting them into equity or refinancing with another convert), the cash-coupon step-up would be far larger than the 50–100bps shown for the straight notes — potentially 400bps+ on that $2,850M slice alone, since a market refinancing near 5.2–5.7% would replace a 0–0.875% coupon. The **straight-notes WAC of 4.67%**, compared against the 5.2–5.7% benchmark range, is the more decision-relevant comparison for what Uber would pay to refinance its conventional senior notes at 2029–2054 maturities.

**Floating-rate sensitivity:** the $2,000M Term Loan reprices with Term SOFR. Term SOFR-linked benchmark rates were ~3.65% as of Aug-06-2026 (Web, indicative/unverified), consistent with the loan's own stated 4.46% rate (SOFR + 0.825% spread). A **+100bp** move in SOFR would raise annual interest cost on this single tranche by **~$20M** (1% × $2,000M) — a small absolute amount given SOFR has already fallen from its 2023–2024 peak (~5.3%), but the loan matures Dec-2026, so any refinancing (rollover of the term loan, or replacement with a new facility) will re-set to whatever the floating benchmark is at that time. The undrawn $5.0bn revolver is also benchmark-linked; if drawn, it would add further floating exposure not counted above.

Unrestricted cash alone ($5,391M) covers the next-24-month wall ($3,261M) **1.65x** over, without touching the undrawn revolver or TTM FCF. TTM FCF alone ($10,116M) covers it **3.1x** over. On the disclosed debt stack, this wall does not require market access to clear.

**The caveat that matters: the same cash pool is being drawn down for other things at the same time.** Uber has a $20.0bn board-authorized share buyback program (July 2025) with **$15.7bn remaining** as of Jun-30-2026 and $6,904M repurchased on a TTM basis; strict net debt rose $3.96bn (+67%) in the year to Jun-30-2026, driven by $3,997M of new long-term debt issued in Q2 FY26 alone to fund buybacks [earnings/01_historical-financials.md §2]. Layered on top, the pending Delivery Hero acquisition ($14.8bn equity value) will be funded "through existing cash on our balance sheet and new debt financing" [Q2 FY26 10-Q, Pending Acquisition of Delivery Hero], and the €14.2bn bridge facility will need to convert into permanent term debt in Q3 2026 — a refinancing event of its own, several times the size of the entire legacy wall mapped in Section 1. S&P rates Uber **BBB+** (Foreign Currency, LT), as of 2026-08-06 [Company Comparable Analysis Uber Technologies Inc.xls, Credit Health Panel tab] — solid investment grade, and the only rating in this pool (no Moody's/Fitch cross-check available); no negative rating action is disclosed. Floating-rate exposure is small (15.5% of the stack, one $2,000M tranche) — a 100bp move in SOFR changes annual interest cost on it by only ~$20M, so rate moves are not the dominant risk here; the dominant forward risk is the scale and timing of Delivery Hero-related financing landing on top of an already-shrinking cash buffer and a buyback program still running.

**Conclusion: self-funded / low refi risk** for the disclosed debt stack mapped in Section 1 — cash alone covers the next 24 months of legacy maturities multiple times over, the stack is investment-grade rated, and the near-term (12-month) wall is small ($2,136M) relative to both cash and FCF. This conclusion applies **only** to the reported gross debt as of Jun-30-2026; it does not extend to the pending €14.2bn Delivery Hero bridge facility, which is a separate, much larger, and still partly undefined refinancing exposure (see Section 5).

## 5. Refinancing Read

The legacy debt wall is small and laddered: only $2,136M (16.4% of the stack) is due within 12 months, the next tranche ($1,125M, 0%-coupon Exchangeable Notes) isn't due until May-2028, and 84.5% of the stack is fixed-rate — none of it needs market access to be repaid given $5,391M of unrestricted cash and $10,116M of TTM free cash flow. The real cost story is in the mix, not the size: about 22% of the principal base sits in two equity-linked instruments (0% and 0.875% coupons) that would face a much steeper step-up than the ~50–100bps estimated for the straight senior notes (4.67% weighted-average coupon vs. an indicative ~5.2–5.7% current market rate, Aug-2026, web-sourced and unverified) if they end up refinanced with cash debt rather than converted or exchanged into equity. **The single biggest refinancing risk is not on this schedule at all: the €14.2 billion Delivery Hero bridge facility**, executed three weeks before this report, undrawn, with no maturity date or pricing found in the extracted filing text, and explicitly intended by Uber to be replaced with permanent term debt in Q3 2026 — a refinancing event roughly the size of Uber's entire current debt stack, landing on a balance sheet that already drew down cash by $2.2bn in H1 FY26 to fund a $20bn buyback program with $15.7bn still authorized. **Under a "market closure" test (no new unsecured issuance for 12 months), Uber survives the next 12 months on the debt reported as of Jun-30-2026** — the only maturity in that window ($2,136M) is fully covered by unrestricted cash alone, without touching the $4.68bn undrawn revolver. That conclusion does **not** extend to whether Uber could complete the Delivery Hero financing plan in a closed market — the bridge facility is itself the untested variable, and its terms are not disclosed in this pool.

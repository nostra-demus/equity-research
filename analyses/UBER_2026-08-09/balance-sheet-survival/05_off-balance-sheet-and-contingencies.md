The $2.3bn standby-LC figure is the largest single component of the quantified maximum-exposure total, but it substantially **collateralizes obligations Uber has already recognized elsewhere** (insurance reserves and lease liabilities already on the balance sheet) rather than representing a fresh, uncollateralized call on capital — so treating the full $2.3bn as incremental risk overstates the picture; it is included above for completeness because the module's own convention (Section 2) records recorded-vs-maximum for every guarantee/LC line, but a reader should not add it to the litigation accrual as if both were equally "surprise" exposures.

## 5. Contingency Read

The largest **quantified** off-balance-sheet items are the $2.3bn of outstanding standby letters of credit (Jun-30-2026) and the $1.8bn UK HMRC VAT assessment Uber has already paid and booked as a receivable pending appeal — both are active, not remote, and neither is inside `01`'s debt stack. Neither, on its own, clears the module's 3x-recognized or 15%-of-equity spike threshold using only disclosed numbers: the LC balance mostly collateralizes obligations already recognized elsewhere, and the HMRC amount is an asset-impairment risk (6.4% of equity) rather than a fresh liability. What is genuinely not quantified — and cannot be filled in without inventing a number — is the "reasonably possible" tail on driver-misclassification litigation running simultaneously in the US (California AG, New Jersey), Switzerland, and France, where Uber discloses active disputes but gives no matter-by-matter dollar range beyond the blended $1.8bn accrual; for a rideshare/delivery platform with a multi-jurisdiction worker-classification exposure this thin, the true tail cannot be ruled out from this pool, and the module's "known-litigious name with off-balance-sheet exposures undisclosed" partial-data rule applies (caps solvency strength at 75 per `MODULE_RULES.md`, to be applied by `01`/`06`/`99`, not this agent).

Partial data: the aggregate "reasonably possible" loss range for active driver-classification, social-security, and other litigation beyond the $1.8bn recorded accrual is not quantified matter-by-matter in the filings reviewed — undisclosed exposures beyond the accrual cannot be ruled out for this known-litigious name.

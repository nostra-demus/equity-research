# Balance-Sheet-Survival Module — UBER (Synthesis)

## Abstract

Uber's disclosed balance sheet is lightly levered, at 1.09x net debt to EBITDA ($8.2bn net debt vs $7.5bn TTM EBITDA), though net debt doubled in six months funding a share buyback. Only 16.4% of debt matures within twelve months, and unrestricted cash alone covers it, with the rest laddered to 2054. Liquidity is a surplus — free cash flow covers the wall 4.7 times over — but no maintenance covenant is disclosed on any instrument, so true headroom is not assessable. Today's illustrative covenant would not break until EBITDA fell 73%; layered with the signed, debt-funded Delivery Hero acquisition and its €14.2 billion bridge facility, that break point collapses to roughly 30% — an ordinary recession. Verdict: Adequate, not Solid.

## 1. Solvency Verdict

- **Verdict:** Adequate
- **Net leverage (net debt / EBITDA):** **1.09x** — net debt $8,163M (strict basis, gross debt $13,033M − cash $4,870M) / TTM reported (unadjusted) EBITDA $7,474M, as of Jun-30-2026. Gross debt/EBITDA = 1.74x. **Pro-forma** (if the signed €14.2bn Delivery Hero bridge facility is drawn in full, as its funding split is not disclosed and the conservative convention applied throughout the module): **3.14x** on today's peak/latest EBITDA, **≈6.0x** on Uber's own 3-year average EBITDA ($3,927M) — a materially more levered credit than today's actual number, before any Delivery Hero-side debt or EBITDA is even added.
- **Liquidity runway:** No finite countdown — TTM free cash flow ($10,116M) covers the entire 12-month debt wall ($2,136M) **4.74x** over; $10,067M of committed, unrestricted liquidity is a second, independent line of coverage. On a conservative zero-FCF cross-check, liquidity alone funds 12-month gross obligations for **≈42.7 months**.
- **Maturity wall (% within 24 months):** **25.0%** ($3,261M of $13,033M) — fully covered by unrestricted cash ($5,391M, 1.65x over) without touching the undrawn $4.68bn revolver or FCF.
- **Tightest covenant + headroom:** **None disclosed** for any Uber debt instrument (all carry only incurrence-style, not maintenance, covenants; two instruments totaling $2.85bn — the 2028 Converts and 2028 Exchangeables — explicitly carry zero financial/operating covenants). True headroom = **Not assessable**. Illustrative labeled-assumption proxy (4.0x max net leverage, *inference, not from filings*): **+72.7%** headroom today; **−12.1% to −30.8% (breached)** pro-forma at a 30–40% EBITDA decline if the Delivery Hero bridge is drawn in full.
- **Stress break point (EBITDA decline that breaks it):** On today's actual balance sheet, the illustrative covenant does not break until EBITDA falls **72.7%**, and committed liquidity does not exhaust at any EBITDA decline mapped in this report (even a literal repeat of FY2021's negative EBITDA/FCF leaves a $6.49bn liquidity cushion). **Pro-forma, with the signed Delivery Hero bridge facility drawn in full, the same illustrative covenant breaks at just ≈30% EBITDA decline** — an ordinary recession, not a tail event.
- Solvency strength /100: **70** (cap 75 applied — off-balance-sheet exposures undisclosed for a known-litigious name; see §4)
- Liquidity runway /100: **90**
- Refinancing risk /100 *(higher = worse)*: **40** — legacy debt refinancing risk is low (self-funded, laddered, S&P BBB+), but the signed, undisclosed-terms €14.2bn Delivery Hero bridge facility is a large, real, near-term refinancing exposure that pulls this score up from what the legacy stack alone would earn (~15–20)
- Covenant headroom /100: **Not assessable** — no maintenance covenant disclosed
- Downside resilience /100: **68** — very strong on today's actual balance sheet (survives a 60% EBITDA decline and a literal FY2021 repeat with no external action required), but materially weaker on the pro-forma, already-signed acquisition scenario (illustrative covenant breaches at ~30% EBITDA decline)
- Data quality /100: **82**
- Overall usefulness /100: **75** (cap applied — no maintenance covenant disclosed)
- Biggest solvency risk (one line): the signed, debt-funded €14.2bn Delivery Hero bridge facility — undisclosed maturity and pricing — which would collapse the illustrative covenant breach point from a 73% to a ~30% EBITDA decline if drawn in full, on a debt structure that carries zero disclosed maintenance covenants to test against in the first place.

## 1A. Module Disconfirmation

- **Strongest bear point:** net debt already doubled in six months (Dec-31-2025 → Jun-30-2026, $3,416M → $7,853–8,163M) funding a discretionary buyback, before the Delivery Hero deal is even layered on; pro-forma with the signed €14.2bn bridge drawn in full, net leverage rises to 3.14x (peak EBITDA) / ~6.0x (recent-average EBITDA) and the illustrative covenant break point collapses from a 72.7% to a ~30% EBITDA decline — inside the range of an ordinary recession [`01` §6; `06` §1a, §2b].
- **Strongest bull point:** on today's actual, disclosed balance sheet, Uber is genuinely resilient — 1.09x net leverage, 16.2x EBITDA/interest coverage, S&P BBB+ rated, 84.5% fixed-rate debt, and a literal replay of Uber's own worst historical result (FY2021's negative EBITDA and negative FCF) still leaves a $6.49bn liquidity cushion (2.8x the draw) with committed liquidity of $10,067M never exhausted [`01` §5; `04` §1; `06` §2a].
- **Single killer risk:** the €14.2bn Delivery Hero bridge facility itself — executed Jul-16-2026, undrawn, with no maturity date or pricing terms located anywhere in this pool, and explicitly intended by Uber to be termed into permanent debt in Q3 2026 — layered onto a debt structure where every instrument carries only incurrence-style covenants and two of the largest ($2.85bn) carry none at all [`02` "Read this first"; `04` §2].
- **Disconfirming evidence already visible:** `05`'s finding that the "reasonably possible" tail on driver-classification and social-security litigation running simultaneously in the US, Switzerland, and France is not quantified matter-by-matter beyond the blended $1.8bn accrual — a real, undisclosed exposure for a known-litigious name that this module cannot rule out, independent of the acquisition risk [`05` §3, §5].

## 3. Reconciliation

Two internal inconsistencies were surfaced by the specialists themselves and are reconciled here, conservatively, rather than silently overridden:

1. **Vendor-vs-filing debt figure.** `01` flags that `earnings/01_historical-financials.md` reports FY2025 "Total Debt" of $12,302M — a Capital IQ combined debt-plus-leases figure that itself carries a $88M finance-lease lag error (using the stale Dec-31-2024 $222M balance instead of the correct Dec-31-2025 $310M balance) — against `01`'s own filing-sourced Note 5 basis of $10,521M (FY2025) / $13,033M (Jun-30-2026, canonical). Per §4/§5 of CLAUDE.md (filings beat vendor exports; cite the source a number actually came from), this synthesis uses `01`'s filing-reconciled figures ($13,033M gross / $8,163M net, strict, as of Jun-30-2026) as canonical throughout.
2. **Net-debt basis within `01` itself.** `01`'s Leverage Trend table (§6) uses a "Note 5 debt only" basis (excluding the $310M of finance leases) to preserve an apples-to-apples trend across periods where finance-lease balances are not disclosed for every quarter, giving a slightly different Jun-30-2026 net-debt figure ($7,853M) than the canonical strict figure ($8,163M). This is not a genuine disagreement — it is a labeled, explained trend-construction choice — and this synthesis uses the canonical $8,163M figure (Section 4/7 of `01`) throughout.

No other material disagreements exist between specialists; `02` through `06` all build consistently on `01`'s canonical gross-debt ($13,033M) and net-debt (strict, $8,163M) figures.

## 5. Survival Summary

Uber is lightly levered and its leverage trend through FY2023–FY2025 was genuinely improving (net debt/EBITDA 2.73x → 1.36x → 0.54x) as EBITDA more than tripled on margin expansion, but that reversed sharply in H1 FY2026: net debt rose $4.4 billion in six months (0.54x → 1.09x) as Uber drew $2.0 billion under a new Term Loan and spent $3.5 billion on buybacks — a real, recent re-leveraging trend that predates the Delivery Hero deal. The near-term maturity wall is small and self-funded: only 16.4% of debt is due within 12 months and unrestricted cash alone covers it 1.65x over, with 84.5% of the stack fixed-rate and laddered out to 2054 — this wall does not depend on market access. Liquidity is a genuine surplus (TTM FCF covers the 12-month wall 4.74x; committed liquidity would fund ~42.7 months of gross obligations even with zero FCF), and no maintenance covenant is disclosed for any instrument, so the tightest-covenant question cannot be answered from filings — only an illustrative, labeled proxy (4.0x max net leverage) can be tested, and on that proxy today's structure would need a 72.7% EBITDA decline to breach. None of this extends to the signed, €14.2 billion Delivery Hero acquisition bridge facility: if drawn in full — the funding split is not disclosed, so the module applies the same conservative "drawn in full" convention used throughout — pro-forma net leverage rises to 3.14x on today's peak EBITDA (and closer to 6.0x on Uber's own recent-average EBITDA), and the illustrative covenant break point collapses to roughly 30%, meaning an ordinary recession, not a tail event, could be enough to pressure it. A normal 30–40% EBITDA decline is comfortably survivable today with no waiver, asset sale, or equity raise required; the same decline is not comfortably survivable on the pro-forma, already-signed structure.

## 6A. Survival Playbook (non-speculative levers)

- **Refi actions taken:** in September 2025, Uber redeemed the higher-coupon 2027 Senior Notes ($700M) and 2028 Senior Notes ($500M), funded by new, lower-coupon 2031/2035 issuance — a proven, already-executed refinancing action, not a hypothetical [`01` §6].
- **Facility management in progress:** Uber states it "expect[s] to enter into a new revolving facility that will replace [the] existing revolving credit agreement" in Q3 2026, and separately expects to "enter into term loan facilities that will reduce the commitments under the bridge credit agreement" for Delivery Hero — both are disclosed, in-motion refinancing actions [`02` §4].
- **Discretionary buyback suspension is available:** the $20.0bn buyback program ($15.7bn remaining, $6,904M repurchased TTM) is board-authorized and discretionary, not contractually committed — it can be paused to preserve liquidity ahead of the Delivery Hero close without breaching any obligation [`03` §2].
- **No dividend to suspend:** Uber pays no dividend, so there is no dividend lever to pull or protect.
- **Asset-sale capacity:** not evidenced or authorized in this pool for debt repayment specifically — this is an absent, not a present, lever.

## 7. Note To The Final Synthesizer

- Net leverage is low and improving through FY2025 (2.73x → 0.54x, FY2023–FY2025 net debt/EBITDA) but re-leveraging fast in H1 FY2026 (0.54x → 1.09x in six months) on a debt-funded buyback — the direction matters as much as the level.
- The near-term maturity wall (16.4% within 12 months, 25.0% within 24 months) is small, laddered to 2054, and fully self-funded by unrestricted cash alone — refinancing is not exposed for the legacy debt stack.
- The liquidity runway is a genuine surplus (FCF covers the 12-month wall 4.74x; ~42.7 months of committed liquidity even with zero FCF) and depends on nothing fragile — it is cash-backed (CFO ran 160–202% of EBITDA in FY2023–FY2025), not a paper-profit proxy.
- No maintenance covenant is disclosed for any Uber debt instrument — true covenant headroom is "Not assessable." Two of the largest instruments ($2.85bn combined) explicitly carry zero financial/operating covenants. Only a labeled, illustrative 4.0x max-net-leverage proxy could be tested (+72.7% headroom today).
- The largest quantified off-balance-sheet items are $2.3bn of outstanding standby letters of credit (largely collateralizing obligations already on the balance sheet) and a $1.8bn UK HMRC VAT assessment already paid and booked as a receivable pending appeal (an asset-impairment risk, 6.4% of equity). Neither alone breaches the module's spike thresholds, but the "reasonably possible" tail on multi-jurisdiction driver-classification litigation (US, Switzerland, France) beyond the $1.8bn blended accrual is genuinely unquantified for a known-litigious name.
- **The stress break point that should carry the most weight:** on today's actual balance sheet, the illustrative covenant does not break until a 72.7% EBITDA decline and liquidity never exhausts. On the pro-forma balance sheet — with the signed, executed €14.2 billion Delivery Hero bridge facility drawn in full — the same illustrative covenant breaks at roughly a 30% EBITDA decline, which is an ordinary recession, not a tail scenario.
- A partial-data cap applied: no maintenance covenant disclosed caps covenant headroom at "Not assessable" and overall module usefulness at 75. A second cap applied: undisclosed off-balance-sheet litigation exposure for a known-litigious name caps solvency strength at 75.
- Biggest missing data point / single highest-value next data request: **the Delivery Hero bridge credit agreement's maturity date, pricing, and covenant terms (Exhibit 10.2 to the Q2 FY26 10-Q), plus Delivery Hero's own consolidating net debt and EBITDA** — together these determine whether pro-forma leverage lands near the 3.14x floor or materially worse, and whether the illustrative covenant break point is really ~30% or tighter.

## 8. Simple Summary

- Uber owes about $13.0 billion gross ($8.2 billion net of cash) against $7.5 billion of trailing profit before interest, tax, and depreciation (EBITDA) — a low 1.09x net leverage ratio, but net debt doubled in the six months to June 2026 to help fund a stock buyback.
- The debt due soonest (16.4% within a year) is fully covered by cash on hand alone; the rest is spread out to 2054, so there is no near-term refinancing scramble on the disclosed debt.
- Liquidity has no finite countdown: free cash flow alone covers the next year of debt payments almost five times over, and committed cash-plus-facilities would last roughly 42 months even with zero cash flow.
- No debt instrument discloses a real financial covenant to test against — every bond and loan uses only "customary" language, so true covenant headroom cannot be measured from the filings; two large instruments ($2.85 billion) have no covenants at all.
- The biggest off-balance-sheet exposure is a mix of $2.3 billion in letters of credit (mostly backing things already on the balance sheet) and multi-country driver-classification lawsuits whose full potential cost is not disclosed beyond a $1.8 billion combined accrual.
- Today's balance sheet survives even a 60% profit decline with room to spare; but the signed €14.2 billion Delivery Hero acquisition financing, if drawn in full as structured, would mean an ordinary 30% profit decline — not a crisis-level one — could pressure an illustrative covenant test.
- A current credit rating was available (S&P: BBB+, investment grade) and the filings were complete; the real gap is the terms of the Delivery Hero bridge loan itself, which are not yet disclosed.
- This module is useful for the master synthesizer: the current-balance-sheet read is strong and well-evidenced, but the single most important number to carry forward is the ~30% pro-forma stress break point once the acquisition financing is included.

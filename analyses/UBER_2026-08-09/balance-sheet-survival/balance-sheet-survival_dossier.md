# Balance-Sheet-Survival Module — UBER (Synthesis)

## Abstract

Uber's disclosed balance sheet is lightly levered, at 1.09x net debt to EBITDA ($8.2bn net debt vs $7.5bn TTM EBITDA), though net debt doubled in six months funding a share buyback. Only 16.4% of debt matures within twelve months, and unrestricted cash alone covers it, with the rest laddered to 2054. Liquidity is a surplus — free cash flow covers the wall 4.7 times over — but no maintenance covenant is disclosed on any instrument, so true headroom is not assessable. Today's illustrative covenant would not break until EBITDA fell 73%; layered with the signed, debt-funded Delivery Hero acquisition and its €14.2 billion bridge facility, that break point collapses to roughly 30% — an ordinary recession. Verdict: Adequate, not Solid.

## 1A. Module Disconfirmation

- **Strongest bear point:** net debt already doubled in six months (Dec-31-2025 → Jun-30-2026, $3,416M → $7,853–8,163M) funding a discretionary buyback, before the Delivery Hero deal is even layered on; pro-forma with the signed €14.2bn bridge drawn in full, net leverage rises to 3.14x (peak EBITDA) / ~6.0x (recent-average EBITDA) and the illustrative covenant break point collapses from a 72.7% to a ~30% EBITDA decline — inside the range of an ordinary recession [`01` §6; `06` §1a, §2b].
- **Strongest bull point:** on today's actual, disclosed balance sheet, Uber is genuinely resilient — 1.09x net leverage, 16.2x EBITDA/interest coverage, S&P BBB+ rated, 84.5% fixed-rate debt, and a literal replay of Uber's own worst historical result (FY2021's negative EBITDA and negative FCF) still leaves a $6.49bn liquidity cushion (2.8x the draw) with committed liquidity of $10,067M never exhausted [`01` §5; `04` §1; `06` §2a].
- **Single killer risk:** the €14.2bn Delivery Hero bridge facility itself — executed Jul-16-2026, undrawn, with no maturity date or pricing terms located anywhere in this pool, and explicitly intended by Uber to be termed into permanent debt in Q3 2026 — layered onto a debt structure where every instrument carries only incurrence-style covenants and two of the largest ($2.85bn) carry none at all [`02` "Read this first"; `04` §2].

## 5. Survival Summary

Uber is lightly levered and its leverage trend through FY2023–FY2025 was genuinely improving (net debt/EBITDA 2.73x → 1.36x → 0.54x) as EBITDA more than tripled on margin expansion, but that reversed sharply in H1 FY2026: net debt rose $4.4 billion in six months (0.54x → 1.09x) as Uber drew $2.0 billion under a new Term Loan and spent $3.5 billion on buybacks — a real, recent re-leveraging trend that predates the Delivery Hero deal. The near-term maturity wall is small and self-funded: only 16.4% of debt is due within 12 months and unrestricted cash alone covers it 1.65x over, with 84.5% of the stack fixed-rate and laddered out to 2054 — this wall does not depend on market access. Liquidity is a genuine surplus (TTM FCF covers the 12-month wall 4.74x; committed liquidity would fund ~42.7 months of gross obligations even with zero FCF), and no maintenance covenant is disclosed for any instrument, so the tightest-covenant question cannot be answered from filings — only an illustrative, labeled proxy (4.0x max net leverage) can be tested, and on that proxy today's structure would need a 72.7% EBITDA decline to breach. None of this extends to the signed, €14.2 billion Delivery Hero acquisition bridge facility: if drawn in full — the funding split is not disclosed, so the module applies the same conservative "drawn in full" convention used throughout — pro-forma net leverage rises to 3.14x on today's peak EBITDA (and closer to 6.0x on Uber's own recent-average EBITDA), and the illustrative covenant break point collapses to roughly 30%, meaning an ordinary recession, not a tail event, could be enough to pressure it. A normal 30–40% EBITDA decline is comfortably survivable today with no waiver, asset sale, or equity raise required; the same decline is not comfortably survivable on the pro-forma, already-signed structure.

## 6A. Survival Playbook (non-speculative levers)

- **Refi actions taken:** in September 2025, Uber redeemed the higher-coupon 2027 Senior Notes ($700M) and 2028 Senior Notes ($500M), funded by new, lower-coupon 2031/2035 issuance — a proven, already-executed refinancing action, not a hypothetical [`01` §6].
- **Facility management in progress:** Uber states it "expect[s] to enter into a new revolving facility that will replace [the] existing revolving credit agreement" in Q3 2026, and separately expects to "enter into term loan facilities that will reduce the commitments under the bridge credit agreement" for Delivery Hero — both are disclosed, in-motion refinancing actions [`02` §4].
- **Discretionary buyback suspension is available:** the $20.0bn buyback program ($15.7bn remaining, $6,904M repurchased TTM) is board-authorized and discretionary, not contractually committed — it can be paused to preserve liquidity ahead of the Delivery Hero close without breaching any obligation [`03` §2].
- **No dividend to suspend:** Uber pays no dividend, so there is no dividend lever to pull or protect.
- **Asset-sale capacity:** not evidenced or authorized in this pool for debt repayment specifically — this is an absent, not a present, lever.

Post-deal (assuming the bridge is drawn in full, per the conservative convention used throughout `01`/`02`/`04`), only a **30% EBITDA decline — an ordinary recession, not a tail event — is already enough to breach it.** This table does not include: (i) Delivery Hero's own EBITDA (which, if positive and material, would improve the ratio — not sourced in this pool), (ii) Delivery Hero's own consolidating net debt (which, if any, would worsen it), or (iii) the higher interest cost of terming out the bridge into permanent debt (which would push EBITDA/interest lower than shown). None of these can be filled in from this pool — they are the open questions that determine whether the pro-forma structure is closer to the 3.14x floor or materially worse.

## 4. Survival Read

On today's actual balance sheet, Uber's structure does not break at any EBITDA decline this report can plausibly construct: the illustrative 4.0x covenant (no real covenant is disclosed [`04`]) needs a 72.7% EBITDA decline to breach, committed liquidity ($10,067M) does not exhaust on an EBITDA decline alone, and even a literal replay of Uber's own worst historical result (FY2021's negative EBITDA and negative FCF) leaves a $6,494M liquidity cushion — a 30–40% EBITDA decline, an ordinary recession rather than a tail event, is comfortably survivable with no waiver, asset sale, or equity raise. That comfortable picture changes entirely once the pending €14.2bn Delivery Hero acquisition bridge is layered on (Section 2b): assuming it is drawn in full — the same conservative convention `01`, `02`, and `04` all use, since the actual cash/debt funding split is not disclosed — pro-forma net leverage rises from 1.09x to 3.14x on today's peak EBITDA and to roughly 6.0x on Uber's own recent-average EBITDA, and the illustrative covenant break point collapses from a 72.7% EBITDA decline to just **22%**. Under a market-closure test (no new unsecured issuance for 12 months), today's legacy debt stack clears every maturity from cash alone with no market access needed; the untested variable is whether the bridge facility itself — undisclosed maturity, undisclosed pricing, and explicitly intended to be termed into permanent debt in Q3 2026 — can be refinanced if unsecured markets are shut, since neither this report nor `02` can assess a facility whose terms are not in the pool. Uber is **not** net cash (strict net debt $8,163M positive per `01`), so the net-cash framing does not apply; today's balance sheet is genuinely resilient to an organic EBITDA shock, but that resilience is being spent down by a large, discretionary, debt-funded acquisition that this stress test's central finding is: the real downside risk to Uber's balance sheet is not a demand-driven earnings decline, it is what the company chooses to do with its own balance sheet.

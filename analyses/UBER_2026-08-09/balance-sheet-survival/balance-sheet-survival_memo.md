## What This Module Found

Uber's balance sheet as reported is lightly levered. Net debt (total debt minus cash) is $8.2bn against $7.5bn of trailing EBITDA — the cash profit before interest, tax, and depreciation — a low 1.09x net-leverage ratio [`01` §4/§7]. But net debt roughly doubled in the six months to June 2026 to help fund a stock buyback, so the direction is getting worse even before the acquisition [`01` §6].

The debt due soonest is not a problem: only 16.4% matures within a year, and cash on hand alone covers it 1.65x, with the rest laddered out to 2054 [`02`]. Liquidity is a genuine surplus — free cash flow (operating cash after all capital spending) covers the next year of debt payments 4.74x over, and committed cash-plus-facilities would last roughly 42 months even with zero cash flow [`03`].

The single most important driver of the "Adequate, not Solid" verdict is the signed €14.2bn Delivery Hero bridge facility (a short-term loan meant to be refinanced into permanent debt) [`02`, `04`]. It was executed 16 July 2026, is undrawn, and its maturity, pricing, and covenant terms are not disclosed anywhere in the pool. The module applies the conservative "drawn in full" convention: on that basis pro-forma net leverage rises to 3.14x on today's peak EBITDA and closer to 6.0x on Uber's own recent-average EBITDA [`01` §6; `06`].

The single most important risk follows directly: on today's actual balance sheet an illustrative covenant test (a labeled 4.0x max-leverage proxy — inference, not from filings) would not break until EBITDA fell 72.7%. Layer in the bridge loan drawn in full and that break point collapses to roughly 30% — an ordinary recession, not a tail event [`06`; `07`]. A separate, unquantified risk is multi-country driver-classification and social-security litigation (US, Switzerland, France) whose full cost is not disclosed beyond a $1.8bn combined accrual [`05` §3, §5].

## The Specialists, Briefly

- **Solvency data triage** → sufficient; full pool, zero extraction failures. Only real gaps are covenant-EBITDA addback detail and change-of-control/cross-default triggers — not blocking.
- **Capital structure and leverage** → not net cash; net debt $8,163M (strict basis), 1.09x. Net debt roughly doubled in H1 FY26 on a debt-funded buyback, and the €14.2bn bridge could roughly double gross debt again.
- **Maturity wall and refinancing** → low refinancing risk on the disclosed stack (16.4% due within 12 months, cash covers it); the real exposure — the €14.2bn bridge — isn't on the maturity schedule at all.
- **Liquidity runway** → free cash flow surplus, no finite countdown; but the same cash pool is being spent down by a $20bn buyback ($15.7bn still authorized) and the pending deal.
- **Coverage and covenants** → coverage very high (16.2x EBITDA/interest); no maintenance covenant disclosed anywhere, and two of the largest instruments ($2.85bn combined) carry none at all.
- **Off-balance-sheet and contingencies** → no single item breaches the module's spike thresholds; $1.8bn litigation/tax accrual plus an unquantified driver-classification tail caps solvency strength at 75.
- **Downside stress test** → today's balance sheet survives every plausible and historical stress; the signed-acquisition scenario does not (illustrative covenant break point collapses from 72.7% to ~30% EBITDA decline).

The module reconciled two internal inconsistencies conservatively (both in the Reconciliation section) and re-adjudication is not needed: it uses filing-sourced debt figures over the vendor export ($13,033M gross / $8,163M net, strict, as of 30 June 2026), per the source hierarchy.

## What Would Change This Read

**Would strengthen it:** disclosure of an actual maintenance covenant with real headroom; confirmation the Delivery Hero bridge is funded mostly with cash or equity rather than debt; a slowdown or pause in the buyback to preserve cash before the deal closes.

**Would weaken it:** the bridge drawn in full as modeled; Delivery Hero's own net debt turning out to be material; an adverse resolution of the driver-classification litigation beyond the $1.8bn accrual; a ratings downgrade from the current S&P BBB+ (investment grade).

**Data needed to resolve it:** the actual covenant-EBITDA definition and threshold, if any; the Delivery Hero bridge's maturity, pricing, and covenant terms; Delivery Hero's own consolidating net debt and EBITDA; a second rating agency's view (only S&P is in this pool).

---

## Bottom Line

- **Verdict: Adequate, not Solid.** Today's balance sheet is strong and well-evidenced; the acquisition financing is what holds it back from Solid.
- **Why it could be better than it looks:** on the actual disclosed balance sheet Uber is genuinely resilient — 1.09x net leverage, 16.2x interest coverage, S&P BBB+, 84.5% fixed-rate debt; a literal replay of its worst year (FY2021's negative EBITDA and cash flow) still leaves a $6.49bn liquidity cushion.
- **Why it could be worse:** net debt already doubled in six months on a discretionary buyback, before the deal; with the signed €14.2bn bridge drawn in full, net leverage rises to 3.14x–~6.0x and an ordinary ~30% profit decline could pressure the illustrative covenant.
- **What evidence is missing:** the Delivery Hero bridge's actual terms (maturity, pricing, covenants) and Delivery Hero's own net debt and EBITDA — plus whether any real maintenance covenant exists at all.
- **The one thing to watch next:** the Delivery Hero bridge credit agreement terms (Exhibit 10.2 to the Q2 FY26 10-Q) and Delivery Hero's consolidating leverage — these decide whether pro-forma leverage lands near the 3.14x floor or materially worse.

## Plain-English Glossary

- **Net debt** — total borrowings minus cash on hand; "strict basis" nets only cash, not other investments.
- **EBITDA** — a rough cash-profit measure: earnings before interest, tax, depreciation, and amortization.
- **Net leverage (net debt / EBITDA)** — how many years of that cash profit it would take to repay net debt; 1.09x means about one year.
- **Free cash flow** — operating cash left after all capital spending; the real cash the business throws off.
- **Bridge facility** — a short-term loan used to close a deal, meant to be refinanced into permanent debt later.
- **Maintenance covenant** — a financial test a borrower must keep passing (e.g. leverage below a set level) or the loan can be called; Uber discloses none, so headroom can't be measured.
- **Coverage (EBITDA/interest)** — how many times cash profit covers the interest bill; 16.2x is high.
- **Pro-forma** — figures adjusted as if a not-yet-completed transaction (here, drawing the bridge) had already happened.

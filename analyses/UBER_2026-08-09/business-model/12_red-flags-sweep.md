## 2. New Red Flags

| Red Flag | Why It Matters | Evidence | Severity /100 *(higher = worse)* |
|---|---|---|---:|
| A meaningful share of reported operating cash flow growth comes from a fast-growing self-insurance reserve build, not pure operating cash generation | Uber's short- and long-term insurance reserves (auto liability, uninsured/underinsured motorist, workers' comp, general liability) grew from $4,754M (Dec-2022) to $6,986M (Dec-2023) to $9,796M (Dec-2024) to $12,463M (Dec-2025) — a 162% increase in three years versus 63% revenue growth over the same period. The company's own MD&A states the "increase in cash from working capital" of $2.4bn (FY2024) and $2.2bn (FY2025) — roughly a fifth to a third of that year's total operating cash flow ($7.1bn FY2024, $10.1bn FY2025) — was "primarily driven by an increase in our accrued insurance reserves...due to liabilities recorded during the period exceeding claims paid out." This is a self-insurance float: cash is retained today against claims that must be paid out later, and the filing separately discloses Uber "also has requirements to post collateral for current and future claim settlement obligations with certain of our insurance carriers, which may have a significant impact on our unrestricted cash." Capital-allocation-governance's working-capital row characterized OCF growth as "genuine cash-conversion improvement, not working-capital-funded earnings" — the company's own MD&A language nuances that read: a material share of the OCF increase is a growing self-insurance liability build, not receivables collection or margin expansion. | [FY25 10-K, Schedule II — Valuation and Qualifying Accounts, insurance reserves rollforward, p.46420–46440 (FY2023 beginning $4,754M, additions $3,544M, deductions -$1,526M, ending $6,986M; FY2024 beginning $6,986M, additions $4,489M, deductions -$1,696M, ending $9,796M; FY2025 beginning $9,796M, additions $4,879M, deductions -$2,421M, ending $12,463M)]; [FY25 10-K, MD&A — Liquidity and Capital Resources, p.13869–13899 ("a $2.2 billion increase in cash from working capital...primarily driven by an increase in our accrued insurance reserves primarily due to liabilities recorded during the period exceeding claims paid out" for FY2025; same language for the $2.4bn FY2024 figure)]; [FY25 10-K, Item 1A Risk Factors, p.6935–6978 ("requirements to post collateral for current and future claim settlement obligations with certain of our insurance carriers, which may have a significant impact on our unrestricted cash and cash equivalents available for general business purposes"; "the ultimate liability could be in excess of our reserves")] | 55 |
| A recurring cost line labeled "not indicative of ongoing operating performance" has recurred at material size for at least two consecutive years, inflating Adjusted EBITDA | Uber's Adjusted EBITDA reconciliation excludes a line item, "Legal, non-income tax, and regulatory reserve changes and settlements," which the filing itself describes as "primarily related to certain significant legal proceedings or governmental investigations related to worker classification definitions, or tax agencies challenging our non-income tax positions" and states these are "distinct from normal, recurring legal, non-income tax and regulatory matters." The dollar amounts excluded were $1,123M in FY2024 (17.3% of that year's $6,484M Adjusted EBITDA) and $564M in FY2025 (6.5% of that year's $8,730M Adjusted EBITDA) — a combined $1,687M excluded from two consecutive years of the company's primary non-GAAP profitability metric. A cost that recurs at double-digit-percentage-of-Adjusted-EBITDA size for two straight years is not what "non-recurring" ordinarily means, and it directly inflates the Adjusted EBITDA margin trend that business-quality's margin-stability and segment-map's segment-margin reads both build on. This sits alongside — and reinforces — the disqualifier-scan's already-noted discontinuation of the consolidated Adjusted-EBITDA-to-GAAP reconciliation starting Q1 FY2026: taken together, the two items show a pattern of the company's headline profitability metric becoming both harder to fully reconcile to GAAP and increasingly dependent on excluding a cost line that keeps showing up. | [FY25 10-K, MD&A — Key Terms for Our Key Metrics and Non-GAAP Financial Measures, p.13030–13070 ("Legal, non-income tax, and regulatory reserve changes and settlements" defined; "distinct from normal, recurring legal, non-income tax and regulatory matters and related expenses incurred in our ongoing operating performance")]; [FY25 10-K, MD&A — Adjusted EBITDA reconciliation table, p.13380–13475 (FY2024 $1,123M, FY2025 $564M; FY2024 total Adjusted EBITDA $6,484M, FY2025 total Adjusted EBITDA $8,730M)] | 65 |

## 3. Most Severe New Flag

The recurring "non-recurring" Legal, non-income tax, and regulatory reserve changes and settlements exclusion is the most material of the three. It is not a one-off: it appeared at $1,123M in FY2024 and $564M in FY2025 — 17.3% and 6.5% of the company's own reported Adjusted EBITDA in those years respectively — while being defined in the filing itself as "distinct from normal, recurring" items. Every other business-model report that leans on Adjusted EBITDA margin trend (business-quality's margin-stability factor, segment-map's segment margin reads, capital-allocation-governance's leverage-to-EBITDA ratios) is implicitly trusting a metric that has excluded a double-digit-percentage-of-EBITDA cost line two years running. Combined with the disqualifier-scan's already-flagged discontinuation of the consolidated Adjusted-EBITDA-to-GAAP reconciliation starting Q1 FY2026, this is a genuine data-quality risk to any forward view built on Uber's Adjusted EBITDA trajectory, not just a labeling quibble.

RF-RFS-001 (aggressive accounting practice pattern)

## 4. Cross-Cutting Patterns

Three items line up into one pattern the synthesizer should weight together rather than separately: (1) the disqualifier-scan's already-noted discontinuation of the full Adjusted-EBITDA-to-GAAP reconciliation starting Q1 FY2026, (2) the "Legal, non-income tax, and regulatory reserve changes and settlements" line excluded from Adjusted EBITDA at a double-digit percentage of that metric for two straight years despite being labeled non-recurring, and (3) a growing self-insurance reserve build that the company's own MD&A names as the primary driver of recent operating-cash-flow "working capital" gains. None of the three is a proven misstatement or a going-concern issue — the auditor's opinion is clean and the insurance-reserve development has actually been mildly favorable in FY2024–FY2025 (per Schedule II footnote 1) — but together they describe a company whose two most-watched performance metrics (Adjusted EBITDA and operating cash flow) both carry real, quantified, recurring adjustments that a reader taking the headline numbers at face value would miss.

## 2. Classification

**Capital allocation concerns.**

Day-to-day capital discipline has genuinely improved: buybacks now outrun stock-based-compensation dilution (share count down from a 2,091mm peak in Mar-2025 to 2,036mm by Mar-2026), leverage is modest (1.4x LTM Total Debt/EBITDA before the pending deal), the auditor is stable and unqualified, and cash conversion is strengthening. But this is overridden by a clear serial-acquirer pattern: Uber has done multiple material deals every year since 2019 (Careem, Postmates, Drizly — later shut down — Transplace, Foodpanda Taiwan, Trendyol GO, pending Getir), and on Jul-16-2026 signed the largest deal in its history — a $14.8bn equity-value takeover of Delivery Hero SE financed by a €14.2bn ($15.3bn) committed debt bridge that would roughly double gross indebtedness if drawn. Per this module's rejector-filter rule (CLAUDE.md §24, Filter 4), a debt-funded deal of this scale relative to the company's own balance sheet caps this row's severity at ≥70 and the overall Capital Allocation Score at 50/100 regardless of how clean the buyback/leverage/auditor signals are on their own.

## 3. Most Material Signal

The acquisition pattern — specifically the pending Delivery Hero SE takeover — is the signal that would most change this classification if it deteriorates further. If the deal closes on the announced terms (equity value $14.8bn, financed via a €14.2bn debt bridge, expected close H2 2027), Uber's leverage would roughly double from the current 1.4x Total Debt/EBITDA, its multi-year record of shrinking net debt would reverse, and the newly-built buyback discipline (net share count falling since early 2025) would compete directly with debt service for cash. This single transaction is large enough, relative to Uber's own $140-190bn market capitalization and ~$12.4bn pre-deal debt stack, to overwhelm every other positive signal in this table — the buyback trend, the low leverage, and the stable auditor — if execution or integration disappoints, echoing the earlier Careem/Postmates/Drizly/Transplace cycle where at least one large deal (Drizly) was wound down within roughly three years of closing.

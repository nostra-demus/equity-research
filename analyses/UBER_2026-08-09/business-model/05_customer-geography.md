## 4. Read

Uber's concentration risk is geographic, not customer-based: no single customer clears even the 10% disclosure threshold because revenue comes from hundreds of millions of individual, pay-per-transaction riders and eaters [FY25 10-K, Note 2]. Geographically, the business still leans on the United States and Canada for just over half of revenue (50.9% in FY2025) and the UK alone for a further 20.4% — together 71.3% of revenue sits in two of the four regions — though the US share has been shrinking each year as EMEA (led by the UK) grows faster [FY25 10-K, Note 13]. This geographic weight is naked in the sense that it rests on transaction volume, not contracts — there is nothing locking in next year's US or UK Gross Bookings — and the company's own risk factors flag both a "large metropolitan areas" dependency and, within Delivery, an unquantified reliance on "a limited number of large restaurant groups and other merchants" [FY25 10-K, Item 1A]. The single biggest dependency the synthesizer should know about is the US + UK combination (66.1% of FY2025 revenue) sitting inside a business model where a single regulatory or accounting change in either market — as already happened with the UK Mobility revenue-recognition change that cut Q2 FY26 reported revenue by $1.1 billion even as Gross Bookings kept growing [FY26 Q2 10-Q, p.17094–17099] — can move consolidated results without any change in underlying demand.

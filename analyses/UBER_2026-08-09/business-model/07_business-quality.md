## 4. Read

Uber is a capital-light, high-frequency two-sided marketplace that took roughly a decade to become durably profitable (consolidated EBITDA margin only turned positive in FY2022 and has since risen steadily to 12.1% in FY2025) but operates in a structurally crowded field with low switching costs on both the rider and Driver side, active driver-classification litigation across multiple countries, and an unresolved autonomous-vehicle transition where Waymo already runs a competing robotaxi fleet. This reads closer to a scaled, asset-light operating platform than a durable, hard-to-dislodge compounder — the moat, to the extent one exists, rests on network liquidity and habit rather than switching costs or regulatory protection. The industry rate-of-change / disruption-risk row scored 32 (≤40), so this thesis is closer to a sector / technology-cycle bet than a durable compounder (CLAUDE.md §24, Filter 5): the eventual balance of power between platform aggregators (Uber) and vertically integrated AV operators (Waymo) is not yet knowable, and that uncertainty should cap conviction independent of Uber's current financial performance.

RF-BQ-005 (fast-changing industry: rate-of-change ≤40)

The single quality factor a buyer should watch over the next 24 months is regulatory dependence — specifically driver-classification outcomes in the US, EU, and other active jurisdictions (score 28, the joint-weakest factor). FY2025 EBITDA margin of 12.1% and Mobility segment Adjusted EBITDA margin of 26.6% are near-peak, post-recovery readings on a business that ran negative EBITDA margins for its first six disclosed years (FY2016–FY2021) [Financials_Annual.xls, Ratios tab]; the through-cycle level is meaningfully lower than the current print once the one severe demand shock on record (FY2020 revenue down 14.3%, COVID) and the still-cyclical Freight segment (FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA margin -0.6%, "challenging freight market cycle") are weighted in [FY25 10-K, MD&A p.57]. A moderate Cyclicality score of 47 should be read alongside these peak-adjacent margins, not separately from them — the moat and DCF layers should treat FY2025's margin level as a recent high-water mark still building a multi-year track record, not a stabilized steady state.

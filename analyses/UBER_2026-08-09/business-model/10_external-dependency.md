# External Dependency Check — UBER

## 1. Dependency Table

**Note on direction:** the risk score in Section 4 is inverted — higher number means MORE dangerous external dependence, not better.

Uber does not publish a formal "X% move in currency = $Y revenue/EPS impact" sensitivity table (unlike commodity-heavy industrials); the FX read above is inferred from the reported-vs-constant-currency growth gap, which is Level 4–5 evidence (the filing's own reconciliation) but not a stated coefficient.

## 3. Classification

**Partly externally driven** — Uber has real, working management levers: it does not own vehicles (fuel/vehicle-cost risk sits with Drivers, not Uber), it hedges transaction-level FX with forward contracts, it can and does adjust fares, service fees, and Driver/Courier incentives to manage supply and demand, and it has repeatedly defended its independent-contractor model in court and at the ballot box (Prop 22 in California, the 2024 Massachusetts settlement, favorable 2025 French Supreme Court rulings). But the exposure is not cosmetic: government tax/labor policy has already cut a full quarter's revenue by roughly 8% in one country alone (the UK VAT change), a US-style employee reclassification remains explicitly disclosed as capable of forcing Uber to "fundamentally change" its business model, ~49% of revenue is non-US-dollar, and one full segment (Freight, ~10% of revenue) tracks an external industrial cycle Uber does not control. This is a company with strong internal levers sitting on top of a policy and regulatory environment that is actively, not hypothetically, moving results.

## 4. External Dependency Risk Score

**54/100** (inverted scale — higher = worse / more dangerous external dependence)

This sits in the "material external exposure, mixed mitigation" band. It is pulled up from the bottom of that band by government policy already having a demonstrated, quantified P&L effect (the UK VAT/business-model change) rather than being a purely hypothetical risk-factor line, and by the driver-classification risk being explicitly disclosed as capable of forcing a "fundamental" business-model change if it goes against Uber in a major market. It is kept out of the 61–80 "mostly externally driven" band because Uber's asset-light model, active FX hedging, segment mix skewed toward the two faster-growing and less-cyclical segments (Mobility + Delivery = ~90% of FY2025 revenue), and a real track record of winning or settling classification fights (Prop 22, Massachusetts, the 2025 French rulings) give management levers that a pure pass-through business would not have.

## 5. The Single Biggest Lever

Government/regulatory policy on Driver classification and revenue-recognition rules is the single biggest lever: the UK's January 2026 VAT/business-model change already cut one quarter's revenue by $1.1 billion (about 8% of that quarter's $14.2 billion) in a single mid-sized market, and the FY25 10-K discloses that a similar employee-reclassification ruling in a major market such as the United States "would require us to fundamentally change our business model" — a shock of that kind in Uber's largest market (US&CAN was 50.9% of FY2025 revenue) would do more damage than an equivalent 20% adverse move in FX, fuel costs, or the freight cycle.

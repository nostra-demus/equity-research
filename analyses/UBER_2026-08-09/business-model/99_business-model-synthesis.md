# Business Model Reality Check — UBER (Synthesis)

## Abstract

Uber runs a two-sided marketplace that matches riders, eaters, and shippers with independent drivers, couriers, and carriers, taking a fee on each transaction without owning vehicles. Mobility supplies 57% of FY2025 revenue and 69% of segment profit, and its contribution margin on Gross Bookings improved to 8.10% from 7.83%. The strongest positive is capital efficiency: capex is roughly 0.6% of revenue, and Uber is the only one of its three named peers (Lyft, DiDi, Bolt) with a positive EBIT margin (12.1% LTM). The strongest negative is durability: five-year average return on capital sits at just 0.85% against a roughly 9.7% cost of capital, and a pending $14.8 billion debt-funded Delivery Hero deal caps the governance score. No disqualifier triggered; this is an average business worth deeper work only if valuation is cheap, given a narrow, still-unproven moat and open driver-classification and autonomous-vehicle risk.

## 3. Reconciliation

- **Mobility FY2024 Adjusted EBITDA margin — 22.4% vs. 25.9%.** `07_business-quality.md` and `09_moat.md` both cite "22.4% (FY24) to 26.6% (FY25)" from FY25 10-K, Note 13, p.114. `03_segment-map.md` and `04_unit-economics.md` independently compute 25.9% from the same underlying dollars ($6,497M / $25,087M). `04_unit-economics.md` flags this explicitly as an arithmetic error upstream, not a different figure — the recomputed math ($6,497M ÷ $25,087M = 25.9%) is verifiable and internally consistent with the FY25 figure (26.6%) showing a plausible ~90bp year-over-year improvement, whereas 22.4%→26.6% would imply an implausible ~420bp jump in one year that no other evidence in the pool supports. **Reconciled view: 25.9% is the correct FY2024 Mobility Adjusted EBITDA margin.** The 22.4% figure carried in `07_business-quality.md` and `09_moat.md` should be read as an upstream transcription error; it does not change either report's underlying verdict (both already describe the trend as "improving" and treat FY2025's level as a recent high, not a stable floor).
- **Return on capital — CIQ vendor figure (9.4%/10.6%) vs. own-calculated figure (14.7%).** `09_moat.md` already surfaced and resolved this: the vendor's exact denominator/tax treatment cannot be fully reconciled from the pool, so per the conservative default (CLAUDE.md §4) the lower, vendor-sourced figure is used as the primary read for the moat verdict, with the own-calculated figure shown for transparency. This synthesis carries forward the same conservative choice — the moat verdict's "at-to-modestly-above cost of capital" read uses the lower CIQ figures.

- **Strongest business-model positive:** Mobility's contribution margin on Gross Bookings is positive and rising (7.83% FY24 → 8.10% FY25), inside a structurally asset-light model — capex is ~0.6% of FY2025 revenue and Uber owns no vehicles or trucks, the strongest single quality factor scored (85/100) [`04_unit-economics.md`; `07_business-quality.md`].
- **Strongest business-model negative:** Business quality's aggregate score (46/100) is capped by two factors the company's own risk factors admit directly — competitive intensity (28/100: "well-established and low-cost alternatives... low barriers to entry, low switching costs") and regulatory dependence (28/100: driver reclassification could force a "fundamental" business-model change) [`07_business-quality.md`]. - **Most important segment:** Mobility — 57.0% of FY2025 revenue, 69.1% of segment profit, and the segment with the clearest evidenced (if partial) unit-economics trend [`03_segment-map.md`; `04_unit-economics.md`].
- **Cleanest unit-economics read:** Mobility's contribution margin on $1 of Gross Bookings (the closest disclosed proxy to a per-trip figure, since Uber does not break out Mobility-only trip counts). - **Where the company sits vs. named peers:** Top of the three CIQ-covered peers on margin and capital efficiency — LTM gross margin 40.8% and EBIT margin 12.1% beat Lyft (35.6% / -2.2%) and DiDi (15.6% / -2.6%); Uber is the only one with a positive, disclosed return on capital [`09_moat.md`, §3–4].

## 5. Simple Summary

- **What it does:** Runs an app that matches riders, eaters, and shippers with independent drivers, couriers, and trucking carriers — Uber owns none of the vehicles.
- **How it makes money:** Takes a cut (a "take rate") of every ride and delivery booked through the app — about 30% on Mobility, 19% on Delivery, and near-zero (pass-through) on Freight, roughly 27% blended in FY2025.
- **Whether each new unit creates value:** Yes on the one measure Uber discloses — each dollar of Mobility Gross Bookings earned 8.10 cents of segment profit in FY2025, up from 7.83 cents in FY2024 — but the strict test (does the profit from each new rider or driver beat what it cost to win them) cannot be checked because Uber doesn't publish that data.
- **Which segment matters most:** Mobility — more than half of revenue and over two-thirds of segment profit; Delivery is real and growing faster but earns less; Freight is small and currently loses money.
- **Whether it has a moat, and against whom:** A narrow, still-young moat built on network effects, scale, and cost advantage against Lyft, DiDi, and Bolt — but the company's own filings admit riders and drivers can and do switch platforms with no lock-in, and the moat has only cleared its cost of capital for the last one to two years, not through a full economic cycle.
- **What external variables it depends on:** Government and regulatory policy above all (driver-classification rulings, tax/VAT rule changes already cut one quarter's revenue by $1.1 billion in the UK alone), plus roughly half of revenue converting from non-US-dollar currencies and a small but genuinely cyclical Freight segment.
- **Whether capital is allocated well:** Mixed — buybacks and low leverage look disciplined day to day, but a serial-acquisition pattern culminating in a $14.8 billion, debt-funded takeover of Delivery Hero caps the governance score at 50/100.
- **Whether it deserves deeper work:** Only if the price is cheap enough to compensate for a mixed-quality, narrow-moat business carrying open regulatory, competitive, and accounting-quality risk.

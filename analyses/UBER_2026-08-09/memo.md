## 2. What the Company Does

Uber runs the app that matches riders, eaters, and shippers with independent drivers, couriers, and trucking carriers. It owns none of the vehicles. It takes a cut of every transaction.

The revenue model, as a simple formula:

> **Revenue ≈ Gross Bookings × take rate.** Gross Bookings were $193.5bn in FY2025 (the total dollar value of all trips and orders), and Uber keeps roughly 27% of that as its own revenue.

Because it owns no cars or trucks, it needs very little of its own money to grow: capital spending (capex) is about 0.6% of revenue. Mobility (ride-hailing) is the biggest piece — 57% of FY2025 revenue and 69% of segment profit. Delivery is next; Freight is small and loss-making.

## 3. The Variant Perception (What We See That Others May Not)

Edge score: **35/100** — below the 50 bar that would count as a proven edge. This is a real, quantified divergence from the crowd, but not strong enough to build a high-conviction position on.

- **What everyone already knows:** Uber is the largest, most profitable ride-hailing and delivery platform, growing Gross Bookings above 20% for four straight quarters, and is buying Delivery Hero in the biggest deal in its history.
- **What is probably already priced in:** A slowing, not speeding-up, growth story. Working backward from today's price (a reverse-DCF, which asks what growth the price already assumes), the market is only pricing in a 5.05% ten-year free-cash-flow growth rate and a 10.78% long-run profit margin — a margin Uber has already beaten (its trailing EBIT margin is 12.13%). Revenue estimates were also cut 1.04% in the three days after the last quarterly print.
- **What the market may be missing — two things pulling in opposite directions:** (1) The reported revenue slowdown (from 18% to 12.2% growth) is largely a one-time UK accounting change, not weaker demand — real underlying growth may be closer to 20–25%, which the market may be under-crediting. (2) At the same time, the "clean four-quarter profit-beat streak" the market trusts leans on a company-defined profit number that still adds back a recurring "one-off" legal reserve (17.3% of adjusted EBITDA in FY2024, 6.5% in FY2025), and the company stopped fully explaining that number starting Q1 FY2026. That is a real quality gap the beat-streak story does not price.
- **What would prove we are right:** The Q3 FY2026 print (Nov-03-2026) showing a genuine, UK-adjusted cost improvement without a legal-reserve reversal, plus the FY2026 annual report restoring the full profit reconciliation. Checkable within roughly six months.

Because these two reads pull in opposite directions — one bullish, one bearish — this is not a high-conviction call either way. That is why the edge score is only 35.

## 4. Why It Could Work — Bull Case

1. **Volume-driven margin inflection.** Trips and Gross Bookings are genuinely speeding up — 24% growth (constant-currency) for four straight quarters. The UK-adjusted cost-of-revenue ratio improved 377 basis points (3.77 percentage points) in Q2 FY2026 alone — a bigger improvement than the entire quarter's net profit-margin gain. If that leverage holds while volume compounds, the asset-light model can earn more on each dollar. `[earnings/03]`
2. **The multiple could recover.** Uber's EV/Sales multiple (enterprise value divided by sales — how many dollars the market pays per dollar of revenue) has fallen from a 5-year average of ~3.5x to 2.71x. A partial return toward its own history implies real upside: the own-history method points to $87.37/share, 28.1% above the base fair value. `[valuation/02]`
3. **Share-count reduction.** Uber only started genuinely shrinking its share count in the last 15 months — down 2.66% in that window. Fewer shares means each remaining share owns more of the company. `[mgmt-gov/02]`
4. **Debt optionality on the deal.** Uber says it "expects to enter into term loan facilities that will reduce the commitments under the bridge." If the Delivery Hero purchase is funded partly with cash or equity rather than all debt, the leverage picture improves materially. `[bs-survival/02]`

The strongest single counter to our own "no position" call: the market may already be discounting both Critical flags more than the evidence supports — the lawsuit rests only on unverified web articles (confidence 2/5), and Uber's four-year delivery record argues for management competence. A clean court ruling would decouple the "good business" story from the "unresolved integrity" cap.

## 5. Why It Could Fail — Bear Case & The Killer Risk

1. **Board-wide lawsuit (RF-MGT-005, Critical).** A federal shareholder lawsuit names the CEO, the Chairman, and every sitting director, alleging the board ignored an internal safety-risk model for five years and that its 2024–2026 proxy statements about safety oversight were false. Unproven (web-sourced only, confidence 2/5) — but it names the exact people this report is judging. `[mgmt-gov/01, 05, 06]`
2. **Debt-funded serial acquisitions (RF-CAP-004, Critical).** The $14.8bn Delivery Hero deal is mostly borrowed and follows a poor M&A record: Drizly ($1.1bn) was shut down entirely as a full write-off ~2.5 years after purchase, and Careem was sold to a minority stake then bought back to control. `[mgmt-gov/02]`
3. **Pro-forma leverage.** Today's balance sheet is fine (1.09x net leverage). But if the €14.2bn Delivery Hero bridge loan is drawn in full, net leverage rises to 3.14x (or ~6.0x on a 3-year average profit basis), and an illustrative covenant break point collapses from a 72.7% profit decline to roughly a 30% decline — an ordinary recession, not a rare event. `[bs-survival/06]`
4. **Earnings-quality gap.** The two headline numbers most investors default to are both distorted: GAAP earnings per share were inflated two years running by one-time tax releases ($6.4bn FY2024, $5.0bn FY2025), and the guided profit metric still nets a recurring "one-off" legal reserve. Trust the cash flow, not the headlines. `[earnings/06, 08]`
5. **Regulation.** A ruling that reclassifies drivers as employees in the US (50.9% of FY2025 revenue) would, in Uber's own words, "require us to fundamentally change our business model." `[bizmodel/06]`

**The killer risk, in one sentence:** the lawsuit survives a motion to dismiss and discovery shows the board's proxy statements were false — turning an unproven allegation into a proven integrity finding — at the same moment the €14.2bn Delivery Hero bridge is drawn in full on undisclosed, possibly expensive, terms.

## 6. Avoid-Big-Risks Check (§24)

Three of the six standing risk filters tripped on evidence:

- **Filter 1 — Crooks and integrity (RF-MGT-005, Critical).** The unresolved board-wide lawsuit alleging false safety disclosures. Caps conviction at Watchlist with no edge-score override. This is the primary, binding cap on the rating.
- **Filter 4 — Serial acquirers (RF-CAP-004, Critical).** The $14.8bn debt-funded Delivery Hero deal on top of the Drizly write-off and Careem reversal. Caps the capital-allocation score at 50/100 and conviction.
- **Filter 5 — Fast-changing industry (RF-BQ-005, Medium).** Industry rate-of-change scored 32/100, flagging autonomous-vehicle disruption risk (Waymo already runs a commercialized fleet). Lowers business quality and marks this partly as a technology-cycle bet.

Filters 2 (turnaround) and 6 (unaligned owner) were tested and **not** tripped. Filter 3 (survival) was **not** tripped: on today's balance sheet net leverage is 1.09x, interest coverage is 16.2x, S&P rates it BBB+, and liquidity would survive a literal repeat of Uber's worst year (FY2021) with room to spare. The survival concern is forward-looking (the Delivery Hero bridge), not present-day.

## 7. Valuation & Fair Value

**Verdict: fairly valued.** Base-case fair value is $74.77/share against the $68.18 price — an 8.82% margin of safety (the cushion between price and fair value). That is just inside the ±10% "fairly valued" band.

| Case | 12-month fair value | vs. $68.18 price |
|---|---:|---:|
| Bull | $104.17 | +52.8% |
| Base | $74.77 | +9.7% |
| Bear (cyclical) | $47.24 | −30.7% |
| Bear (structural, 24–36 months) | $43.38 | −36.4% |

The main method is peer-relative NTM EV/EBITDA (comparing Uber's value against similar companies on forward profit — 38% weight), which lands within 1.2% of an independent own-history check. Working backward, the price only assumes 5.05% ten-year free-cash-flow growth and a 10.78% long-run margin — a conservative bar Uber has already cleared on a trailing basis. So the operating story is not demanding.

The problem is the shape, not the point estimate: an 8.8% cushion is thin against a 30.7% downside to the 12-month bear case and 36.4% to the longer structural floor. The market is not paying investors to carry the two Critical unknowns. And none of the fair-value levels above yet prices in the lawsuit or the debt-funded deal, so the true risk-adjusted fair value is arguably below $74.77, not above.

**Methods disagreed and were not averaged away.** A sum-of-the-parts read comes in at $48.22 (35.5% below base), but 74.2% of that gap is a single peer-choice artifact, so it is discounted as a standalone signal. The own-history method points the other way at $87.37. The dossier reconciled this 81.2% raw spread with named causes rather than splitting the difference.

- A third straight revenue miss beyond the ±0.6% range, or profit below the guided low end — breaking the beat streak.

**Toward more confidence (would remove a cap):**
- The lawsuit is dismissed or settled without an adverse finding against the board.
- The bridge terms come in at or below Uber's ~4.67% existing coupon with no new maintenance covenant.
- The 2026 proxy is filed and shows pay tied to per-share value, not size.
- A third straight quarter of genuine, UK-adjusted cost leverage without a legal-reserve reversal.

## 13. Bottom Line

- **What it does:** runs the app matching riders/eaters/shippers with independent drivers and couriers, keeping ~27% of $193.5bn in bookings; owns no vehicles.
- **Why it may go up:** real demand is genuinely speeding up (24% bookings growth, four quarters running) while the price assumes much slower growth than Uber has already delivered.
- **Why it may go down:** an unresolved board-wide fraud lawsuit and a $14.8bn mostly-borrowed acquisition, on top of a thin 8.8% cushion against a 30–36% downside.
- **What the data supports:** the "real business is fine" half — strong cash generation (CFO/EBITDA 160–202%), low debt today (1.09x).
- **What the data does not support:** the "everything is clean" half — the beaten profit number includes a recurring "one-off" cost the company stopped explaining.
- **Buy now or wait:** wait. The math itself (a 0.05 risk/reward) does not reward getting in early.
- **The one thing to watch next:** the Delivery Hero loan terms — they decide whether the balance sheet stays Adequate or slides toward stress.

## 14. Plain-English Glossary

- **Gross Bookings** — the total dollar value of all trips and orders booked through the app, before Uber's cut.
- **Take rate** — the share of Gross Bookings Uber keeps as its own revenue (~27%).
- **Capex (capital spending)** — money spent on long-lived assets; here just 0.6% of revenue.
- **Basis points** — hundredths of a percent; 377 basis points = 3.77 percentage points.
- **EBIT / EBITDA** — profit before interest and tax (EBIT), and before depreciation too (EBITDA); rough measures of operating profit.
- **Margin** — profit as a percentage of revenue.
- **Adjusted EBITDA** — the company's own version of operating profit, with certain items added back; here it still adds back a recurring "one-off" legal reserve.
- **GAAP EPS** — earnings per share under standard US accounting rules; here inflated two years by one-time tax items.
- **Free cash flow (FCF)** — cash the business generates after capex; the reverse-DCF works backward from price to the FCF growth it implies.
- **CFO/EBITDA** — how much of reported operating profit actually shows up as operating cash; 160–202% here means cash generation is strong.
- **Reverse-DCF** — starts from today's price and solves for the growth rate the market must be assuming.
- **EV / EV/EBITDA / EV/Sales** — enterprise value (the whole business, debt included) divided by profit or sales; how many dollars the market pays per dollar of profit or revenue.
- **NTM** — "next twelve months," a forward-looking version of a metric.
- **Sum-of-the-parts (SOTP)** — valuing each segment separately then adding them up.
- **Margin of safety** — the cushion between price and estimated fair value (8.82% here).
- **Net debt / net leverage** — total debt minus cash (strict basis: $8,163M), and that figure divided by profit (1.09x).
- **Interest coverage** — profit divided by interest owed (16.2x here); higher is safer.
- **Bridge loan / term loan** — a short-term loan (bridge) that is later replaced by longer-term debt (term loan).
- **Maintenance covenant** — a rule in a loan the borrower must keep meeting (e.g. a leverage ceiling); a breach can force a renegotiation.
- **Constant-currency (cc)** — growth stripped of currency-exchange swings.
- **ROIC / WACC** — return on invested capital (profit per dollar of money put in, 0.85% over 5 years) versus the cost of that capital (~9.7%); ROIC below WACC means the company earned less than its money cost, until the last 1–2 years.
- **Probability-weighted expected return** — the average return across all scenarios, each weighted by its odds (+1.6% here).
- **Risk/reward** — upside to a target divided by downside to the bear case; 0.05 here is very unattractive.

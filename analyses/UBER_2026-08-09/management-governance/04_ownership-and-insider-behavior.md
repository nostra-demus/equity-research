## 5. Ownership / Insider Read

Skin in the game is thin: insiders own 0.18% of Uber, and every identifiable "purchase" in the last six months is either a $0-value RSU vesting credit or an ambiguous nonzero-value transaction tied to the new CFO's onboarding — no executive bought Uber stock with their own cash in the window covered by this pool. The net insider signal is mildly negative in dollar terms (led by the CEO's $18.9mm March sale) but immaterial in share-count terms and explained by mechanical vest-and-withhold administration rather than a discretionary bearish call, and no evidence ties any sale to timing ahead of bad news. The one real structural risk to watch for minority holders is not pledging (contractually banned and undisclosed — a clean read) or dual-class control (none exists) but the absence of any controlling or founder shareholder at all combined with a Saudi sovereign-wealth-fund board seat (PIF, 3.6%, one director) — a governance configuration to monitor via board independence and related-party screening (`05`), not a value-trap or control-abuse signal on the evidence here.

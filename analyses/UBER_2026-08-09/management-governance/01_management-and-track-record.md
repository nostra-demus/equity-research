## 3. Stated Strategy & Targets

Management describes Uber's strategy as continuing to compound Gross Bookings growth (Trips × Monthly Active Platform Consumers) across Mobility, Delivery, and Freight while expanding the take rate and converting a growing share of Adjusted EBITDA into free cash flow, layering in advertising and Uber One subscription revenue as incremental, largely-margin-accretive revenue streams, and building an autonomous-vehicle (AV) partnership network (Waymo, WeRide, Nvidia, Pony.ai and others) rather than developing self-driving technology in-house [Q2 FY26 transcript, prepared remarks; `business-model/02_business-identity.md`]. The most specific, checkable numeric targets on record are the **February 2024 Investor Day three-year (FY2024–FY2026) framework**: Gross Bookings growth of mid-to-high-teens % CAGR (constant currency), Adjusted EBITDA growth of high-30s-to-40% CAGR, and free-cash-flow conversion of 90%+ of Adjusted EBITDA [Web: Uber Investor Update, Feb-2024, hosted on Uber's own IR site (s23.q4cdn.com), and multiple contemporaneous financial-media reports (Seeking Alpha, "Uber gains after setting aggressive three-year targets") — dated, company-sourced investor material, not in this data pool, labelled unverified per this module's web-source rule]. On a shorter horizon, Uber guides quarterly (adjusted) EBITDA and, since Q1 FY2026, EPS Normalized — it does not issue formal Revenue, Capex, or GAAP-Net-Income guidance [`earnings/04_guidance-consensus.md`, §2].

## 5. Management Read

Dara Khosrowshahi has run Uber for nearly nine years, not as a founder but as the executive brought in to clean up a governance crisis — and the numbers back up that he did it: EBIT and free cash flow improved every year for four straight years (FY2021–FY2025), and the company has beaten its own quarterly Adjusted EBITDA guidance in four consecutive quarters, clearing the top of the guided range (not just the midpoint) in the two most recent prints. Beneath the CEO, the bench has turned over quickly — four C-suite/president-level changes in roughly a year, all internal promotions with no disclosed cause for concern, which is a stability watch item rather than a red flag on its own. The single biggest management signal, however, is negative and unresolved: a shareholder derivative lawsuit filed weeks before this report names the sitting CEO and entire board, alleging they sat on a safety-risk model for five years (2017–2022) and that recent proxy statements about board safety oversight were false. That allegation is unproven, but it is serious, it names the people this report is judging, and — combined with the company's own decision to stop reconciling its headline Adjusted EBITDA metric to GAAP starting this year — it caps how much credit this agent can give management on candor and integrity until the litigation resolves.

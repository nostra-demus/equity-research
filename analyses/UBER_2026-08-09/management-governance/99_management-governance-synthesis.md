# Management-Governance Module — UBER (Synthesis)

## Abstract

Uber's stewardship read is capped by one unresolved critical red flag: a federal derivative lawsuit filed mid-2026 names CEO Dara Khosrowshahi, Chairman Ronald Sugar, and every sitting director, alleging the board's own proxy statements on safety oversight were false — forcing this module's verdict no better than serious governance concerns. Beneath that cap, the operating record is real: four straight years of delivered EBIT and free-cash-flow improvement, and mostly-kept promises. Capital allocation is separately capped by a serial-acquirer pattern — a written-off $1.1bn Drizly deal, a reversed Careem stake, and a pending $14.8bn debt-funded Delivery Hero bid. Insider ownership is thin (0.18%), and incentive alignment cannot be assessed — the governing proxy statement was never filed into this data pool.

## 1A. Module Disconfirmation

- **Strongest bear point:** The unresolved derivative lawsuit (RF-MGT-005) lands at the same time as a serial-acquirer pattern that already produced one full write-off (Drizly, $1.1bn, shut within 2.5 years) and one reversed divestiture (Careem, sold to minority in 2023, bought back to control by mid/late-2026) — and management's response is to commit $14.8bn, funded mostly by new debt, to the largest deal in company history (Delivery Hero) [02, 01].
- **Strongest bull point (steelman):** The CEO delivered a real, checkable operating turnaround — four straight years of EBIT and free-cash-flow improvement, four straight quarters of Adjusted EBITDA guidance beaten (above the high end the last two), a 90%-independent board with a split chair/CEO and fully independent committees, no promoter, no dual-class stock, and a contractual pledging ban that reconciles cleanly with the disqualifier scan [01, 05].
- **Single killer risk:** If the derivative lawsuit survives a motion to dismiss and discovery produces evidence that the board's own proxy statements about safety oversight were knowingly false, this converts from an unproven allegation into a proven integrity finding implicating the entire sitting board — which would very likely escalate to a hard disqualifier at the next module refresh [01, 05, 06].
- **Disconfirming evidence already visible:** The CFO-transition statement was specific and disclaimed any accounting cause; pledging is contractually banned company-wide and confirmed clean by the disqualifier scan; the §24 Filter 6 unaligned-owner test does not trip (no controlling holder exists); and buybacks have genuinely reduced net share count in the most recent 15 months for the first time since the 2019 IPO [01, 04, 02].

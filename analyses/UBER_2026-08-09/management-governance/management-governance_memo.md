## What This Module Found

The operating record is real and checkable. The CEO delivered four straight years of improving EBIT (operating profit) and free cash flow (FCF — the cash left after running costs and investment), with FY21–FY25 EBIT margin moving from −22.0% to +10.7% and FCF from −$743mm to +$9,763mm [earnings/01]. Adjusted EBITDA (a profit measure that strips out some costs) has beaten the company's own guidance four quarters running, twice above the high end [earnings/04].

The single most important driver of the verdict is one unresolved risk: a federal shareholder derivative lawsuit (filed mid-2026) names the sitting CEO, Chairman, and every director, alleging the board ignored an internal safety-risk model for five years (2017–2022) and that its own 2024–2026 proxy statements about safety oversight were false [01, 05, 06 — RF-MGT-005]. The allegation is unproven and rests on two dated, unverified web articles (confidence 2 of 5), but it names the exact people this report judges, so the module's rules cap the whole verdict.

The single most important other risk is how the company spends on acquisitions. Of the deals with a knowable outcome, Drizly ($1.1bn) was a full write-off shut within 2.5 years, and Careem ($3.1bn) was sold to a minority stake in 2023 then bought back to control by mid/late-2026 [02]. Management is now committing $14.8bn — funded mostly by a €14.2bn ($15.3bn) bridge loan that would roughly double gross debt if drawn — to Delivery Hero, the largest deal in company history [Q2 FY26 10-Q Note 3 — RF-CAP-004].

Two things cannot be checked. Executive pay is a genuine unknown: the 10-K defers every compensation detail to a 2026 proxy statement that is not in the data pool [03]. And insiders own about 0.18% of the company (the CEO holds 0.060%, worth $87.8mm), with no identifiable open-market cash purchase in six months and modest net selling [04]. Treat these as unknowns, not as a clean bill of health.

## What Would Change This Read

**Would strengthen it:** (1) the lawsuit is dismissed or settled without an adverse finding against the board; (2) the 2026 proxy shows pay tied to per-share value (return on capital, EPS, FCF-per-share, total shareholder return) rather than revenue or deal count; (3) Delivery Hero closes and delivers without repeating the Drizly/Careem pattern; (4) the Adjusted-EBITDA-to-GAAP reconciliation returns in the FY2026 10-K; (5) insiders begin genuine open-market cash buying.

**Would weaken it:** (1) the lawsuit survives a motion to dismiss and discovery corroborates that board proxy disclosures were false; (2) a third large failed acquisition; (3) more insider selling ahead of a weak print; (4) a formal restatement or internal-controls weakness; (5) the Delivery Hero bridge is drawn in full and triggers a covenant or leverage concern.

**Data needed:** the 2026 DEF 14A proxy (pay metrics, exact ownership, insider-trade history, related-party policy, AGM vote tallies); lawsuit docket updates; Delivery Hero post-close disclosure; the next 10-K's legal-proceedings naming and non-GAAP reconciliation.

## Bottom Line

- **Verdict: Serious governance concerns**, Governance Rating Weak (43/100 raw, 26/100 confidence-adjusted) — a rule-driven cap from two Critical red flags, not a finding of proven fraud.
- **Why it could be better than it looks:** the operating turnaround is real and checkable — four straight years of EBIT and FCF improvement and four straight guidance beats — and the board is structurally clean (90% independent, split chair/CEO, single share class, no promoter, pledging banned).
- **Why it could be worse than it looks:** the board-wide lawsuit could convert from an unproven allegation into a proven integrity finding implicating every sitting director, which would very likely escalate to a hard disqualifier at the next refresh.
- **What is missing:** the 2026 proxy — it would resolve pay, exact ownership, insider history, related-party policy, and AGM votes at once. Its absence makes incentive alignment unassessable.
- **The one thing to watch next:** the lawsuit docket — a motion-to-dismiss ruling, discovery, or settlement is what moves this verdict.

## Plain-English Glossary

- **EBIT / operating profit:** profit from running the business, before interest and tax.
- **Free cash flow (FCF):** the cash left after running costs and investment.
- **Adjusted EBITDA:** a company profit measure that strips out certain costs; not a standard accounting figure.
- **GAAP:** the standard US accounting rules; "reconciling to GAAP" means showing how the adjusted number bridges to the official one.
- **Derivative lawsuit:** a suit brought on the company's behalf against its own directors/officers.
- **Bridge loan:** short-term debt used to fund a deal before permanent financing is in place.
- **Incremental return on capital:** the profit earned on each new dollar invested in the business.
- **Overboarded director:** a director sitting on so many boards that their capacity to oversee any one is in question.
- **Entrenchment tools:** governance features (e.g. limits on calling special meetings) that make it harder for shareholders to force change.
- **Proxy (DEF 14A):** the annual filing that discloses executive pay, ownership, and governance policies.

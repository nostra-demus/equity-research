# Candor & Disclosure Quality — UBER

**Data available:** two verbatim earnings-call transcripts (Q1 FY2026, May-06-2026; Q2 FY2026, Aug-05-2026) — no paraphrase or press-release substitute used. No shareholder letters exist for this filer (US SEC regime; the module rule requires transcripts OR letters, and transcripts are present, so no partial-data cap applies on that ground). `earnings/06_earnings-quality.md` is available and used as the primary non-GAAP cross-check. Both available transcripts cover quarters management itself characterized as strong ("outstanding," "exceptional start") — there is no genuinely bad quarter in the pool to test tone against; the closest available "bad news" data points are a narrow revenue miss vs. consensus, a take-rate compression, and the quiet discontinuation of a headline metric, all assessed below. This is flagged as a real limitation on the "tone in bad times" read (Section 4), not papered over.

`earnings/06_earnings-quality.md` scored overall earnings quality 71/100 ("mostly clean but some adjustment noise") — the underlying cash generation is genuinely strong (CFO exceeded unadjusted EBITDA 160–202% of EBITDA in FY2023–2025), but the two headline profitability numbers most investors default to (GAAP EPS, Adjusted EBITDA) both carry disclosed, real distortions. This module's read is narrower and harsher than that overall 71: candor is not about whether the cash is real (it is), it is about whether the *labels* on the adjustments are honest, and the "non-recurring" legal-reserve line recurring two years straight fails that specific test.

## 3. Disclosure Transparency

Segment-level KPIs (Gross Bookings, Trips, Monthly Active Platform Consumers, Mobility/Delivery/Freight growth rates) remain disclosed consistently, and the segment profit metric was formally renamed from "Segment Adjusted EBITDA" to "Segment Operating Income" with prior periods recast for comparability — a metric change, but not a silent one. Disclosure got **less transparent** in one specific and material way: the company-level "Adjusted EBITDA" reconciliation that investors used through FY2025 to track margin progress was dropped starting the Q1 FY2026 10-Q, with zero acknowledgment on either subsequent earnings call (full-text search, both transcripts) — a genuine reduction in like-for-like visibility precisely as the two most recent quarters showed take-rate compression [`earnings/06_earnings-quality.md` §4]. Separately, two adjudicated MDL jury verdicts were left unnamed in the three most recent filings even though an aggregate litigation reserve is disclosed, which is a timeliness gap on a specific, checkable fact rather than a general "opacity" complaint (`RF-REG-002`).

## 4. Tone in Bad Times

Both transcripts in the pool are from quarters management itself framed as strong — "another outstanding quarter," "an exceptional start to 2026" — so this module cannot test tone against a genuinely weak quarter; that is a real gap, not glossed over. Within what is available, the tone stayed promotional even where the numbers were mixed: the CEO opened Q2 FY26 with "another outstanding quarter... record audience and engagement" before any mention of the revenue miss, the take-rate compression, or the Brazil competitive pressure — all of which surfaced only in Q&A, prompted by analysts, not volunteered. Where analysts did ask pointed quantitative questions (UK take-rate math, Brazil 2-wheeler competition, AI-driven headcount moderation), the CFO and CEO answered with specific numbers rather than deflecting — there is no visible evasiveness on the questions that were actually asked. The gap is not in how management answers hard questions; it is in what gets raised unprompted and what gets left for a sell-side analyst to dig out.

## 5. Candor Read

Uber's management answers specific questions specifically — the UK take-rate breakdown, the Brazil competitive dynamics, and the AI/headcount tradeoff were all quantified clearly under direct questioning, and the guidance-beat record (four straight quarters, above the high end the last two) is genuine and checkable. But the record this module can actually see is thin on genuinely bad news, and on the two disclosure decisions that mattered most — dropping the company-level Adjusted EBITDA reconciliation and leaving two named litigation verdicts out of the filings — nothing was volunteered, nothing was explained, and no analyst caught it either; both were found only by cross-referencing filings and a full-text transcript search. Layered on top is the unresolved shareholder derivative lawsuit (routed from `01_management-and-track-record.md`, `RF-MGT-005`) alleging the sitting CEO and full board made false statements in the 2024–2026 proxies about board safety oversight — an unproven but serious integrity signal naming the exact people this report is judging, which this module lets weigh against the trust read rather than setting aside, per CLAUDE.md §24 Filter 1. The combination of promotional-first framing, a quietly dropped headline metric, and an unresolved integrity allegation is enough to keep this out of "candid/high-trust," while the genuine specificity under direct questioning and the clean cash-generation record (per `earnings/06`) keep it out of "promotional/low-trust." **Verdict: mixed.**

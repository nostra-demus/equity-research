**Serial-acquirer + opportunity-cost test (CLAUDE.md §24, Filter 4) — applied.** Uber has run a near-continuous acquisition program since 2019 (at least eight material deals: Careem, Postmates, Cornershop, Drizly, Transplace, Foodpanda Taiwan, Trendyol GO, Getir) and is now pursuing the largest deal in its history (Delivery Hero, $14.8bn equity value) financed by a debt bridge that would roughly double gross indebtedness. Of the deals with a knowable outcome: **one was a full write-off (Drizly, closed and shut down within 2.5 years)**; **one was substantially reversed (Careem — divested to minority in 2023, then re-acquired to a controlling stake by mid/late-2026, implying the original $3.1bn strategic thesis was abandoned and then partially re-bought at a fraction of the price)**; and the Foodpanda Taiwan and pending Getir/Trendyol GO deals show no disclosed synergy realization yet. Charging the pattern its opportunity cost (per CLAUDE.md §24, not just each deal's own reported return): Uber has repeatedly redirected capital and management attention into new verticals (alcohol, freight software, Latin American grocery, Turkish delivery, and now German-listed global food delivery) rather than compounding around its core Mobility/Delivery platform, and the Drizly/Careem reversals show real capital was destroyed and strategic focus was lost, not merely "delayed synergies." Bare rationale language ("expand our Delivery offering," "further consolidate the market") is not evidence of value creation on its own — it is scored as such here.

**RF-CAP-004 triggered** (serial-acquirer / value-destructive M&A pattern, debt-funded deal near/above own value) — consistent with the upstream `business-model/11_capital-allocation-governance.md` finding, which this module's own deal-level review corroborates and deepens rather than merely repeats.

| Incremental ROIC (Δ NOPAT / Δ invested capital), FY2022→FY2025 | **~29.0%** (Inference/estimate, not a filed metric — methodology below) | See computation and caveats below |

**Incremental ROIC computation (labelled estimate, not from filings).** NOPAT = EBIT × (1 − tax rate), using EBIT (unadjusted: income from operations + D&A basis consistent with Section 1 of `earnings/01_historical-financials.md`) and a flat 21% US statutory tax-rate assumption — a normalization choice made because Uber's actual GAAP effective tax rate is distorted by large, non-cash releases of a deferred-tax-asset valuation allowance ($5,758M benefit in FY2024, $4,346M benefit in FY2025) that have nothing to do with operating capital efficiency [`earnings/01_historical-financials.md`, §3]. FY2022 EBIT −$1,832M → NOPAT ≈ −$1,447M; FY2025 EBIT $5,565M → NOPAT ≈ $4,396M. ΔNOPAT ≈ $5,843M. Invested capital (Total Capital: debt + common equity + preferred + minority interest) rose from $20,221M (Dec-2022) to $40,385M (Dec-2025), ΔInvested capital ≈ $20,164M [CIQ Financials_Annual.xls → Capital Structure Summary, "Total Capital" row]. Incremental ROIC ≈ $5,843M / $20,164M ≈ **29.0%**.

**Caveat (material, must be read with the number above):** this Δ-invested-capital base is NOT a clean organic-reinvestment figure — it mixes (a) retained-earnings-funded organic growth, (b) the debt raised to fund buybacks (roughly capital-structure-neutral, since buybacks reduce equity by a similar amount as debt increases it), and (c) M&A-driven goodwill/intangible additions (e.g., the $712M Trendyol GO goodwill in FY2025 alone). It is best read as a rough, favorable-direction signal that Uber's overall capital base is earning a high accounting return relative to its cost of capital, not as a precise measure of "returns on newly invested organic capital" — labelled **Inference, not from filings**, and the tax-rate normalization further limits precision. Capex itself is small and shrinking ($336M FY2025 vs. $52.0bn revenue) — the real organic-reinvestment story is operating-leverage-driven margin expansion (EBITDA margin 12.1% FY2025, up from a loss in FY2021), not heavy fixed-asset spend [`earnings/01_historical-financials.md`, §1, §6].

## 5. Capital-Allocation Read

Capital has created real per-share value in the core operating business — EBIT and free cash flow improved every year for four straight years (FY2021–FY2025: EBIT margin −22.0% → +10.7%; FCF −$743M → $9,763M) [`01_management-and-track-record.md`, §4A] — but the *deployment* of the cash that growth has produced is mixed to concerning. The best use of capital has been organic reinvestment: capex is small and shrinking relative to revenue, and the estimated ~29% incremental ROIC (caveated above) shows the operating business earns a high return on the capital already inside it. The worst use has been M&A: a near-continuous acquisition program since 2019 has produced at least one full write-off (Drizly, $1.1bn, shut down within 2.5 years) and one reversed divestiture (Careem, sold down to a minority stake in 2023 and bought back to a controlling stake by mid/late-2026 at a fraction of the original valuation), and it now culminates in a $14.8bn debt-funded bid for Delivery Hero that would roughly double gross debt — a serial-acquirer pattern that trips CLAUDE.md §24 Filter 4 and caps this module's capital-allocation score. The single biggest capital-allocation signal is the sequencing risk sitting underneath the recently-improved buyback record: Uber only started genuinely shrinking its share count in the last ~15 months, financed that acceleration partly with new debt (net debt +67% in the year to Jun-2026) at prices ($88.90 disclosed Q4-2025 average) well above where the stock has traded since ($68.18 currently), and is about to layer a ~$15bn acquisition-driven debt bridge on top of that same balance sheet. **Verdict: mixed** — value-creative at the operating level, value-destructive to neutral at the M&A level, and buyback discipline that has only recently improved and remains untested against the coming debt load.

## 2. What This Module Found

Karoon owes $338.8m in total debt (carrying value; $351.1m face). Net debt — what it owes after subtracting its cash — was $132.7m on the audited FY2025 books, or 0.36x EBITDA (EBITDA is roughly the cash profit the business throws off before interest, tax, and accounting depreciation charges) [01]. That is low. It has also been falling structurally: net leverage went from 0.77x in FY2022 to a stable 0.34x–0.36x over FY2024–FY2025, and stays inside a narrow 0.27x–0.41x band across every basis tested — latest, peak-year, mid-cycle, and with one-off gains stripped out [01, §5].

The single most important driver is the shape of the debt, not its size. It is a barbell, not a ladder: 99.7% of gross debt is one $350.0m bullet repayment due 14-May-2029, and only 0.3% ($1.1m of finance leases) falls due inside 24 months [02]. Nothing meaningful needs paying for years.

The single most important risk is what happens when that bullet arrives. The revolving reserves-based facility (RBL) matures Sep-2028 and the Notes bullet lands May-2029 — both need refinancing within roughly eight months of each other, against a sub-investment-grade credit rating (S&P B / Fitch B, Stable — a rating below the "safe" investment-grade band) and a fully unhedged, oil-price-driven earnings base [02, 04].

Committed liquidity — cash plus the undrawn, disclosed availability on that facility — runs $307.0m to $546.1m, covering near-term obligations for 24 to 43 months depending on which anchor date is used [03]. Every basis clears the 12-month test comfortably.

The stress test is the module's clearest finding. The first thing to break, even on an estimate, is an indicative debt-service covenant (a loan test comparing cash generated to interest owed) at roughly a 54.6%–58.4% EBITDA decline. Liquidity itself never exhausts at any haircut tested — including a full 100% EBITDA wipeout combined with a $100m working-capital shock, where a $230.9m surplus still remains [06].

The visible counter-evidence: net debt more than doubled in the six months after the audited anchor, from $132.7m (31-Dec-2025) to $269.7m (30-Jun-2026), while cash fell 61% ($206.1m to $80.3m) [01 §6, 03]. That was driven by a heavy, front-loaded capital programme, not by weaker earnings or new borrowing. It does not change the verdict — 0.83x on clean EBITDA is still low — but it is a real, disclosed deterioration in direction that the FY2025 figures alone would understate.

## 3. The Specialists, Briefly

- **solvency-data-triage** — Data is sufficient: full audited balance sheet, debt note, maturity schedule, and cash flow statement are present. Covenant thresholds are the one material gap; no indenture or credit agreement for the Notes or the RBL exists in the pool.
- **capital-structure-and-leverage** — Low but non-zero leverage, 0.27x–0.41x net debt/EBITDA on every basis. Net debt rose $132.7m → $269.7m in six months on capex, not new borrowing; the sub-investment-grade B/B+ rating sits at odds with the low ratios.
- **maturity-wall-and-refinancing** — Self-funded and low refinancing risk for the next 12–24 months; a barbell, not a ladder. 99.7% of gross debt is the single $350.0m 2029 bullet, and the RBL's Sep-2028 maturity compresses both into an 8-month window.
- **liquidity-runway** — Runway is at least 24 months even on the most conservative, most current snapshot. It rests on cash plus a secured, asset-backed facility with disclosed availability, not on free cash flow (FY2025 FCF was negative $37.1m and was deliberately not netted).
- **coverage-and-covenants** — Interest is covered 9.33x by EBITDA on every measured basis. Covenant headroom is Not assessable against a real threshold; the only disclosure is a one-line compliance statement, so the wide indicative headroom is unmeasured.
- **off-balance-sheet-and-contingencies** — Total maximum quantifiable contingent exposure is $47.6m, 4.6% of equity — below the RF-OBS-001 red-flag threshold. The filing's "up to $285m" Petrobras earn-out headline is a stale, mostly-already-paid cumulative programme cap; the correctly scoped forward residual is only ~$42.4m.
- **downside-stress-test** — Survives a 30–60% EBITDA decline on every real, measured metric. Committed liquidity does not exhaust at any tested haircut, including a 100% EBITDA wipeout plus a $100m working-capital shock ($230.9m surplus remains).

**Disagreements:** none on measured facts — every specialist used the same canonical debt stack, net debt, and EBITDA bases. The synthesis named two unresolved tensions rather than conflicts. The more important is the **rating-versus-ratio gap**: leverage ratios of 0.27x–0.41x look investment-grade-like while the actual rating is sub-investment-grade B/B+, and neither specialist resolves why. The synthesis carries that gap forward unresolved, and states it is the reason the verdict is "Solid" rather than "Fortress." (The second: the FY2025 anchor versus the post-anchor rise to $269.7m — resolved by stating leverage on both bases everywhere.)

## 4. What Would Change This Read

**What would strengthen it (toward "Fortress"):** the Notes indenture or the RBL credit agreement, disclosing real, measured covenant thresholds that confirm the wide indicative headroom — provided the RBL's reserves-based redetermination mechanic (the periodic re-test of how much the lenders will lend against oil reserves) is also shown to be resilient to a severe, sustained oil-price decline.

**What would weaken it:** a continuation of the post-FY2025 net-debt rise ($132.7m → $269.7m already) without the guided 2H26 free-cash-flow improvement materialising; a rating downgrade; or a Brent price decline severe enough to trigger an adverse RBL borrowing-base redetermination ahead of the disclosed amortisation schedule ($340.0m → $283.3m → $226.7m by 30-Sep-2026).

**Data needed to move the read either way:** the indenture / credit agreement (covenant thresholds plus the covenant-EBITDA definition); an H1 FY2026 interim balance sheet to confirm the current trajectory; and disclosure of the RBL's borrowing-base redetermination formula and its sensitivity to oil price.

**Levers already evidenced, not speculative:** the Notes carry a call date of 3-Sep-2026 at 105.25% of par, and the bond trades above par with a market yield-to-worst of 8.211% below its own 10.50% coupon — an evidenced chance to refinance cheaper, though management's intent is not disclosed. 85% of the FY2026 capex budget was already spent by 30-Jun-2026 ($214.6m across 1Q26–2Q26), with management guiding to lower capex and higher free cash flow in 2H26. The dividend (20–40% of underlying NPAT under a stated policy) and the buyback ($4.0m in 2Q26) are discretionary, not contractual, so both are genuinely cuttable. No asset-sale programme, debt restructuring, waiver request, or covenant amendment is disclosed anywhere in the pool.

## 5. Bottom Line

- **Verdict: Solid.** Low leverage on every basis (0.27x–0.41x at the audited anchor, 0.83x on the latest unaudited snapshot), a near-empty 24-month maturity wall (0.3%), and 24–43 months of committed liquidity.
- **Biggest reason it could be better than it looks:** liquidity does not exhaust even at a 100% EBITDA wipeout plus a $100m working-capital shock — $230.9m still left over — and the bond market currently prices Karoon's refinancing risk as favourable, not adverse (yield 8.211% below the 10.50% coupon).
- **Biggest reason it could be worse:** the 2028–2029 refinancing compression. The RBL matures Sep-2028 and the $350.0m bullet lands May-2029, eight months apart, against a B/B+ rating and a reserves-based facility whose borrowing base could shrink faster than its disclosed schedule in exactly the severe oil-price decline that would also cut EBITDA.
- **Missing evidence:** the loan documents. No indenture or credit agreement for either instrument exists in the pool — so covenant headroom is Not assessable against a real threshold (capped at 60), "addback illusion" risk on covenant EBITDA is unassessed rather than ruled out, and any change-of-control puts or cross-default triggers cannot be ruled out either. That single document is the highest-value next data request.
- **Watch next:** whether the guided 2H26 free-cash-flow improvement actually arrives and halts the net-debt climb from $132.7m → $269.7m; capex was 85% spent by 30-Jun-2026, so the lever exists — the question is delivery.

## 6. Plain-English Glossary

- **EBITDA** — roughly the cash profit a business makes before interest, tax, and accounting depreciation charges. Used here as the earnings yardstick debt is measured against.
- **Net debt** — total borrowings minus cash. Karoon's is $132.7m on the audited FY2025 books, $269.7m at 30-Jun-2026 (unaudited).
- **Net leverage (net debt / EBITDA)** — how many years of cash profit it would take to repay net debt. 0.36x means about four months' worth.
- **Bullet maturity** — a loan that repays nothing along the way and the whole principal on one date. Karoon's is $350.0m due 14-May-2029.
- **RBL (reserves-based lending facility)** — a revolving credit line secured against oil reserves, where lenders periodically re-test how much they will lend as reserves and oil prices move.
- **Covenant headroom** — the gap between where a financial test currently sits and the level at which the loan agreement would be breached. Not assessable here because the agreements are missing.
- **Debt-service / DSCR covenant** — a loan test comparing cash generated (EBITDA less recurring capex) to interest owed.
- **Sub-investment-grade (B / B+)** — a credit rating below the band regarded as safe by lenders; borrowing costs more and refinancing is less certain.
- **Free cash flow (FCF)** — cash from operations minus capital spending. Karoon's FY2025 FCF was negative $37.1m.
- **Yield-to-worst (8.211%)** — the return a bond buyer earns at today's price assuming the least favourable repayment timing. Below the 10.50% coupon here, meaning the bond trades above face value.
- **Unhedged** — no contracts in place to lock in an oil price, so earnings move fully with the market. Karoon's last Brent collar expired unreplaced.

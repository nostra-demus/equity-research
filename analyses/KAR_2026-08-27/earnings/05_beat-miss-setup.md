# Beat / Miss Setup — KAR

## 1. Next Reporting Period Context

Karoon's next filing is the **H1 CY2026 half-year result (six months to 30-Jun-2026)** — a standalone half-year period, not a cumulative report — scheduled for release **today, 27-Aug-2026** [`2Q26 Activities Report (Jul-22-2026)`, p.7; `04_guidance-consensus.md` §1A]. This is not a seasonally distinct period in the calendar sense (`01_historical-financials.md` §5 finds no provable calendar seasonality), but management itself frames 2026 as "two contrasting halves" — a capex- and cost-heavy first half intended to enable a stronger second half (`03_margin-drivers.md` §9).

**Bar (carried verbatim from `04` §1A, basis-labelled):** FH1 2026 consensus, restated from AUD to USD via a derived FX rate (AUD 1.4292 = USD 1.00, inferred by reconciling CIQ's own AUD guidance entries against the company's USD guidance, not a filed rate — labelled *Inference, not from filings*) — **Revenue ≈ US$243.3m, EBITDA ≈ US$125.9m, EPS Normalized ≈ US$0.0478/share**. This is a **thin, single-analyst estimate** (an "Initiation of Coverage" entry dated 2026-08-04), not a broad consensus — the FY2026 annual figures carry 8–9 analysts, but the specific half-year bar rests on one. Since Karoon does not guide Revenue/EBITDA/NPAT (only production, unit costs, and capex — `04` §2), there is no company-guidance bar to compare against for these three lines; the restated consensus is the only bar available. Revenue is unusually well pinned ahead of the print: the company's own already-disclosed Q1+Q2 2026 cash "sales revenue" of US$244.6m sits within 1% of the restated consensus revenue (ratio 0.99, `04` §1A), so the real swing factor for this print sits below the revenue line — costs, D&A, tax, FX, and one-offs.

## 6. Seasonality Read

There is no provable calendar seasonality in this pool — Karoon has only 2.5 fiscal years of full half-year P&L history, short of the three years needed to separate seasonality from company-specific events [`01_historical-financials.md` §5]. What the half-year data does show is company-specific, not calendar-driven: H1 has outweighed H2 on gross margin in the periods observed, but the H1 2025 EBITDAX-margin spike (+1,340bps YoY) was itself shown to be a one-off gain, not a repeatable H1 pattern [`01_historical-financials.md` §3]. For H1 2026 specifically, management's own "two contrasting halves" framing — heavy capex and cost (85% of the year's capex already spent by 30-Jun-2026, the bulk of the flotel spend already incurred) concentrated in H1, with a cleaner, lower-cost H2 targeted — cuts against any assumption that H1 is the seasonally easier half to beat; if anything, the cost load is front-loaded into the period reporting today.

## 7. Historical Pattern

Karoon has reported full-year results annually since FY2023 (only three full-year prints exist in this pool), and the pattern across the last two is consistent: Revenue and EBITDA missed Street consensus in both FY2024 (-1.2%/-3.5%) and FY2025 (-3.3%/-3.9%), while Net Income (Excl. Excep.) and EPS Normalized beat in both years (+9.2%/+7.7% and +4.5%/+5.3%) [`04_guidance-consensus.md` §6]. This is judgment informed by an n=2-consecutive-year sample (well below the ~8 observations needed for an empirical base rate per CLAUDE.md §10), not a measured frequency, and it is a genuine divergence — not noise — since the same split shows up at the half-year level too (H1 2025 revenue -25% YoY, H1 2025 NPAT +15% YoY). The synthesizer should weight it as a real but thin signal that revenue/EBITDA and NPAT/EPS can diverge for this company, not as a reliable predictor of the exact size of any H1 2026 divergence.

## 8. Setup Verdict

**Setup is balanced.** Revenue is unusually well pre-known (0.99 match to already-disclosed cash sales revenue), removing it as a source of surprise either way. Below that line, the evidence pulls in two directions at similar strength: EBITDA/margin faces multiple, specific, judgment-based headwinds (US$46.5m of flotel spend already incurred against a US$125.9m EBITDA bar, the new export tax, unit-cost dilution from the 2Q26 shut-ins, and rising interest costs on widened net debt — §3), corroborated by broad-based negative Street revision breadth over 1–3 months (§5), while the two-year historical pattern of NPAT/EPS beating even as Revenue/EBITDA missed (§7) argues for resilience below the EBITDA line. The single most important factor is whether the US$46.5m of already-incurred flotel/FPSO integrity spend was fully captured by the single analyst who set the thin FH1 2026 EBITDA estimate on 2026-08-04 — if it was not, EBITDA misses; if it was, the historical below-the-line pattern has room to produce another NPAT/EPS beat. The biggest risk to this verdict is a further, undisclosed CY2026 guidance cut or export-tax extension announced alongside the H1 print itself, which would tip the balance decisively toward miss.

## 9. Second-Quarter Look-Ahead

The setup for H2 2026 could look meaningfully different: management's own framing is that the heavy 1H26 capex and cost load (flotel campaign, SPS-92 and Who Dat interventions, 85% of full-year capex already spent) is intended to enable a "materially better" second half, with Baúna FPSO efficiency already back to 97.2% in 2Q26 and the E-riser and A1 sidetrack work targeted for resolution through 3Q26–4Q27 [`03_margin-drivers.md` §9; `02_revenue-drivers.md` §4]. That thesis is unproven and depends on execution the company does not fully control (Who Dat is non-operated by LLOG) and on whether the Brazilian export tax (currently extended to ~7-Sep-2026) lapses or is extended again — neither of which this report can resolve ahead of the H1 print.

## 10. Pre-Mortem

If this setup read turns out wrong, the most likely reason is over-reliance on a single-analyst, three-week-old FH1 2026 estimate as if it were a broad consensus — a one-analyst bar can be stale or mis-modelled on a specific cost line (flotel spend, export tax) in ways a nine-analyst FY2026 consensus would average out. A secondary likely reason is that management discloses a further guidance cut or cost item alongside the H1 print itself, which this report — built entirely from pre-print data — could not see coming.

## 5. Earnings Setup Summary

### Revenue Setup

The current revenue trajectory is not a single sustainable trend — it is two offsetting forces moving in opposite directions at the same time. The FY2025 decline was driven almost entirely by realized price (68% of the US$147.9m drop), a variable Karoon does not control and does not hedge, while the 2Q26 recovery is the same variable moving the other way on a geopolitical risk premium that management's own 2H26 planning assumption (US$60–70/bbl) explicitly does not treat as durable [`02_revenue-drivers.md` §7]. The single factor that would flip the direction cleanly is whether the Middle East/Strait of Hormuz risk premium persists or reverses before 2H26 volumes are sold — with zero hedges, there is no cushion either way. A second, independent factor pulling the other way regardless of price is volume: CY2026 production guidance has been cut ~11% at the midpoint since January 2026 on the Who Dat E-riser leak (no confirmed resumption inside the 12-month window) and the Baúna SPS-92 shut-in, so even a favourable price outcome sits on top of a shrinking volume base. Reported and underlying revenue tell the same story here (no accounting-adjustment gap on the revenue line itself), but the reported-vs-run-rate gap is real: the 2Q26 price level is roughly 42–52% above management's own 2H26 planning assumption, a gap this synthesis treats as the key limit on reading the 2Q26 print as "revenue accelerating."

### Margin Setup

Current statutory margins are not sustainable as reported: the FY2025 statutory EBITDA-margin "expansion" (+257bps) and statutory NPAT-margin "expansion" (+357bps) are both artefacts of two large one-off gains (US$35.3m FPSO-disposal gain, US$21.2m contingent-consideration fair-value gain) that will not repeat, and once stripped, adjusted EBITDA margin actually compressed 641bps and Underlying NPAT margin compressed 1,046bps [`01_historical-financials.md` §6; `03_margin-drivers.md` §3]. The cleaner, decision-useful measure — Underlying EBITDAX margin — compressed a much smaller 155bps, because unit production costs are largely fixed and moved favourably (US$13.20/boe FY2025 vs US$13.60/boe FY2024) [`03` §4]. The segment or driver that would take the largest bite out of consolidated margins on a 10–20% adverse move is the same one dominating revenue — realized oil price, worth ~US$110.7m of average EBITDAX swing against ~US$21.9m for volume, ~US$11.55m for unit production cost [`07_earnings-sensitivity.md` §3] — because cost of sales moved only 2.5% against a 19% revenue decline in FY2025, so nearly all of a price shock flows straight through to margin. Karoon has no contractual pass-through or hedging mechanism protecting margin: it sells every barrel at a same-day benchmark-linked price with no negotiated escalator to lag and holds zero active price hedges, so it is a pure price-taker on its dominant margin driver, with only the mechanical, non-discretionary offset of ad valorem royalties (which fall automatically as price falls, providing a small buffer estimated at ~9.5% of a given revenue shock) [`03` §3, citing `business-model/06_value-chain.md` §2].

### Quality Check

The single largest gap between reported and economic earnings is the effective tax rate, which swung from 46.7% (FY2024) to 12.4% (FY2025) on a non-cash, Brazilian-real-denominated deferred-tax FX translation line worth more than US$87m across the two years — a gap `06_earnings-quality.md` names as the single biggest quality concern, ahead of the FPSO-disposal and contingent-consideration gains, and one that is widening in unpredictability rather than narrowing, since it flipped sign year over year [`06` §8, §10]. The adjustments management uses to bridge statutory to "Underlying" results are not genuinely stable one-offs: a different basket of items has been labelled "non-recurring" in every disclosed year (flotel/transition/relocation costs in FY2025; advisory/transaction costs in FY2024), while unsuccessful-exploration-well write-offs (US$10.9–12.0m/year) recur in both disclosed years yet are still excluded as if one-off [`06` §4]. To model normalized earnings for next year, this synthesis would start from Underlying EBITDAX (not statutory EBITDA, not GAAP/IFRS NPAT, and not the company's "Underlying NPAT" line as published), because Underlying EBITDAX is the only measure `03_margin-drivers.md` §4 finds is not currently distorted by the FY2025 one-off gains — and even then, the analyst should add back the unsuccessful-well write-offs as a recurring cost the company's own "Underlying" reconciliation improperly excludes.

### Consensus Bar

For Karoon to beat the current H1 2026 bar by a material margin, the swing would have to come from below the EBITDA line, not revenue — revenue is unusually well pre-known (0.99 ratio to already-disclosed Q1+Q2 cash sales revenue, `04_guidance-consensus.md` §1A) — specifically via unit production cost tracking the low end of guidance or D&A/interest/tax items landing more favourably than the single H1 analyst modelled, echoing the FY2024/FY2025 pattern of Net Income/EPS beating even when Revenue/EBITDA missed. The bar is most likely set incorrectly on the cost side, not the revenue side: the entire H1 2026 EBITDA estimate comes from one "Initiation of Coverage" analyst dated 2026-08-04, and US$46.5m of already-incurred flotel/FPSO integrity spend — disclosed before that estimate was set — is roughly 37% of the US$125.9m EBITDA bar, leaving real risk that this single-analyst model either fully captured or materially missed that cost line [`05_beat-miss-setup.md` §3, §10]. A meaningful share of the currently improving consensus revision picture is not a durable tailwind but a lagging response to a real, filed operational deterioration (the twice-cut CY2026 production guide and twice-raised capex guide), so revision breadth being negative should not be read as sandbagging — `04` §7 states plainly the cuts reflect genuine operational deterioration, not analyst over-caution, meaning any reversal of that negative breadth would itself require a genuine operational improvement, not just conservative repositioning unwinding.

## 6. Key Trend Summary

Revenue is **inflecting negative**: three years of strong growth under the old fiscal-year convention (+125.5% FY22, +47.1% FY23) gave way to a -19.0% decline in FY2025 (Revenue $776.5m → $628.6m) [FY2025 Annual Report, p.78], with both halves of FY2025 down double digits year-on-year (H1 -24.7%, H2 -12.8%; §3). Margins are **genuinely compressing, not expanding, despite what the reported EBITDA line shows**: gross margin fell 1,043bps (48.8% → 38.4%) in FY2025, while the company's own reported EBITDA margin rose 257bps (58.0% → 60.6%) over the same year [FY2025 Annual Report, Financial Summary p.48] — a contradiction this report will not average away (CLAUDE.md §3). The arithmetic: FY2025 EBITDA of $380.7m included a $35.3m gain on disposal of the Baúna FPSO right-of-use asset and a $21.2m positive change in fair value of contingent consideration (both cited in §4); stripping both ($380.7m − $35.3m − $21.2m = $324.2m) drops the adjusted EBITDA margin to 51.6% of revenue, a **641bps compression** versus FY2024's 58.0% — directionally consistent with, and roughly 60% the size of, the gross-margin decline. The reported EBITDA-margin "expansion" is a one-off-driven artefact, not an improvement in the underlying business; a reader relying on the reported EBITDA margin alone would draw the wrong conclusion. There is no material calendar seasonality provable from this pool (§5); the swings observed are company-specific (the FYE transition, the FPSO buyout, and falling realized prices — the last two belong to `03_margin-drivers` and `02_revenue-drivers` to fully attribute). The clearest inflection point in the five-year window is FY2025 itself: revenue, gross margin, and (once one-offs are stripped) EBITDA margin all turned down together after three years of expansion, while leverage stayed low (Net Debt/EBITDA 0.34x FY2024 → 0.36x FY2025, both CIQ-basis) because the FPSO buyout was funded largely from cash on hand rather than new debt.

## 6a. Decomposition Attribution and Residual (MODULE_RULES "Driver Attribution" / §15)

**Price and Volume rows: asserted from primary disclosure, not modelled ratios.** The CFO stated the $100.4m (price) and $47.5m (volume) figures directly on the FY2025 results call — these are reported numbers, not this report's own multiplication of a ratio, so no derivation is shown for them; they are cited at face value from a verbatim transcript [FY2025 Earnings Call, 26-Feb-2026, p.5].

**Independent cross-check — this report's own segment-level reconstruction, showing the arithmetic (§15).** Built from the `Q4 2025 Activities Report`'s year-to-date (Jan–Dec) production, sales, revenue and average-realized-price table (p.3) — the FY2024 vs FY2025 full-year columns, same basis both years (annual sales volume × annual average realized price per product stream). This ratio (each stream's FY2024 average realized price) is applied only to the FY2024→FY2025 volume change for that same stream, never carried to a different period or a different stream:

```
Baúna oil:
  Volume effect = (Sales_FY25 − Sales_FY24) × Price_FY24 [Q4 2025 Activities Report, p.3]
                = (7.35 − 7.83) MMbbl × $77.60/bbl = −0.48 × 77.60 ≈ −$37.2m
  Price effect  = (Price_FY25 − Price_FY24) × Sales_FY25
                = ($66.57 − $77.60)/bbl × 7.35 MMbbl = −11.03 × 7.35 ≈ −$81.1m
  Modelled Baúna revenue change ≈ −$118.3m vs actual −$116.8m (606.2→489.4)
  → basis matches (same product, same two adjacent years, no cross-basis use); residual +$1.5m (rounding of 2-decimal-place disclosed volumes/prices)

Who Dat liquids (oil/condensate/NGLs, NRI):
  Volume effect = (1.95 − 2.07) MMbbl × $75.84/bbl ≈ −$9.1m
  Price effect  = ($63.35 − $75.84)/bbl × 1.95 MMbbl ≈ −$24.4m
  Modelled change ≈ −$33.5m vs actual −$33.3m (157.1→123.8); residual −$0.2m

Who Dat sales gas (NRI):
  Volume effect = (3.66 − 4.49) Bcf × $2.95/mcf × 1,000 ≈ −$2.4m
  Price effect  = ($4.20 − $2.95)/mcf × 3.66 Bcf × 1,000 ≈ +$4.6m
  Modelled change ≈ +$2.2m vs actual +$2.0m (13.3→15.3); residual −$0.2m

Sum of this report's own model: Volume ≈ −$48.7m, Price ≈ −$100.9m, Total ≈ −$149.6m
  → basis matches throughout (all six effects use each stream's own FY2024→FY2025 volume/price, no substitution across products or periods)
```

**Reconciliation:** this report's independent bottom-up model (Volume −$48.7m, Price −$100.9m, Total −$149.6m) sits within **$1.7m (≈1.1%) of the CFO's own stated total decline of $147.9m**, and within $1.2m of the CFO's own Volume figure and $0.5m of the CFO's own Price figure. This is a small, rounding-driven residual (the Activities Report discloses volumes and prices to two decimal places, not to the precision the audited P&L uses) — **not a material unexplained gap.** Both the primary-disclosed bridge (§6) and this report's own independent segment-level reconstruction agree that **price explains roughly 68% of the FY2025 revenue decline ($100.4m of $147.9m)** and volume/sales-timing explains the remaining ≈32%. Section 7's "single biggest driver" claim rests on this well-reconciled arithmetic, not a rounded-away gap.

## 7. The Single Biggest Revenue Driver

**Realized oil/liquids price (Brent-linked at Baúna, Mars-differential-linked at Who Dat) is the single biggest revenue driver.** It explains roughly 68% of FY2025's $147.9m revenue decline by both management's own disclosed bridge and this report's independent segment-level reconstruction (§6a) — more than double the volume/sales-timing effect. A 10–20% move in realized price would swing group revenue by roughly $60–125m (10–20% of FY2025's $628.6m), against a production base that is ~93% oil/liquids-weighted [business-model `10_external-dependency.md`] and currently carries **zero active price hedges** [FY2025 Annual Report, Note 20(b); `2Q26 Activities Report`, p.5] — so essentially none of that move would be cushioned. The current direction is genuinely two-sided and should not be read as a clean "improving" signal: FY2025's full-year trend was a 14–16% YoY price decline, while 2Q26 realized prices spiked 33–55% quarter-on-quarter and 47–58% year-on-year, explicitly attributed by management to a geopolitical risk premium (Middle East conflict, Strait of Hormuz shipping disruption risk) rather than a demand recovery [Shareholder/Analyst Call, 21-May-2026, p.5]. Critically, **management's own 2H26 planning assumption is $60–70/bbl** [`2Q26 Activities Report`, p.2] — well below the $94.56/bbl Baúna price actually realized in 2Q26 — which is the company's own evidence that the recent spike is not being treated internally as a durable run-rate. Any reader extrapolating the 2Q26 price level forward into the imminent H1 2026 result or beyond is working from a base the company itself does not use.

Brazil (Baúna) is 77.9% of FY2025 revenue and 91.2% of FY2025 gross profit, clearing the >85%-of-one-segment threshold on gross profit though not on revenue — Karoon is read here as a Brazil-led, two-asset E&P company with a persistent Corporate/other financing drag, not a genuinely single-segment business [`business-model/03_segment-map.md` §2]. Business-model module output is available and used throughout (`03_segment-map.md`, `06_value-chain.md`, `10_external-dependency.md`, `02_business-identity.md`).

**Pass-through: no lag, because there is no negotiation to lag.** Karoon sells every barrel at a same-day benchmark-linked price (Brent-linked for Baúna, US Gulf Coast/Henry Hub-linked for Who Dat) — there is no bilateral contract with an escalator clause to lag, so "price" moves into revenue instantly [`business-model/06_value-chain.md` §2]. The only genuine lag in this business sits on the cost side: royalties (ad valorem, so they track price/volume mechanically and reduce automatically when price falls) provide a small, non-discretionary buffer, but management's own disclosed cost recovery against the FY2025 revenue shock was only ~9.5% (US$14m of favourable, named cost items against a US$147.9m revenue fall), most of it either mechanical (royalties) or non-repeatable (the FPSO lease-cost saving) [`business-model/06_value-chain.md` §2].

## 4. Margin Walk — Which Margin Level Matters Most?

For Karoon, **underlying EBITDAX margin (and the underlying unit netback that sits behind it) is the most decision-useful margin**, not statutory gross margin or statutory EBITDA/NPAT margin. The reasons: (1) statutory EBITDA and statutory NPAT are both currently distorted by one-off, non-repeating gains (FPSO disposal, contingent-consideration fair-value change) large enough to flip their direction versus the underlying business (Section 3); (2) gross margin, while directionally honest, mixes a large non-cash D&A swing (the FPSO ownership transition) with the cash cost-of-sales story, making it noisier than necessary for a business whose real driver is realised price against a largely fixed cash cost per barrel; (3) the company itself reports and is judged by underlying EBITDAX and underlying NPAT as "best present[ing] the key business drivers and performance" [FY2025 Annual Report, p.49]. Underlying EBITDAX margin fell only 155bps in FY2025 versus a 1,043bps gross-margin decline — this is the correct, decision-useful read: real compression happened, but it is far smaller than gross margin alone suggests once one-offs and the FPSO D&A mechanics are stripped out.

## 7a. Bridge Attribution and Residual

The dollar bridge above is not a modelled/sensitivity-derived bridge — every line is a reported segment-note figure, and revenue minus cost of sales equals gross profit by construction, so the dollar-level reconciliation is exact with **zero residual**. The one place a sensitivity/modelled figure is used is the translation of these dollars into the **basis-point margin change** the Section 7 template asks for, and that translation is shown explicitly below because a linear bps conversion does not reconcile exactly when the revenue denominator itself moves 19%:

```
Price:          -US$100.4m ÷ US$776.5m (FY24 revenue base) = -12.93% of revenue = -1,293bps
Volume:          -US$47.5m ÷ US$776.5m (FY24 revenue base) =  -6.12% of revenue =   -612bps
Royalties:         +US$6.8m ÷ US$776.5m (FY24 revenue base) =  +0.88% of revenue =    +88bps
Operating costs:  -US$28.3m ÷ US$776.5m (FY24 revenue base) =  -3.64% of revenue =   -364bps
Flotel + transition (one-off): -US$25.5m ÷ US$776.5m         =  -3.28% of revenue =   -329bps
D&A, production assets:  +US$11.9m ÷ US$776.5m               =  +1.53% of revenue =   +153bps
FPSO D&A net (owned +13.9m headwind, ROU lease -31.0m tailwind) = +US$17.1m net ÷ US$776.5m = +2.20% = +220bps
Inventory timing: +US$26.3m ÷ US$776.5m                       =  +3.39% of revenue =   +339bps
Transport/carbon: +US$1.8m ÷ US$776.5m                        =  +0.23% of revenue =    +23bps
  → Modelled sum: -1,905 (revenue) + 130 (cost, matching the +US$10.1m dollar tailwind) = -1,775bps
  → Basis: every component above uses FY2024 (US$776.5m) as a FIXED denominator — this basis matches
    the dollar figures it was built from, but NOT the actual FY25 vs FY24 margin-percentage denominators
    (US$628.6m vs US$776.5m), which is why it does not equal the observed change.
```

**Reconciliation:** the modelled bps sum is **-1,775bps**; the observed gross-margin change (Section 3) is **-1,043bps**. Gap = **+732bps**. This gap is **not an unexplained driver** — the dollar bridge above already reconciles exactly to zero residual (Section 7) — it is a mechanical artefact of translating dollar changes into percentage-margin bps against a fixed prior-year revenue base while revenue itself fell 19%. As revenue shrinks, the same dollar cost movements represent a smaller share of the current, smaller revenue base than they do of the fixed prior-year base used above, so a linear bps bridge overstates the compression versus the true percentage-point change. This is stated explicitly rather than rounded away: **the dollar-level bridge is the trustworthy version of this table; the bps translation is directionally correct (both point to price as the dominant driver, cost items as a modest net offset) but should not be read as decomposing the exact -1,043bps to the basis point.**

## 8. The Single Biggest Margin Driver

**The realised Baúna crude price (Brent-linked).** Of the US$147.9m FY2025 revenue decline, US$100.4m (68%) was management's own disclosed price effect versus US$47.5m (32%) volume — and on the cost side, unit production costs are largely fixed short-term, so a given price move flows almost directly to cash margin ("costs largely fixed, that revenue reduction flowed directly to underlying EBITDAX" [2025 Earnings Call, Feb-26-2026]). This single driver clears well over half the observed revenue-side change and, because cost of sales moved only 2.5% against it, dominates the gross-margin outcome too — the arithmetic in Section 7 supports calling it the single biggest driver, not merely "a" driver. **Its current direction is reversing, but not on a run-rate basis:** 2Q26 saw Baúna crude spike to US$94.56/bbl (+33% q/q) on Middle East/Strait of Hormuz-driven geopolitical disruption — a level above every full-year average in the FY2021-2025 history (group-blended range US$59.00/bbl to US$84.74/bbl [`business-model/04_unit-economics.md` §4]) — yet management's own CY2026 guidance assumes a materially lower US$60-70/bbl for 2H26 [2Q26 Activities Report, p.4]. Per the Cycle-Position Rule: **the 2Q26 realised-price level is not run-rate and should not be extrapolated** — it is a one-time geopolitical spike that management itself is not budgeting to persist, and the company has no active hedge to lock in the current elevated level should it reverse before 2H26 volumes are sold.

## 9. Investment Spend — Both Signs

Karoon's aggregate capex is **not clearly running well above its own history** — CY2026 guided total capex of US$178-202m (plus US$28m of the final Petrobras contingent-consideration instalment) sits close to FY2024's US$218.6m and below FY2025's US$288.5m (which included the one-off US$115m FPSO purchase) [`01_historical-financials.md` §1; 2Q26 Activities Report, p.6]. Within a single quarter, though, 2Q26 capex jumped to US$126.6m from US$39.5m in 1Q26 (+221% q/q) on the SPS-92 intervention, the Baúna flotel campaign, and the Who Dat A1 sidetrack [2Q26 Activities Report, p.4] — all sustaining/workover spend aimed at restoring or extending existing production, not new-field growth capex. Two capacity-adding decisions are pending and would change this picture: the Who Dat East Final Investment Decision (FID, expected 3Q26) and the Neon development milestone (expected 4Q26) [2Q26 Activities Report, p.5].

| Reading | What it would show | Evidence here |
|---|---|---|
| Spend as a future COST | Rising unit DD&A (guided US$15-17/boe CY26 vs US$15.19/boe FY25 actual, vs US$14.90/boe FY24) as the owned FPSO and any FID-sanctioned Who Dat East / Neon capex is depreciated; net interest costs already rose 52% as cash was drawn down to fund the FPSO buyout | FY2025 Annual Report, p.49-50; 2Q26 Activities Report, p.6 |
| Spend as a DEMAND/RESERVES signal | An E&P producer has no sales backlog in the SaaS/cloud sense — the closest analogue is proved-reserve conversion: Baúna 2P reserves grew via 2C-to-2P conversion in 2025, group 2P reserve life extended from 6.5 to 7.1 years, and the FPSO buyout itself was underwritten to a mid-teens-plus post-tax IRR and a disclosed 4-year payback | `business-model/04_unit-economics.md` §2; FY2025 Annual Report, p.36 |

## 7. Bar Assessment

**Bar is fair — provisional, and moot within hours: the H1/FY2026 result is scheduled for release today (2026-08-27), the same date as this report, and none of the figures above reflect it.**

The revenue side of the imminent H1 2026 print is largely pre-known: the company's own already-disclosed Q1+Q2 2026 sales revenue (US$244.6m) matches the restated FH1 2026 consensus (≈US$243.3m) almost exactly (ratio 0.99, §1A), so a revenue surprise in either direction is unlikely. What has moved, and moved consistently in one direction, is the **operating plan itself**: management cut CY2026 production guidance by ≈11% at the midpoint and raised capex guidance by ≈55% at the midpoint between January and July 2026 (§2), and Street revision breadth over the trailing 1–3 months is net negative across Revenue, EBITDA, EBIT, and EPS Normalized with no offsetting upward-revision period in the data (§5) — consensus has been chasing the guidance cuts down, not sitting ahead of them or lagging them into a sandbagged low bar. Against that, the historical pattern over the last two full-year prints has been to miss Street on Revenue/EBITDA while beating on Net Income and EPS Normalized (§6) — a genuine, unresolved divergence that could repeat and would read as a "beat" on the headline EPS line even if revenue/EBITDA disappoint again. Net of these: the bar is not obviously low (a genuine operational deterioration, not analyst over-caution, drove the guidance cuts) and not obviously high (consensus has already re-based down to track the cuts, and the revenue print itself is largely de-risked by the quarterly disclosures) — call it fair, with the real swing factor for this print being costs, D&A, one-offs (the US$46.5m flotel spend already incurred in H1, per §2) and tax/FX below the EBITDA line, exactly where the last two years' beats have come from.

## 6. Seasonality Read

There is no provable calendar seasonality in this pool — Karoon has only 2.5 fiscal years of full half-year P&L history, short of the three years needed to separate seasonality from company-specific events [`01_historical-financials.md` §5]. What the half-year data does show is company-specific, not calendar-driven: H1 has outweighed H2 on gross margin in the periods observed, but the H1 2025 EBITDAX-margin spike (+1,340bps YoY) was itself shown to be a one-off gain, not a repeatable H1 pattern [`01_historical-financials.md` §3]. For H1 2026 specifically, management's own "two contrasting halves" framing — heavy capex and cost (85% of the year's capex already spent by 30-Jun-2026, the bulk of the flotel spend already incurred) concentrated in H1, with a cleaner, lower-cost H2 targeted — cuts against any assumption that H1 is the seasonally easier half to beat; if anything, the cost load is front-loaded into the period reporting today.

## 7. Historical Pattern

Karoon has reported full-year results annually since FY2023 (only three full-year prints exist in this pool), and the pattern across the last two is consistent: Revenue and EBITDA missed Street consensus in both FY2024 (-1.2%/-3.5%) and FY2025 (-3.3%/-3.9%), while Net Income (Excl. Excep.) and EPS Normalized beat in both years (+9.2%/+7.7% and +4.5%/+5.3%) [`04_guidance-consensus.md` §6]. This is judgment informed by an n=2-consecutive-year sample (well below the ~8 observations needed for an empirical base rate per CLAUDE.md §10), not a measured frequency, and it is a genuine divergence — not noise — since the same split shows up at the half-year level too (H1 2025 revenue -25% YoY, H1 2025 NPAT +15% YoY). The synthesizer should weight it as a real but thin signal that revenue/EBITDA and NPAT/EPS can diverge for this company, not as a reliable predictor of the exact size of any H1 2026 divergence.

## 8. Setup Verdict

**Setup is balanced.** Revenue is unusually well pre-known (0.99 match to already-disclosed cash sales revenue), removing it as a source of surprise either way. Below that line, the evidence pulls in two directions at similar strength: EBITDA/margin faces multiple, specific, judgment-based headwinds (US$46.5m of flotel spend already incurred against a US$125.9m EBITDA bar, the new export tax, unit-cost dilution from the 2Q26 shut-ins, and rising interest costs on widened net debt — §3), corroborated by broad-based negative Street revision breadth over 1–3 months (§5), while the two-year historical pattern of NPAT/EPS beating even as Revenue/EBITDA missed (§7) argues for resilience below the EBITDA line. The single most important factor is whether the US$46.5m of already-incurred flotel/FPSO integrity spend was fully captured by the single analyst who set the thin FH1 2026 EBITDA estimate on 2026-08-04 — if it was not, EBITDA misses; if it was, the historical below-the-line pattern has room to produce another NPAT/EPS beat. The biggest risk to this verdict is a further, undisclosed CY2026 guidance cut or export-tax extension announced alongside the H1 print itself, which would tip the balance decisively toward miss.

## 9. Second-Quarter Look-Ahead

The setup for H2 2026 could look meaningfully different: management's own framing is that the heavy 1H26 capex and cost load (flotel campaign, SPS-92 and Who Dat interventions, 85% of full-year capex already spent) is intended to enable a "materially better" second half, with Baúna FPSO efficiency already back to 97.2% in 2Q26 and the E-riser and A1 sidetrack work targeted for resolution through 3Q26–4Q27 [`03_margin-drivers.md` §9; `02_revenue-drivers.md` §4]. That thesis is unproven and depends on execution the company does not fully control (Who Dat is non-operated by LLOG) and on whether the Brazilian export tax (currently extended to ~7-Sep-2026) lapses or is extended again — neither of which this report can resolve ahead of the H1 print.

## 10. Pre-Mortem

If this setup read turns out wrong, the most likely reason is over-reliance on a single-analyst, three-week-old FH1 2026 estimate as if it were a broad consensus — a one-analyst bar can be stale or mis-modelled on a specific cost line (flotel spend, export tax) in ways a nine-analyst FY2026 consensus would average out. A secondary likely reason is that management discloses a further guidance cut or cost item alongside the H1 print itself, which this report — built entirely from pre-print data — could not see coming.

## 4. The Single Highest-Sensitivity Variable

**The group-blended realized oil/liquids price** moves earnings the most by a wide margin — its ranked impact (~US$110.7m) is roughly five times the next variable (production volume, ~US$21.9m). It is entirely external and company-controlled in name only: Karoon is a spot/benchmark price-taker with zero active hedges, so essentially none of a price move is cushioned before it reaches the P&L (§2). Its current direction is genuinely two-sided, not a clean signal: FY2025 fell 14-16% year-on-year, while 2Q26 spiked 33-58% quarter-on-quarter on a geopolitical risk premium (Middle East conflict, Strait of Hormuz shipping-disruption risk) that management itself attributes to events outside the company's control [Shareholder/Analyst Call, 21-May-2026, p.5], and management's own 2H26 planning assumption (US$60-70/bbl) sits *below* the actual 2Q26 print — the company's own evidence that the recent spike is not being budgeted as durable. For the bear case to materialize, the reverse of the 2026 spike would need to happen: de-escalation of the Middle East/Strait-of-Hormuz risk premium combined with continued OPEC+ supply growth and subdued global demand, pushing the group-blended realized price back toward or below the FY2021 trough of US$59.00/bbl — a level the company has actually realized before, within the last five years, so this is not a hypothetical tail case.

## 5. Interaction Effects

Several of these variables move together rather than independently. First, oil price and geopolitics are the same channel restated: the 2Q26 price spike is management's own named geopolitical-risk-premium event, so a scenario that assumes an adverse price move should not also independently assume calm geopolitics — they are the same driver counted once. Second, oil price and the Brazilian export tax interact in a way that partially offsets the price bull case: because the tax is ad valorem, a higher realized price mechanically increases the dollar tax bill on every taxed cargo, so the pure upside from a price rally is somewhat smaller than the price coefficient alone implies (this is not modelled as a joint scenario above, but the direction of the interaction is real and should discount the price bull case slightly). Third, production volume and unit production cost are mechanically linked through operating deleverage: a volume shortfall (the Who Dat E-riser leak, a Baúna FPSO reliability event) does not just cut the volume line — it simultaneously worsens unit cost per barrel because largely-fixed operating costs are spread over fewer barrels, exactly as already observed at Who Dat (unit cost +7.6% YoY in FY2025 on lower production) [`03_margin-drivers.md` §2]. Compounding these two rows together, rather than treating them as independent, would produce a materially worse combined outcome than either row's isolated bear case suggests. Fourth, a sustained weakness in either price or volume could reduce the Reserve-Based Lending facility's redetermined borrowing base [`business-model/10_external-dependency.md` §5], a second-order link from the operating variables to the financing-cost variable that the static US$60-70m interest guidance range does not itself capture.

## 6. Non-Linear Or Asymmetric Risks

- **Operating deleverage on volume shortfalls.** As described in §5, a volume miss does not scale linearly with the disclosed contribution-margin-per-boe coefficient — it also raises unit production cost, so the true downside of a volume shortfall is larger than the symmetric bull/bear figures shown in §2 imply. This is asymmetric: a volume beat does not receive the mirror-image benefit (unit costs do not fall as sharply on a volume upside as they rise on a shortfall, because a meaningful share of the cost base — crew, maintenance, flotel campaigns — is scheduled/contracted regardless of production rate).
- **Zero hedges means zero floor.** With no active price hedges at 31-Dec-2025 or as of the 2Q26 filing, Karoon carries no asymmetric protection against a price decline the way a hedged producer with a collar would — the full downside of the price coefficient in §2 is live, not capped.
- **Single-FPSO, single-well concentration at Baúna.** Because the entire Brazil segment (91.2% of FY2025 gross profit) runs through one vessel, a reliability event does not degrade output proportionally across a diversified asset base — it can, and did in 2Q26, cut segment production by 46% quarter-on-quarter in a single planned-maintenance window, and an *unplanned* event of similar scale would hit with no warning.
- **Non-operated Who Dat asset.** Karoon holds a 30% working interest but does not control the timeline of the E-riser repair (targeted 3Q26 by operator LLOG) or the Who Dat East FID (expected 3Q26) — the company's own largest near-term US recovery lever is on someone else's schedule, which is a binary/step-change risk (production resumes on the operator's timetable or it does not) rather than a smooth, linearly-modellable one.
- **Export tax ad valorem structure.** As noted in §5, the tax's dollar cost rises automatically with price, meaning the price bull case and the export-tax bear case are not fully independent — a reader modelling both a price rally and an extended/expanded export tax simultaneously should not simply add the two impacts, because part of the tax increase is itself a function of the price increase.

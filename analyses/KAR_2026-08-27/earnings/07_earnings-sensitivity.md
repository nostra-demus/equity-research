# Earnings Sensitivity — KAR

## 1. Variable Selection

All three required upstream outputs (`01_historical-financials.md`, `02_revenue-drivers.md`, `03_margin-drivers.md`) are present and used, together with the optional cross-module input `business-model/10_external-dependency.md`. The six variables below were pulled from the "Magnitude" columns of `02`'s Revenue Driver Table (§4) and `03`'s Margin Driver Table (§5), keeping every driver rated **High** and the highest-evidenced **Mid**-rated drivers, and dropping drivers that are either non-recurring (the FY2025 FPSO ownership transition, already completed), structural-but-non-numeric (non-operated status, segment mix), or zero-rated (FX translation, market share — both explicitly "None"/"0" in `02`). The realized oil/liquids price and total production volume are both rated **High** in `02`/`03` and are corroborated by `business-model/10_external-dependency.md`, which independently scores commodity price as the single **High**-dependency external variable and names it "the single biggest lever" (§5 of that report). Unit production cost, net interest & finance costs, and unit DD&A are company-guided **Mid**-magnitude items with clean CY2026 guidance ranges (`04_guidance-consensus.md` §2). The Brazilian export tax is a **Mid** (currently) / **Mid-High if extended** policy variable flagged in both `02` and `03` and in `business-model/10_external-dependency.md` §1A as a named, currently-live, already-once-extended programme. Royalties/government take (Mid magnitude in `03`) is *not* given its own row — it moves mechanically with the oil-price variable (ad valorem, ~7.0% of revenue) and is folded into that variable's realized-offset calculation instead, to avoid double-counting the same underlying price move twice.

## 4. The Single Highest-Sensitivity Variable

**The group-blended realized oil/liquids price** moves earnings the most by a wide margin — its ranked impact (~US$110.7m) is roughly five times the next variable (production volume, ~US$21.9m). It is entirely external and company-controlled in name only: Karoon is a spot/benchmark price-taker with zero active hedges, so essentially none of a price move is cushioned before it reaches the P&L (§2). Its current direction is genuinely two-sided, not a clean signal: FY2025 fell 14-16% year-on-year, while 2Q26 spiked 33-58% quarter-on-quarter on a geopolitical risk premium (Middle East conflict, Strait of Hormuz shipping-disruption risk) that management itself attributes to events outside the company's control [Shareholder/Analyst Call, 21-May-2026, p.5], and management's own 2H26 planning assumption (US$60-70/bbl) sits *below* the actual 2Q26 print — the company's own evidence that the recent spike is not being budgeted as durable. For the bear case to materialize, the reverse of the 2026 spike would need to happen: de-escalation of the Middle East/Strait-of-Hormuz risk premium combined with continued OPEC+ supply growth and subdued global demand, pushing the group-blended realized price back toward or below the FY2021 trough of US$59.00/bbl — a level the company has actually realized before, within the last five years, so this is not a hypothetical tail case.

## 5. Interaction Effects

Several of these variables move together rather than independently. First, oil price and geopolitics are the same channel restated: the 2Q26 price spike is management's own named geopolitical-risk-premium event, so a scenario that assumes an adverse price move should not also independently assume calm geopolitics — they are the same driver counted once. Second, oil price and the Brazilian export tax interact in a way that partially offsets the price bull case: because the tax is ad valorem, a higher realized price mechanically increases the dollar tax bill on every taxed cargo, so the pure upside from a price rally is somewhat smaller than the price coefficient alone implies (this is not modelled as a joint scenario above, but the direction of the interaction is real and should discount the price bull case slightly). Third, production volume and unit production cost are mechanically linked through operating deleverage: a volume shortfall (the Who Dat E-riser leak, a Baúna FPSO reliability event) does not just cut the volume line — it simultaneously worsens unit cost per barrel because largely-fixed operating costs are spread over fewer barrels, exactly as already observed at Who Dat (unit cost +7.6% YoY in FY2025 on lower production) [`03_margin-drivers.md` §2]. Compounding these two rows together, rather than treating them as independent, would produce a materially worse combined outcome than either row's isolated bear case suggests. Fourth, a sustained weakness in either price or volume could reduce the Reserve-Based Lending facility's redetermined borrowing base [`business-model/10_external-dependency.md` §5], a second-order link from the operating variables to the financing-cost variable that the static US$60-70m interest guidance range does not itself capture.

## 6. Non-Linear Or Asymmetric Risks

- **Operating deleverage on volume shortfalls.** As described in §5, a volume miss does not scale linearly with the disclosed contribution-margin-per-boe coefficient — it also raises unit production cost, so the true downside of a volume shortfall is larger than the symmetric bull/bear figures shown in §2 imply. This is asymmetric: a volume beat does not receive the mirror-image benefit (unit costs do not fall as sharply on a volume upside as they rise on a shortfall, because a meaningful share of the cost base — crew, maintenance, flotel campaigns — is scheduled/contracted regardless of production rate).
- **Zero hedges means zero floor.** With no active price hedges at 31-Dec-2025 or as of the 2Q26 filing, Karoon carries no asymmetric protection against a price decline the way a hedged producer with a collar would — the full downside of the price coefficient in §2 is live, not capped.
- **Single-FPSO, single-well concentration at Baúna.** Because the entire Brazil segment (91.2% of FY2025 gross profit) runs through one vessel, a reliability event does not degrade output proportionally across a diversified asset base — it can, and did in 2Q26, cut segment production by 46% quarter-on-quarter in a single planned-maintenance window, and an *unplanned* event of similar scale would hit with no warning.
- **Non-operated Who Dat asset.** Karoon holds a 30% working interest but does not control the timeline of the E-riser repair (targeted 3Q26 by operator LLOG) or the Who Dat East FID (expected 3Q26) — the company's own largest near-term US recovery lever is on someone else's schedule, which is a binary/step-change risk (production resumes on the operator's timetable or it does not) rather than a smooth, linearly-modellable one.
- **Export tax ad valorem structure.** As noted in §5, the tax's dollar cost rises automatically with price, meaning the price bull case and the export-tax bear case are not fully independent — a reader modelling both a price rally and an extended/expanded export tax simultaneously should not simply add the two impacts, because part of the tax increase is itself a function of the price increase.

## 7. Earnings Volatility Score

**74/100** (0-100 scale, **higher = WORSE / more volatile** — inverted score, High-volatility band 61-80).

Karoon is ~93% oil/liquids-weighted, sells 100% of production at same-day benchmark-linked prices with zero active hedges, has its dominant segment (91.2% of FY2025 gross profit) running through a single FPSO, has its second segment non-operated with an unresolved multi-quarter production shut-in, is currently exposed to a live and already-once-extended government export tax with no established end date, and saw net debt roughly double in 1H26 (US$132.7m to US$269.7m) while funding a capex-heavy first half — several High-magnitude variables (price, volume) are stacking simultaneously with limited near-term company-side mitigation levers (no hedges, no control over the operator's repair timeline, no lever over a foreign government's tax policy). This keeps the score in the upper half of the High-volatility band rather than the lower half. It is not placed in the Extremely-volatile band (81-100) because Karoon does retain some real, evidenced levers not captured in this table — low unit production costs relative to realized prices, discretionary and price-linked capital-returns spending, hedging optionality it currently chooses not to use, and diversification across two producing basins and fiscal regimes rather than one [`business-model/10_external-dependency.md` §3]. Confidence in this score is Medium-High: three of the six sensitivity rows (unit production cost, unit DD&A, net interest) rest on direct company-disclosed guidance ranges, while the two highest-ranked rows (price, volume) rely on this report's own per-unit derivations from disclosed inputs — the MODULE_RULES cap requiring Low confidence when sensitivities are "only inferred" does not strictly apply here, since the majority of the dollar-material rows are guidance-anchored, not purely inferred.

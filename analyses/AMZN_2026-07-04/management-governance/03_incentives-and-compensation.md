**Conclusion on metrics:** Amazon uses exclusively time-vested RSUs with no explicit performance hurdles of any kind. No annual bonus plan. No LTIP with ROIC, EPS, or TSR hurdles. The link to per-share value is entirely through stock price — but it is implicit and unconditional: executives receive shares regardless of operating performance, as long as they remain employed. This is a structurally unusual model among S&P 500 mega-caps. Its effect is directional: Jassy cannot earn a cash bonus by growing revenue but hurting margins; his wealth accumulates only if the stock price rises. However, it also means mediocre but stable performance would still vest shares, since there is no performance condition.

## 4. Incentive Alignment Read

Amazon's compensation model is the simplest in the S&P 500 mega-cap peer group: no annual bonus plan, no explicit performance metrics of any kind, and a single vehicle — time-vested restricted stock units — that link executive wealth entirely to stock price appreciation over a multi-year horizon. The stated philosophy (per the 2026 DEF 14A) is that this "directly aligns executives' compensation with the returns we deliver to shareholders." That claim has economic substance: Jassy cannot enrich himself by growing revenue while destroying margins; his financial outcome is bound to the equity market's verdict on Amazon's long-term per-share value. The biggest limitation of this model is that vesting is unconditional — there is no performance hurdle (no ROIC minimum, no TSR threshold, no EPS floor) that would prevent RSU vesting even if operating results deteriorated. A management team that ran the company at mediocre-but-stable results could still vest their full grant. In practice, the model has worked during the Jassy era because operating income grew 6.5x (FY2022–FY2025) and the stock re-rated, but this is not a structural protection — it is a favorable outcome that happened to coincide with the time-vested schedule.

The single biggest incentive signal: **management is paid in equity that they cannot convert to cash except by holding for years (the back-loaded vesting schedule), which removes the short-termism of quarterly earnings management**. It does not, however, create an explicit incentive to deliver returns above the cost of capital (ROIC) or to improve per-share metrics beyond the stock price.

**Verdict: Mixed.** The structure is directionally per-share-oriented (equity only, no revenue or EBITDA bonuses), but lacks explicit returns-based or per-share performance hurdles. The absence of an annual bonus or any size-based metric is a structural positive; the absence of any performance condition on RSU vesting is a structural limitation.

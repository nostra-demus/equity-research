## 3. Stated Strategy & Targets

Management's stated strategy, as set out across the FY2025 Shareholder Letter (Andy Jassy, Apr 2026), the FY2024 Shareholder Letter (Andy Jassy, Apr 2025), and the FY2025 10-K, rests on three explicit bets: (1) continuing to lower the cost of and accelerate delivery speed in the Stores/retail business, targeting grocery and same-day delivery as the next expansion layer, with an explicit investment of over $4 billion in rural delivery and plans to reach 1 billion additional packages per year to 13,000 zip codes; (2) investing aggressively in AWS and AI infrastructure — approximately $200 billion of capital expenditures in 2026 — to capitalize on enterprise cloud migration and generative AI demand, with management explicitly stating they are "investing to be the meaningful leader" and citing the AWS AI revenue run rate exceeding $15 billion annually in Q1 2026; and (3) building new long-cycle businesses (Amazon Leo satellite broadband, Project Kuiper, Zoox autonomous vehicles, Amazon Pharmacy, robotics) where management explicitly accepts near-term FCF compression in exchange for what it describes as large long-term ROIC. The explicit, checkable targets management has set are: AWS growth continuing above 20% year-over-year (delivered 20% in FY2025 and 28% in Q1 2026); operating margin improvement toward and beyond 11% (delivered 11.2% in FY2025); reduction in cost-to-serve per unit (delivered in 2023 for the first time since 2018, continued in 2024); and FCF recovering after the capex surge once capacity is monetized — though management has not set a specific FCF dollar target for the recovery.

## 5. Management Read

Andy Jassy has been CEO for five years and has delivered substantially on what he promised: the 2022–2023 efficiency restructuring translated into operating income growth from $12.2B (FY2022) to $80.0B (FY2025) — a 6.5x increase — while AWS has re-accelerated from 13% to 28% growth exactly as management telegraphed. The CFO (Olsavsky, 11-year tenure) and team have set and met guidance consistently, and the one material miss relative to investor expectations — free cash flow compression in FY2025 — was explicitly warned in advance in the FY2024 shareholder letter and is management-chosen rather than a surprise. The single biggest management signal, positive in nature, is the combination of deep institutional knowledge (Jassy spent 25+ years at Amazon before becoming CEO, building AWS from scratch), a record of set-and-meet promises over a multi-year window, and stable leadership depth (no CEO/CFO changes in three years, orderly AWS succession). The primary uncertainty is not management competence but the binary risk of the $200B annual capex bet on AI infrastructure — a bet management is making consciously and with disclosed customer pre-commitments, but one where execution risk and demand-realization risk are real.

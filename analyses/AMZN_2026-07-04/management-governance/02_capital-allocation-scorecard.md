**Key findings:**

1. **The $10B buyback program authorized in March 2022 has been 39% used (only $3.9B) in four years.** $6.1B remains unused as of December 31, 2025. [FY2025 10-K, Note 8, p.62]

2. **Buybacks never offset SBC dilution.** Shares outstanding rose from 10,242M (FY2021) to 10,754M (FY2025), a net increase of 512M shares (+5.0% over five years or ~+1.0%/year). RF-CAP-002 is triggered (buybacks not reducing share count). Gross SBC vesting over the period was approximately 558M shares; the $6B buyback offset only 46.2M of those shares in one year.

3. **Intrinsic-value discipline not fully assessable from this module** (valuation module has not run). However, on observable evidence: the $129.87/share average buyback price in FY2022 was not at the post-split trough (~$102 in late 2022). The company bought $6B before the stock fell and did nothing as the stock spent much of FY2023 well below the buyback price. This is not a pattern of patient, opportunistic repurchasing.

4. **Insiders sold into the dormant buyback period.** Jeff Bezos adopted a Rule 10b5-1 plan to sell up to 15M shares through February 2026; Andy Jassy adopted a plan to sell up to 142,224 shares through December 2026. The company was simultaneously sitting on $6.1B in unused buyback authorization. [FY2025 10-K, Item 9B, p.74]

5. **Counterpoint:** Management's stated rationale for not buying back stock — that all capital should be reinvested in AWS/AI infrastructure — is internally consistent. The $131.8B capex in FY2025 dwarfs the $6.1B buyback authorization by 21x. The decision not to buy back shares is a defensible opportunity-cost trade-off (reinvest at 15–20% incremental ROIC rather than repurchase at a market multiple), but it does leave per-share value creation to organic earnings growth rather than share count reduction.

**ROIC computation methodology:**
- NOPAT = EBIT × (1 − 10%), using a conservative flat 10% tax rate (effective tax rates vary: ~7% FY2025, ~9% FY2024). The 10% rate is used as a through-cycle normalisation.
- Invested capital (IC) = total common equity + total financial debt (short-term borrowings + current LT debt + LT debt) − total cash and short-term investments. Lease obligations are excluded from financial debt (they are tracked separately via the broad net-debt basis).
- Beginning-of-year IC uses prior FY year-end balance sheet. FY2020 IC = $99,870M (equity $138,245M + fin. debt $57,674M − cash $96,049M).
- [Capital IQ Balance Sheet; FY2025 10-K, p.39 (Balance Sheet); FY2021 10-K Balance Sheet]

**Incremental ROIC detail:**

| Period | ΔNOPAT | ΔInvested Capital | Incremental ROIC |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| FY2021→FY2022 | ($10,378)M | +$27,789M | (37.3%) |
| FY2022→FY2023 | +$21,154M | +$61,050M | 34.6% |
| FY2023→FY2024 | +$28,567M | +$115,074M | 24.8% |
| FY2024→FY2025 | +$10,244M | +$64,378M | 15.9% |

**Interpretation:**
- FY2022 incremental ROIC was negative because Amazon significantly over-built fulfillment capacity during COVID and absorbed large equity investment losses from Rivian. This was a recognized misallocation year (EBIT fell from $24.9B to $13.3B on a $27.8B increase in invested capital).
- FY2023–FY2024 incremental ROIC of 35% and 25% respectively represents strong compounding evidence: each incremental dollar of capital deployed in those years generated well above the 9–10% estimated cost of capital.
- FY2025 incremental ROIC of 15.9% remains above cost of capital, but signals the initial wave of AWS/AI capex is compressing returns per-dollar-deployed. AWS segment capex jumped from $53.3B (FY2024) to $96.5B (FY2025), and the full revenue benefit of that spend is a 12–24 month lag.
- The FY2025 decline from 24.8% to 15.9% incremental ROIC is expected and not alarming — it reflects the front-loading of capex ahead of confirmed AWS backlog ($364B as of Q1 2026 per Q1 2026 Earnings Call, Apr 29, 2026). However, if FY2026–2027 incremental ROIC falls below 12%, the AWS investment thesis weakens materially.

**Balance-sheet prudence:**
- Net debt / EBITDA (broad basis, including leases): declining from 0.87x (FY2023) to 0.38x (FY2025). Strict net cash position at FY2025: ~$46.1B. [FY2025 10-K, p.39; Capital IQ Ratios]
- LTM Mar-31-2026 net debt (broad basis) rose to $92.5B, driven by $15.7B in new long-term debt issued November 2025 and further Q1 2026 debt issuance ($54B+ increment per Capital IQ). Strict basis at Mar-31-2026 is approximately net cash ~$12.6B (Q1 2026 10-Q balance sheet).
- Total contractual commitments: $439.7B as of Dec 31, 2025 — a very large off-balance-sheet obligation set, but all disclosed and asset-backed. [FY2025 10-K, Note 7, p.59]
- EBIT/interest coverage: 35.2x (FY2025). No solvency risk. [Capital IQ Ratios]

## 5. Capital-Allocation Read

Amazon's capital allocation over FY2021–FY2025 is **value-creative but with a clear flaw**: organic reinvestment in AWS and AI infrastructure is the dominant use (90.5% of CFO) and is generating returns (18–20% ROIC in FY2024–FY2025) that comfortably exceed the cost of capital; however, persistent SBC dilution (+5.0% net share count over five years) with a dormant buyback program means per-share value is created entirely through earnings power growth, not capital return. The best use of capital is unambiguously organic reinvestment — incremental ROIC of 25–35% in FY2023–FY2024 is exceptional by any standard. The worst use is the share-dilution-without-offsetting-buybacks dynamic: management sits on a $6.1B authorization while issuing equity-equivalent compensation at scale. M&A (MGM, One Medical) is bolt-on, rationally priced, and not the story — these are small deals relative to Amazon's scale. The single biggest capital-allocation signal is the $131.8B capex in FY2025 (a 3.1x capex-to-D&A ratio) guided to approximately $200B in FY2026: this is the largest organic capital bet in corporate history by dollar quantum, and the degree to which AWS backlog converts to revenue over the next 12–24 months will determine whether the incremental ROIC holds at 15%+ or collapses below cost of capital.

**Verdict: value-creative / disciplined**, with one ongoing blemish (share dilution not offset by buybacks).

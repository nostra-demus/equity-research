## 5. Minority-Shareholder Protection Read

Amazon's board is genuinely independent in structure: 83% independent directors, all major committees fully independent and chaired by independents, a Lead Independent Director (Gorelick) who engages directly with shareholders, majority voting for directors, and no defensive measures that suppress shareholder voice. The chair/CEO split (Bezos/Jassy) is real — Bezos' influence is economic (8.8% ownership), not structural (he has one vote per share, same as every other shareholder). There is no dual-class shield, no classified board, no supermajority protection, and no poison pill. Rights — special meetings at 25%, written consent at 50%, proxy access, no supermajority barrier — are better than typical for a company of this size and complexity.

The single biggest risk to minority holders is not structural but behavioral: the Board's compensation committee has failed to bring say-on-pay support above 80% despite three years of effort, and ISS has recommended against management's pay proposals in three consecutive years. This is a governance friction point that cannot be dismissed, even as it has materially improved from the 2022 nadir. The long tenures of Stonesifer (29 years) and Rubinstein (15 years) raise standard questions about director freshness, though no evidence of captured independence exists.

Additionally, Wendell Weeks — Audit Committee member — is a sitting CEO and Chair of Corning Incorporated; his dual commitment is a mild structural concern. Brad Smith's recent departure from Humana (April 2025) reduced one overboarding concern.

There is no §24 Filter 6 (Structurally Unaligned Controlling Owner) concern here. Bezos' ownership aligns his interests with per-share value; he does not control a separate parent entity extracting value from Amazon, nor is Amazon a subsidiary of a value-maximizing third party. The RF-OWN-004 filter does not trip.

**Verdict: Adequate.** The board is structurally independent, rights are intact and genuinely shareholder-friendly, and no entrenchment mechanism is in place. The persistent ~22% say-on-pay opposition and the long tenure of two directors keep the rating from "strong protection."

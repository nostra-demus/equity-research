## What This Module Found

Amazon is run by aligned, competent stewards who did what they said they would do. From FY2022 to FY2025, operating income grew 6.5x, AWS revenue growth re-accelerated from 13% to 28% year-over-year (by Q1 2026), and the international segment turned profitable ($4.8B operating income in FY2025). The FCF squeeze from the AI capex cycle was explicitly pre-warned in the FY2024 shareholder letter and then delivered exactly as flagged — CFO Brian Olsavsky has 11 years in the seat, CEO Andy Jassy 5 years, and neither had unexplained departures around them.

The single most important driver is the capital record on organic reinvestment: return on invested capital (ROIC, the profit earned on each dollar put into the business) ran at 18–20% in FY2024–FY2025, and incremental ROIC (the return on the newest dollars invested) peaked at 35% in FY2023 before decelerating to 15.9% in FY2025. That is above management's estimated ~10% cost of capital, but the direction is what matters — and the FY2026 ~$200B capex plan is what makes or breaks that read going forward.

The single most important risk is that this same $200B AI capex plan carries no formal floor on the return it must earn, no split between maintenance and growth capex, and no separately-disclosed AI revenue inside AWS. If incremental ROIC falls below 12% in FY2026, the capital-allocation thesis weakens, and the dormant $6.1B buyback authorization means shareholders bear the full ~1.7%/year dilution cost with no share-count reduction as a safety valve.

## What Would Change This Read

**What would strengthen it:**
- Add an explicit ROIC or total shareholder return (TSR) performance condition to the next CEO RSU grant.
- Activate the $6.1B buyback authorization in FY2027 once FCF recovers, reducing net share count.
- FY2026 incremental ROIC holds at ≥15%, confirming the AI capex thesis.
- Amazon starts disclosing AI-specific revenue inside AWS (as Microsoft does for Azure).

**What would weaken it:**
- FY2026 incremental ROIC falls below 12% — the AI capex is over-earning what it costs.
- Management moves KPIs or the guidance window after the capex cycle underperforms.
- A large debt-funded acquisition of an unrelated business while AI FCF is still negative.
- Bezos accelerates stake sales below 5% before any operating inflection.

**What data would confirm or overturn this:** The 2026 DEF 14A proxy is the single highest-value next data request — it would lift the incentive alignment cap (from 50 toward 60–65) and the shareholder friendliness cap (from 60 toward 75–80), and would move the Confidence-Adjusted Governance Score from 54 to roughly 65–70. FY2026 annual operating results (incremental ROIC on the $200B capex year) is the most important forward data point.

## Bottom Line

- **Verdict: Aligned & competent.** Governance Score 72 (Good); Confidence-Adjusted 54 (dragged by proxy absence, not by the facts).
- **Biggest reason it could be better than it looks:** The promise-versus-delivery record is unusually clean — operating income up 6.5x in three years, FCF squeeze pre-warned, charges named plainly in opening sentences of prepared remarks. This is what candid operators look like.
- **Biggest reason it could be worse than it looks:** Pay rewards time served, not results — RSUs vest on the calendar, with no ROIC minimum, no TSR hurdle, no earnings condition. If the $200B AI capex bet delivers incremental ROIC below 12%, the compensation structure provides no automatic brake.
- **What is missing:** The 2026 DEF 14A proxy (compensation, beneficial ownership, board composition, voting results, related-party transactions). Its absence is what caps three of the scores.
- **The one thing to watch next:** FY2026 incremental ROIC — the single forward number that tells you whether the $200B capex commitment is creating per-share value or destroying it.

## Plain-English Glossary

- **Operating income:** The profit from running the business, before interest and tax.
- **Free cash flow (FCF):** Cash from operations minus what the company spends on property, plant, and equipment (capex) — the recurring cash the business throws off.
- **Capex (capital expenditure):** Money spent on long-lived assets like data centers and servers.
- **ROIC (return on invested capital):** The profit the business earns on each dollar of capital put into it.
- **Incremental ROIC:** The return on the newest dollars invested — a leading signal for whether growth spending is paying off.
- **Cost of capital:** What the money used to run and grow the business actually costs to raise.
- **RSU (restricted stock unit):** A share grant that vests to the employee over time.
- **Say-on-pay:** The annual shareholder vote (advisory) on the executive pay package.
- **Net debt / EBITDA:** Total debt minus cash, divided by earnings before interest, tax, and depreciation — a common leverage yardstick.
- **10b5-1 plan:** A pre-set trading plan that lets insiders sell on a schedule set in advance, insulated from any inside knowledge they later acquire.
- **DEF 14A (proxy statement):** The annual filing that discloses executive pay, board composition, ownership, and shareholder-vote results.

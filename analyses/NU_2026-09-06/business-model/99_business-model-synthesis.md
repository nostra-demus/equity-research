## Abstract

Nu Holdings is a branchless digital bank serving 139 million consumers in Brazil, Mexico and Colombia through one mobile app. It is a lender first: 85.2% of FY2025 revenue was interest income, and Brazil supplied 91.4% of the revenue the filing splits by country. The strongest positive is a measured cost advantage — 14,314 customers per employee against incumbents' 1,234 — producing a 31.6% return on equity against the 16.51% Brazilian cost of equity the company itself discloses. The strongest negative is that the whole profitable record sits inside one benign credit cycle, with the five-year loss-inclusive average return of 12.40% sitting below that same cost of equity. No disqualifier triggered; this reads as a cyclical business needing a timing edge.

## 3. Reconciliation

Eight material disagreements or basis conflicts were found between specialists. Each is resolved below on the conservative default (CLAUDE.md §4) with the reconciled view named.

**1. Loan-to-deposit ratio — three figures for the same quarter (`06` vs `07`, flagged by `12`).**
`06_value-chain §1 and §4` used the CFO's **35%** ("lends out only 35 cents of every dollar it holds"); `07_business-quality §1` used the deck's **average LDR 58%** (Q2'26, up from 50% in Q4'25) and the vendor's gross-loans-to-deposits of **88.9%** (LTM Jun-2026, from 76.8% FY2024 and 80.3% FY2025). `12 §2` (severity 50) established why they differ: the deck's 58% has a published definition (net credit portfolio ÷ available funding less compulsory deposits, plus financial bills) and the vendor's 88.9% is gross loans ÷ total deposits on the audited balance sheet, while **the 35% appears nowhere in the deck, carries no definition and no reconciliation in any document in this pool**.
**Reconciled view carried forward:** use the two *defined* measures — **58% (managerial, company-defined)** and **88.9% (vendor, audited balance sheet)** — and state which. **Do not carry the 35% as if it were the same measure.** Both `06` and `07` were quoting the company faithfully; the defect is the company's, not either specialist's. Conservative reading: the balance sheet is being pushed harder, not held at 35%.

**2. Operating FX — "matched" versus a 26.9% dollar-linked cost base (`10` vs `12`).**
`10_external-dependency §4` listed "Operating FX matched — no significant unhedged FX exposures" as a **mitigating** driver of the 57 score, concluding "cash flows are insulated even though reported USD figures are not". `12 §2` (severity 45) showed that the Item 11 statement covers **financial-instrument** exposures only, while a separate risk factor states "**around 26.9% of our costs in the year ended December 31, 2025 were directly or indirectly linked to the U.S. dollar**, whereas the majority of our revenue was denominated in *reais*".
**Reconciled view:** the two statements are not strictly contradictory but **they cannot both be summarised as "matched"**. The conservative reading is adopted: financial-instrument exposure is matched; **the operating cost base is not**. Sized as a **zero-mitigation bound (§9), not a forecast**: 26.9% of FY2025 total expenses of US$12,032,723k ≈ US$3,236.8m of dollar-linked cost, so a 10% BRL depreciation adds roughly **US$324m** of cost in local-currency terms, about 11.3% of FY2025 net income, before any repricing or re-sourcing. The pool discloses no realised offset, so none can be computed.

**3. Five-year acquisition spend and the Filter-4 evidence base (`11` vs `12`).**
`11 §1` tested Filter 4 against the vendor "Cash Acquisitions" line: US$131.9m across FY2021–FY2025, severity 15, filter does not trip. `12 §2` (severity 42) found the **US$153.0m Tyme Group commitment** (18.0% stake, US$102.4m in associates plus call options and warrants, no voting rights, US$3,689k loss in associates in FY2025) sitting outside that line.
**Reconciled view:** the corrected five-year figure is roughly **US$285m**, about 4% of FY2025 revenue on the vendor base and 1.2% of equity. **Filter 4 still does not trip and no cap follows** — `12` said so itself. ## 4. Note To The Final Synthesizer

**MANDATORY RED-FLAG PROPAGATION — all eight new flags from `12_red-flags-sweep` score ≥40 and all eight appear below. None is omitted.**

- **Strongest business-model positive — a cost advantage measured on both sides of the spread, not asserted.** Cost to serve and G&A per active customer roughly **85% below** Brazilian incumbents' (the company's own estimate off incumbents' published FY2025 accounts); **14,314 customers per employee against an incumbent average of 1,234** (11.6×), while incumbents each run 1,685–3,955 branches and 49,661–85,206 employees; deposits bought at **88% of the local interbank rate, 3pp cheaper than a year earlier**, all US$41.9bn "acquired directly, without the use of third-party brokers or intermediaries" `[09_moat §2; FY25 Form 20-F, Item 4.B and Item 5; Q2'26 deck, p.14]`. What this MEANS: the advantage is a *cost* advantage, not pricing power — Nu can lower what it pays; it largely cannot raise what it charges.
- **Strongest business-model negative — the entire profitable record was earned in one favourable window, and three separate modules say so independently.** Credit: 30%+ ROE and a 12.4% risk-adjusted NIM (best of six quarters) earned with Brazilian unemployment falling 6.2% → 5.1%, on a book 92% unsecured `[07 §4]`. Unit economics: Q2'26 contribution of US$6.17 per active customer per month is a **peak-quarter reading**; the defensible annual figure is US$4.88 `[04 §3]`. Tax: **deferred tax credits are 30.9% of the whole US$5,874,284k profitable record**, and current tax payable *fell* 7.3% while pre-tax profit *rose* 38.4% `[12 §2, severity 58]`. What this MEANS: the reported earnings level is not a run rate, and the tax leg does not need a downturn to bite.

Inside it, **Brazil is the segment that matters**: 91.4% of geographically-attributed revenue, 90.2% of the regulatory capital in the regulated operating entities, and 80.4% of deposits. ## 5. Simple Summary

- **What it does.** A bank with no branches. 139 million people in Brazil, Mexico and Colombia get a credit card, a loan, a deposit account and some investments through one phone app.
- **How it makes money.** By lending, not by apps. **85% of FY2025 revenue was interest** — US$13,434.7m of US$15,774.8m — against 15% in fees. The free app and free transfers are how it gets the customer; credit is how it gets paid.
- **Does each new unit create value?** Yes, clearly. One active customer threw off **US$6.17 a month after every variable cost and the full cost to serve in Q2'26** — about US$74 a year — against a one-off **US$7.4** to acquire. But Q2'26 is the best quarter ever disclosed; **the honest through-cycle figure is nearer US$4.88 a month**, and the cost of serving a *new* customer's credit risk is not published at all.
- **Which segment matters most.** There is only one segment, and inside it only one country: **Brazil, 91.4% of the revenue the filing splits by geography** (no lower than 70% of total revenue). Mexico is growing fastest and is still nowhere near balancing it.
- **Does it have a moat, and against whom?** **A narrow one, and it is a cost moat.** Against Itaú, Bradesco, Santander Brasil, Banco do Brasil, Caixa, BTG, Inter & Co, C6, MercadoPago, PicPay, PagSeguro and StoneCo — all named by Nu itself. It serves **14,314 customers per employee against their 1,234**, and out-earns every listed one of them. It is narrow rather than strong because the whole profitable record is three and a half years old, all of it inside a good Brazilian credit cycle, and the **five-year average return of 12.40% is below the 16.51% cost of equity the company itself discloses**.
- **What external variables it depends on.** The Brazilian real (unhedged translation exposure worth 13.3% of equity on the company's own shock), the Brazilian central bank's rate (it pays 88% of the interbank rate for its deposits), Brazilian law (the price of 38% of revenue is capped by statute; interchange on another slice is capped at 0.7%), and the Brazilian consumer's ability to pay (the book is 92% unsecured).
- **Is capital allocated well?** Mostly yes on hygiene — no dividends but a 30% return on retained profit, near-zero acquisitions, six clean audit opinions, a share count that has just turned down, US$0.9m of related-party balances. **But the score is capped at 50 here** because borrowings jumped 151.6% in one year, and because bad loans are rising (6.7% → 8.6%) while the regulator's capital cushion is falling (16.2% → 13.4%) and the company is buying back stock. Also: one man can veto any dividend by contract, down to owning about 2.5% of the shares.
- **Does it deserve deeper work?** **Yes — but as a cyclical bet that needs timing, not as a business to own through anything.** The earnings are peak-cycle for three separate reasons (credit conditions, per-customer contribution, and non-cash tax credits worth 30.9% of the entire profitable record), and the industry it operates in has had its rules rewritten five times in six years.

## 3. Most Severe New Flag

**The reliance on non-cash deferred-tax credits.** Over FY2023–FY2025 — which is Nu's entire profitable history — the group booked **US$1,816,376k of deferred tax benefit against US$5,874,284k of cumulative reported net income, 30.9% of the whole record**, while the tax it actually owed in FY2025 *fell* 7.3% (US$1,536,521k → US$1,424,006k) as profit before tax *rose* 38.4% (US$2,795,198k → US$3,868,419k) [`FY25 Form 20-F, Note 30 (Income taxes) a), pp.F-81–F-82`]. The synthesizer should weight this because it attacks the same number two other modules have already put weight on from a different direction: `07 §4` calls the 30%+ return on equity a peak-of-favourable-conditions figure on **credit** grounds, and `04 §3` rings the Q2'26 per-customer contribution as a peak-quarter reading. This is a third, independent and arithmetically checkable reason the reported earnings level is not a run rate — and unlike the credit argument it does not need a downturn to bite. Three disclosed facts set the direction of travel: Brazilian rules cap the use of these assets at 30% of taxable profit a year; the effective rate is 25.8% against a 40% statutory rate, a US$550,621k gap whose largest single component (US$243,158k, "Other amounts") is explained in one clause; and Complementary Law No. 224/2025 "reduced certain federal tax benefits and incentives by 10% starting in January 2026", with the filing stating the company "cannot guarantee" the rest survive [`FY25 Form 20-F, Note 30 b)`; `Item 3.D — tax risk factor`]. Carry the qualifier exactly: this is fully disclosed accounting, correctly applied, and recognising deferred tax assets is what a newly profitable company does. The flag is about the **durability** of the reported profit, not about the accounting being wrong.

`RF-RFS-001 (aggressive accounting practice pattern)` — qualifying row: **internally developed software capitalisation** (severity 52), where additions grew 84.8% against 37.0% revenue growth, US$223,167k of cost stayed off the FY2025 income statement (5.8% of pre-tax profit, up from 4.2%), and 19.5% of the year's additions were written off in the same year.

## 4. Cross-Cutting Patterns

**Pattern one — cost is being moved off the income statement in three separate places at once.** Internally developed software (US$223,167k net of amortisation kept off FY2025 profit), credit-card issuance costs including printing, packing and shipping ("deferred expenses… amortized based on the card's estimated useful life methodology"), and the US$185,488k FGC levy sitting in other assets [`Q2 FY26 Interim Report (14 Aug 2026), Other assets note (ii) and (iv), p.25`; intangibles movement table as cited above]. Each one is disclosed, each one is defensible on its own, and none is large enough alone to change a verdict. The pattern is that all three run the same way — current cash out, future expense — in a year when the reported profit was also carrying a US$427,259k deferred tax credit. A reader rebuilding FY2025 profit on a cash-cost basis would land materially below US$2,871,672k.

**Pattern two — the most flattering version of a metric is repeatedly the one with the least definitional discipline.** The liquidity figure quoted on the call (35%) is the lowest of three and the only one with no published definition; the efficiency ratio was redefined in Q4'25 so that the same period reads 41.0% instead of 45.8% [`07 §1`]; the LTV/CAC ratio and the "payback in under 12 months" claim were withdrawn from the FY2025 20-F with nothing put in their place [`04 §2`]; and every headline operating metric sits on a company-defined non-IFRS managerial basis that does not tie to the audited statements [`02`, `04 §head`]. No single instance proves intent, and the audited IFRS statements are clean, unqualified and reviewed. But the direction is consistent enough that the synthesizer should treat any company-defined metric in this file as requiring its definition quoted alongside it, and should not let a managerial figure stand in for an audited one.

**Pattern three — one person is the single point of failure in both directions.** The Shareholder's Agreement gives Vélez a veto over dividends, mergers, share issuance, large asset moves and executive pay that survives down to 10% of voting power (about 2.5% of the shares in issue), while the 20-F names him personally as the key-personnel risk and no succession plan is disclosed. Entrenchment and dependence are usually opposite worries; here they are the same fact.

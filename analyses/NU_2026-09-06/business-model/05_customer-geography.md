# Customer And Geography Map — NU

**Company:** Nu Holdings Ltd. (NYSE:NU) — Cayman-incorporated, operations in Brazil, Mexico and Colombia. **Reporting standard:** IFRS as issued by the IASB. **Reporting currency:** US dollars (figures in thousands in the filing; restated to millions here). **Fiscal year:** ends 31 December. Latest annual filing = Form 20-F for FY2025, filed 8 Apr 2026; latest interim = unaudited condensed statements for the three and six months ended 30 Jun 2026, filed 14 Aug 2026.

**Two revenue bases are used below, and they are not the same — keep them apart.**
- **Total revenue, FY2025 = US$15,774.7m** — interest income and gains net of losses on financial instruments US$13,434.7m plus fee and commission income US$2,340.1m [`FY25 Form 20-F, Note 6(a) and 6(b)`]. The filing itself says interest income and gains were 85.2% and fee and commission income 14.8% of total revenue in FY2025 [`FY25 Form 20-F, Item 5 — Operating Results, "Fees and Commission Income"`].
- **Geographic-note revenue, FY2025 = US$12,083.8m** — the narrower base the geography table uses. Its own footnote defines it as interest income (credit card, loan and other receivables) plus credit and prepaid card income, late fees, insurance commission and other fee and commission income [`FY25 Form 20-F, Note 34(b), footnote (i)`]. That is **76.6%** of total revenue (12,083.8 ÷ 15,774.7). The missing 23.4% (US$3,690.9m) is interest on other assets at amortised cost US$2,284.6m, interest and gains on fair-value instruments US$1,359.5m, and other fair-value income US$46.8m [`FY25 Form 20-F, Note 6(a)`] — the securities and treasury book, which **the filing does not split by country at all.** Every geography percentage in Section 2 is a share of the US$12,083.8m base, not of total revenue.

| One customer or geography >30% with no long-term contract disclosed | **Y** | Brazil is 91.4%, far above 30%, and **no long-term customer contract is disclosed anywhere in the filing.** The relationships are cancellable at will on both sides: deposits are repayable on demand, the core Nu card is a no-annual-fee product, and the filing discloses no minimum term, no committed volume and no switching penalty. Nu's own contractual language is with suppliers, not customers — the Mastercard licences are *non-exclusive* and *"No consideration is due to Mastercard under this agreement"* [`FY25 Form 20-F, Item 10.C — Material Contracts`]. ## 4. Read

This is a **geography-concentrated business with essentially zero customer concentration** — the two sides point in opposite directions, and the filing states both plainly. On the customer side there is no single-name risk at all: no customer has reached 10% of revenue in any of the last four reported periods, and the only individually named customer in the entire pool (Circle Internet Group) comes from a tier-5 vendor export with no size attached. On the geography side Brazil is 91.4% of the revenue the filing splits by country, and the pull toward the rest is slow — Mexico moved the needle from 5.8% to 7.2% over eighteen months while growing 87% year on year, which shows how far a fast-growing second market still is from balancing the first.

The concentration is **naked, not contractually secured.** There is no long-term customer contract, no committed volume and no switching penalty disclosed; what holds 139 million customers in place is habit and pricing (86% activity rate, ~90% of Brazilian customers keeping a balance), which can move faster than a contract can. The offsetting point is that naked concentration across millions of small, unsecured, on-demand relationships is a very different animal from naked concentration in one industrial contract — no single counterparty can walk away and dent the revenue line, but a Brazilian consumer credit downturn hits all of them at once.

**The single biggest dependency the synthesizer should know: Brazil.** At 91.4% of geographically-allocated revenue (and no lower than 70.0% of total revenue on the most generous possible treatment of the unallocated treasury book), NU is a bet on one country's consumer credit cycle, one central bank's rate path, and one currency — sharpened by the filing's own disclosure that ~26.9% of FY2025 costs are US-dollar-linked while most revenue is in reais.

**Caveats carried forward.** (1) The geography table covers 76.6% of total revenue; the remaining 23.4% (US$3,690.9m of securities, compulsory-deposit and interbank income) has no country split in the filing, so any "Brazil = 91%" statement must carry that qualifier. (2) Colombia has not been disclosed as a separate revenue line since FY2023 — its contribution is not measurable from this pool. (3) The customer-by-type split of revenue (consumer versus SME) is not disclosed; only a customer count is. (4) The 15-page deep-dive memo dated 30 Aug 2026 in the pool is a prior engine output, not evidence; nothing above is taken from it.

## 1. Natural Unit

**The natural economic unit for Banking (the whole Group — Nu's single reportable segment) is one monthly active customer.**

The company itself runs the business on this unit and says so: *"we evaluate performance metrics exclusively on the customers that we define as active. Doing so allows us to track performance based on revenue (defined as Monthly ARPAC) and cost (defined as Monthly Average Cost to Serve per Active Customer)"* `[FY25 Form 20-F, Item 5 (Key Business Metrics), p.157]`. Segment-map confirms one operating and reportable segment covering the whole Group, 100% of revenue and 100% of profit, so the unit is group-level and no sub-segment unit is available `[FY25 Form 20-F, Note 34, p.F-97]`.

**Secondary unit types, named but not tabulated:** US$1 of credit portfolio (US$39.4bn at Q2'26, priced at a 22.9% net interest margin and a 12.4% risk-adjusted margin, both managerial) and US$1 of deposits (US$45.3bn at Q2'26, costing 88% of the local interbank rate) `[Q2'26 investor deck, slides 13, 14, 15]`. A per-customer read and a per-dollar-of-credit read are not independent here — the same credit book drives both.

**Denominator used throughout.** The filings give customer counts and activity rates but not the average active-customer count the ARPAC formula uses. I derive it from the company's own two disclosed numbers — managerial gross revenue ÷ months ÷ Monthly ARPAC — and it reconciles: Q2'26 5,876 ÷ 3 ÷ 17.1 = **114.5m**; Q2'25 3,772 ÷ 3 ÷ 12.5 = **100.6m**; FY2025 16,319 ÷ 12 ÷ 13.3 = **102.1m** `[Q2'26 investor deck, slides 21 and 28; FY25 Form 20-F, p.159]`. Independent check: FY2025 year-end actives 131.0m × 83.4% = 109.3m and FY2024 year-end 114.2m × 83.0% = 94.8m average to 102.0m — the same answer `[FY25 Form 20-F, Key Business Metrics table, p.159]`. Second check: cost to serve rebuilt from IFRS lines on this denominator gives (127,816 + 226,246) ÷ 3 ÷ 114.5 = US$1.03 for Q2'26, against the deck's US$1.0 `[Q2 FY26 Interim Report, Consolidated Statements of Income, p.6; Q2'26 investor deck, slide 28]`. *This denominator is my arithmetic on filed and deck figures — inference, not a disclosed number.*

**Full per-unit monthly P&L, Q2'26 [IFRS], denominator 114.5m average active customers** — my arithmetic on `Q2 FY26 Interim Report (14 Aug 2026), Consolidated Statements of Income, p.6`. Components sum to the total:

Revenue 16.05 − funding cost 4.53 − transactional 0.37 − expected credit loss 4.32 = **gross profit 6.83** − customer support and operations 0.66 = **contribution after cost to serve 6.17** − general and administrative 1.75 − marketing 0.30 − other net 0.51 − associates 0.01 = pre-tax 3.60 − tax 0.51 = **net income US$3.09 per active customer per month (US$37 per year)**.

**Adjudicating the cost-to-serve contradiction, by name.** Two of the company's own numbers point in opposite directions. On the **annual** basis cost to serve *fell*, US$0.9 (FY2024) → US$0.8 (FY2025) `[FY25 Form 20-F, p.159]`. On the **quarterly** basis it *rose*, US$0.8 (Q2'25) → US$1.0 (Q2'26), +14% FX-neutral `[Q2'26 investor deck, slide 28]`. Both are right on their own basis; the quarterly series is the more recent and the more granular, and it is confirmed by the IFRS lines underneath it — customer support and operations rose 40.1% year on year in Q2'26 (226,246 vs 161,434) against a 13.8% rise in average active customers `[Q2 FY26 Interim Report, p.6]`. **The current direction of cost to serve is deteriorating.** It has not yet mattered to the read: cost to serve is US$1.0 of a US$16.05 revenue per unit, and contribution per unit still rose 34%.

**Adjudicating the margin-ratio contradiction, by name.** The contribution *dollars* per unit are rising while the contribution *ratio* is below where it was: 45.61% in FY2024, 42.00% in FY2025, 40.18% in H1'26. The Q2'26 standalone ratio of 42.56% is above Q2'25's 42.20% but three percentage points below FY2024. The ratio does not overturn the dollar read — ARPAC grew faster than the margin fell, so each unit still throws off more cash — but it does mean the improvement is coming from selling more per customer, not from a wider spread on what is sold.

## 3. Value Creation Read

**Verdict: Creates value — clearly, and by a wide margin on the disclosed numbers — but at Q2'26 returns that are the highest in the disclosed series, so the level should not be treated as structural.** One active customer produced US$6.17 of contribution per month after every variable cost and the full cost to serve in Q2'26, roughly US$74 a year, against a stated cost to acquire of US$7.4 once `[Q2 FY26 Interim Report, p.6; FY25 Form 20-F, p.157]`. Even on the blended base that is about a 1.5-month payback, and net churn of 0.1% a month means the customer is still there for years afterwards `[FY25 Form 20-F, p.25]`. The honest version is longer, because a *new* customer is not an average one: the cohort curve starts at US$1.3 of monthly revenue and does not reach the group average until roughly month 48 `[Q2'26 investor deck, slide 28]`. Applying the group contribution ratio to that curve — *inference, not from filings, and the direction of the error is known* — a first-year cohort averages about US$4.6 of monthly revenue, roughly US$1.1 of monthly contribution, and repays US$7.4 somewhere around months seven to nine. That squares with what the company itself used to publish and no longer does: *"cumulative contribution margins in less than 12 months on average"* and *"LTV/CAC … greater than 30x"* `[FY24 Form 20-F]`. The inference understates the payback, because new cohorts sit at the front of the "low and grow" credit curve where loss rates are higher than the blend `[FY25 Form 20-F, "Low Cost of Risk", p.157]`.

**Ring-fencing the level.** Q2'26 is the best quarter Nu has disclosed. Risk-adjusted net interest margin — the spread left after credit losses — was **12.42%, the highest of the six quarters shown, in a range of 9.30% to 12.42% with a six-quarter average of 10.40%**, and it was 9.48% only one quarter earlier `[Q2'26 investor deck, slides 15 and 16]`. Return on equity of 33% was described by the CFO as a record `[Q2 FY26 transcript, CFO prepared remarks]`. The contribution ratio peaked in FY2024, not now. And the profitable record is three years long — FY2022 was a net loss of US$364.6m `[Capital IQ Financials → Income Statement export (NU) — vendor export]`. So the defensible through-cycle per-unit figure is nearer the **FY2025 annual contribution of US$4.88 per active customer per month** than the Q2'26 figure of US$6.17; state the Q2'26 number as a peak-quarter reading, not a constant.

**The single most valuable missing disclosure: cohort-level contribution margin or cost of credit.** Revenue by cohort is published; cost by cohort is not, anywhere. Without it, the payback on a genuinely new customer cannot be computed from disclosure — it can only be inferred by borrowing the mature book's margin, which is the one assumption most likely to be wrong in the optimistic direction. The company's withdrawal of the LTV/CAC ratio and the "under 12 months" payback statement from the FY2025 20-F, with no replacement figure, made this gap worse rather than better.

## 4. Sensitivity

**Revenue per unit is the biggest arithmetic lever; cost of credit is the input that has actually moved 20% in a quarter.** Move Monthly ARPAC 20% and contribution moves nearly one-for-one, because the cost side is largely fixed in the short run: US$16.05 of IFRS revenue per unit × 20% = US$3.21 a month, which would take contribution after cost to serve from US$6.17 to either US$2.96 or US$9.38 — a 52% swing either way, and at the low end the payback on a new cohort would roughly double. Move expected credit loss 20% and the effect is a third of that: US$4.32 × 20% = US$0.86 a month, taking contribution to US$5.31 or US$7.03. Move CAC 20% and almost nothing happens — US$1.48 once, against roughly US$74 of annual contribution. **On history, though, the volatile one is credit, not price.** Monthly ARPAC has risen in every one of the six disclosed quarters, US$11.6 → US$17.1, and has never fallen `[Q2'26 investor deck, slide 28]`. Cost of credit moved **+33% FX-neutral in one quarter (Q4'25 US$1,313m → Q1'26 US$1,794m) and then −9% the next (Q2'26 US$1,691m)**, which is what dragged risk-adjusted margin from 10.49% to 9.48% and back to 12.42% inside six months `[Q2'26 investor deck, slides 15 and 16]`. The live evidence that this input is not settled: 90-plus-day delinquency rose 35 basis points in Q2'26 to 6.9%, the credit book grew 37% year on year FX-neutral so a large share of it is unseasoned, and the CFO attributes 24 basis points of the early-delinquency bridge to *"intentional risk expansions"* — deliberate lending to cohorts with higher expected losses `[Q2'26 investor deck, slides 13 and 17; Q2 FY26 transcript, CFO prepared remarks]`. A 20% worsening in credit cost sustained for a year would not break the unit — contribution stays positive at US$5.31 a month against a US$7.4 acquisition cost — but it would remove about a seventh of the per-unit cash and push the risk-adjusted margin back below its six-quarter average.

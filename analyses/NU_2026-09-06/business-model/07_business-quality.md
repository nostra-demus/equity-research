## 4. Read

This is **not a durable compounder on the evidence available; it is a high-quality operator inside a cyclical, regulator-shaped industry** — a single-country, single-segment consumer lender whose book is 92% unsecured, whose flagship price is capped by law, and whose funding cost is a fixed share of whatever the Brazilian central bank sets. What is genuinely good about it is the customer franchise: 0.1% monthly churn, an 83.5% activity rate, and cohort revenue that keeps climbing to month 90. What is genuinely fragile is everything that stands between the 22.9% headline NIM and the shareholder: 10.5 percentage points of it already disappear into credit cost, and that credit cost is set by an economy management does not control.

**The headline returns are being earned near a favourable point in the cycle, and must be anchored as such.** Return on equity of 33% in Q2'26 — which the CFO called a record `[Q2 2026 Earnings Call (13 Aug 2026), CFO prepared remarks]` — and a risk-adjusted NIM of 12.4%, the highest in the six-quarter series, were produced with Brazilian unemployment at 5.1%, improved from 6.2% a year earlier `[FY25 Form 20-F, Item 5 macroeconomic indicators table]`, and with the managerial loan-to-deposit ratio pushed from 50% to 58% in three quarters `[Q2'26 deck, p.16]`. **A through-cycle level cannot be computed from this pool**: the entire profitable record runs FY2023 to H1'26, and contains no Brazilian consumer downturn at anything like the current book size — the FY2021 and FY2022 losses (ROE −6.8% and −7.8% `[CIQ Ratios export — vendor export]`) were a scaling business, not a credit cycle. So the high return and the low cyclicality score are not in conflict; they are the same fact seen twice. A 22.9% NIM on an unsecured book **is** the compensation for loss risk, and the company's own worst disclosed scenario — a 100% downside macro weighting, +US$381.5m of ECL, 13.3% of FY2025 net income `[FY25 Form 20-F, Note 4]` — is a weighting shift, not a recession, so it should not be read as the floor. Downstream moat and valuation work should treat 30%+ ROE as a peak-of-favourable-conditions number, not a normalised one.

**The one factor a buyer should keep their eye on over the next 24 months: risk-adjusted NIM, read alongside the 90+ NPL ratio and the Tier 1 ratio in the same quarter.** These three have been moving in a way that only holds together while conditions stay benign — risk-adjusted NIM 9.5% → 12.4% in two quarters, 90+ NPL 6.9% and rising 35bp in the quarter, Tier 1 16.2% → 14.4% → 13.4% over eighteen months `[Q2'26 deck, pp.15–17; CIQ Ratios export — vendor export]`. The falsification test is dated and cheap: the Q3 2026 results, due November 2026. Coverage is the one series that has *not* deteriorated (244% of 90+ NPL, allowance build 113% of new formation `[Q2'26 deck, p.19]`), and that is the fact to watch break first.

Because the industry rate-of-change row scored **38 (Weak, ≤40)**, this thesis is closer to a **sector / technology-cycle bet on Brazilian digital financial services than to a durable-compounder holding** (`CLAUDE.md` §24, Filter 5): the rails (Pix), the data rules (open finance), the price ceilings, the capital regime and the asset class (virtual assets) have each been rewritten inside six years, and Nu's own rise from nothing to 118m Brazilian customers is the proof that this competitive set reorders faster than a compounder thesis assumes. Per `MODULE_RULES.md` this caps the business-quality aggregate at 65 (not binding at 46), flags the thesis type to the synthesis, and discounts moat durability.

## 2. Classification

**Owner-operator discipline** — with two named structural caveats that stop this being a clean sweep.

What supports the classification: acquisitions are near-zero and there is no serial-acquirer pattern; the share count has just turned **down** (−0.51% in H1 2026) after years of low-single-digit creep; related-party dealings are US$0.9m against US$6,991m of revenue; the auditor has been KPMG since 2018 with six straight unqualified opinions and only 7.2% non-audit fees; there is no restatement; contingent legal exposure is under US$40m; and the founder's 74.4% voting stake is stable, so his economics move with the minority's.

The two caveats, stated plainly rather than averaged away: (1) Nu is a **"controlled company"** under NYSE rules and so may opt out of the requirements for a majority-independent board and a fully independent compensation committee — in practice it has not needed to (8 of 10 directors are external independents, and the audit, risk and compensation committees are entirely independent), but the *option* to opt out belongs to one man and cannot be withdrawn by minority holders [`FY25 Form 20-F, Item 6.C — Controlled Company Exception`; `CIQ Board Members and Committees exports, as of Aug-2026 — vendor export`]. (2) Because Cayman law does not require it, **no individual executive's pay is disclosed** — only the US$91.3m aggregate — so pay-for-performance cannot be tested at the person level [`FY25 Form 20-F, Item 6.B — "Under Cayman Islands law, we are not required to disclose compensation paid to our management team on an individual basis"`]. That is a real disclosure gap under §11; it is a legal-regime gap, not a language one, and it is not treated as opacity in the sense §27 forbids.

## 3. Most Material Signal

The signal to watch is **asset quality set against the capital cushion the buyback is drawing down** — the working-capital-equivalent row (severity 35) read together with the debt/capital row (30). Non-performing loans have climbed every year, 6.7% → 7.8% → 7.9% → 8.6% of loans, while the Tier 1 ratio has fallen 16.2% → 14.4% → 13.4%, and the company began returning capital (US$500.4m spent, US$499.6m still authorised) in the middle of that trend. Today none of that is alarming: return on equity is 30.3%, equity grew from US$7.6bn to US$13.3bn in eighteen months, the allowance stands at US$6,612.9m, and the buyback is a quarter of one half-year's profit. But the arithmetic of the combination is what matters — if the credit cycle turns in Brazil and the non-performing ratio pushes materially past 8.6% while Tier 1 keeps sliding, the same repurchase stops being surplus cash returned and becomes capital taken out of a thinning cushion, and the off-balance-sheet US$28.8bn of unused card limits (up 63% in one year) is precisely the exposure that draws down hardest when customers get stressed. That would move the classification from *owner-operator discipline* to *capital allocation concerns* without any single new red flag appearing. The falsification test is simple and dated: the Q3 2026 results, due November 2026, showing whether the 90-day non-performing ratio and the Tier 1 ratio move together or apart while repurchases continue.

### Evidence notes and limits

- **Duplication was not counted as corroboration.** The pool mirrors `Filings/` as `Filings 2/`, and several Capital IQ workbooks have "(1)" twins; every figure above is taken once, from the named source.
- **The 15-page deep-dive memo dated 30 Aug 2026 in the pool is a prior engine output**, a §4 tier-9 user note. It was not read as evidence and none of the conclusions above are drawn from it.
- **Vendor figures are labelled as vendor figures.** Where the audited 20-F carries its own number for the same item (shares outstanding, related-party balances, provisions, the buyback, unused limits), the filing's number is used and cited to the filing. Capital IQ numbers appear only where the filing does not present the series in that form (multi-year cash-flow lines, ratio history, Tier 1 ratio, insider-trade aggregation), and are cited as vendor exports.
- **`net_debt_m` −9,274.2 in `ciq_facts.json` is the CIQ vendor basis** (it nets short-term and liquid investments, a *broad* basis, not the strict total-debt-minus-cash figure) and is deliberately not used above as a headline; the debt row quotes gross debt and equity from their own sources instead (§15).
- **No EBITDA exists** on the bank template, so no leverage-to-EBITDA, interest-coverage or EV/EBITDA figure is offered.
- **Not proven from available data:** the purchase price of Banco Porto Real; individual executive compensation; any board-level statement of a capital-return framework beyond the twelve-month repurchase authorisation.

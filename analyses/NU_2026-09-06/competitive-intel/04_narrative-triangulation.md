**Zero contradictions, and the reason is the absence of the second side.** Every candidate on the §1 list failed at the first step: there is no peer figure to place against the subject's figure, so no gap can be measured and no incompatibility can be proven with numbers. Under the module's hard rule — *"if it is not in a document in the pool, it does not exist for this module"* — no contradiction is asserted, inferred, or borrowed from a vendor comps row.

**The distinction that must survive the trip upstream (§3).** "No contradiction was found" and "the subject's narrative was tested and survived" are different findings, and only the first is true here. Whoever restates this section carries that qualifier or does not restate it.

### 2a. One structural finding that does not need a peer, and should travel

NU's demand claim is **scoped to its own book, not to the market**. Both formulations are explicitly bounded — *"in our portfolio"* (CFO) and *"in our numbers"* (CEO) — and the CFO's sentence carries its own hedge, *"yet we remain vigilant as always"* `[NU Q2 FY26 call, 13 Aug 2026]`.

That scoping is analytically important in both directions, and neither direction is a candor finding:

- **It is the more defensible way to say it.** A claim about one's own portfolio is a claim management can actually observe. It is not the over-reach of asserting that Brazilian consumer credit as a whole is fine.
- **It also makes the claim nearly uncontradictable by a peer (G3).** A future Itaú or Bradesco statement that market-level consumer credit weakened would sit *beside* NU's claim, not against it — a company with 7% of the profit pool can genuinely see nothing in its own book while the other 93% deteriorates. Scope-matching would fail, and the honest label on such a row would be **Untestable**, not **Contradict**. A later run must not manufacture a contradiction by comparing a market-level peer claim to a portfolio-level subject claim.

What *would* be a genuine cross-check on the same scope is a peer's own **portfolio-level** delinquency and cost-of-risk path over the same Apr–Jun 2026 window — that is a like-for-like unit and is exactly what the missing calls would supply.

### 2b. The market-share cross-check, pre-armed (the "everyone claims share gains" test)

The test is **not runnable this run**: it requires the subject and ≥1 peer to both make a share claim, and there are **zero peer claims**. NU makes its side — *"we continue to see the conditions to continue growing and taking share"*, against *"7% market share of that profit pool"* `[NU Q2 FY26 call, Q&A]` — but there is no second claim to set beside it.

Because this is the module's classic trap, the guard is stated now, with NU's own numbers, so a future run does not fall into it:

- **The sampled peer set will not be exhaustive, and cannot be made exhaustive here.** NU's own figure puts it at **7%** of a ~US$100bn Brazil consumer + SME gross profit pool `[NU Q2 FY26 call, Q&A — management estimate, not a filed number]`. On management's own arithmetic that leaves **~93% of the pool with everyone else**, spread across at least thirteen named competitors of which **four will never file a call at all** — Caixa Econômica Federal (state-owned, unlisted), Banco C6 (unlisted), PicPay (unlisted) and Mercado Pago (a MercadoLibre subsidiary with no standalone call) `[01 §Peer Set; 00 §1a(ii)]`.
- **Therefore an exhaustive share bridge is not constructible for NU, in this run or a future one.** Even a complete set of the nine listed peers' calls would leave the unlisted and state-owned share unmeasured, so route **(a)** of the step-4 test — subject plus gain-claimants accounting for substantially all of the market — is permanently unavailable for this subject.
- **Route (b) — gains that numerically exceed the share all other players could have ceded — remains the only viable proof**, and it needs same-segment, same-scope, same-window share figures from each claimant, which no source in this pool provides.
- **So the standing instruction for the next run is:** if NU and one or more Brazilian peers all claim share gains in the same window, record it as ***"jointly possible — unsampled competitors may be ceding share"***, **not** as a contradiction, and do **not** route it to the candor read or the §8 register. Several banks gaining from Caixa, Banco do Brasil, C6, PicPay and the unlisted long tail is arithmetically ordinary, not evidence of anyone misspeaking. Penalising the thesis for a contradiction that has not been shown is the §3 error in reverse.

**One further trap, already sprung once upstream and re-armed here (G5).** The pool contains an analyst on **NU's own call** benchmarking NU's >US$1bn quarterly net income against *"Itau Unibanco… around $1.1 billion"* — Yuri Rocha Fernandes, JPMorgan `[NU Q2 FY26 call, 13 Aug 2026, Q&A]`, and a second analyst employed by Itaú Corretora asking questions about NU. Both are **analyst framing, context only, never evidence**, and neither is Itaú's management speaking about Itaú. Neither may be converted into a peer share, size, or profitability claim in this or any later run. `01` and `02` both stripped these; the strip is carried, not quietly dropped.

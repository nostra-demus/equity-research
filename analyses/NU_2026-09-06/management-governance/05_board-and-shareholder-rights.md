**Counterparty facts (the disclosure that is missing).** The related counterparty is **not a financial entity** and is **not named**. Two of Nu's nine directors are sitting CEOs — **David Marcus (Lightspark)** and **Jacqueline Reses (Lead Bank)** — and `07` concludes Marcus is the near-certain counterparty. **That identification is an inference, not from filings.** It matters for one reason and one only: if it is Marcus, the counterparty is the same director whose independence designation the company dropped in the FY25 20-F while leaving him on the committee that sets executive pay.

**The minority-protection read.** The gate exists and is the right one on paper: since **March 2024** a written related-party policy requires certain RPTs to be approved by the board **or a designated committee, including the Audit and Risk Committee**, and "reviewing related-party transactions" is an express duty in that committee's charter — a committee that is **100% independent** and chaired by a genuine financial expert. What does **not** exist is any **majority-of-minority** vote, any **independent-directors-only** vote requirement, or any disclosure of **who approved this particular arrangement, when, or with what dissent**. There is also a structural oddity worth naming: the Shareholder's Agreement gives Vélez a veto over the company "entering into transactions with our officers, directors or other affiliates" — with the words "**(excluding our founding shareholder)**" in the text. He cannot veto his own related-party deals through that clause; his own would run through the board / audit-committee policy instead.

**Cross-reference.** `business-model/01_disqualifier-scan` computed related-party sales ÷ revenue ≈ 0.0% and related-party purchases/expenses ÷ total expenses = 0.76%, against the >25% hard-disqualifier threshold — **not tripped, by more than an order of magnitude twice over**. `RF-RPT-001/002/003 considered and NOT fired`: US$265k at the latest balance-sheet date is below every materiality band in the registry, and no loan, advance or guarantee to a related party is disclosed. **Is economic value leaking to the controller? On the disclosed evidence, no — Nu is one of the cleanest RPT files this module will see.** The leakage risk here is not through transactions. It is through control rights, which is §4.

## 4. Takeover Defenses & Voting Rights

| Feature | Present? | Detail | Source |

| Poison pill | **No active pill** — but the board **cannot adopt one** without the founder | No active poison pill reported. ## 5. Minority-Shareholder Protection Read

The board itself is better than it needed to be — 7 of 9 independent when the controlled-company exemption required none, a **100%-independent audit committee chaired by a real financial expert**, a genuinely trivial related-party file (US$265 thousand at 30 June 2026), no pledging, no leakage, and a CFO succession executed the way it should be — so this is not a story about a captured or lazy board, and the honest verdict on stewardship conduct is that nothing here has been abused. But a board's quality is capped by its authority, and this one has almost none that the founder has not chosen to leave it: **20:1 voting with a sunset that will effectively never trigger, a contractual right for the founder to designate five of nine directors and remove them by notice, a standing veto over dividends, share issues, mergers, officer pay and any rights plan, no shareholder right to nominate a director or put a single proposal to a meeting, no right to requisition a meeting at all once his stake falls below half, a staggered board that arms itself at that exact moment with his nominees given the longest terms, and Class B-only anti-dilution rights so every future share issue dilutes the minority and not him.** Two specific things sharpen it from structure into conduct: the company **quietly dropped David Marcus's independence designation between the FY24 and FY25 20-F while leaving him on the committee that sets executive pay, and explained nothing**; and an independent director elevated to audit-committee observer in December 2023 (Daniel Goldberg) simply disappeared from the board with no reason ever given.

**Conclusion: weak / entrenched.** Not because value is being taken — on the disclosed evidence it is not — but because nothing in the architecture would stop it, and the one number that hints at how minority holders actually feel (roughly **15–18% of non-founder votes cast against the board slate**, derived, versus a headline 96.23%) is the only vote they get to cast, on a slate they cannot amend, for a board five of whose seats are not theirs to fill.

*On §24 Filter 6 / RF-OWN-004:* it does **not** apply here. Vélez is a founder-controller whose own economics are per-share, not a government, not a value-maximising parent of a listed subsidiary, and not an unrelated conglomerate — so the mandatory "no better than weak/entrenched" lock is not engaged by that route. **`04_ownership-and-insider-behavior` has not run and is not available to this agent** (same layer); if it reaches a different read on control alignment, the synthesis reconciles it. I reach "weak / entrenched" on the rights evidence above, independently.

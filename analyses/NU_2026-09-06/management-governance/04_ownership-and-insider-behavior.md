# Ownership & Insider Behavior — NU

**Company:** Nu Holdings Ltd. (NYSE:NU) — Class A ordinary shares, USD, NYSE primary listing. Incorporated in the Cayman Islands; SEC foreign private issuer filing on Form 20-F; operations principally in Brazil, Mexico and Colombia. Reporting standard IFRS, fiscal year ends 31 December.

**Jurisdiction note (CLAUDE.md §27, MODULE_RULES Jurisdiction-Aware Source Mapping).** There is no "promoter" concept, no SEBI shareholding-pattern filing and no SAST pledge-disclosure regime for a Cayman-incorporated NYSE issuer. The local equivalents used throughout are: the **Form 20-F Item 7.A beneficial-ownership table** (ownership), the **Capital IQ Public Ownership → Insider Trading export of Form 4 records** (insider transactions), **Schedules 13G** cited inside the 20-F (5% holders), and the **Memorandum and Articles of Association / Shareholder's Agreement** as summarised in Item 10.B (control structure). Every A3 and A15 band below is applied at its stated US/Cayman equivalent, and where a band names an Indian statute the regime substitution is stated on the row.

**Data sufficiency for this agent.** Full — the pool carries five consecutive 20-Fs (FY2021–FY2025), a complete beneficial-ownership table, a mechanically-parsed insider-transaction export that reconciles exactly to `ciq_facts.json`, and the full Articles/Shareholder's Agreement summary. **No partial-data cap applies.** Web sources were not available in this runtime (all network hosts denied) and were not needed: no ownership or insider fact below is web-sourced, and nothing below is memory-sourced.

**Distinguishing the three kinds of transaction, as required.**
1. **Open-market purchases: none.** Zero, by anyone, in the entire observed record — including through the 52-week low of US$11.20 and the 15-May-2026 print at US$12.24, at which the Lead Independent Director was a seller rather than a buyer `[CIQ Equity Listings export (NU), 52-week low US$11.20, as of 2026-08-28 — vendor export]`.

**The "sale to issuer" cluster — read carefully, because it is not a conviction sell.** Five insiders sold to the company on 23 Apr 2026 at a single uniform US$14.44 and three sold on 23 Jul 2026 at a single uniform US$13.39. Corroborating arithmetic: Vélez's Class A balance rose from 2,724,260 (27-Apr) to 3,105,007 immediately before the 23-Jul sale — he acquired ~380,747 Class A in between, through vesting the export's filter hides. **Labelled inference, not a filing fact** — the Form 4 transaction code is not in the export. Treated as neutral, not as selling.

**Trade timing (feeding A15-04 and RF-MKT-001).** Every disclosed sale falls **after** an earnings release, never before one: 23-Mar-2026 (Q4'25 released 25-Feb-2026), 15-May-2026 (Q1'26 released 14-May-2026), 14-Aug-2026 (Q2'26 released 13-Aug-2026), and the two sale-to-issuer dates mid-quarter. **No insider sale in the record precedes a results announcement. RF-MKT-001 does not fire** — the pattern is the opposite of the one it tests. One residual worth naming rather than burying: the two largest open-market sales (US$4.3m combined, Junqueira and Fragelli) were executed the day after a strong Q2'26 print (net income US$1,060.2m vs US$636.8m a year earlier `[CIQ Key Developments export, Announcements of Earnings, 2026-08-13 — vendor export]`) at US$15.80–15.81, and the stock closed at **US$14.30 on 28 Aug 2026**, ~9.5% lower two weeks later `[CIQ Equity Listings export, NYSE:NU close 2026-08-28 — vendor export]`. That is legal, in-window, well-timed selling. It is not a red flag; it is also not a vote of confidence.

**Two honest qualifications, because a zero from a permissive regime is not the same as a certified zero.**
1. **The company's own policy permits pledging.** "While pledging Company securities is allowed, Nubankers should be aware of the associated risks… securities held in a margin account may be sold by a broker without the customer's consent if the customer fails to meet a margin call… Notwithstanding the above, Company securities may not be pledged during the 15-day period preceding the release of quarterly or annual earnings" `[FY25 Form 20-F, Exhibit 11.1 — Insider Trading Policy, "Pledging or margin accounts for Nubankers"]`. A best-practice policy prohibits pledging by executive officers and directors outright. This one allows it and only blacks out 15 days a year.
2. **There is no affirmative pledge-disclosure duty here.** Unlike SEBI's SAST Reg 31 regime, a Cayman/NYSE foreign private issuer has no standing obligation to report insider encumbrance quarter by quarter. So the correct statement is **"no pledge is disclosed anywhere in the filed record"**, not "it is proven that nothing is pledged". Confidence on the zero is 4, not 5. Follow-up recorded.

**A3-03 — control-versus-cash-flow wedge (Red on the number, benign on the mechanism).** Vélez holds **18.6% of the economics and 74.4% of the votes: a wedge of 55.8 percentage points**, far past the registry's >20pp red band. But the mechanism matters for what the reader should do with it. There is **no pyramid**: one wholly-owned, named holding vehicle (Rua California Ltd., 100% his), one spousal holding of 1,500,000 Class B, no cross-holding, no circularity, no promoter investment company sitting between a family and the listed entity. **The entire wedge comes from the disclosed 20:1 dual-class structure**, which is A3-04's subject. Both rows are marked Red because both bands are genuinely tripped, and the cross-reference is stated on each so the synthesis prices the fact **once**, not twice.

**Unaligned-owner test (CLAUDE.md §24, Filter 6) — worked, and negative.** Filter 6 asks whether the controlling owner's objective is long-term per-share value creation, and names three structures that are inherent conflicts:

- **(a) Government control — no.** "State Owned Shares" total **1,477,013 shares, 0.03%** of shares outstanding `[CIQ Public Ownership → Summary export — vendor export]`. No golden share, no state director, no directed-lending or social-policy mandate. The Executive Vice-Chairman is a former central-bank governor, which is a personal-history and conflict question for `07`, not state ownership.
- **(b) Listed subsidiary of a value-maximising parent — no.** Nu Holdings Ltd. **is** the top holding company; Rua California Ltd. is a personal holding vehicle with no operations that could receive steered economics, and the controller's only material asset is this stock `[FY25 Form 20-F, Item 7.A fn.(2)/(4); Item 4.C Organizational Structure]`.
- **(c) Sprawling unrelated conglomerate — no.** One reported segment: **Banking, US$6,991m = 100% of revenue** `[ciq_facts.json — segments_revenue, source CIQ Financials → Segments, FY2025]`. Geographically Brazil 91% / Mexico 7% / other 2%. **So the conclusion is the opposite of a value trap, and should be stated plainly: the controller's US$13.0bn stake means his own wealth moves one-for-one with the Class A price, and he is not paid by anyone else.** The reason to buy is the same reason he holds. **No shareholder-friendliness cap, no governance-risk floor and no value-trap note flow from Filter 6, and none should be applied downstream.**

## 5. Ownership / Insider Read

**Skin in the game is real and it was built, not granted.** Insiders own **21.99% of the company** — David Vélez alone holds 906,212,615 shares worth about **US$13.0bn** at the 28-Aug-2026 close of US$14.30, and his Class B stock is founder equity from a business he started in 2013 that **cannot be issued under either compensation plan**; his stake has not moved in five annual filings (88.6% → 88.3% of Class B). Against that, the incremental behaviour is one-directional: in the entire observed transaction record there were **zero open-market purchases by anyone** and four open-market sales of 592,707 shares for about **US$9.0m**, and the eye-catching "+4,015,012 net" figure is RSU grants and a US$0.29-strike option exercise, not accumulation — quote it as buying and you have inverted the signal. Nobody bought at the US$11.20 low; the Lead Independent Director sold 11.9% of her holding at US$12.24.

**The insider signal is therefore mild net selling with clean conduct.** Every sale landed after a results release, never before one; the quarterly uniform-price sales to the issuer look like tax withholding on vesting and are treated as neutral; there is **no pledge disclosed by any insider (0% of holding, 0% of capital)**, so RF-OWN-001, RF-OWN-002 and RF-OWN-003 all stay unfired, consistent with the disqualifier scan's independent 0% computation. The two things to keep watching are that the company's own policy **permits** insiders to pledge (banning it only 15 days before earnings, where best practice bans it outright), and that the largest sales, US$4.3m, went off one day after a strong quarter at US$15.80 with the stock 9.5% lower two weeks later.

**The biggest risk to a minority holder is not misalignment — it is powerlessness.** Filter 6 is tested and clean: no government, no parent creaming value off at another listing, one segment and 91% of revenue in one country, and a controller whose entire fortune is this share price — so this is **not** a value trap and must not be discounted as one. But the price of that alignment is a 20:1 dual-class with a sunset that requires Class B voting power to fall below 10% when it is 84.2% today, controlled-company exemptions actually taken (no stand-alone nominating committee, no requirement for a fully independent compensation committee), a founder veto over dividends, share issuance, M&A, affiliate transactions and executive pay that survives until he falls below **10%** of the vote, and — the provision that deserves the most attention — a board that **staggers itself into three classes and a shareholder body that loses the right to requisition a meeting at the precise moment the founder drops below 50%**. Class A holders own 22% of the economics alongside insiders and about 15.6% of the vote in total. On today's evidence the controller has used none of this against them; there is simply no mechanism by which they could stop him if that changed.

## 3B. Lineage & Phoenix Read (A17-02, A17-03)

(c) *The company's own history pages:* the corporate About URL returned HTTP 403, so the recipe was completed on the company's own IR governance pages and newsroom releases, which say only "founded in 2013 / operations in Brazil 2014, Mexico 2019, Colombia 2020" — **no lineage language of any kind**. (d) *The gap itself:* the three-year difference resolves to **Nu Pagamentos S.A. – Instituição de Pagamento, CNPJ 18.236.120/0001-58, opened 4 June 2013** — a **disclosed, wholly-owned, active, consolidated subsidiary**, named in Item 4 and in the corporate-structure tree, and the registered proprietor of the NUBANK and NU BANK marks. **2. The phoenix test's first question therefore returns a hard negative rather than an unknown.

**3. Did assets, brands, staff, address, products or customers continue out of a failed entity?** **No — nothing continued out of anything, because nothing failed.** The brands did not migrate: Nu Pagamentos has owned NUBANK and NU BANK since 2014 and still owns them today, and has litigated as **plaintiff** to protect them (`Nu Pagamentos S.A. v. **4. ## 7. People & Network Integrity Read

Twenty-five named people and forty-three entities were swept, and the headline is that **the people who actually control this company are clean**: David Vélez — founder, chairman, chief executive and holder of 74.4% of the votes — his co-founder Cristina Junqueira, and the incoming CFO Rob Livingston returned nothing on any criminal, civil, securities-regulator, sanctions, disqualification, insolvency, credential or adverse-media axis, no undisclosed entity, and no cross-linkage; the controller's holding vehicle is named, quantified, 13G-reported and unpledged. The lineage question that triage correctly refused to pre-judge **closed clean on evidence**: the three-year gap between the 2013 founding claim and the 2016 Cayman incorporation resolves to Nu Pagamentos S.A. (CNPJ 18.236.120/0001-58, opened 4 June 2013), a disclosed, active, consolidated subsidiary that also owns the NUBANK marks — so there is no predecessor, no phoenix, no undisclosed former name, and no brand sitting outside the group.

**The riskiest node is not a person's own record — it is a linkage, and it sits on the wrong seat.** Rogério Calderón, chair of Nu's Audit and Risk Committee and its designated audit-committee financial expert, sat on the board and the audit and finance committees of Grupo Casas Bahia S.A., which filed for *recuperação judicial* over R$17.3 billion on about 16 August 2026 — three weeks before this report — and left that board in the same days. **Nothing adverse was found against him personally**; he joined that board in September 2019, the month the anonymous complaints that exposed its R$1.19bn accounting fraud arrived, so he was appointed into a clean-up rather than present for the failure, and his exit came *after* the filing, not ahead of it, which is why the "smart rat" test does not fire. But the proceeding is admitted, live and unresolved, so it is a Material-equivalent fact at a hop-1 entity: it floors his grade at Minor concerns, fires **RF-NET-003 band (b)**, and caps People & network integrity at 50. The second-order items are a KMP's own record — Campos Neto's Pandora-Papers-era offshore vehicles held while he ran Brazil's central bank, an unfavourable ethics-commission report terminated judicially rather than decided, and a six-month walk from regulator to regulated firm that the Brazilian government is now legislating to slow — and Douglas Leone's partnership at Sequoia Capital, a defendant in the pending, unadjudicated FTX investor class actions.

The discovery loop reached **hop 2** and stopped on `breadth_budget`, with sixty-five subjects named in the Scope-Boundary Register rather than dropped; it recovered one thing the filings' own governance section no longer shows — **Adam Edward Wible, Nu's third named co-founder** — and it identified the unnamed related-party counterparty as **Lightspark Group, Inc.**, David Marcus's company, from the matching Q2-2024 partnership date. **No §24 Filter 1 integrity signal is open:** `01_disqualifier-scan` routed nothing, an independent short-seller and forum sweep found nothing, and the one piece of partisan buzz that did surface (the Campos Neto union coverage) was chased to mainstream sources and resolved, so **RF-MGT-005 does not fire.**

**What a rational minority holder should conclude.** The people running Nu Holdings are not a reason to walk away — there is no crook here, no phoenix, no shadow network, no hidden brand, and no controller with a graveyard behind him, and the filings named almost everything we found. What a holder should hold onto instead are two specific, checkable things: that the board seat responsible for challenging the accounts is currently the seat carrying an unresolved insolvency exposure, three weeks old, and the board's response to it is not yet on the record; and that the company's public policy is now run by the man who wrote the rules it profits from, lawfully and after the required quarantine, but fast enough that Brazil is changing the rule. Neither fails the Non-Negotiable Gate. Both cap conviction, and both are exactly the kind of fact that looks small until the quarter it does not.

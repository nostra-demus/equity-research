## 3. Stated Strategy & Targets

Management's stated plan is to keep compounding a single business — a low-cost digital bank — along three lines: grow the customer base and deepen it (the "Nu Ecosystem"), extend the same model across Brazil, Mexico and Colombia, and lay the foundation for a US launch (`FY25 Form 20-F, Item 4, "Our Growth Strategies"`; `Q2 2026 earnings-call transcript, Aug-13-2026, prepared remarks`). The economics they say drive it are an average revenue per active customer that rises with cohort age while cost to serve stays "at or below $1 per customer" (`Q1 2025 earnings-call transcript, May-13-2025, prepared remarks`).

**On explicit, checkable targets, there are almost none, and that is itself the finding.** The 20-F's strategy section contains no numeric multi-year target — no revenue, margin, return, leverage or customer goal. The company issues **no revenue, EPS, EBITDA or capex guidance at all**, and said so on the record when asked for a growth number: *"I won't necessarily give you a specific number of growth"* (`Q2 2026 earnings-call transcript, Aug-13-2026, Q&A — David Vélez`; corroborated by `earnings/04_guidance-consensus.md`). The only explicit numbers management has put a period against are three call-derived modelling parameters — a **full-year 2026 efficiency ratio of "approximately 20%"**, an **IFRS effective tax rate of 15–20% for the remainder of 2026**, and a **cap of "less than 100 basis points" of efficiency-ratio drag from US investment in each of 2026 and 2027** — plus a qualitative statement that the current risk-adjusted net interest margin (interest margin after credit losses) is sustainable (`Q1 2026 earnings-call transcript, May-14-2026, prepared remarks`; `Q2 2026 earnings-call transcript, Aug-13-2026, prepared remarks and Q&A`). A management team that publishes no targets cannot miss them; it also cannot be held to them. That structurally limits how much of the promise-vs-delivery test below can bite.

## 5. Management Read

The people running NU are the people who built it: a founder-CEO of thirteen years who controls 74.4% of the votes, a co-founder still in an executive seat, and a risk officer of eight years — sitting alongside a bench that has been rebuilt at speed, with five of the nine officers named in the FY25 20-F (plus the CFO and the product officer appointed since) under about fifteen months in post. The delivered record is real and not in dispute: profit to parent has gone from a US$364.6m loss in FY2022 to US$3,607.1m over the twelve months to June 2026, risk-adjusted NIM has recovered from 8.2% to 12.4%, and the Mexican banking licence that was applied for in 2023 was granted in August 2026. Where explicit promises exist they are few and mostly met — the exception being the tax-rate range, which was missed on its first test and then dropped from the script.

The single biggest management signal is a negative one, and it is about disclosure rather than performance: **the President & Chief Operating Officer, the CEO's number two and the executive who personally presented asset quality on the earnings calls, left between the Q1 2025 and Q2 2025 calls with no announcement, no stated reason and no successor — and the Chief Technology Officer went the same way — while the one seat that functioned as a deputy was quietly eliminated in a company where no shareholder vote can change the CEO.** That is not evidence of wrongdoing; it is evidence that this company does not tell you when its most senior people leave, which is precisely the thing an outside holder cannot check for themselves.

# Incentives & Compensation — NU

**Jurisdiction & regime detected (CLAUDE.md §27).** Nu Holdings Ltd. is a Cayman Islands exempted company listed on the NYSE and filing as a **foreign private issuer**. Its proxy-equivalent is therefore **not** a DEF 14A — it is **Item 6.B (Compensation), Item 6.C (Board Practices), Item 6.D (Employees) and Item 6.F (Clawback) of the Form 20-F**, plus the audited notes (Note 10 Share-based payments, Note 28 Related parties) and Exhibit 97.1 (Compensation Clawback Policy). Reporting standard is **IFRS as issued by the IASB**; reporting currency **US dollars**; fiscal year = **calendar year ended 31 December 2025**. India's Companies Act Sec 197 caps and the SEBI LODR Reg 17(6)(e) promoter-pay gates named in the checklist have **no Cayman/NYSE equivalent** — those prongs are answered Not Applicable with the reason, and the substantive tests behind them (pay vs profit, pay growth vs profit growth, controller pay) are run on the local numbers instead.

Cross-module inputs used: `01_management-and-track-record.md` (this module) and `business-model/11_capital-allocation-governance.md`. `earnings/` outputs were not read for this agent's scope.

## 2. Performance Metrics

This is where the read gets thin, and the thinness is the finding. **Neither plan discloses a single named metric or a single weight.**

| **Terminated — 2021 Contingent Share Award (CEO)** | **The only fully specified metric Nu has ever published, and it was a pure per-share one:** 1% of fully diluted shares when the Class A VWAP held **≥ US$18.69** for 60 consecutive trading days, plus a further 1% at **≥ US$35.30**, both requiring five years' continued service | 100% of the CEO's variable pay while it existed | **Per-share / market-based.** Terminated 29 Nov 2022 at the CEO's own request | `FY21 20-F, Item 6.E — 2021 Contingent Share Awards, p.245`; `FY24 20-F, Item 6.E — CSA, p.288` |

**What the pay therefore rewards.** Two things: staying (time-vested RSUs over a 3.8-year average) and the share price (because the award is settled in shares). Nothing disclosed rewards revenue, absolute EBITDA, headcount, customer count, or deals done — so there is **no evidence of a size-based or empire-building metric anywhere in the pay structure**, which matters for a company that grew revenue from US$8.0bn to US$15.8bn in two years. But the converse is equally true: there is **no disclosed returns metric either** — no return on equity, no return on capital (ROIC — the profit earned per US$100 of capital put in), no earnings per share, no total shareholder return, and no hurdle of any kind on a single RSU that vested in 2025.

**Where the returns discipline actually sits.** Not in the pay plan — in the controller's own balance sheet. Vélez holds 3,088,117 Class A and 903,124,498 Class B shares, 906,212,615 of 4,860,599,241 shares outstanding = **18.6% of the economics**, worth roughly **US$13.0bn** at the 29 Aug 2026 close of US$14.30. That is about **142 times the entire US$91.3m aggregate pay pool** for the whole board and management team combined [`FY25 20-F, Item 7.A — Major Shareholders, p.203-204`; `ciq_facts.json current_price 14.3 / shares_outstanding_m 4,830.7 — CIQ Comps→Financial Data, as-of 2026-08-29, vendor export`]. Computation by this agent from those figures.

**The single most informative pay event in the record.** On 29 November 2022 Vélez unilaterally terminated the 2021 CSA and the board and compensation committee agreed, "on the basis that it has constituted a unilateral transfer of future value from Mr. Vélez to the Company – and, consequently, all shareholders." He "has not received any consideration for the termination." The stated reasons were (i) removing US$355.6m of future share-based expense and (ii) avoiding "potential dilution of the shareholders of the Company in an amount equivalent to up to 2% of the total number of ordinary shares in issue" [`FY24 20-F, Item 6.E — CSA, p.288`]. The award's grant-date fair value was **US$422.6m** [`FY21 20-F, Item 6.E, p.245`]; accounting acceleration cost US$355.6m in Q4 2022.

**Both sides of that fact, stated (§3, §8).** It is a real, dated, per-share-positive act by a controller who did not have to do it, and it is the strongest single alignment signal in the file. It is also true that the award was, and still is, **out of the money**: the first milestone required a US$18.69 60-day VWAP, and the stock closed at **US$14.30** on 29 Aug 2026 — roughly four years and nine months after grant, that milestone has still not been reached [`ciq_facts.json current_price 14.3, as-of 2026-08-29`]. So what he gave up was a contingent claim, not cash in hand. Both facts are the finding; neither cancels the other.

## 4. Incentive Alignment Read

**Verdict: mixed.**

The pay actually rewards two things — staying, and the share price. RSUs vest on time (weighted-average 3.8 years) with no performance condition attached, the annual bonus is a union-negotiated profit-sharing formula whose goals are never published, and the last time Nu attached a real metric to executive pay it was a pure per-share one (a US$18.69 and US$35.30 60-day VWAP) that the CEO handed back for nothing in November 2022, taking a claim on up to 2% of the fully diluted share count off the table [`FY24 20-F, Item 6.E, p.288`]. Nothing in the disclosed structure pays for revenue, absolute EBITDA, headcount or deals, which is the important negative in a business that doubled revenue in two years — **the empire-building channel is simply not wired into this pay plan** — but nothing pays for return on capital or earnings per share either, so the returns discipline is not in the contract; it is in the fact that the controller owns 18.6% of the economics, worth roughly 142 times the entire US$91.3m board-and-management pay pool [`FY25 20-F, Item 7.A, p.203`; `ciq_facts.json, as-of 2026-08-29`].

The single biggest incentive signal is that **the CEO's incentive is his shareholding, not his pay package — and Nu's disclosure makes it impossible to check that this is also true one layer down.** Aggregate pay fell 4.9% while profit rose 45.6% and now takes 3.18% of net income against 5.83% two years ago, equity burn is 0.77% a year against a 4.70% reserve, repricing is barred without a shareholder vote, and a Rule 10D-1 no-fault clawback with a three-year lookback is in force. Those are real and they are cited. Against them: no individual pay figure for anyone, no named metric, no weight, no named peer, no CEO-to-median ratio, no attrition rate, and no vote that any shareholder can cast on remuneration. That combination — good pay *conduct* on every number available, no ability to *audit* it at the level that matters — is what "mixed" means here, and it is why this score does not go higher.

*Note on the partial-data cap.* The MODULE_RULES cap "No proxy / compensation disclosure → Incentive alignment max 50" is **not** applied. Compensation disclosure exists and is substantive (Item 6.B plan terms, Note 10 award tables, Note 28 aggregate pay, Exhibit 97.1 clawback policy). What is absent is *individual* disclosure, a legal-regime feature of Cayman/FPI reporting rather than an absence of the disclosure the cap contemplates. The gap is instead charged directly against the "metric clarity and weights" and "minority-holder alignment" components below.

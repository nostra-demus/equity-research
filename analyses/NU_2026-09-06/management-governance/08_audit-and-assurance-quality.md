# Audit & Assurance Quality — NU

**Company:** Nu Holdings Ltd. (NYSE: NU) — Cayman Islands holding company, operating banks and payment institutions in Brazil, Mexico and Colombia.
**Regime (from triage `00`):** US SEC **foreign private issuer**. Annual filing = **Form 20-F** (not 10-K); interim = unaudited condensed statements furnished on 6-K; **no DEF 14A** — governance and auditor-fee disclosure sit in 20-F Items 6, 15 and 16A–16K. Reporting standard **IFRS Accounting Standards as issued by the IASB**; currency **US dollars, in thousands**; fiscal year ends **31 December**. Audit oversight: **SEC + PCAOB** at the listed level, **BCB / CMN** and **CVM** in Brazil, **CNBV** in Mexico, **SFC** in Colombia.
**Regime translation applied (CLAUDE.md §27):** India-specific items — CARO annexure (A4-11) and the MR-3 secretarial audit (part of A4-08) — do not exist in this regime. The PCAOB-form equivalents are used instead: Critical Audit Matters in place of KAMs, the ICFR attestation in place of the IFC annexure, and the SOX 302/906 certifications plus the BCB-mandated internal-controls officer in place of the secretarial compliance report. Where no local equivalent exists, the item is marked **Not Applicable (regime)** with the regime named — never left blank.

**Hard-lock deference (CLAUDE.md §13; MODULE_RULES Disqualifier Deference).** `business-model/01_disqualifier-scan.md` fired **no** audit-related hard lock. Quoted verbatim, test 1: *"Auditor qualification or going-concern note (last 3 years) — **N**. Unqualified opinion in all three years."* Test 4: *"Auditor changed twice in last 3 years without disclosed reason — **N**. Zero changes, let alone two."* Test 5: *"Material restatement (>5% of revenue or net income) in last 2 years — **N**. Computed: restatement amount = US$0."* Nothing below re-decides those calls; this report documents the full spectrum around them.

**A4-06 — economic independence. Green.** Non-audit fees are **7.8%** of audit fees (US$226.8k against US$2,923.2k), far inside the 25% green band and nowhere near the red band of non-audit ≥ audit. The Enron pattern — Andersen earning more from consulting than from auditing — is absent by a wide margin. What the non-audit money bought is disclosed by category: audit-related work is accounting consultations, acquisition-related audits, internal-control reviews and non-statutory attest work; tax fees are compliance, advice and planning; and **"all other fees are fees billed for assurance services related to environmental, social and governance and sustainability assurance"** — that last line grew from US$6.5k to US$99.3k, which is a sustainability-assurance engagement starting up, not a consulting relationship forming. **None of the disclosed categories is on the SOX §201 prohibited list** (bookkeeping, financial-information-systems design, appraisal/valuation, fairness opinions, actuarial, internal-audit outsourcing, management or HR functions, broker-dealer or investment-banking services, legal and expert services). A pre-approval process exists and is described: every proposed engagement, audit and permitted non-audit alike, goes to the Audit and Risk Committee before it starts, per SOX and SEC rules, under a written policy posted publicly.

**One honest limitation.** Item 16C reports fees **billed to the company by "our independent registered public accounting firm"**. It does not separately itemise amounts paid to other **KPMG network firms** in Mexico or Colombia. The MODULE_RULES band requires network-firm fees to be included, and I cannot prove they are. If material amounts sat outside this table the ratio would rise — but for it to breach even the 25% green band, undisclosed network non-audit fees would have to exceed **US$504k**, roughly **2.2×** everything disclosed. Green stands; the limitation is on the record.

**A4-07 — fee level and trend. Amber, and the two halves point different ways.**

- **Trend: clean.** The audit fee rose **+51.6%** (US$1,928.3k → US$2,923.2k) while revenue rose **+26.8%** and total assets rose **+50.0%**. The fee is growing **faster than revenue and in step with the balance sheet it has to audit**. The red band this test exists to catch — a fee **falling** while revenue grows more than 20%, the signature of low-balling (pricing an audit too cheap to do it properly) — is the opposite of what happened.
- **Level: on the low side, and it deserves saying out loud.** US$2.92m buys the audit of a **US$74.9bn-asset** bank with **131 million customers** across three countries, an expected-credit-loss allowance of **US$5.0bn** estimated from internal scoring models, **and** a full ICFR attestation. That is **~90% of the US$3.26m average fee across more than 6,600 SEC registrants in 2024** — most of which are far smaller and simpler. The fee equals **0.042% of revenue**.
- **Why Amber rather than Red.** The red band is a fee **below 50%** of the peer median for the size band. I could not obtain a size-band peer median: an attempt to read Item 16C from Inter & Co's FY2025 20-F returned XBRL markup rather than narrative text, and no other LatAm digital-bank fee table is in the pool or was retrievable. The all-registrant average is a **cruder** benchmark than the band asks for, and NU sits at 90% of it rather than below 50%. Conservative default (§4) applied to the residual uncertainty: **Amber**, with the peer comparison marked coverage-limited.

**Writing the absence as the finding, as required.** Nu has **34 subsidiaries**, at least four of them regulated financial institutions across three jurisdictions, and **no component-auditor disclosure exists anywhere in the pool.** That absence is the answer to A4-03 and A4-14 — not a blank and not a routine N/A.

**Why Amber and not Red, and why it is not nothing.** The Wirecard/Satyam pattern the red band describes is a cash- or revenue-rich component sitting at an unknown local firm while the principal auditor quietly excludes it. **There is no evidence of that here**, and two structural facts push against it: KPMG's report is a **single undivided opinion** covering the whole consolidation, which under PCAOB standards means KPMG owns the work whoever performed it; and the entity holding the assets is a **BCB-supervised Brazilian bank**, itself subject to mandatory independent audit, internal audit and a statutory audit committee. So the coverage is very probably fine.

**But "probably fine" is not the same as verified, and there is a specific reason to press.** The disclosure that would let anyone check — PCAOB **Form AP**, which lists the other accounting firms participating and their share of hours, and the **AS 1301** communication of those firms' names and responsibilities to the audit committee — is **precisely what the PCAOB censured KPMG Auditores Independentes Ltda. for getting wrong on 11 March 2025**, across its practice, "including, among others, component auditors [and] shared service centers." The one control that would close this gap is the one the auditor's own regulator found defective. Investors cannot see it from the 20-F; the audit committee is supposed to be told, and its regulator says firms of this kind were not telling. That combination is what carries the item to Amber rather than Green, and it fires no red flag only because there is no affirmative evidence of an uncovered component. **RF-AUD-005 does not fire.**

## 10. Read

The chain that checks Nu's numbers is **able and, on the evidence, unafraid — but it is thinly lit.** Eight years with one auditor, five clean opinions on the accounts and four on the internal controls, no resignation, no restatement, no qualification, non-audit fees at 7.8% of audit fees with a working pre-approval policy, and an audit fee rising 51.6% while the balance sheet it covers rose 50.0% — none of that is bought, starved or scared off. The pre-IPO IT material weaknesses were found by management itself and closed in 2022.

**The single weakest link is not inside Nu — it is the auditor's own regulatory record, sitting on top of a disclosure Nu does not make.** In one twelve-month window KPMG Auditores Independentes Ltda. was censured by the PCAOB (11 Mar 2025) for practice-wide quality-control failings and for **not disclosing which other firms participate in its audits and not telling audit committees about them**; had the confidential quality-control section of a 2021 inspection report published (25 Sep 2025) for failing to remediate; and paid **R$1,782,000** to settle a CVM proceeding (20 May 2025). Meanwhile Nu discloses **no component auditor for any of its 34 subsidiaries**, and **names no signing partner in any of five years** — so neither the coverage of the consolidation nor the partner's disciplinary record can be checked at all. The one control that would close the first gap is the one the auditor's regulator says the firm got wrong. None of this is evidence that Nu's numbers are wrong; it is evidence that an outsider cannot independently confirm who audited what, or who signed it.

To trust these numbers more, three things would do it, in order: **(1)** the PCAOB Form AP for the FY2025 Nu audit, which names the engagement partner and every participating firm with its share of hours — swept against PCAOB and CVM records; **(2)** a statement that the Audit and Risk Committee meets the auditor and the head of internal audit **without management present**, and how often; and **(3)** disclosure of who runs internal audit and what it found. On top of that, a dated plan for the audit-firm succession that Brazil's ten-year cap makes due around FY2028.

**Conclusion: mixed — watch the named link (the auditor's own regulatory record combined with zero component-auditor visibility).**

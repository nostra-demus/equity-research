## 3. Disclosure Transparency

On the things it chooses to show, NU is specific and quantified — cohort ARPAC curves out to month 90, a basis-point bridge for early delinquency (seasonality −37bp, deliberate risk expansion +24bp in Q2'26), coverage of 90-plus balances at 244%, and per-country deposits, customers and regulatory capital ratios. What it does not show is **profit**: the group reports **one operating segment** and discloses **no country margin, no product margin and no country net income**, and roughly a fifth to a quarter of what it earns (US$3,691.0m of FY2025 interest and fee income, 23.4%) sits outside the geographic revenue split entirely `[FY25 Form 20-F, Note 34; business-model/03_segment-map.md, §1–3]`. It also issues no revenue, EPS, EBITDA or capex guidance of any kind `[earnings/04_guidance-consensus.md, §2]`.

**Two disclosures got LESS transparent, and both are warnings.** First, the **geographic breakdown narrowed**: the FY2023 20-F named Brazil, Mexico, Colombia, the Cayman Islands, the United States, Germany and Argentina separately; from the FY2024 20-F onward everything except Brazil and Mexico is collapsed into "Other countries" — a bucket that is only 2.0% of revenue but **13.9% of group non-current assets** and that now conceals Colombia, an operating bank with 5m+ customers and US$3.3bn of deposits, plus the new US entity `[FY23 Form 20-F (filed 2024-04-19), Segment information note; FY25 Form 20-F, Note 34(b); business-model/03_segment-map.md, §3]`. Second, **cost to serve moved from a spoken "pillar of our strategy" with a stated ceiling to an unspoken appendix chart in the same quarter the metric reached that ceiling** (US$1.00, Q1'26 and Q2'26, from US$0.70 in Q1'25) `[Q4 2023 and Q4 2024 transcripts; Q2'26 Earnings Presentation, Appendix slide 28]`.

## 4. Tone in Bad Times

The worst recent periods are **Q1'25** (risk-adjusted NIM trough of 8.2%) and **Q1'26** (a double miss — revenue −1.8%, GAAP EPS −5.2% against consensus). In both, the tone was **candid rather than promotional on the specific bad number**: the 8.2% NIM and its 130-basis-point decline were volunteered in prepared remarks with a 3/4–1/4 causal split before any question was asked, and the flattering 17.6% efficiency ratio was actively talked down by management itself — *"should not be extrapolated as our run rate"* — a call that was then proved right one quarter later. Q&A is answered directly and with numbers rather than deflected: on the hardest question of the last two years (Q4'25, the tax rate) the CFO itemised three one-offs including two negatives and volunteered that the same law change becomes *"a headwind"* later, and in Q2'26 the new CFO corrected an analyst who put words in his mouth — *"I would correct one thing you said… you said that 12% is a floor. I didn't say that it was a floor… I don't want to overcommit there."*

The countervailing feature of the tone is **framing, not falsehood**: the CEO opens every call with the same growth formula (*"13 years later, that hypothesis continues to play out exactly as we envisioned"*), attributes a record profit to *"customer obsession translated into an earnings-generating formula"*, and the Q1'26 script called a quarter that missed both consensus lines *"another clean expression of that model"*. The numbers management named as the sequential drivers of that record quarter — revenue +8% sequentially, risk-adjusted NIM up almost 3 points — are corroborated by the filings, so this is promotional packaging around accurate operating detail, not commentary contradicting the numbers.

## 5. Candor Read

**Verdict: mixed — leaning candid, held back by one specific and repeatable pattern.** On the evidence that matters most, management does tell the truth when the number is bad: it volunteered an 8.2% risk-adjusted margin with a weighted decomposition before being asked, it talked down its own record-low 17.6% efficiency ratio as *"not a run rate"* and was proved right, it itemised two negative one-offs alongside one positive when only the positive was being asked about, and its CFO publicly refused to be pinned to a floor he had not given. That behaviour is uncommon and it is the strongest evidence for trusting this management in a bad quarter.

What holds the verdict at mixed is that the **selectivity runs one way**. The guide the company was beating — the ~20% full-year efficiency ratio — was explicitly reiterated on the Q2'26 call. The guide it had just missed **to the flattering side** — the 15–20% IFRS effective tax rate — was not mentioned once on that call, in a quarter where `earnings/04` shows **96.7% of a 12.6% net-income beat came from the tax line** while pre-tax profit landed within 0.5% of consensus, and where the deferred tax asset that generates the benefit has reached **27.5% of parent equity**. The same pattern appears in the cost-to-serve commitment, retired without comment in the quarter the metric hit the US$1.00 ceiling management had twice promised to hold, and in the geographic disclosure that narrowed from seven named countries to three buckets. Weighed against the trust read, and not set aside, are two items routed from upstream: `01`'s finding that the **President & COO and the CTO both left with no announcement, no reason and no successor**, and `01`'s confirmation that `business-model/01_disqualifier-scan` routed **no unresolved adverse integrity signal** (so `RF-MGT-005` is not emitted here either) — the first weighs against, the second does not clear the first.

**The single biggest candor signal:** management is candid about what the operating business is doing and silent about the line that is actually producing the earnings surprise.

**Standalone red-flag tags (CLAUDE.md §13).**

`RF-DISC-002 (recurring "one-off" / aggressive non-GAAP add-backs)` — triggered at **Medium** severity: share-based compensation, a cost incurred in every period on record (US$212.6m / US$272.4m / US$271.8m / US$216.8m H1'26), is added back to Adjusted Net Income every year; and the CSLL deferred-tax remeasurement the CFO called *"a nonrecurring one-off event"* (+US$58.5m FY2025) recurred the next period (+US$28.4m H1'26). Severity is Medium, not High, because the add-back is +7.1% of reported profit and falling, and the company headlines the IFRS figure.

`RF-DISC-001 (commentary contradicting the numbers)` — **tested and NOT triggered.** The sequential drivers management named for the record Q2'26 quarter (revenue +8% sequentially, risk-adjusted NIM +~3 points, cost of credit contributing 115bp) are corroborated by the filings and the deck. The defect found is omission of the tax line, not an assertion the numbers contradict, so the tag is not emitted.

`RF-REG-002 (delayed results / material-disclosure timeliness)` — **tested and NOT triggered.** Results have been published 44 days after each of Q3'25, Q1'26 and Q2'26 and 56 days after Q4'25; the FY2025 Form 20-F was filed 2026-04-08, 98 days after the 31-December year-end and inside the four-month foreign-private-issuer deadline (prior years 2025-04-16, 2024-04-19, 2023-04-20, 2022-04-21). Timeliness (A7-01) is owned by `12_regulatory-legal-and-compliance`; the unannounced senior departures are a disclosure-**completeness** matter owned by `01`, not a timeliness breach.

## 1. RPT Intensity & the Applicable Regime (A5-01)

**Fix the regime first.** NU is **not** an Indian issuer, so **SEBI LODR Reg 23 does not apply** — neither the pre-Dec-2025 "lower of 10% of turnover or Rs 1,000 cr" line nor the Dec-2025 graded slabs. Naming them and setting them aside is the point of this step: applying either would be a bad-extraction error (CLAUDE.md §20, §27).

NU is also **not** subject to **Item 404 of Regulation S-K** and its US$120,000 disclosure floor. Item 404 binds domestic US registrants filing a 10-K / DEF 14A. As a **foreign private issuer**, NU discloses under:

| Layer | Rule that actually governs | What it requires | Where NU answers it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Narrative | **Form 20-F, Item 7.B** | Transactions with related parties **material to the company or to the related party** over the last three financial years; and **amounts outstanding on loans** to key management | `FY25 Form 20-F, Item 7.B (Related Party Transactions), p.205-206` |
| Financial statements | **IAS 24** | The **nature of the relationship**, the **amounts** of transactions and outstanding balances, and **key management personnel compensation** in aggregate. IAS 24 does **not** require the counterparty to be named | `FY25 Form 20-F, Note 28 (Related parties), p.F-77` |
| Approval | The company's own **Related-Party Transactions Policy** | "In March 2024, we updated our related party transaction policy, which requires certain related party transactions to be approved by our board of directors or a designated committee thereof, including our Audit and Risk Committee" | `FY25 Form 20-F, Item 7.B, p.206` |

**There is no majority-of-minority shareholder vote requirement in this regime, and none was held.** That is the rule, not a governance failure — but it also means the only check on an NU related-party transaction is a board / committee approval whose evidence is not published. That is scored at A5-07 below, not here.

**Basis notes (CLAUDE.md §15).**
- The **926 / 1,795 / 265 figures are period-end BALANCES** (a liability), not flows. FY2025 and FY2024 disclose **no** related-party revenue or expense line at all - only the balance. So "% of revenue" sets a **point-in-time balance** against a **full-year flow**: the bases do not match. The matched version cannot be built because the company does not disclose the flow. Both bases are labelled inline every time the ratio appears, and **no monitoring threshold is derived from the unmatched ratio.**
- FY2021 and FY2022 figures **are** flows (revenues / expenses) and match the revenue denominator properly.
- The H1-2026 cell divides a 30-Jun-2026 balance by a trailing-twelve-month revenue figure. Mismatch labelled inline; shown only for direction of travel (1,795 -> 926 -> 265, falling), never as a share.
- The **% of assets** column pairs a FY2025 period-end balance with a FY2025 period-end asset base - that ratio **is** matched, and is the one clean ratio in this table.

**Verdict A5-01: Green.** On the strictest reading - every disclosed outside related-party item plus all key-management compensation, against consolidated revenue - related-party intensity is **0.58% of revenue in FY2025**, inside a Green band of <1-5%. Excluding compensation (remuneration is `03_incentives-and-compensation`'s subject, not a leakage channel), it is **0.0059%**.

**The >25% hard lock.** `business-model/01_disqualifier-scan.md` computed RP sales / revenue = 0.0% and RP purchases-expenses / total expenses = 0.76%, and returned **N**. My independent read reconciles: 91,269 / 12,032,723 total expenses = **0.76%**. **This module reports the ratio and defers to `business-model/01_disqualifier-scan` (hard lock) - it is not re-adjudicated here.**

**Why the note is short, and why that is structure rather than opacity.** Note 28 gives the mechanical reason: "all entities within the Group are consolidated in these consolidated financial statements. Therefore, related party balances and transactions, as well as unrealized gains or losses arising from intercompany transactions, are eliminated" [`FY25 20-F, Note 28, p.F-77`]. Every entity in the group is majority- or wholly-owned and fully consolidated. There is **no associate, no joint venture and no equity-method entity** through which value could move without appearing in the consolidated numbers, and **no promoter operating company** anywhere in the picture. A short RPT note in a wholly-consolidated single-controller group with no promoter trading entities is the expected shape. The partial-data rule is therefore **not** triggered: the related-party note exists, in all five years, in English, audited.

### Three things this ledger says that the headline number does not

**1. The Note 28(a) counterparty is Lightspark Group, Inc. — verified independently, and the identification holds.** I did not take `07_people-integrity-dossiers`' conclusion on trust; I re-read the note text myself. Note 28(a) fn.(i) reads, verbatim: *"In the second quarter of 2024, Nu entered into a commercial relationship with a company where one of its Directors serves as CEO. As part of this agreement, Nu received a cash incentive, which will be used to support projects costs upon the Company's satisfaction of certain conditions"* [`FY25 20-F, Note 28(a) fn.(i), p.F-77`]. Four facts have to coincide, and they do: (i) exactly **two** NU directors are sitting CEOs — David Marcus of Lightspark and Jacqueline Reses of Lead Bank [`FY25 20-F, Item 6.A`]; (ii) the **Nubank-Lightspark partnership was announced 2024-06-25 = Q2 2024**, matching the quarter in the note (web sweep 2026-09-06, first-party company newsroom and PRNewswire); (iii) the **direction of payment** is *to* NU — a partner start-up paying an integration incentive to a bank with 100m+ customers is the commercially sensible direction, the reverse is not; (iv) the stated use, **"support project costs"**, matches a technical integration. `07`'s sweep found **no NU-Lead Bank transaction**. This is an **inference from four matching facts, labelled as such (CLAUDE.md §3)** — the filing does not name the counterparty. Confidence: high, not certain.

**2. The single largest disclosed number in this ledger flows the WRONG way for a leakage thesis.** The 926 is a **liability of NU** — money NU *received* and has not yet earned. Over three periods it has run down 1,795 -> 926 -> 265 as conditions were satisfied. There is no year in which NU **paid** an outside related party anything larger than the roughly US$1.1-1.7m of Rodamoinho and Reprograma costs in 2021-2022, both of which ended more than three years ago.

**3. One printed figure in the FY2021 note does not tie, and it has never been repeated.** The note states NU "sold 240,072 Series G-1 preferred shares at a purchase price of US$39.988768 per share to the Company's Board Members, for a total amount of US$1,600." At the stated price, 240,072 x 39.988768 = **US$9,600 thousand**, not US$1,600 thousand — a factor of six. In a statement presented in US$ thousands, the printed total does not reconcile to the printed price and quantity. I record it as an **unreconciled figure in the FY2021 note**, materiality **Low** (either reading is under US$10m against a company that has since earned US$2.9bn in a year), never repeated in FY2022-FY2025, and I do **not** silently pick whichever number suits. It is an accuracy observation on a five-year-old pre-IPO disclosure, not a leakage finding. [`FY21 20-F, Note 24`]

**Unnamed "entities where KMP exercise influence" carrying material value: none.** The one unnamed counterparty carries **US$0.926m** — 0.0059% of revenue. That is not a material unnamed exposure. **A5-08 is graded on disclosure practice below, not on this amount.**

**The brand-ownership question, answered rather than assumed (A5-02 x A17-04).** The rule that matters here is not "is there a royalty line" but "who owns the marks the company trades under, and is the licence priced?" `07_people-integrity-dossiers` ran the trademark-registry check and I adopt its finding after checking its basis: **NUBANK (USPTO serial 86386363) and NU BANK (serial 86963402) are registered to NU PAGAMENTOS S.A. - INSTITUICAO DE PAGAMENTO**, a wholly-owned, fully consolidated subsidiary named in Note 3 [USPTO via Justia Trademarks, read 2026-09-06; `FY25 20-F, Note 3, p.F-19`].

The marks therefore sit **inside the consolidated group** - not with a controller vehicle, not licensed in from outside, not in dispute. There is consequently **nothing to price**: no licence fee is disclosed because none is owed to anyone outside the consolidation, and an intra-group licence would be eliminated in consolidation anyway. This is the one case where "no fee disclosed" is the correct answer rather than the finding.

**RF-NET-004 does not propagate.** The registry's ADVERSE conditions are none of them met: the owner is not controller-linked; there is no external licence whose terms, fee basis or duration could be undisclosed; there is no licence in dispute or terminable at short notice; and no unrelated live company was found using the marks in an overlapping class. External brand ownership alone would not have fired it in any case, and here there is not even external ownership.

**One adjacent brand payment, correctly outside this table.** NU holds the naming rights to the Palmeiras arena in Sao Paulo (the "NeoQuimica"/"Allianz"-class deal, announced publicly). That is a **payment by NU to an unrelated sports club** - a marketing cost, not a related-party royalty. It is named here only because the FY2021-22 Rodamoinho item was *also* a naming-rights agreement, and the two must not be conflated: Rodamoinho was related-party (a director's company) and ended in 2022; the arena deal is arm's-length and current.

## 14. Read

**Roughly US$0.9m of value sits between Nu Holdings and any outside related party, and it is owed TO the company, not by it.** The entire five-year related-party record outside remuneration amounts to a US$1.7m combined services bill in 2021-22 (a director's events company and the founder's family's coding-education project, both named, both ended), a pre-IPO share purchase by board members, and a Q2-2024 partnership under which a start-up whose CEO sits on NU's board **paid** the company an integration incentive that has since run down from US$1,795k to US$265k. Including all key-management compensation, related-party intensity is **0.58% of FY2025 revenue and 3.21% of profit** [`FY25 20-F, Note 28`; denominators from `earnings/01`]; excluding it, **0.0059% of revenue**. The single largest channel is the unnamed Lightspark arrangement, and its direction of value favours the minority shareholder.

**The group structure would not hide much, but it is not fully lit.** Thirty-four entities, **zero associates and zero joint ventures**, no entity parked at 49% below a consolidation line, no unconsolidated SPE, and the one securitisation-shaped fund (a FIDC) consolidated the moment control was obtained. Against that: depth runs to **at least four layers** once Note 3's own "Indirect" control labels are honoured against the vendor tree; the audited filing names only **6 of 34** entities; and **seven entities have no stated purpose in any filing** - Nu Cayman Ltd and six sequentially paired US LLCs (Nu 1-A through 3-B) whose naming convention is that of financing vehicles, a reading I label as my inference and not a disclosure. Consolidated cash of **US$15.0bn** sits inside the group's own **regulated** banks - BACEN, BANXICO/CNBV, SFC - with **6.00% pledged as margin-loan collateral** (US$899.8m over US$15,003.6m, both 31-Dec-2025, a matched period-end ratio). It is trapped by prudential regulators, not by a controller, and the parent/subsidiary split of that cash is not disclosed at all.

**What actually caps this read is detection capability, not exposure.** NU offers a **bare "similar terms" assertion** with no benchmarking methodology, **never quantifies** the credit cards and loans it issues to its own directors and executives despite Item 7.B asking for exactly that, publishes **no approval threshold** and **no record of any specific approval**, and **declines to name** its only current related counterparty when it named the previous two. None of that is a violation in this regime, and none of it is evidence of leakage. It does mean that a mid-sized related-party arrangement could exist without a reader being able to price it - and the honest conclusion is a **low measured leakage risk with a moderate blind spot**, not a clean bill.

**The one disclosure that would settle the biggest open question:** **the quantified balance of credit cards and loans outstanding to NU's directors, executive officers, key employees and their close family members at 31 December 2025** - the figure Form 20-F Item 7.B already asks for and NU has never given. It is the only unquantified related-party number in the whole file, it sits at a **lender** where such lending is the natural leakage channel, and one line in the next 20-F closes it. (Second, and separable: the identity of the **Banco Porto Real** selling shareholders, and the content of **Exhibit 8.1**, which would resolve the six unexplained US LLCs.)

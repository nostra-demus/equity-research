# Contingent Liabilities & Commitments — NU

**Company:** Nu Holdings Ltd. (NYSE: NU) — Cayman Islands holding company, operating banks and financing companies in Brazil, Mexico and Colombia.
**Reporting regime:** SEC foreign private issuer → **Form 20-F** is the audited annual filing (the local equivalent of a 10-K); IFRS Accounting Standards; presentation currency **US dollars**; fiscal year ends **31 December**. There is no Indian-style "Contingent Liabilities and Commitments" schedule and no CARO annexure — the equivalent disclosures are **Note 25 "Provisions and contingent liabilities"**, **Item 8.A "Legal Proceedings"**, the borrowings note's **Guarantees** paragraph, and the market-risk section. All note figures below are **US$ thousands as printed**, restated to US$ millions where I compare them to equity.

**Cross-module inputs.** `balance-sheet-survival/05_off-balance-sheet-and-contingencies.md` does not exist in this run root (`analyses/NU_2026-09-06/balance-sheet-survival/` contains only `00_solvency-data-triage.md`) — **balance-sheet-survival cross-module input not available; proceeding on this module's own read of the data pool.** No figure below is taken from, or recomputed against, that module. Denominators come from the 20-F balance sheet and `earnings/01_historical-financials.md`.

**Sector overlay.** This is a bank/financial group (MODULE_RULES sector overlay: banks/NBFCs). Undrawn credit-card limits are the business itself, not a governance contingency — they are shown once, labelled, and left to the survival module.

**The flat-total trap, in the mirror.** The headline "contingent liability" number (possible losses) **fell 56% in FY2025**, from US$20.351m to US$8.904m. Read alone, that is an improving trend. It is not. Two things moved the other way inside it: (i) the underlying consumer-litigation book migrated from *possible* into *probable* and was provided — the civil provision rose from US$2.096m (Dec-2022) to US$30.739m (Jun-2026), 14.7×, against customer growth of 75.6%, and the PROCON/small-claims caseload rose from 8,714 to 32,703 active proceedings (litigation intensity per million customers roughly doubled, from ~117 to ~250); (ii) the single largest claim the company faces — the Colombia GMF class action (§4) — is classified *possible* and appears **nowhere** in that US$8.904m. **Never grade this company's contingency trend from the possible-loss total.**

**Vanished / unexplained items.** Labor possible losses dropped from US$17.738m to US$4.532m (−74%) and civil possible losses from US$14.212m to US$2.613m in the prior year (−82%), each with no reversal, payment or narrative — because IAS 37 does not require a movement table for possible losses and Nu does not volunteer one. Recorded as a movement-honesty Amber, not accepted silently.

**Crystallization test (A7a-13) — RF-CL-003 does NOT fire.** Payments out of provisions ran US$22.226m (2023), US$15.719m (2024), US$23.738m (2025), US$14.147m (H1-2026). That is recurring cash crystallization, and FY2025 payments (US$23.738m) **exceeded the opening provision balance (US$22.551m)** — the stock of provision is under one year of outflow. But the pattern is a fast-turning consumer-claims book where each year's claims are booked as *probable* and provided **before** they are paid, not items that sat in "possible" and then landed. The one genuine crystallization of a disputed item — the PIS/COFINS tax matter, US$15.747m — was fully provided from 2021 and settled against a judicial deposit in May 2023. Provisions were not understated. Verdict: **Amber on coverage depth, Green on candor.**

**Why this is the module's central finding.** The GMF claim is the largest single legal item Nu faces by an order of magnitude, it is classified *possible*, it is unprovided, and it is disclosed **only in Item 8.A** — it does not appear in Note 25(c), which is where a reader looking for the company's possible-loss universe would look, and which totals US$8.904m. The 20-F's own explanation for not sizing it is reasonable and is stated (52 defendants, apportionment requires transaction-level data Nu does not hold), so this is not concealment. It is a **placement and completeness defect**: IAS 37.86 asks for the estimate of the financial effect, or a statement that it cannot be made, in the contingency disclosure — and the statement that it cannot be made lives in a different item of the filing. Compounding it, the FY2025 20-F's litigation narrative ends with *"For further information, see note 23 to our audited consolidated financial statements"* — the contingency note is **note 25**, not note 23. A reader following the filing's own signpost lands in the wrong place.

**Posture (web, coverage-limited).** The public reporting that the class action was admitted on 25-Aug-2025 is a *dated, unverified* web source; admission ("admisión de la demanda") is a procedural gate, not a merits ruling, and a dismissal motion or appeal can still be live. But the 20-F, filed nearly eight months later, tells the reader the case is "in its initial stages awaiting a court resolution" without saying the action cleared admission. This is the IndiaMART pattern in a different dress: **the number stayed at zero while the procedural posture moved.** I have not read the primary docket — see the Sweep Log.

## 11. Read

**The measured off-P&L exposure is trivially small and honestly provided: US$39.824m of provisions plus disclosed possible losses against net worth of US$11,321.562m — 0.352%, against a Green band of under 5% — and the one tax dispute that ever crystallized (PIS/COFINS, US$15.747m) was fully provided from 2021 and paid from a judicial deposit in May 2023.** But do not read the trend off that total: the possible-loss line fell 56% in FY2025 (US$20.351m → US$8.904m) while the thing underneath it got worse — the civil provision is up 14.7× since Dec-2022 (US$2.096m → US$30.739m at Jun-2026) against customer growth of 75.6%, and active PROCON and small-claims proceedings went from 8,714 to 32,703, so consumer-litigation intensity per customer has roughly doubled; the number shrank because claims migrated from *possible* into *provided*, which is the honest direction but not the reassuring story the total tells.

**The single worst item is the Colombian GMF class action: a COP 9.3 trillion (~US$2.5bn) restitution claim against 52 financial institutions including Nu Colombia, classified "possible", unprovided, and disclosed only in Item 8.A — it does not appear in Note 25(c)'s US$8.904m possible-loss total, and the filing's own signpost sends the reader to "note 23" when the contingency note is note 25.** Nu's stated reason for not sizing its share (apportionment needs transaction-level data across 52 defendants) is reasonable and is on the record, so this is a placement-and-completeness failure rather than concealment — but public court reporting says a Bogotá judge **admitted** the action on 25-Aug-2025, nearly eight months before the 20-F that still calls it "in its initial stages awaiting a court resolution." That posture change is not in the filing, and I could not read the docket to confirm it (see the Sweep Log).

**On provisioning candor, management provides rather than parks:** probable losses are booked ahead of payment, the probable/possible/remote policy is now spelled out in the H1-2026 note, and there is no dispute anywhere in the record that lost at multiple levels while being carried unprovided — so RF-CL-002 does not fire. Against that, one thing in the audited note simply does not add up: judicial deposits are reported as **US$92,738k at 31-Dec-2025 in Note 25(d)**, as **US$6.6m for the same date in Item 8.A of the same 20-F**, and as **US$6,614k in the H1-2026 interim's comparative** — with the FY2024 comparative silently restated from US$5,711k to US$31,143k. A 14× internal contradiction inside an audited contingency note, unexplained and uncorrected, is a candor problem out of all proportion to the dollars involved.

**What a rational minority holder should watch next, in order:** (1) whether the FY2026 20-F moves the GMF matter into Note 25 with a quantified or explicitly-unquantifiable statement, and whether it discloses the admission and any ruling on Nu Colombia's dismissal motion; (2) the civil provision run-rate — it added US$21.762m and paid out US$14.147m in H1-2026 alone, so the full-year provision is tracking toward roughly double FY2025, and payments have already exceeded the opening balance once; (3) whether the judicial-deposit figure is corrected or explained, since which of the three published numbers is right determines whether a US$86m asset exists; and (4) the derivative notional, which more than doubled to US$5.25bn in a year against a still-small net mark of +US$14.779m — plain instruments today, worth re-reading each year.

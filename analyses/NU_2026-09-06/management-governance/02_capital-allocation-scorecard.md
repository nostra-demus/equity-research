**Company:** Nu Holdings Ltd. · **Line read:** NYSE:NU Class A ordinary shares, USD · **Date:** 2026-09-06
**Jurisdiction (CLAUDE.md §27):** Cayman Islands incorporation, US **foreign private issuer**. The annual filing is **Form 20-F**, not a 10-K; there is no DEF 14A and no 8-K. Material events surface in 6-K/press releases (captured here through the `CIQ Key Developments export`) and in the interim condensed financial statements. The absence of a US domestic form is not missing data.
**Reporting standard:** IFRS Accounting Standards as issued by the IASB. **Reporting currency: US dollars (USD).** Financial-statement figures are in **thousands of USD** in the filings; this report converts them to **millions (US$m)** and says so in each table. **Fiscal year ends 31 December.**
**Evidence base:** the supervisor-frozen extraction generation only, cited logically as `data/NU/`. No web source was used.
**Upstream inputs read:** `management-governance/01_management-and-track-record.md`; `business-model/11_capital-allocation-governance.md` (the quick-read this deepens and supersedes); `earnings/01_historical-financials.md`.

**Two basis warnings that govern every number below (CLAUDE.md §15).**
1. **There are two different operating-cash-flow bases for this company and they differ by roughly US$13bn a year.** The **company-basis (IFRS, as filed)** cash-flow statement puts the increase in customer deposits *inside* operating activities; the **Capital IQ bank-template** export moves that deposit inflow *out* of operating and into financing. Worked reconciliation, FY2025: 3,500.5 (company) − 12,861.3 (deposit inflow) = **−9,360.8**, which is exactly the CIQ figure [S9][S21]. **This report uses the company (filed) basis for every "% of CFO" figure and labels it every time.** The CIQ figure is shown alongside where relevant, never mixed into the same row.
2. **Return on invested capital in the normal `Δ NOPAT / Δ invested capital` form is not computable for this company.** There is no EBITDA, no EBIT and no interest-expense line on the bank template — `ciq_facts.json` records `ltm_ebitda_m` as `unknown` with the note "Income Statement sheet has no 'EBITDA' row" [S18]. The bank-appropriate substitute, **Δ net income to parent ÷ Δ total equity**, is used and is labelled as that substitute wherever it appears. It is not a NOPAT-based ROIC and must not be quoted as one.

The board's own announcement states the programme "will be funded using cash balances derived from retained and future earnings" [S15]. On the evidence, this buyback was paid for out of profit.
5. **The capital ratio is a real qualifier, and it is not resolved.** The Tier 1 capital ratio fell **16.2% (FY2024) → 14.4% (FY2025) → 13.4% (30 Jun 2026)**, and non-performing loans rose **6.7% (FY2023) → 7.8% → 7.9% → 8.6% of loans** over the same span [S11]. The buyback began inside that trend. Two facts cut the other way and must be given their weight: Tier 1 capital in dollars *rose* through the buyback quarter (US$4,276.4m at 31 Mar 2026 → US$4,785.8m at 30 Jun 2026) and the Tier 1 *ratio* rose from 12.8% to 13.4% in that same quarter [S14]. So the buyback did not draw the cushion down in the period it ran; the multi-year direction of the ratio is still down.
6. **No share has been cancelled.** All 40,659,600 shares sit in treasury, carrying no voting or dividend rights while held [S3]. The equity note already deducts them from shares issued and fully paid, so the per-share arithmetic above is on an ex-treasury basis. Treasury shares can be reissued; cancellation would make the reduction permanent. The announcement says the shares "will either be retained in treasury or canceled" [S15] — the choice has not been made.

## 5. Capital-Allocation Read

**Verdict: value-creative / disciplined — with the qualification that the record is short and the credit book has never been through a downturn.** On the numbers this engine can actually verify, capital has created per-share value rather than absolute size: profit retained rather than distributed has earned an incremental **29.2%** (Δ net income to parent ÷ Δ equity, FY2024 → LTM Jun-2026) against a reported return on equity that has climbed 18.2% → 31.6%, diluted earnings per share have gone from −US$0.08 (FY2022) to US$0.734 (LTM Jun-2026), and book value per share is up 72.8% in eighteen months to US$2.743.

**Best use of capital: organic reinvestment in the loan book** — US$33.7bn of gross-loan growth, roughly nine times cumulative operating cash flow, funded by deposits rather than by shareholders, at incremental returns in the high-twenties to high-thirties percent. Everything else in the capital-allocation table is a rounding error beside it. **Worst use of capital: nothing has been wasted, so the "worst" is an absence rather than an act** — acquisitions of US$131.9m over five years bought two useful regulatory licences and two products whose returns are **neither disclosed nor computable**, because the company reports a single segment; the buyback, though well priced, is a single half-year with no stated rationale; and there is no documented dividend policy telling a shareholder when, if ever, capital comes back.

**The single biggest capital-allocation signal is not the buyback — it is the arithmetic of the buyback sitting inside a falling capital ratio and a rising bad-loan ratio.** Non-performing loans have risen every year, 6.7% → 7.8% → 7.9% → 8.6% of loans, while the Tier 1 ratio has fallen 16.2% → 14.4% → 13.4%, and the company began returning capital in the middle of that trend, with US$28.8bn of undrawn credit-card limits sitting off the balance sheet as the exposure that draws hardest when customers are stressed. Today that combination is comfortably financed — Tier 1 capital in dollars rose through the buyback quarter, the repurchase was 25.9% of one half-year's profit and was paid from earnings not debt, and the loan-loss allowance stands at US$6,612.9m. **The falsification test is dated and simple: the Q3 2026 results, due November 2026, showing whether the non-performing ratio and the Tier 1 ratio move together or apart while repurchases continue.** If they diverge further, the same repurchase stops being surplus profit returned and becomes capital taken out of a thinning cushion — and this verdict moves from *value-creative* to *mixed* without a single new red flag appearing.

**Two things this report deliberately does not say.** It does not say the buyback was made below intrinsic value — this module runs before valuation and cannot know that. And it does not say the acquisitions created value — no return on any of them is disclosed or computable, and the doctrine forbids assuming one.

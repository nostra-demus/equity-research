# Management-Governance Module — UBER (Synthesis)

## Abstract

Uber's management is competent but only partly aligned with shareholders. The CEO, nine years in the seat, has a clean, checkable record of beating its own profit guidance, and capital spent building the core business earns strong returns — but capital spent buying other companies has mostly failed (two of the two largest deals with observable outcomes, Transplace and Drizly, did not deliver), and a $14.8 billion bet on Delivery Hero, funded mostly with new debt, is still pending. Executive pay cannot be assessed because the 2026 proxy statement is missing from the data pool, and insiders own only about 0.18% of the company with no open-market cash buying identified in six months. The single biggest flag: Uber quietly stopped publishing the reconciliation behind the profit number it keeps beating. Verdict: misaligned-to-weak stewardship — competent operators, not clearly aligned with minority shareholders.

## 1A. Module Disconfirmation

- **Strongest bear point:** The serial-acquirer pattern is not hypothetical — of the two largest deals with observable post-close results, Transplace/Uber Freight ($2.25bn, FY2021) has posted three straight years of revenue decline and stayed loss-making, and Drizly (~$1.1bn, FY2021) was shut down entirely within three years — and the team is now committing $14.8bn (funded mostly by a new ~€14.2bn bridge facility that roughly doubles total debt) to Delivery Hero, a company Uber already competes with, while insiders hold 0.18% of the stock and made no identifiable open-market cash purchase in six months [02, 04].
- **Strongest bull point (steelman):** The CEO has a nine-year, non-founder tenure built on a real, delivered turnaround (first positive free cash flow in 2022, sustained GAAP profitability since), a checkable four-for-four Adjusted EBITDA guidance-beat streak through Q2 FY2026, a 90%-independent board with a split chair/CEO and fully independent committees, no promoter, no dual-class stock, and a contractual pledging ban — genuinely clean structural governance, not decoration [01, 05].
- **Single killer risk:** The pending $14.8bn Delivery Hero acquisition repeating the Transplace/Drizly value-destruction pattern while roughly doubling debt — precisely as the company's headline profitability metric has become harder to independently verify because the GAAP reconciliation behind it was discontinued [02, 06].
- **Disconfirming evidence already visible:** FY2025 buybacks reduced net share count for the first time in the record (−1.9%), and incremental ROIC on the organic platform since profitability was reached (FY2023→FY2025) computes to roughly 24.6% — real evidence that capital kept inside the core business, as opposed to M&A, has been per-share value-creative [02].

## 3. Reconciliation

Two disagreements surface across the specialists, both resolved conservatively:

1. **Strong operating execution (01: 74/100) vs. capped capital allocation (02: 50/100).** Agent 01 documents a genuinely strong, checkable promise-vs-delivery record on the metric management actually guides. Agent 02 shows that the same management team's M&A record — the mechanism by which it is now deploying its largest sums of capital ($14.8bn on Delivery Hero) — has been value-destructive on the only two large deals with observable post-close results. Reconciled view: operating competence and capital-allocation discipline are not the same skill, and this module treats them separately rather than letting the strong operating score launder the weak M&A record. The capital-allocation score stands at its capped 50, not blended upward.

2. **Structurally clean board (05: 72/100) vs. thin ownership and no incentive visibility (03/04: Insufficient Data / 52/100).** Agent 05 finds a well-formed governance box — 90% independent, split chair/CEO, no promoter, no pledging. Agents 03 and 04 find that the people this board oversees have almost no personal capital riding on the stock (0.18% insider ownership, no identifiable cash buying) and that the incentive design cannot be checked at all because the proxy is missing. Reconciled view: procedural independence does not substitute for evidenced alignment — a board can be structurally sound and still oversee a management team whose comp cannot be verified as per-share-value-linked. Shareholder friendliness is capped at 60, not credited with 05's uncapped 72.

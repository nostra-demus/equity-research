Two verbatim earnings-call transcripts are in the data pool (Q1 FY2026, May-06-2026; Q2 FY2026, Aug-05-2026) [`Uber Technologies, Inc., Q1 2026 Earnings Call, May-06-2026`; `Uber Technologies, Inc., Q2 2026 Earnings Call, Aug-05-2026`]. No shareholder letters exist for this filer (Uber does not publish one) — the module rule requires "transcripts OR prior letters," and transcripts alone satisfy it, so no partial-data cap applies to this agent [`00_governance-data-triage.md`, §5]. Both transcripts are read here as primary evidence for tone and Q&A directness. `earnings/06_earnings-quality.md` is available and used for the non-GAAP cross-check. Both transcripts fall inside a beat streak (Adjusted EBITDA cleared above the guided high end in both quarters) — the pool contains no quarter in which Uber missed its own guided metric, so the sharpest test of "candor in genuinely bad times" (a guided miss) cannot be run from this data; that limitation is stated wherever it matters below rather than papered over.

**RF-DISC-002 (recurring "one-off" / aggressive non-GAAP add-backs)** — triggered on the "legal, non-income tax, and regulatory reserve changes and settlements" line: $1,123M (FY2024) and $564M (FY2025), both years disclosed, both material, both framed as an "adjustment" despite recurring. ## 3. Disclosure Transparency

Disclosure got **less transparent, not more, over the period covered by this pool** — the single biggest finding in this report. Through FY2025, Uber disclosed a full GAAP-to-Adjusted-EBITDA reconciliation table in its 10-K, itemizing every add-back (SBC, D&A, legal/regulatory reserves, restructuring, impairments) [`earnings/06_earnings-quality.md`, §4, §7]. Beginning Q1 FY2026, that consolidated reconciliation vanished from the 10-Q body entirely, and the segment-level "Segment Adjusted EBITDA" measure was replaced with "Segment Operating Income" [Q1 FY26 10-Q; Q2 FY26 10-Q: "Beginning in the first quarter of 2026, we changed our segment operating performance measure from Segment Adjusted EBITDA to Segment Operating Income"]. The term "EBITDA" does not appear anywhere in either FY2026 10-Q outside that one methodology sentence, and does not appear in either FY2026 earnings-call transcript [`earnings/01_historical-financials.md`, §4]. Yet Uber continues to guide and report quantified beats on an "EBITDA" line through the Capital IQ guidance/consensus channel — beating the guided high end by ~2.5% in both Q1 and Q2 FY2026 [`earnings/04_guidance-consensus.md`, §6] — a claim that can no longer be verified against a primary-source GAAP reconciliation, because the company stopped publishing one. `earnings/08_earnings-red-flags.md` independently found it cannot bridge the $2,085M GAAP-basis EBITDA figure derivable from the FY2026 10-Q to the $2,819M "EBITDA" figure Uber is credited with beating, a $734M (35.2%) unexplained gap, precisely because the reconciliation is gone [`earnings/08_earnings-red-flags.md`, §1]. Separately, segment economics are disclosed at a reasonable level (Mobility, Delivery, Freight operating income and margin are all broken out), and the take-rate mechanics behind the UK Mobility change were explained with real precision in the Q2 Q&A (see §1). The litigation-naming gap described in §1 is the second transparency concern: a nationwide MDL with two adverse jury verdicts is not named in the "material legal proceedings" list of three consecutive filings, while smaller matters are named specifically.

**RF-DISC-001 (commentary contradicting the numbers)** — triggered. Management (via the guidance process and Capital IQ consensus channel) continues to make specific, quantified "beat guidance" claims on an EBITDA-type metric whose GAAP reconciliation the company itself stopped publishing starting Q1 FY2026 — the commentary about that metric can no longer be checked against a primary-source bridge to GAAP. Severity: High (materiality: this is the company's single most-cited profitability metric, used in every guidance cycle and in the promise-vs-delivery record scored in `01_management-and-track-record.md`).

**RF-REG-002 (delayed results / material-disclosure timeliness)** — triggered. The Arizona jury verdict ($8.5M, Feb-06-2026) landed one week before the FY25 10-K was filed (Feb-13-2026) and is not named in that filing's Legal Proceedings item; the Charlotte verdict ($5,000, Apr-20-2026) landed before the Q1 FY26 10-Q (filed May-06-2026) and is likewise not named there, nor in the subsequent Q2 FY26 10-Q. Severity: Medium (the aggregate legal reserve figure is disclosed and each individual verdict is small relative to the $1.8–2.1bn reserve, so this is a naming/specificity gap rather than a concealment of financial exposure).

## 4. Tone in Bad Times

Neither available call covers a genuine "bad quarter" — both Q1 and Q2 FY2026 beat Uber's own Adjusted-EBITDA-type guidance above the high end, so the sharpest test (owning an actual guided miss) cannot be run from this pool; that is a real limit on this section's conclusions, not glossed over. Prepared remarks in both calls are consistently promotional in register: Q2 opened with "Q2 was another outstanding quarter for Uber with record audience and engagement... record cash generation," and the pending Delivery Hero deal was framed with "we believe this deal will create significant long-term shareholder value" — a forward claim with no delivered evidence yet (the deal has not closed) [Q2 FY26 transcript, prepared remarks]. On the one occasion a headline number looked negative on its face — the ~500bps mobility take-rate decline — the CFO answered with an exact, checkable attribution (400 of 500bps mechanical/UK, not a real margin deterioration) rather than a vague "headwind" [Q2 FY26 transcript, Q&A]. That is a real, positive, specific data point on directness. Litigation matters (the MDL verdicts) were not raised by any analyst and were not volunteered by management on either call — an absence, not an evasive answer to a direct question, so it cannot be scored as Q&A deflection, but it does mean the calls contain zero acknowledgment of the company's highest-profile ongoing legal risk in the period covered.

## 5. Candor Read

Management is specific and quantitatively direct when questioned on operating mechanics it chooses to discuss — the CFO's exact-basis-point walk of the UK take-rate "decline" is a real candor signal, not boilerplate, and the CFO-transition statement ruling out accounting disputes has held up against the subsequent record. Set against that: the company discontinued its own GAAP-to-Adjusted-EBITDA reconciliation in FY2026 even as it kept claiming quantified guidance beats on that exact metric, a "one-off" legal/regulatory non-GAAP add-back has recurred at material size for two straight years, and a nationwide sexual-assault MDL with two adverse jury verdicts is absent from the named legal proceedings in three consecutive filings while smaller matters are named specifically. None of this is fabrication — every hard number checked against a primary filing was there to be checked, and the aggregate legal reserve is disclosed — but the pattern is one of selective specificity: precise and quantified on metrics management wants to explain, and quietly less verifiable or less named on metrics and matters it does not. **Verdict: mixed.** The single biggest candor signal is the vanished EBITDA reconciliation — a company cannot be graded "candid" for beating its own guidance on a profitability metric it has simultaneously made harder for outside readers to independently verify from the primary filing. This module found no unresolved adverse integrity signal about current senior management (per `01_management-and-track-record.md` §4A and `business-model/01_disqualifier-scan.md` §3, which cleared the routed "buzz" as legacy, pre-Khosrowshahi conduct) — the disclosure concerns in this report are about candor and completeness of current-period reporting, not about executive integrity, and are scored on that narrower basis.

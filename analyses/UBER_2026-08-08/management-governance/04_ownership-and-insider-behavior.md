All ownership and insider-transaction figures below are cited to this pool document; confidence is capped at 3/5 (vendor-aggregated, not the primary SEC filing) rather than the 5/5 a DEF 14A or raw Form 4 would carry. That is a corporate balance-sheet financing matter, not insider personal-holding pledging, and belongs to the balance-sheet-survival module, not this one — flagged here only to avoid conflation.

## 5. Ownership / Insider Read

Insiders own a rounding error of Uber — about 0.18% of shares outstanding, and the CEO's own $87.8 million stake is just 0.06% of the company. Nearly every insider "purchase" in the trailing six months is a $0-value RSU vesting event rather than a cash buy, so this is compensation cycling through the register, not conviction; no officer or director made an identifiable open-market cash purchase in the period, and insiders were modest net sellers (down 33,973 shares over six months) in a pattern that looks scheduled rather than opportunistic. The biggest risk to minority holders here is not control or pledging — Uber has one class of stock, no promoter, and a contractual pledging ban — it is that the people running the company have almost no personal capital riding on the per-share outcome, which puts the weight of alignment entirely on incentive design (module 03) rather than on ownership (this module).

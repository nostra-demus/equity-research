## 3. Stated Strategy & Targets

Management describes the current phase as extending "the proven strategy that has underpinned our growth for years" along two fronts: (1) geographic and category expansion — most concretely the pending acquisition of a further 63.21% of Delivery Hero (bringing Uber's economic interest toward 100%) for approximately $14.8 billion, which management says will "expand our reach to nearly 100 markets" by roughly doubling the number of markets where Uber can run its full mobility-plus-delivery platform; and (2) autonomous vehicles, where the CEO's stated ambition is "to become the world's leading commercialization platform for autonomous vehicles" by partnering with multiple AV technology providers (Waymo, Wayve, Zoox, and others) rather than building the technology itself [Q2 FY26 transcript, prepared remarks — Dara Khosrowshahi]. On the numbers side, Uber's only current formal, checkable quarterly guidance covers Adjusted EBITDA and (since Q1 FY26) EPS Normalized; it does not guide revenue, capex, or free cash flow as ranges [earnings/04_guidance-consensus.md]. CEO commentary at the Q2 FY26 print pointed to Gross Bookings growth above 20% for a fourth consecutive quarter and trailing-12-month free cash flow exceeding $10 billion for the first time — offered as evidence the growth-plus-margin-expansion strategy is working, not as a forward numeric target [Q2 FY26 transcript, prepared remarks].

## 5. Management Read

The CEO seat has been stable for nine years under a professional operator brought in specifically to clean up founder-era governance failures, and the record since then — IPO execution, the 2022 shift to positive free cash flow, and now four consecutive quarters of Adjusted EBITDA guidance beaten (two of them above the guided high end by a near-identical margin) — is a checkable pattern of promises kept, not a narrative. The CFO seat has turned over twice in about eight years, most recently in February 2026, and a second senior departure (the Chief People Officer) landed three months later; both changes were internal promotions handled with advance notice and explicit statements ruling out accounting disputes, so this reads as a stability signal to monitor rather than a red flag. The single biggest management signal, positive or negative, is the now-pending $14.8 billion Delivery Hero acquisition: it is being pursued by a management team with a genuinely strong operating and guidance-delivery record, but it is also the clearest test yet of whether that same team allocates capital as disciplined as it operates — a question this agent does not score (see `business-model/11_capital-allocation-governance.md`, which already caps the capital-allocation score for the serial-acquirer pattern) but that materially conditions how much credit the operating track record above should be given going forward.

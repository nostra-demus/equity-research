## What This Module Found

The CEO has run Uber for nine years and has a checkable record of beating the company's own profit guidance — Adjusted EBITDA (a profit measure that strips out interest, tax, depreciation and some other items) beaten four quarters in a row, twice above the guided high end by about 2.5% [01, earnings/04]. Money kept inside the core business has paid off: incremental return on invested capital (ROIC — the profit earned on each new dollar put to work) was roughly 24.6% from FY2023 to FY2025, and FY2025 buybacks cut the share count for the first time, by 1.9% [02].

The single most important driver of the weak verdict is the opposite side of that ledger: money spent buying other companies. The two largest deals with visible results both failed — Transplace/Uber Freight ($2.25bn, still loss-making at −$33mm FY2025 EBITDA after three years of declining revenue) and Drizly (~$1.1bn, shut down within three years) [02]. The team is now committing $14.8bn to buy Delivery Hero — a company Uber already competes with — funded mostly by a new ~€14.2bn bridge loan that roughly doubles total debt, and requiring a $1.6bn forced sale of assets [02]. That pattern tripped the §24 serial-acquirer filter and capped the capital-allocation score.

The single most important risk is that this largest-ever bet repeats the Transplace/Drizly outcome — and it lands just as the headline profit number got harder to check. Uber stopped publishing the reconciliation from GAAP (audited, standardised accounting) to Adjusted EBITDA starting Q1 FY2026, while still claiming quantified beats on that exact metric; a sister module found a $734mm (35.2%) gap that can no longer be bridged from the filing [06, RF-DISC-001].

Two things cannot be judged at all. Executive pay is Insufficient Data — the 2026 proxy statement (the document that shows how bosses are paid) is missing from the data pool, so no bonus or long-term-incentive metric is checkable [03]. And insiders own only about 0.18% of the company (the CEO 0.060%, worth $87.8mm), with no open-market cash buying found in six months and modest net selling [04]. The synthesis is explicit: read this absence of evidence as a genuine unknown, not a clean bill of health.

## The Specialists, Briefly

- **Governance data triage (00):** Partial sufficiency — most inputs present, but the 2026 proxy is genuinely absent, which alone blocks the pay, ownership-percentage, and insider-history reads.
- **Management & track record (01):** Strong, 74/100 — stable, delivering team; four straight Adjusted EBITDA beats. CFO and Chief Product Officer both left within three months of each other in 2026 (rated Amber, watch, not Red).
- **Capital allocation (02):** Mixed, capped at 50/100 — ~24.6% return on money reinvested in the core, but Transplace and Drizly both failed and the $14.8bn Delivery Hero deal roughly doubles debt.
- **Incentives & compensation (03):** Insufficient Data — every pay item in the 10-K points to the missing proxy; nothing is extractable.
- **Ownership & insider behavior (04):** 52/100 — thin skin in the game (0.18%), no cash buying, modest net selling, no pledging (contractually banned).
- **Board & shareholder rights (05):** 72/100 — 90% independent board, split chair/CEO, fully independent committees, but no special-meeting right, no written consent, no cumulative voting, and a July 2026 derivative lawsuit naming the entire board over an alleged sexual-assault-reporting gap (12,522 disclosed vs. 400,181 alleged).
- **Candor & disclosure (06):** Mixed, 51/100 — specific answers on questions asked, but the discontinued EBITDA reconciliation and an unnamed 3,000+-case litigation matter pull it down.

**Disagreements the synthesis resolved (not re-adjudicated here):** Strong operating execution (01: 74) was not allowed to launder the weak M&A record — capital allocation stands at its capped 50, not blended upward. And the structurally clean board (05: 72) was not allowed to stand in for evidenced alignment — shareholder friendliness is capped at 60 because the people the board oversees have almost no personal capital in the stock and their pay cannot be checked.

## What Would Change This Read

**Would strengthen it:** (1) the 2026 proxy shows bonus/long-term pay tied to ROIC, EPS, free-cash-flow-per-share or total shareholder return rather than revenue or deal count; (2) insiders start buying stock with their own cash; (3) Delivery Hero closes and delivers its promised accretion without repeating Transplace/Drizly; (4) a GAAP-to-Adjusted-EBITDA reconciliation returns in the FY2026 10-K.

**Would weaken it:** (1) a third failed large acquisition; (2) the derivative lawsuit survives a motion to dismiss with evidence supporting the alleged reporting gap; (3) further insider selling ahead of a weak result; (4) a formal restatement or internal-controls weakness.

**Data needed:** the 2026 DEF 14A proxy (pay tables and metrics, exact ownership %, insider-transaction history, AGM vote tallies, related-party policy); Delivery Hero post-close integration disclosure; the next 10-K's legal-proceedings naming and non-GAAP reconciliation.

---

## Bottom Line

- **Verdict: Misaligned or weak stewardship** — competent operators, not clearly aligned with minority shareholders. Governance Rating: Weak (46/100; confidence-adjusted 27/100).
- **Could be better than it looks:** the core business genuinely compounds — ~24.6% incremental ROIC since 2023, and FY2025 was the first year buybacks actually shrank the share count (−1.9%).
- **Could be worse than it looks:** the largest capital bet in company history ($14.8bn Delivery Hero, mostly debt-funded) uses the same debt-funded, competitor-overlapping M&A playbook that failed the two other times it was tried at scale.
- **What's missing:** executive pay and exact ownership cannot be assessed at all — the 2026 proxy is absent. Treat this as a real unknown, not a clearance.
- **Watch next:** whether the withdrawn GAAP-to-Adjusted-EBITDA reconciliation returns in the FY2026 10-K, and the first outcome of the board-wide derivative lawsuit filed July 2026.

## Plain-English Glossary

- **Adjusted EBITDA:** a profit measure that removes interest, tax, depreciation and certain other costs; the number Uber guides to and is judged against.
- **GAAP:** standardised, audited accounting rules — the baseline that the adjusted number is supposed to reconcile back to.
- **ROIC (return on invested capital):** the profit earned on each dollar of money put into the business.
- **M&A:** mergers and acquisitions — buying other companies.
- **Proxy statement (DEF 14A):** the annual filing that discloses how executives are paid and who owns the stock.
- **Bridge loan:** short-term borrowing used to fund a deal before permanent financing is arranged.
- **Buyback:** a company using cash to repurchase its own shares.
- **Derivative lawsuit:** a suit brought on the company's behalf against its own directors for alleged failures of duty.

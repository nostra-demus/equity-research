# Incentives & Compensation — UBER

**Partial-data notice.** No compensation disclosure (proxy / DEF 14A) is present in this data pool. Uber's FY2025 10-K explicitly incorporates every governance/comp item by reference to a document not in the pool: Item 11 (Executive Compensation), Item 12 (Security Ownership / Equity Compensation Plan Information), Item 13 (Related-Person Transactions), and Item 14 (Accounting Fees) are all stated as "included... in the 2026 Proxy Statement and... incorporated herein by reference" [FY25 10-K, Items 11–14, p. ITEM 11/12/13/14 section]. That Proxy Statement (a DEF 14A/DEFR14A was filed with the SEC in April 2026, per a web search) was not ingested into this pool and is therefore not available as a citable source under this module's evidence rules. Per this agent's partial-data rule, **incentive alignment cannot be assessed** from the pool. What follows extracts the only high-level structure the 10-K itself discloses (equity-plan mechanics and company-wide stock-based compensation expense — not NEO-specific pay) and uses the web strictly for peer-comparison context, labeled as such. No metric weights are fabricated or asserted for Uber.

Reporting currency would be USD; reporting period would be FY2025 (calendar year ended Dec 31, 2025) under a 2026 proxy — both stated for completeness, not as evidence of actual disclosed figures, since no Summary Compensation Table is present in this pool.

**What the 10-K does disclose (equity-plan mechanics, not individual pay):** Uber grants awards under the 2019 Equity Incentive Plan and the 2019 Employee Stock Purchase Plan (ESPP), covering "officers and other employees, directors, and consultants" — not broken out by named executive [FY25 10-K, Note 10 – Stockholders' Equity]. Three award types exist: (1) **service-based** RSUs/options vesting over a multi-year requisite service period; (2) **performance-based** awards (RSAs, RSUs, SARs, options) that vest on both service and "specified performance targets, such as our financial or operating metrics" — the specific metrics are not named in the 10-K; and (3) **market-based** awards that vest on "achievement of specified fully-diluted equity values, as determined based on our stock price," valued using a Monte Carlo model [FY25 10-K, Note 10, Stock-Based Compensation Expense section]. The 10-K states market-based awards granted in FY2023–FY2025 were "not material," implying the legacy market-based structure (economically a form of total-shareholder-return/stock-price hurdle) is a smaller, possibly legacy, component of the current program rather than its core [FY25 10-K, Note 10]. Total fair value of RSUs vested was $1.7 billion in each of FY2023, FY2024, and FY2025 (company-wide, all employees) [FY25 10-K, Note 10]. Total stock-based compensation expense by function: FY2023 $1,935mm, FY2024 $1,796mm, FY2025 $1,826mm (company-wide, all cost centers combined — not CEO/CFO-specific) [FY25 10-K, Note 10, Stock-Based Compensation Expense table].

## 4. Incentive Alignment Read

Incentive alignment cannot be assessed from the data available in this pool: the 10-K's Items 11–14 all point to the 2026 Proxy Statement for the actual pay tables, bonus/LTIP metrics, weights, targets, ownership, and say-on-pay results, and that document was not ingested. The only structural facts the 10-K itself discloses are that Uber's long-term equity program mixes pure time-vesting awards with performance-based awards tied to unnamed "financial or operating metrics" and a smaller, currently "not material" stock-price-hurdle (market-based) tranche — which is not enough to classify the program as per-share/returns-based or size-based with any confidence. The single biggest incentive signal available is the absence of the primary evidence itself: without the Compensation Discussion & Analysis, this agent cannot say whether Khosrowshahi and Krishnamurthy are paid on revenue/deal growth (empire-building) or on ROIC/EPS/FCF-per-share/TSR (per-share value) — that is the single highest-value follow-up data request for this module. **Conclusion: Insufficient data — not assessable** (neither "well-aligned," "mixed," nor "misaligned" can be supported by evidence in this pool; do not read the absence of red flags here as a clean bill of health).

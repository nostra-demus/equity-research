# Segment Map — UBER

## 1. Segment Table

Figures below are FY2025 (year ended Dec 31, 2025), the latest audited annual period. Revenue and profit shares are computed on the three reportable segments only; a small Corporate G&A / Platform R&D cost line (not a segment) sits below segment profit and is covered in Section 3.

Revenue shares sum to 100.0% ($29,670M + $17,248M + $5,099M = $52,017M total revenue). Profit shares are computed against total segment Adjusted EBITDA of $11,438M (Mobility + Delivery + Freight, before the Corporate G&A / Platform R&D deduction of -$2,708M that brings the company to consolidated Adjusted EBITDA of $8,730M) [FY25 10-K, Note 13, p.113–114]. There is no disclosed "Other" revenue bucket in the current three-segment structure — total segment revenue reconciles exactly to consolidated revenue.

Most recent quarter for directional context (three months ended June 30, 2026, unaudited): Mobility revenue $7,363M (51.9% of $14,191M quarterly total), Delivery $5,245M (37.0%), Freight $1,583M (11.2%); Segment Operating Income (the new profit measure — see Section 3) of $2,215M / $1,055M / -$24M respectively, i.e. Mobility 68.2% and Delivery 32.5% of the $3,246M segment total [Q2 FY26 10-Q, Note 10, and MD&A "Segment Results of Operations"]. Delivery's revenue share has risen roughly 4 points versus the FY25 full-year mix (33.2% → 37.0%) while Mobility's has fallen a similar amount (57.0% → 51.9%) — a real but still early mix shift, not yet large enough to change which segment dominates.

## 2. Dominant Segment

Mobility is the dominant segment on both counts: 57.0% of FY25 revenue and 69.1% of FY25 total segment Adjusted EBITDA ($7,899M of $11,438M) [FY25 10-K, Note 13, p.112–114; MD&A "Segment Results of Operations," p.57–58]. Because profit and revenue agree on the same segment, there is no conflict to resolve. Mobility's segment Adjusted EBITDA of $7,899M on its own exceeds Uber's entire consolidated Adjusted EBITDA of $8,730M — meaning Mobility alone funds all of the company's unallocated Corporate G&A and Platform R&D spend ($2,708M) plus the whole of consolidated profit, while Delivery and Freight net out to roughly breakeven against that overhead once Freight's small loss is included. Uber is not a single-segment business — Mobility is 57%, below the >85% threshold for calling this effectively one business — but Delivery is a real, growing second engine (31.2% of segment profit, 45% Adjusted-EBITDA growth in FY25) while Freight is currently a profit drag, not a contributor.

## 3. Segment Disclosure Quality

Segment definitions have been stable as Mobility / Delivery / Freight since at least FY2023 and remain unchanged through the Q2 FY26 10-Q [FY25 10-K, Note 13, p.112; Q2 FY26 10-Q, Note 10]. There is no meaningful "Other" or unallocated-revenue bucket — all revenue rolls up into the three named segments — but there is a material unallocated cost bucket: Corporate G&A and Platform R&D of $2,708M in FY25, equal to 23.7% of total segment Adjusted EBITDA ($11,438M), that is not attributed to any segment [FY25 10-K, Note 13, p.113]. Profit is disclosed at the segment level, not just revenue, but the profit metric itself changed within the last three years: beginning Q1 FY26, Uber switched its segment operating-performance measure from "Segment Adjusted EBITDA" (used through FY25) to "Segment Operating Income," and recast the comparable prior-year period on the new basis [Q2 FY26 10-Q, Note 10, "Beginning in the first quarter of 2026, we changed our segment operating performance measure from Segment Adjusted EBITDA to Segment Operating Income"]. The two measures are not identical (Segment Operating Income appears to include D&A and other items excluded from Adjusted EBITDA), so FY25 segment Adjusted EBITDA in this table and Q2 FY26 Segment Operating Income above are not strictly comparable margin-for-margin — a downstream user (`unit-economics`, `competitive-map`) should treat any FY25-vs-FY26 segment margin comparison with that caveat. Separately, the company does not disclose segment-level balance-sheet assets: "The CODM does not evaluate operating segments using asset information and, accordingly, we do not report asset information by segment" [FY25 10-K, Note 13, p.112], so segment-level capital-intensity or ROIC cannot be computed from filings directly; the "Low" capital-intensity call above rests on the business-model description (asset-light marketplace, no owned vehicle/truck fleet), not on a disclosed asset base per segment.

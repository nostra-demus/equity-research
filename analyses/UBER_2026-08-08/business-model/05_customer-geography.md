## 4. Read

This is a geography-concentrated business, not a customer-concentrated one. On the customer side, concentration is essentially absent by design — Uber's own audited filing states no customer reached 10% of revenue in any of the last three fiscal years, because revenue comes from millions of individual, one-off consumer transactions (FY25 10-K, Concentration of Credit Risk note). On the geography side, the United States and the United Kingdom together are 66.1% of FY2025 revenue ($34,380M of $52,017M), and the UK's share has been rising each year (17.5% → 19.0% → 20.4%, FY23–FY25). This concentration is naked, not contractually secured: Uber has no long-term revenue contracts with consumers or merchants in either market — every dollar is a discrete, cancellable transaction, so a regulatory shock (e.g., a driver-classification ruling) or a strong local competitor in the US or UK has nothing standing between it and Uber's reported revenue. The single biggest dependency the synthesizer should know: the United States alone is still 45.7% of global revenue and its share, while declining, remains the largest block Uber has — any US-specific regulatory, labor-classification, or demand disruption is the one geographic event capable of moving the whole company's results.

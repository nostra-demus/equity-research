# Competitive Map — UBER

## 1. Dominant Segment

Mobility (ride-hailing) is the dominant segment: 57.0% of FY2025 revenue ($29,670M of $52,017M) and 69.1% of FY2025 total segment Adjusted EBITDA ($7,899M of $11,438M) [upstream: `03_segment-map.md`, citing FY25 10-K, Note 13, p.112–114].

## 2. Named Competitors

Uber's own FY25 10-K names its Mobility competitors explicitly: "We also compete with other ridesharing companies for Drivers and Riders, including Bolt, Didi, Lyft, and Ola," plus autonomous-vehicle developers Alphabet (Waymo), Amazon (Zoox), and Tesla [FY25 10-K, Item 1, "Competitive Environment," p.5]. Of the four named ridesharing rivals, Lyft and DiDi Global are public with disclosed financials; Ola (ANI Technologies, India) is privately held with no financials in this data pool; Bolt (Bolt Technology OÜ, Estonia) is privately held but discloses annual results via press statements. The three most credible — a comparable-scale public peer, a global-scale public peer, and a named regional rival — are profiled below.

### Competitor A — Lyft, Inc.

- **Ticker / listing:** NasdaqGS: LYFT
- **Where they compete:** US and Canada ridesharing marketplace (peer-to-peer rides, Express Drive vehicle rental, bikes/scooters); no meaningful Delivery or Freight overlap [Capital IQ Comp export, Business Description sheet, as-of 2026-08-06].
- **Scale:** LTM total revenue $6,516.6M (filing date 2026-05-08), roughly one-eighth of Uber's LTM total revenue of $55,227M over the same comp window [Capital IQ Comparable Analysis export, Financial Data sheet, as-of 2026-08-06]. LTM revenue grew 9.36% year-over-year, versus 16.68% for Uber over the same comp window [Capital IQ Comparable Analysis export, Operating Statistics sheet, as-of 2026-08-06].
- **Profitability / return on capital:** LTM EBITDA margin -0.1% (EBITDA -$6.7M on $6,516.6M revenue) and LTM EBIT margin -2.2% (EBIT -$145M) [Capital IQ Comparable Analysis export, Financial Data and Operating Statistics sheets, as-of 2026-08-06]. LTM net income margin is reported at 43.8% in the same export — this is inconsistent with a negative EBIT and is very likely a one-off item (e.g., a tax-related gain), which the export does not itemize; treat the net-margin figure as unreliable for operating comparison and not from filings-level detail. ROIC/ROE: not disclosed in this data pool.
- **Source named in:** FY25 10-K, Item 1, "Competitive Environment," p.5 (named directly); also Uber's own Capital IQ comparable-company set [Capital IQ Comparable Analysis export, Business Description sheet].
- **One-line read:** The closest same-business, same-geography peer — a smaller, thinner-margin US/Canada rideshare pure-play that Uber outgrew on revenue over the trailing year (16.68% vs. 9.36%) [Capital IQ Comparable Analysis export, Operating Statistics sheet, as-of 2026-08-06].

### Competitor B — DiDi Global Inc.

- **Ticker / listing:** OTCPK: DIDI.Y (delisted from NYSE in 2021; trades over-the-counter)
- **Where they compete:** Ride-hailing and related mobility services in China (China Mobility segment), plus an International segment covering Brazil, Mexico, and other markets — geographic overlap with Uber mainly in Latin America, not China [Capital IQ Comparable Analysis export, Business Description sheet, as-of 2026-08-06]. Note: Uber itself holds a minority equity stake in Didi, taken when Uber sold its China ride-hailing business to Didi in 2016 — the two are simultaneously named competitors and Uber is a minority shareholder in Didi [FY25 10-K, Item 1A Risk Factors, p. (line ref ~3095), "a significant portion of our assets includes minority ownership positions, including in Didi, Grab, and Aurora"].
- **Scale:** LTM total revenue $34,391.3M (filing date 2026-06-02) — larger than Uber's LTM total revenue of $55,227M is not the case; DiDi's LTM revenue is about 62% of Uber's consolidated LTM revenue, and roughly 116% of Uber's Mobility-segment-only FY25 revenue ($29,670M), reflecting DiDi's own China-scale ride volume [Capital IQ Comparable Analysis export, Financial Data sheet, as-of 2026-08-06]. LTM revenue grew 10.04% year-over-year [Capital IQ Comparable Analysis export, Operating Statistics sheet, as-of 2026-08-06].
- **Profitability / return on capital:** LTM EBITDA margin -1.6% (EBITDA -$564.3M), LTM EBIT margin -2.6% (EBIT -$910M), LTM net income margin -1.1% [Capital IQ Comparable Analysis export, Operating Statistics sheet, as-of 2026-08-06]. ROIC/ROE: not disclosed in this data pool — DiDi has not reported a positive operating profit in this comp window, so a return-on-capital figure would be negative and is not separately broken out.
- **Source named in:** FY25 10-K, Item 1, "Competitive Environment," p.5 (named directly as a Mobility competitor); also disclosed as a minority-investment holding in FY25 10-K, Item 1A Risk Factors.
- **One-line read:** Global-scale by revenue but structurally unprofitable over the trailing year, and only a partial competitor to Uber in practice — the two compete in Latin America while Uber holds a minority stake in Didi's dominant China business rather than contesting it directly.

### Competitor C — Bolt Technology OÜ

- **Ticker / listing:** Private (Estonia) — no public listing, no SEC/exchange filings.
- **Where they compete:** Ridesharing and micromobility marketplace focused on Europe, Africa, and parts of Latin America — a regional rival rather than a global-scale one [FY25 10-K, Item 1, "Competitive Environment," p.5, named alongside Didi, Lyft, and Ola].
- **Scale:** Reported 2025 revenue of €2.27 billion (up 14% year-over-year), equivalent to roughly $2.6 billion at the 2026-08-08 EUR/USD spot rate of ~1.155 [Web: "Bolt hits €2.27 billion in revenue, posts first-ever annual profit," Trade with Estonia, 2026 (unverified, dated); FX rate: Federal Reserve H.10, 2026-08-06]. This is roughly one-eleventh of Uber's Mobility-segment-only FY25 revenue ($29,670M) — outside the ~5x comparable-scale guideline, but included as a named, clear regional rival per the selection criteria rather than for scale comparability.
- **Profitability / return on capital:** Reported 2025 operating profit of €19.5 million (operating margin ~0.9%), reversing an €87.7 million operating loss in 2024, and a net profit of €0.9 million — Bolt's first profitable full year since founding in 2013 [Web: "Bolt posts first-ever profit in 2025," Zag Daily, 2026 (unverified, dated)]. ROIC/ROE: not public / not disclosed — Bolt does not publish a balance sheet.
- **Source named in:** FY25 10-K, Item 1, "Competitive Environment," p.5 (named directly).
- **One-line read:** A much smaller, only-just-profitable regional challenger in markets (Europe, Africa) where Uber's own risk factors admit it does "not have a leading category position" in every geography.

## 3. Competitive Position

Position vs peers: **Holding to gaining in the US, not disclosed globally.** In the US — Uber's home market and the one segment with third-party share data in this pool — Uber holds an estimated 76% of the US rideshare market versus Lyft's 24% [Web: Bloomberg Second Measure, US rideshare data, 2026 (unverified, dated)]; Uber's own FY25 10-K discloses no numeric market-share figure, only a qualitative claim to "global leadership position" [FY25 10-K, Item 1, p.4] alongside an explicit admission that "in certain geographic markets and regions, we do not have a leading category position" [FY25 10-K, Item 1A Risk Factors, p. (line ref ~2577)]. As a growth proxy, Uber's LTM total revenue grew 16.68% versus 9.36% for Lyft and 10.04% for DiDi over the same trailing window [Capital IQ Comparable Analysis export, Operating Statistics sheet, as-of 2026-08-06] — consistent with share stability-to-gains against its two named public peers, though the comparison blends currency and cross-segment effects and is not a clean like-for-like Mobility-only read. Outside the US, position is **not disclosed**: Uber does not compete head-to-head with Didi in China or with Grab in Southeast Asia (Uber exited both markets via 2016 and 2018 stock-swap deals and now holds minority equity stakes in each instead of operating there directly) [FY25 10-K, Item 1A Risk Factors, p. (line ref ~3095)], so no consolidated global share figure is meaningful.

## 4. Competitive Shape

Uber's own 10-K frames the market as globally fragmented with low barriers to entry: "We compete on a global basis in highly fragmented markets... in the future we expect to face competition from new market entrants given the low barriers to entry that characterize these industries" [FY25 10-K, Item 1, "Competitive Environment," p.5]. In practice the shape is better described as **regionally concentrated, globally fragmented**: the US is close to a duopoly (Uber ~76% / Lyft ~24%) [Web: Bloomberg Second Measure, 2026 (unverified, dated)]; China is dominated by Didi, where Uber holds a minority stake rather than competing directly; Southeast Asia is dominated by Grab, on the same minority-stake basis; and Europe/Africa/Latin America remain more fragmented, with Bolt as the largest independent regional challenger alongside Uber, Didi (in Brazil/Mexico), and local players (e.g., Ola in India, not profiled above for lack of public financials). A separate but adjacent signal of ongoing consolidation: Uber has agreed to acquire a majority stake in Delivery Hero SE — one of the companies its own 10-K names as a Delivery competitor — for an equity value of roughly €12.9 billion ($14.8 billion), expected to close in the second half of 2027, explicitly described in deal reporting as a move to "better compete with DoorDash Inc. outside the US" [Uber-Technologies-Inc-NYSE-UBER-Key-Developments.txt, M&A announcement 2026-07-16 / pre-deal reporting]. This is a Delivery-segment, not Mobility-segment, development, but it signals that Uber is actively consolidating named competitors rather than only competing against them.

## 5. Caveat

Ola (ANI Technologies), the fourth Mobility competitor named directly in Uber's FY25 10-K, is privately held with no financial disclosures in this data pool; it could not be profiled with sourced scale or profitability figures and is therefore omitted from the three-competitor list above rather than profiled with invented numbers. The Bolt and Bloomberg Second Measure figures used in Sections 2–4 are dated web sources (Tier 10 in the source hierarchy) rather than filings or Capital IQ exports, and are labelled accordingly — they should be treated as directional, not audited. DiDi's segment-level China vs. International revenue split (needed to isolate the actual overlap with Uber in Latin America) is not broken out in the Capital IQ comp export available in this pool.

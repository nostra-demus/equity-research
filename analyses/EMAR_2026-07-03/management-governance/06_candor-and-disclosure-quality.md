## 2. Non-GAAP / Adjustment Aggressiveness

| Signal | Detail | Source |

The net cash definition and headline FCF require investor interpretation but are not manipulative. This is a higher-quality disclosure posture than most regional peers on the adjustments dimension.

## 3. Disclosure Transparency

Emaar provides good structural segment disclosure: four operating segments (UAE Development, International Development, Emaar Malls, Emaar Hospitality) each reported with revenue, gross profit, EBITDA, and net profit in its preliminary annual reports, and with KPIs (units sold, units delivered, backlog, occupancy, WALT) that are consistent period-over-period. The UAE Development slide is particularly detailed — project-level disclosure (Slide 19 of the FY2025 deck shows each development's units, area, % sold, % collected, gross margin achieved, and remaining GFA by type). This level of granularity is above average for the sector.

The two disclosure gaps that matter most are: (a) the English-language audited annual report is not yet filed for FY2025 as of the analysis date (July 3, 2026), and the FY2024 audited annual report (filed March 14, 2025) is in Arabic only in the data pool — meaning English-language investors have access to preliminary (unaudited) results and investor presentations but not to audited notes, related-party transaction disclosures, accounting policy changes, or auditor communications in English; (b) the RPT note and details of Alabbar's Eagle Hills relationship are accessible only in Arabic, making an English-language conflict-of-interest read impossible from available pool sources. These are not retrograde changes — the regime has been the same throughout — but they represent a structural gap in accessibility. There is no evidence that disclosure has become less transparent over time; if anything, the KPI set in the deck has expanded. However, the partial-English-disclosure structure means Emaar's de facto English disclosure is less complete than an SEC-registered or fully bilingual issuer's disclosure would be.

## 4. Tone in Bad Times

The FY2025 preliminary annual report (the worst period for the international segment, released Feb 12, 2026) is the right test. The cover slide reads "Prepared for the Future — Built on Strength, Prudence & Resilience." The consolidated results slide leads with "2025: Another record year with highest ever Sales, Revenue and Profitability." The Alabbar quote in the Q1 2025 press release (April 8, 2026, Arabic-language version) emphasised "new standards of operational excellence" and "sustainable momentum." No English-language CEO/MD statement in any filing specifically addressed Emaar Misr's margin collapse, the unexplained CFO departure, or the risks attached to the AED 200 bn masterplan launch at a cycle peak. The tone is uniformly promotional across every period reviewed — the FY2022 press release (Feb 14, 2023, a year of EPS underperformance vs estimates) was similarly positioned around record property sales, with Alabbar's quote closing on a forward-looking positive commitment. No earnings call transcripts are available in the data pool, so Q&A behaviour under analyst questioning cannot be assessed. Management appears candid in the sense that numbers are reported without manipulation; but the narrative framing in the worst period actively foregrounded positives and marginalised the one significant operational failure.

---

## 5. Candor Read

Emaar's financial numbers are reported honestly — the IFRS cash flow statement discloses buyer advance payments, the investor presentation discloses the EBITDA adjustment and provides project-level detail, and no evidence of accounting manipulation is present. On that narrow definition, management is reporting truthfully. But the higher standard for candor — owning misses, foregrounding problems, giving investors the information they need to monitor what went wrong — is not met. The Emaar Misr income collapse (EBITDA −69%, net profit −65% in AED terms in one year) was disclosed in a table on one slide, immediately followed by a banner reading "Local Market Leader, Delivering Superior Growth," with bullets emphasising sales growth and backlog size. No management quote anywhere in the English-language data pool addressed this collapse, explained its cause, or committed to a recovery path. The AED 200 bn masterplan announcement offers no phased targets, no pre-sale gates, and no cost-commitment schedule — management is asking investors to trust a plan of unprecedented scale with no checkable evidence. These two patterns — burying a large operational failure under promotional framing, and announcing a major commitment without accountability detail — are the clearest candor signals in this dataset. The absence of formal guidance also structurally limits accountability: with no numerical forward commitments, there is nothing specific to own when outcomes disappoint. The one area where management is clearly above average on transparency is structural segment disclosure and KPI consistency, which protects the verdict from a "low-trust" rating.

**Verdict: mixed.** Management discloses numbers without manipulation and provides above-average structural transparency, but it does not own operational misses, uses persistently promotional tone even in adverse periods, and announces major commitments without any accountability detail.

**Key RPT observations:**

The absence of an English-language RPT note is the single largest governance data gap in this pool. However, three structural RPT-risk channels are visible from English disclosures:

1. **Government-counterparty channel.** The dominant shareholder (Dubai Holding / Emirates Power Investment LLC, 29.73%) is a government entity, and Emaar's largest recent acquisition (Dubai Creek Harbour, AED 7.5 bn, FY2022) was from a government-related entity (Dubai Holding). Whether this was done at arm's length — i.e., market price — is not proven from available data. If it was priced at a discount to independent appraisal, this is value leakage from minority holders to government. The FY2022 acquisition price of AED 7.5 bn for Dubai Creek Harbour is a material data point: the land bank sits in Emaar's balance sheet at acquisition cost, and future development profits belong to all shareholders including minorities. The concern is on the acquisition side: overpaying for government assets would reduce returns.

2. **Alabbar conflict-of-interest channel.** Mohamed Alabbar simultaneously chairs Eagle Hills Properties LLC, a private developer directly competing in UAE and regional real estate and hospitality markets. Eagle Hills acquired Four Seasons Hotel Marrakech on Jan 30, 2026, competing with Emaar's hospitality strategy. No English-language disclosure addresses whether Emaar has any commercial transactions with Eagle Hills or Alabbar-affiliated entities, or whether conflict-of-interest procedures are in place. Until an English RPT note is available, this cannot be quantified or cleared.

3. **Minority leakage through listed subsidiaries.** Emaar's minority interest (non-controlling interest) has grown from AED 1.9 bn (FY2021) to AED 4.7 bn (FY2025), a 152% increase. While this is structurally driven by the listed subsidiary structure (Emaar Development PJSC and Emaar Misr are both independently listed), EMAR common shareholders capture only ~79% of consolidated net income. This is not classical RPT leakage but it is a structural dilution of economics for EMAR holders that grows with the subsidiary earnings.

The disqualifier scan did not find RPTs above 25% of revenue/expenses — the hard disqualifier threshold. No RF-RPT-001 is triggered at this time. However, the data gap prevents confirmation of arm's-length terms on government-counterparty transactions or Alabbar-linked transactions. An Amber flag applies pending English-language disclosure.

## 5. Minority-Shareholder Protection Read

The board is **not a genuine check on management or the controlling government shareholder.** With only 20% formally independent directors (2 of 10), a non-independent Chairman, a non-independent director sitting on both the Audit and Nominating Committees, and three new 2024 directors who are government-career professionals nominated by or aligned with the dominant 29.73% government bloc, the board's structural function is to ratify management decisions rather than challenge them. The two genuinely independent directors (Abdul Razzaq, Al Mulla) chair the Compensation and Nominating Committees — a positive signal — but are structurally outvoted on any contested matter and have no disclosed record of dissent or opposing management on any issue in the available data.

The single biggest entrenchment risk to minority holders is the combination of (a) Mohamed Alabbar's dual role as Emaar's MD and simultaneous chairman of Eagle Hills Properties LLC, a direct competitor, with no disclosed conflict-of-interest procedure in English, and (b) the government shareholder bloc's ability to approve or block any ordinary resolution without minority input, in a company where related-party pricing on government-land transactions (including the AED 7.5 bn Dubai Creek Harbour acquisition in FY2022) is not independently verifiable from available English-language disclosures.

Shareholder rights are structurally weak: no poison pill (a positive for takeover protection but irrelevant given government dominance), no staggered board, no dual-class structure (formally clean), but no mechanism for minority shareholders to meaningfully contest management decisions or force disclosure of RPT terms. AGM outcomes are not publicly disclosed in dissent-detail format. Written consent and EGM calling rights are limited under UAE law. Per MODULE_RULES.md §24 Filter 6: the dominant 29.73% owner is a government-controlled entity (Dubai Holding / Emirates Power Investment LLC) whose objective is not necessarily long-run per-share value maximisation for Emaar minorities — it is the broader development agenda of the Dubai government. This structural misalignment between the controlling owner's goal and minority-shareholder interests permanently caps the minority-protection read.

**Verdict: weak / entrenched.** The government-controlled bloc, lack of board independence, unresolved Alabbar conflict of interest, and unavailability of RPT terms in English combine to create structural minority-shareholder exposure. No critical red flag (RPT breach, audit qualification, enforcement action) is proven from available data — but the architecture is not one where minority holders can rely on the board as an independent agent. Applying §24 Filter 6: the controlling owner is a government entity; the board cannot offset a structurally misaligned owner; minority protection is capped at "weak / entrenched" on this basis alone, independent of the other findings.

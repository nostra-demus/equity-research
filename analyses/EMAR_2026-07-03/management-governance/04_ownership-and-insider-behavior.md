**Controlling-owner objective alignment (Filter 6 / CLAUDE.md §24):**

Emaar's 29.73% controlling bloc is a cluster of Dubai government-controlled investment vehicles (Emirates Power Investment LLC, Dubai Holding Corporate LLC, Eitl Difc Spc 1 Ltd). This is **government control**, one of the three structures that creates an inherent conflict with minority holders under CLAUDE.md §24, Filter 6 and MODULE_RULES.md Score Cap Rules.

The conflict is structural, not hypothetical. Dubai's government pursues multiple objectives beyond per-share value maximisation: the Emaar platform serves as an instrument of urban policy (delivering master-planned communities, creating employment, developing Dubai's built environment), a credit for the city's branding (Downtown Dubai, Dubai Mall, Burj Khalifa), and a vehicle for selective land transactions between government entities. The AED 200 bn new masterplan announced on June 11, 2026 — with 4.5 million sqm GFA, 150,000 residents, no pre-sale gate, no phased targets — is consistent with a government agenda to create an iconic new Dubai district, regardless of whether the financial return to EMAR minority shareholders justifies committing this capital at a probable cycle peak.

Additional evidence of the government-owner priority conflict: (a) Emaar's most significant recent acquisition (Dubai Creek Harbour) was transacted with a government entity; (b) the RPT notes in the FY2024 annual report are available only in Arabic, limiting minority-shareholder ability to monitor government-counterparty terms; (c) three of the four board members added in 2024–2025 carry Dubai government backgrounds, deepening government influence at the board level over this period.

**This triggers RF-OWN-004.** The shareholder-friendliness score is capped at max 55 and the governance risk floor is set at 55 (per MODULE_RULES.md Score Cap Rules). Persistent cheapness under a government-aligned owner reflects the structural conflict, not a temporary mispricing to arbitrage. The valuation module should treat government control as a structural cap on the P/E and EV/EBITDA multiple Emaar can sustainably trade at versus a private-sector developer, not as a temporary margin of safety. This is a value-trap risk if the multiple gap persists without minority-shareholder-friendly catalysts.

**Emaar as a listed parent of listed subsidiaries:** Emaar also partially owns two listed subsidiaries — Emaar Development PJSC (DFM:EMAARDEV, ~80% owned) and Emaar Misr for Development (CASE:EMFD, ~73.79% owned). The structure means minority interests in these subsidiaries create earnings leakage (AED 4.7 bn of the group's AED 22.3 bn net income in FY2025, up 152% since FY2021). While Emaar itself is not a subsidiary of a parent maximising value elsewhere, the listed-subsidiary dynamic creates an opposite version of the conflict: the parent (EMAR) could theoretically extract value from the listed subsidiaries at minority-holder expense, and conversely the growing minority leakage means EMAR holders receive only ~79% of consolidated group earnings.

## 5. Ownership / Insider Read

The founder (Alabbar) holds a negligible economic stake (~0.03% of shares outstanding in the entire "Individuals/Insiders" category, which may include multiple individuals) — there is essentially no cash skin-in-the-game for individual insiders, and shares were not purchased by Alabbar in the open market at any point visible in the data pool. The alignment between Emaar management and minority shareholders rests entirely on the founder's reputational capital and the government bloc's long-term interest in Emaar's success as a vehicle for Dubai urban policy, not on meaningful personal financial alignment.

The net insider signal is **neutral** — no individual buys, no individual sells, with the qualification that the UAE disclosure regime makes it structurally impossible to observe individual insider activity. The most material ownership event of the past 12 months (ICD → Emirates Power Investment LLC transfer) was a government portfolio reorganisation that deepened Dubai Holding's grip on the company without injecting a single new outside shareholder.

The biggest control risk to minority holders is the government-owner's structural incentive to use Emaar as a city-development vehicle rather than a pure returns-maximising enterprise. The AED 200 bn masterplan, the opacity of RPT terms (Arabic-only FY2024 notes), the growing government presence on the board (three new government-linked directors added 2024–2025), and the absence of a formal minority-protection mechanism all point in the same direction: Emaar is a genuinely outstanding developer operating in a world-class asset base, but the government-control structure means minority holders bear development-cycle risk without the protection of a controlling owner whose sole interest is maximising per-share value.

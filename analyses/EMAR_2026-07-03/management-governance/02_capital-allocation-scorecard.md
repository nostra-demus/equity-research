### 4B. Organic Reinvestment Returns (Incremental ROIC)

Incremental ROIC = Δ NOPAT / Δ Invested Capital.

**Computation method:** Using CIQ EBIT as a proxy for NOPAT pre-tax; applying 10% notional tax rate (pre-UAE-CIT average for the period). Invested capital = Total Assets − Non-interest-bearing Current Liabilities (approximated as Total Equity + Total Debt − Cash; or alternatively Total Assets − Current Liabilities excluding IBL). Given that Emaar's business model is fundamentally a real-estate developer whose "invested capital" includes substantial customer advances on the liability side, a conventional invested-capital computation is structurally distorted. The most meaningful measure is the incremental return earned on incremental equity deployed.

| Period | Δ NOPAT (AED mn, EBIT × 0.90) | Δ Invested Capital (AED mn, Δ Equity) | Incremental ROIC | Note |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| FY2021 → FY2022 | +1,351 (ΔEBIT 1,502 × 0.90) | +10,048 (equity AED 67,004mn to 77,052mn) | 13.4% | Source: Capital IQ Balance Sheet (Equity) and Income Statement (EBIT) |
| FY2022 → FY2023 | +4,456 (ΔEBIT 4,952 × 0.90) | +9,779 (equity 77,052mn to 86,831mn) | 45.6% | FY23 peak EBIT driven by POC mix; not representative |
| FY2023 → FY2024 | +2,812 (ΔEBIT 3,128 × 0.90) | +9,239 (equity 86,831mn to 96,070mn) | 30.4% | |
| FY2024 → FY2025 | +5,773 (ΔEBIT 6,416 × 0.90) | +11,606 (equity 96,070mn to 107,676mn) | 49.7% | |
| **FY2021–FY2025 (full period)** | **+14,392** | **+40,672** | **35.4%** | Full period computed from endpoint figures |

**Interpretation:** Incremental ROIC on equity invested was 35.4% over the full period (FY2021–FY2025). Even after adjusting for the distortive FY2023 POC spike, incremental returns were consistently high (13–50% range) across each two-year step. These returns reflect Emaar's ability to earn high gross margins (55%+) on development properties sold before construction begins (off-plan model), funded primarily by customer advances rather than equity capital. The organic reinvestment engine is the clearest source of per-share value creation in the capital-allocation record.

**Caveat:** This ROIC computation uses equity as the denominator because customer advances structurally reduce the equity-financed portion of working capital. The absolute return is very high but reflects the capital-light structure of off-plan development (customer deposits fund construction), not leverage of traditional debt. This is a feature of the UAE property model and is consistent with KPMG's clean audit (no going-concern or ICFR flags per the disqualifier scan). ## 5. Capital-Allocation Read

Over FY2021–FY2025, Emaar's capital allocation has been a clear net creator of per-share value, driven overwhelmingly by the organic development engine rather than financial engineering. EPS grew from AED 0.52 (FY2021) to AED 1.99 (FY2025), a 39.7% CAGR (Capital IQ Annual Income Statement) — per-share value creation is unambiguous at the fundamental level. Dividends grew 6.7x and are now comfortably covered by both reported and normalised FCF. Debt was cut by more than half (from AED 19bn to AED 9.8bn) while the balance sheet moved from AED 16.4bn net debt to AED 17.3bn broad net cash — the combination of paying down debt and growing the business simultaneously is the hallmark of a business generating more cash than it needs. The best capital use was organic reinvestment into the UAE masterplan pipeline (35.4% incremental ROIC), which has compounded EPS at nearly 40% per year. The worst use — or more precisely, the missed opportunity — is the absence of buybacks during a period when the company traded at a 40–65% discount to its own stated book value and NAV; management preferred dividends and organic reinvestment, which proved to be NPV-positive given the high reinvestment returns available. The M&A decisions were coherent: both the DCH acquisition and the Malls merger were consolidation moves within Emaar's core franchise, not diversifying bets, and the Namshi disposal recycled non-core capital at a gain. The single capital-allocation signal to watch is the AED 200bn masterplan announced in June 2026 — a commitment of unprecedented scale at a market-cycle peak, without disclosed phasing or pre-sale gates, which will test whether management can sustain the same capital discipline at a 3–4x larger canvas.

**Overall verdict: value-creative / disciplined.**

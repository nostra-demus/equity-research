## Sector Overlay (Step 3b)

**Sector overlay applied: REIT / real estate — margin analysis uses property NOI margin (development gross margin, segment EBITDA margins, and NOI-equivalent metrics) rather than a generic COGS / freight / labour table.**

Emaar is classified in `02_business-identity.md` §3a as a "UAE-dominant luxury residential and master-community developer with a high-margin captive mall and hotel portfolio." The closest SECTOR_OVERLAYS row is **REIT / real estate**, with the note that Emaar is a developer-first business, not a pure REIT. Accordingly:

- The primary margin metric for the **development segment** is gross margin on recognised revenue (the spread between selling price per project and construction/land cost at IFRS 15 percentage-of-completion, or POC — the method by which costs and revenues are recognised in stages as construction progresses).
- The primary metric for the **malls segment** is NOI margin / EBITDA margin on rental income (the net operating profit a landlord keeps after property costs but before depreciation and financing; EBITDA is the closest reported proxy).
- Depreciation is **partially** non-economic for Emaar's investment properties (mall and hotel assets), consistent with the REIT overlay instruction to back it out for operating margin purposes. D&A is reported separately in the IFRS income statement and is shown in Section 3 but not treated as a cash cost for the recurring-business analysis.
- SG&A and corporate costs are disclosed in aggregate as "selling, general and administrative expenses."
- No AFFO / FFO is disclosed, which is a data gap flagged in `02_business-identity.md`.

## 1. Segment Decomposition Status

Segment P&L is disclosed and complete. Three IFRS reportable segments plus an unallocated corporate line are available for FY2025 and prior years. A five-bucket management view (UAE Development, International Development, Emaar Malls, Emaar Hospitality, Entertainment/Leasing/Others) is also disclosed in the investor deck and carries segment-level EBITDA margins. Both views are used below; the IFRS segment figures are authoritative.

**Dominant segment: Real Estate (UAE Development) — 80% of FY2025 revenue, 77% of segment NPBT.**

The business is a single-dominant-segment company by the >85% test at the individual-segment level: UAE Development alone is 73% of group revenue and 66% of group EBITDA. Within the IFRS "Real Estate" segment (80% of revenue), the UAE portion is ~92% and the international portion (mainly Emaar Misr in Egypt) is ~8%. The analysis below proceeds at both the consolidated level and the segment level, with the development segment receiving priority treatment.

**Pass-through lag note:** For the development segment, pricing is locked in at the point of property sale — before construction begins. There is no mechanism to pass through subsequent cost increases to buyers after the sale contract is signed. The lag between a cost increase and its margin impact is therefore the time between when the project was priced (launch date, often 2–4 years before delivery) and when units deliver and costs are charged to COGS under IFRS 15. In practice, construction cost inflation manifests in delivered project margins with a 2–4 year lag. Projects already under construction for delivery in 2026–2028 have locked-in selling prices; any construction cost overshoot directly compresses the "gross margin achieved" of 46–47% reported on the current backlog. For the malls segment, rent escalation clauses are contractual and annual — Emaar can pass through inflation to tenants within 1–3 years as leases (WALT ~2.9 years) renew.

## 4. Margin Walk — Which Margin Level Matters Most?

For Emaar, **EBITDA margin** is the right primary operating metric at the group level, but it must be read segment-by-segment rather than consolidated. Here is why:

The group's P&L mixes two structurally different businesses: (a) a property developer where "gross margin achieved" (project-level development margin) and the EBITDA margin on UAE Development (46% in FY2025) are what matter, and (b) an investment property landlord (malls, entertainment, commercial leasing) where NOI margin and EBITDA margin (86% for malls) are the economically meaningful measures and depreciation is non-cash. At the consolidated level, the blended EBITDA margin (48.7% CIQ, 51.6% company-adjusted) is distorted by the development/recurring mix: as the development segment grows faster (73% of revenue) it pulls the consolidated margin toward the lower-margin development margin (46%), even when the malls and hospitality businesses are performing well.

The net profit margin is the least useful primary metric because it now reflects the tax-regime inflection (UAE Corporate Tax + DMTT) as a permanent structural drag — the gap between EBIT growth and EPS growth has permanently widened. Analysts tracking net profit margin trends against history (pre-FY2024 comparisons) are comparing across different tax regimes and will overstate margin compression.

EBIT margin is near-flat (45.5% FY2025 vs 45.4% FY2024) and is the cleanest cross-tax, cross-IFRS-9-adjustment comparison, but D&A inflation from hotel key additions makes it slightly less useful than EBITDA margin for the investment-property arms.

**Summary: Use segment EBITDA margins as the primary operating performance indicator — UAE Development EBITDA margin (currently 46%), Emaar Malls EBITDA margin (currently 86%), and Emaar Hospitality EBITDA margin (currently 48%). At the consolidated level, use CIQ EBITDA margin (48.7%) as the cross-company comparison baseline and flag that net profit margin comparisons are cross-regime post-FY2024.**

## 8. The Single Biggest Margin Driver

**Construction cost inflation on the AED 134.3 Bn UAE development backlog is the single driver that, if it moved adversely at scale, would compress margins the most.**

Here is the mechanism: Emaar pre-sells units at launch, locking in the buyer's price. It then spends 5–6 years constructing and delivering those units, with revenue recognised under IFRS 15 percentage-of-completion as milestones are certified. All 50,800+ units under construction in the UAE are being built at costs that will be charged to COGS as each milestone is reached. If Dubai's building boom (the same demand wave that drove AED 71.1 Bn of FY2025 property sales) creates sustained contractor cost inflation — tighter skilled labour, higher steel and concrete prices, longer lead times on facade and MEP systems — the cost side of the COGS equation rises while the revenue side (the locked-in selling price) cannot change. The "gross margin achieved" on the backlog, currently disclosed at 46–47%, would compress toward 40% or below on the most-affected cohorts.

Emaar partially protects itself by fixing construction costs with contractors at or shortly after project launch. But in Dubai's current environment, re-tendering and change-order risk is real: the pipeline of concurrent major projects (Emaar alone is building 50,800+ UAE units plus the Dubai Creek Harbour mega-scheme) strains contractor capacity. No single contractor dependency has been disclosed, and no sensitivity table for a given percentage rise in construction costs is available in the data pool — the IFRS 7 market-risk disclosures are in the full FY2025 Annual Report, which has not been filed at the date of this analysis.

**Current direction: Headwind, but contained.** The gross margin achieved on existing backlog is disclosed as stable at 46–47% as of 31 Dec 2025 — construction cost inflation has not yet materially eroded this figure in the reported data. The risk is forward-looking and tied to the pace of Dubai's building activity over the next 2–4 years as the largest cohort of the AED 134.3 Bn backlog delivers.

The tax regime change (UAE Corporate Tax + DMTT) is already embedded in run-rate and is the biggest **actual** margin compressor in the most recent period (−413 bps on net profit margin FY2024 to FY2025). It is not a future risk — it is a permanent structural reset. Construction cost inflation is the biggest **forward-looking** margin risk.

## Cycle-Position Note (MODULE_RULES §8)

Emaar is in the **mid-to-peak phase of the Dubai property development cycle** as of the latest reported period (FY2025 / Q1-2026):

- UAE property sales of AED 71.1 Bn in FY2025 are the highest in the company's history by a wide margin, and AED 71.1 Bn compares to AED 65.4 Bn in FY2024, AED 37.4 Bn in FY2023, and AED 30.7 Bn in FY2022. This is a cycle high, not a trough.
- Revenue backlog of AED 134.3 Bn (UAE) is the highest on record. This provides 5–6 years of forward revenue coverage at the FY2025 pace.
- Customer default rate of 0.5% is a cycle-high (low default) metric: in a downturn (COVID 2020), revenue dropped 34% year-on-year from FY2019.
- The precedent cycle: FY2020 saw revenue fall from AED 27.9 Bn (FY2019) to AED 18.4 Bn (−34%) in a single year. The current AED 49.6 Bn revenue base would, in a comparable shock, compress to AED 32–33 Bn — roughly FY2023 levels.
- **The 5–6 year backlog is the key cycle buffer**: even in a demand downturn, Emaar would continue recognising revenue on units already sold (AED 134.3 Bn backlog) for several years. New sales would slow but recognised revenue would not immediately collapse to zero, unlike a spot-delivery model.
- **The government-directed urbanisation tailwind** (Al Maktoum Airport expansion, Dubai Urban Master Plan 2040, Dubai Square Creek Harbour designation) is real but is policy-conditional, not a guaranteed permanent driver. It is treated as a current tailwind, not a run-rate assumption for future margin modelling.

**Reporting standard:** IFRS | **Currency:** AED millions | **Fiscal year end:** 31 December
**All 4 upstream outputs read and used.** No upstream data is missing.

## 1. Next Quarter Context

The next reporting period is Q2 2026 (April–June 2026), with results expected around August 12, 2026. Q2 is seasonally the second-weakest quarter of the year, averaging 21.2% of annual revenue over FY2023–FY2025, only slightly above the Q1 lows. The consensus bar has been cut sharply: FY2026 EPS consensus fell from AED 2.32 three months ago to AED 1.95 now (−16%), and FY2026 revenue consensus fell from AED 58.2 Bn to AED 53.1 Bn (−9%), with a single large analyst revision in June 2026 driving most of the cut. Q2 2026 EPS consensus sits at approximately AED 0.47, down from AED 0.61 three months ago — the bar has been lowered meaningfully heading into the print.

## 6. Seasonality Read

Seasonality works against Q2 2026. Over the three years FY2023–FY2025, Q2 has averaged 21.2% of annual revenue — the second-weakest quarter, close to Q1's 20.9% average, and well below Q3's 25.3% and Q4's 32.5% [01_historical-financials.md §5]. The dominant Q4 pattern (33% of annual revenue) is structural: IFRS 15 POC milestone certifications for large master-community projects cluster at calendar year-end. For the Q2 2026 print, investors should not expect a seasonal uplift; the relevant question is whether Q2 2026 holds its YoY growth rate of +20–30% (as Q1 2026 did at +22.8% vs Q1 2025). Seasonality in itself does not create a miss risk for Q2 specifically — the consensus bar already reflects the seasonal pattern — but it does mean that any construction delay in Q2 cannot be "made up" within the quarter, only deferred to Q3 or Q4.

## 7. Historical Pattern

Emaar has beaten EPS estimates in every annual period from FY2023 through Q1 2026, with margins of +11% to +23% in FY2023–FY2025 and approximately +9% in Q1 2026 [04_guidance-consensus.md §6]. The one exception in recent history is FY2022, when revenue missed by −3.9% and EPS missed by −10.8% during a normalisation period following the COVID-era property boom. The pattern strongly suggests that Emaar's management does not actively guide the Street, and analysts have consistently set estimates too conservatively relative to what the delivery pipeline and backlog mechanics actually deliver. The synthesiser should weight this pattern meaningfully — it is three consecutive years of material beats against a backlog-driven model that provides structural visibility. However, the FY2022 miss is a live reminder that the pattern can break when the delivery cycle inflects; the current cycle is at or near peak by the metrics in 02_revenue-drivers.md §7, and the risk of a first miss since FY2022 is real if delivery slips or the June 2026 analyst cut proves to be the start of a broader re-rating.

## 8. Setup Verdict

**Setup favors beat.**

The weight of evidence — a sharply lowered consensus bar (EPS cut −16% in three months), three consecutive years of large EPS beats (+11% to +23%), AED 134.3 Bn in UAE revenue backlog providing 3–4 years of forward visibility, and an EBITDA margin corridor that has held 47–51% without degradation for eight consecutive quarters — leans toward outperformance. The single most important factor is the lowered consensus bar: the Jun-11-2026 revision drove EPS consensus from AED 2.46 to AED 1.93 in a single move, meaning the current bar is roughly where Emaar was performing two years ago, while the business has continued to grow. The single biggest risk that could flip this verdict is a construction delivery delay in H2 2026 — if the AED 16.5 Bn quarterly new-sales pace stalls or if large project milestones slip out of FY2026, the Q3/Q4 seasonal heavy-lifting (Q4 alone is 33% of annual revenue) may not materialise, and the FY2026 consensus would need to be cut further.

## 9. Second-Quarter Look-Ahead

Q3 2026 (July–September) is historically Emaar's second-strongest quarter at an average 25.3% of annual revenue, well above Q2's 21.2%, and typically sees a material sequential revenue pickup. If the AED 134.3 Bn UAE backlog continues to release at its current pace and Dubai's Q2 2026 new-sales momentum is confirmed at the Q2 results, Q3 2026 is set up as a stronger print than Q2 — the YoY comparison base from Q3 2025 (AED 13,273 Mn) is demanding but not out of reach. The primary risk for Q3 2026 is a condensed delivery schedule: if milestones slip from Q2 into Q3, Q3 could be artificially elevated, and if Q3 milestones then slip into Q4, the full-year story becomes back-end loaded in a way that increases execution risk on the one quarter (Q4) that already carries the most weight.

---

## 10. Pre-Mortem

If this beat-favoring setup fails, the most likely reason is that the June 2026 consensus cut anticipated a structural deceleration in the Dubai off-plan cycle that this analysis underweights: unit volumes sold in FY2025 already fell −27% year-on-year (13,905 units vs 19,058 in FY2024), and if Q2 2026 new sales confirm a sustained volume decline that the AED value numbers are masking via ASP inflation, the analyst who cut from AED 2.46 to AED 1.93 on June 11 may prove to have been early rather than wrong. The second most likely reason we missed it is that construction cost inflation — the single biggest forward-looking margin risk identified in 03_margin-drivers.md — shows up visibly in Q2 2026 delivered gross margins before it is visible in the disclosed backlog-margin figure, creating a downside surprise on the EBITDA line that the consensus did not fully embed.

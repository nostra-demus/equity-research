## What This Module Found

Emaar's balance sheet is not a marginal call. Gross debt of AED 10.6 bn is just 0.44x FY2025 EBITDA (the profit-before-interest-tax-depreciation number that lenders compare debt against), and on the broad net-debt basis (debt minus cash and liquid investments) the company sits on AED 17.3 bn of net cash at FY2025, rising to AED 24.6 bn by Q1-2026. Gross debt fell from AED 19.0 bn in FY2021 to AED 10.6 bn in FY2025 while EBITDA grew 3.1x — the deleveraging happened organically, without asset sales or share issuance.

The one near-term event is Sukuk 3 (a US$750 mn / ~AED 2.75 bn Islamic bond) maturing September 2026. Unrestricted cash alone covers it 4.4x at Q1-2026; the specialists expect it to be repaid from cash rather than refinanced. Committed liquidity of AED 35.6 bn covers all twelve-month obligations (AED 16.2 bn) 2.2x without touching operating cash flow.

The single largest risk the module names is a prolonged Dubai property-cycle reversal that drains the AED ~43 bn of buyer escrow (money held for buyers, refundable if projects are cancelled), eliminates pre-sale operating cash, and continues the AED 8.9 bn dividend — slowly depleting the liquid buffer over multiple years [FY2025 Investor Presentation p.8; Capital IQ, FY2025].

A key correction the module surfaces: the company's own reported "AED 61.7 bn net cash" figure includes AED 42.9 bn of restricted buyer escrow that cannot be used for debt service. Specialist 01 strips that out. An investor relying on the company's headline would overstate freely usable liquidity by roughly 5x on the strict basis.

## What Would Change This Read

**Would strengthen the verdict:** disclosure of the credit agreement confirming covenant headroom matches or exceeds the labeled 3.0x assumption; formal confirmation that the AED 7.3 bn revolver has no borrowing-base or springing-covenant strings; formal defeasance or escrow of Sukuk 3 for September 2026; gross debt falling below AED 5 bn.

**Would weaken it:** a large undisclosed contingency (guarantee, litigation, regulatory claim) above AED 5 bn; a simultaneous collapse in Dubai off-plan pre-sales together with a wave of buyer cancellations forcing refunds from restricted escrow; a sustained dividend above AED 8.9 bn per year that outpaces deteriorating operating cash flow; discovery that the covenant EBITDA definition includes narrow addbacks that materially shrink effective headroom.

**Data most needed:** the credit agreement (covenants, addbacks, cross-default, revolver mechanics); an English-language contingencies note from the FY2025 annual report; the DMTT tax charge quantum for FY2025.

## Bottom Line

- Verdict: Fortress balance sheet. Broad-basis net cash AED 17.3 bn; gross leverage 0.44x; survives -93.9% EBITDA before the labeled covenant breaks.
- Could be better than it looks: in-hand liquid assets of AED 28.3 bn already exceed the entire AED 10.1 bn gross debt stack — Emaar could retire every debt instrument today and still hold AED 18–24 bn in cash.
- Could be worse than it looks: the company's own AED 61.7 bn "net cash" headline includes AED 42.9 bn of restricted buyer escrow; strip that out or you overstate usable liquidity roughly 5x on the strict basis.
- Evidence missing: the credit agreement (no covenant thresholds, addbacks, cross-default, or change-of-control triggers in the pool); the Arabic-only contingencies note; hedging schedule. Overall usefulness is capped at 75 for this reason.
- Watch next: the September 2026 Sukuk 3 repayment (cash vs. refinance) and any large move in Dubai off-plan pre-sales that would pressure the buyer-escrow refund exposure.

## Plain-English Glossary

- **EBITDA** — earnings before interest, tax, depreciation, and amortisation; a rough proxy for how much operating cash the business throws off before financing costs.
- **Net debt (strict basis)** — gross debt minus unrestricted cash only.
- **Net debt / net cash (broad basis)** — gross debt minus unrestricted cash minus liquid short-term investments; the canonical figure this module uses.
- **Gross leverage** — gross debt divided by EBITDA; how many years of pre-financing profit it would take to repay all debt.
- **Sukuk** — an Islamic-finance bond; repaid from asset returns rather than interest.
- **Covenant** — a promise in the loan contract (e.g. minimum interest coverage 3.0x); breaching it can trigger default.
- **Interest coverage (ICR)** — EBITDA divided by interest expense; how many times over profits cover the interest bill.
- **Revolver** — a committed line of credit the company can draw on as needed.
- **Restricted buyer escrow** — cash held on behalf of off-plan property buyers; refundable to buyers on cancellation, not available for corporate use.
- **BBB+ / Baa1** — investment-grade credit ratings from S&P and Moody's; both stable outlook here.

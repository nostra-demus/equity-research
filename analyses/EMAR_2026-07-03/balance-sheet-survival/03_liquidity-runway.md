## 3. Runway

| Metric | FY2025 Value (AED mn) | Q1-2026 Value (AED mn) |
|---|---:|---:|
| Total committed liquidity (in-hand + undrawn revolver) | 35,594 | 42,025 |
| Annual FCF — reported (CFO − Capex) | 32,524 | 30,982 (LTM) |
| Annual FCF — normalised operating (ex-unearned revenue advance inflows) | 24,295 | Not available at quarterly level |

| FCF surplus over near-term uses (normalised FCF basis) | +8,142 | — |
| Net near-term obligations if FCF contributes zero (in-hand liquidity only) | AED 35,594 mn − AED 16,153 mn = **AED 19,441 mn surplus** | AED 42,025 mn − AED 16,153 mn = **AED 25,872 mn surplus** |
| **Liquidity runway** | **FCF surplus — no finite runway; in-hand liquidity alone covers all 12-month uses 2.2x** | **FCF surplus — no finite runway; in-hand liquidity alone covers all 12-month uses 2.6x** |

**Formula and working:**

Emaar's near-term uses (next 12 months) of AED ~16,153 mn are less than its in-hand committed liquidity alone (AED 28,255 mn unrestricted cash + liquid STI at FY2025). The company does not need any FCF materialisation or revolver draw to cover the next 12 months in full.

Coverage ratio (in-hand liquid assets only, excluding revolver):
- FY2025: AED 28,255 mn ÷ AED 16,153 mn = **1.75x**

Coverage ratio (total committed liquidity including revolver):
- FY2025: AED 35,594 mn ÷ AED 16,153 mn = **2.20x**

Coverage ratio (total committed liquidity):
- Q1-2026: AED 42,025 mn ÷ AED 16,153 mn = **2.60x**

Because in-hand liquidity exceeds 12-month uses without any FCF contribution, the runway is expressed as an **annual FCF surplus** rather than a finite runway in months:

- On reported FCF (AED 32,524 mn) minus total near-term uses (AED 16,153 mn): **surplus of AED 16,371 mn per annum** — the business generates nearly double its annual obligations in cash.
- On normalised FCF (AED 24,295 mn, removing AED 8,229 mn of buyer advance inflows that are real cash but obligated to project delivery): **surplus of AED 8,142 mn per annum** — still a substantial positive buffer.

### Seasonality / Peak Liquidity Need (Hard Check)

Emaar's revenue is **seasonally concentrated in Q4** (32%–33% of annual revenue, per `earnings/01_historical-financials.md` Section 5). Q1 is the weakest quarter (approximately 21% of annual revenue). EBITDA follows the same Q4 concentration, and CFO is therefore also back-weighted.

However, the seasonality does not create a liquidity risk for the following reasons:

1. Emaar's off-plan property model collects buyer advance payments upfront (before revenue is recognised), so cash actually arrives before earnings. The seasonal CFO pattern is therefore less pronounced than the revenue pattern — cash is pre-collected across all quarters as construction milestones are reached and instalments billed.

2. The AED 28,255 mn (FY2025) / AED 34,683 mn (Q1-2026) in-hand liquid assets are a persistent balance that does not depend on a single-quarter cash surge. Even in Q1 — the seasonally weakest quarter — the liquid balance grew to AED 34,683 mn (Q1-2026, the weakest seasonal point in the annual cycle), well above the AED 16,153 mn of annual uses.

3. **Peak working-capital build figure is not separately disclosed** in the investor presentation or Capital IQ data pool (the company does not provide an explicit peak-quarter liquidity-need estimate). However, the AED 34,683 mn liquid balance at Q1-2026 (the trough seasonal point for earnings) is 2.1x the total annual uses, giving a large buffer even at the weakest moment.

**Conclusion:** The seasonal Q4 weighting of EBITDA and cash flow does not create a liquidity pinch. Peak working-capital need not quantified for a specific quarter in available disclosures — runway is not materially overstated given the scale of the in-hand buffer.

## 4. Sources & Uses Bridge

Internal sources (in-hand cash plus liquid investments = AED 28,255 mn at FY2025 year-end) already exceed the full AED 16,153 mn of next-12-month uses by AED 12,102 mn — without a single dirham of FCF contribution, revolver draw, or asset sale. Adding the committed undrawn revolver (AED 7,339 mn) brings total committed liquidity to AED 35,594 mn, a surplus of AED 19,441 mn. The company therefore **does not require any external access** (market refinancing, asset sales, equity issuance) to meet its next-12-month obligations.

Of the AED 35,594 mn total committed liquidity: AED 28,255 mn (79%) is already in hand, and AED 7,339 mn (21%) depends on revolver availability remaining intact (which it does — it is a committed facility maturing 2030 at a BBB+/Baa1-rated issuer with a negligible AED 3.7 mn drawn balance). FCF does not need to materialise at all for the near-term obligations to be met. If normalised FCF (AED 24,295 mn) continues at the FY2025 run-rate, it adds a further AED 24,295 mn of annual cash generation on top of the existing balance, providing an additional compounding buffer.

The only structural "use" that creates cash demand is the AED 8,900 mn declared dividend — which is entirely discretionary in legal terms (the board could reduce or defer it), though not doing so would be a significant negative signal for this high-dividend-yield name. Even including the full dividend, in-hand liquidity covers every obligation with AED 12,102 mn to spare.

## 5. Liquidity Read

Emaar's liquidity runway is not measurable in months because in-hand committed liquidity (AED 28.3 bn at FY2025 year-end, rising to AED 34.7 bn at Q1-2026) exceeds all near-term 12-month obligations (AED 16.2 bn) by 1.75x before counting the committed undrawn revolver; total committed liquidity coverage is 2.2x at FY2025 and 2.6x at Q1-2026. The company generates an annual normalised FCF surplus of AED 8.1 bn above its obligations even after removing the buyer-advance inflows that inflate reported cash flow. The single biggest liquidity risk is not a cash shortfall but rather a scenario where the Dubai property cycle reverses sharply after Sukuk 3 is repaid in September 2026, causing pre-sales to collapse and the AED 8.9 bn dividend to be paid from a shrinking liquid buffer — but even in that scenario, the starting liquid base (AED 28–35 bn) would take several years of simultaneous cycle downturn and dividend payment to exhaust.

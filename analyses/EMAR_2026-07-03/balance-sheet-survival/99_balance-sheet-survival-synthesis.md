# Balance-Sheet-Survival Module — EMAR (Synthesis)

## Abstract

Emaar Properties PJSC enters this analysis with a fortress-grade balance sheet: gross debt of AED 10.6 bn represents just 0.44x FY2025 EBITDA, and on the broad net-debt basis (the canonical measure designated in specialist 01) the company is in net cash of AED 17.3 bn — a position that has deepened steadily since FY2021. The only maturity event in the next twelve months is the Sukuk 3 USD $750 mn (AED ~2.75 bn) bullet in September 2026, which is covered 4.4x by unrestricted cash alone at Q1-2026 and requires no market access to retire. Committed liquidity of AED 35.6 bn at FY2025 year-end covers all twelve-month obligations (AED 16.2 bn) 2.2x; the tightest labeled covenant (minimum interest coverage 3.0x, labeled assumption — actual covenants are not in the data pool) would require a 93.9% EBITDA collapse to breach, or roughly 2.8x worse than Emaar's actual post-COVID trough. Emaar is a fortress balance sheet: the survival verdict is unambiguous and does not change at any EBITDA decline that has historical precedent in UAE real estate.

## 1A. Module Disconfirmation

**Strongest bear point:** The 2026 maturity wall is 50.1% of gross debt (AED 5,317 mn). If Dubai's property cycle reverses sharply in H2-2026 (after Sukuk 3 is repaid in September), pre-sales could stall and the operating cash engine would slow — at the same time the AED 8.9 bn declared dividend exits the balance sheet. The transition from a pre-cycle-reversal to a post-cycle-reversal world compresses the liquid buffer faster than the static balance-sheet snapshot suggests. The restricted escrow of AED 42.9 bn cannot serve as a backup; if buyers cancel at scale, that cash would need to be refunded, not deployed for corporate use.

**Strongest bull point:** The company's in-hand liquid assets of AED 28.3 bn at FY2025 (growing to AED 34.7 bn at Q1-2026) already exceed Emaar's entire gross debt stack of AED 10.1 bn — meaning the company could retire every single debt instrument with existing liquid assets and still hold AED 18-24 bn in cash. This is not leverage management; this is a structurally near-debt-free operating business that happens to carry some fixed-rate sukuk. The broad net cash position of AED 17.3 bn is a strategic asset in the Section 24 Filter 3 sense: it funds counter-cyclical action when the Dubai market turns and removes any dependence on external debt markets.

**Single killer risk specific to solvency/survival:** A sudden and severe cancellation wave across Emaar's AED 154.8 bn off-plan backlog (with Emaar India / Egypt exposures compounding via local-currency facility risk), triggering large buyer-refund obligations from restricted escrow accounts and simultaneously cutting off the pre-sale instalment inflows that fund operating cash flow. This scenario would need to be historically unprecedented in scale — the COVID-2020 shock produced only a 34% revenue drop and did not threaten solvency — but it is the one pathway to financial stress, given that the escrow is not available for corporate purposes and refund obligations would be immediate.

**Disconfirming evidence already visible in specialist outputs:** The AED 42.9 bn of "Net Cash" disclosed in the company's own FY2025 investor presentation is a gross-liquidity figure that includes restricted buyer escrow. The company's headline "net cash" of AED 61.7 bn includes AED 43 bn of buyer money that cannot be used for debt service. An investor relying on the company's own disclosure without stripping the escrow would overstate freely usable liquidity by approximately 5x on the strict basis. Specialist 01 explicitly corrects for this, and this synthesis carries the correction through all sections.

## 3. Reconciliation

No material disagreements between specialists. All seven outputs are directionally consistent: Emaar is in fortress territory on every dimension — leverage, maturity management, liquidity, coverage, and stress survival.

One internal tension worth noting, not a disagreement: specialist 02 reports the 12-month maturity wall at 50.1% of gross debt (AED 5,317 mn), which sounds alarming in isolation. Specialists 03 and 06 confirm this is entirely benign given the AED 28.3 bn in-hand liquid assets that cover the wall 5.2x. The conservative reading here is that the wall is real and concentrated (nearly the entire 2026 amount is a single sukuk instrument), but the liquidity coverage is so large that it cannot reasonably be called a risk. This synthesis retains the 50.1% figure in the verdict block with full context.

The two partial-data limitations that span multiple specialists are consistently applied: (a) covenant headroom is "Not assessable" in specialists 04 and 06, and this synthesis carries that through; (b) the Arabic-only contingencies note means off-balance-sheet exposures are confirmed as partial in specialists 05 and 06, with the conclusion that no individually material exposure is visible and the AED 17.3 bn net cash position provides substantial buffer even against unknown contingencies.

## 5. Survival Summary

Emaar is deeply net cash on the broad basis (AED 17.3 bn at FY2025; AED 24.6 bn at Q1-2026), and gross leverage of 0.44x EBITDA is trivially low. The trend is unambiguously improving: gross debt fell by AED 8.4 bn between FY2021 and FY2025 while EBITDA grew 3.1x, compressing gross leverage from 2.44x to 0.44x entirely organically — no asset sales, no equity issuance. The 2026 maturity wall (50.1% of gross debt, AED 5,317 mn) is real but entirely self-funded: in-hand liquid assets of AED 28.3 bn cover it 5.2x, and the single largest event — Sukuk 3 ($750 mn, September 2026) — is expected to be repaid from cash rather than refinanced; no replacement issuance has been announced. The liquidity runway extends beyond any finite horizon: in-hand committed liquidity (AED 35.6 bn) covers all next-twelve-month obligations (AED 16.2 bn) 2.2x without a single dirham of new operating cash flow, and normalised FCF of AED 24.3 bn per year generates an annual surplus of AED 8.1 bn above those obligations. The tightest covenant headroom cannot be confirmed from pool data (no credit agreement), but on a labeled minimum ICR assumption of 3.0x, a 93.9% EBITDA collapse — more than double the actual COVID drawdown — would be required to breach; the company survived the real COVID trough (-67.7% to FY2021) with ICR of 15.9x and a committed liquidity surplus of AED 19.4 bn. A standard recession (-30%) leaves coverage at 34.3x; a severe recession (-60%) leaves coverage at 19.6x. No scenario grounded in UAE real estate history — including the full COVID shock — breaks the structure or requires external action.

## 6A. Survival Playbook (non-speculative levers)

- **Refinancing actions taken:** No new sukuk or debt issuance publicly announced or committed. Sukuk 3 (Sep-2026) is expected to be repaid from cash rather than refinanced. Prior track record is clean — First and Second Series Trust Certificates matured during FY2021-FY2022 and were repaid on schedule without incident.
- **Revolver backstop:** The AED 7.3 bn committed undrawn revolving credit facility (matures 2030, EIBOR + 1.00%) is available as an emergency liquidity buffer. At the current drawn balance of AED 3.7 mn, the revolver provides a near-full backstop if in-hand liquid assets were unexpectedly constrained.
- **Dividend flexibility:** The AED 8.9 bn declared FY2025 dividend is the single largest twelve-month cash use (55% of total obligations). The dividend is declared by the board and, in an extreme stress, can be reduced or deferred — though this would be a significant negative signal for a high-dividend-yield name. No contractual obligation to maintain a fixed dividend level is evidenced in the data pool.
- **Capex flexibility:** Total capex (AED 934 mn, FY2025) is modest at 3.9% of EBITDA and could be cut; however, Emaar does not separately disclose maintenance vs. growth capex, limiting precision on the deferrable portion.
- **Asset-sale capacity:** Emaar holds a diversified asset base (hotels, malls, international subsidiaries in India, Egypt, Pakistan per business-model/03_segment-map.md). No asset-sale programme is publicly announced. The hospitality and mall assets represent logical candidates if needed, but proceeds are not quantified from available data.
- **Covenant-amendment likelihood:** Not assessable — no credit agreement in pool. At BBB+/Baa1 with 49x coverage, any waiver negotiations — if ever needed — would occur from a position of significant lender goodwill.

**Notes on the debt stack:**

1. **Sukuk Trust Certificates:** All three series are issued as sukuk (Islamic finance instruments structured as trust certificates, economically equivalent to senior unsecured bonds). The Third Series (AED 2,752.6 mn, maturing Sep-2026) is the single largest near-term instrument — it will be the main focus of the maturity wall analysis. Repayment currency is USD; the AED amounts are conversions at the prevailing exchange rate.

2. **Seniority:** All debt in the stack is labelled Senior in the Capital IQ filing data. There is no subordinated or second-lien debt. The small secured term loan (USD, AED 14.7 mn) is the only non-lease secured item — immaterial relative to total debt.

3. **Fixed vs floating:** Fixed-rate debt represents ~66.3% of total principal (AED 7,042 mn) and floating-rate 10.1% (AED 1,070 mn); lease liabilities (AED 808.9 mn, 7.6%) have mixed rates per the filing. [Capital IQ Capital Structure Summary, FY2025]

4. **Revolving credit facility (drawn):** The AED 3.7 mn drawn balance is negligible against a total drawn + undrawn facility of AED ~3,672 mn (AED 3,669 mn undrawn as of FY2025; AED 7,342 mn undrawn as of Q1-2026 per Capital IQ Quarterly Capital Structure Summary). The revolver matures 2030 and is senior unsecured.

5. **International subsidiary debt:** The EGP (Egyptian pound) and PKR (Pakistani rupee) facilities are at the local operating-company level. The INR term loans (AED 1,694 mn equivalent) are at Emaar India. These are not guaranteed at the Emaar Properties PJSC parent level per available disclosure; seniority/security at subsidiary level is not disclosed in the Capital IQ detail.

| Pension / OPEB (End-of-Service Benefits — UAE labour law) | AED 197.9 mn liability on balance sheet (FY2025) | Defined-benefit end-of-service benefit plan; net liability recognised on balance sheet; no separate plan assets disclosed — the obligation equals the balance sheet liability | Capital IQ Annual Pension/OPEB sheet, FY2025, filed Feb-12-2026; Capital IQ Annual Balance Sheet, FY2025 |
| Preferred equity | Nil | No preferred equity outstanding | Capital IQ Capital Structure Summary, FY2025; Historical Capitalization sheet |

**IFRS 16 treatment note:** Emaar reports under IFRS, which requires right-of-use (ROU) assets and lease liabilities to be recognised on the balance sheet for virtually all lease contracts. The AED 808.9 mn lease liability is already included in the Total Debt figure used throughout this report. Unlike under old US GAAP (where operating leases were kept off the balance sheet), there is no "hidden" operating lease obligation to add back separately.

**Pension note:** The AED 197.9 mn end-of-service liability represents the group's obligation to employees under UAE labour law (gratuity payments). It is not a funded defined-benefit plan with plan assets — the liability and the annual expense (AED 50.5 mn in FY2025) are fully on-balance-sheet. This is small relative to EBITDA and debt.

**Key cash notes:**

- The **AED 42,878.9 mn** of restricted cash is the dominant item on the cash line at first sight. This is customer advance payments held in project escrow accounts required by Dubai real estate regulations. It belongs to the buyers and is released as construction milestones are certified. It is NOT available for debt service or general corporate use. It must not be netted against debt.

- The company's own "Net Cash" figure of AED 61,655 mn (FY2025, from the investor presentation) is a gross liquidity measure that includes AED 52,633 mn "Cash & Cash Equivalents" (which in the company's own definition includes the AED ~43 bn of escrow cash and short-term deposits) minus AED 9,806 mn debt. This figure is NOT comparable to net debt computed on the strict or broad basis used in this report — see Section 4.

- Capital IQ's AED 28,254 mn "Total Cash & ST Investments" (FY2025) = AED 9,754 mn unrestricted cash + AED 18,148 mn short-term investments + AED 352.8 mn trading securities. This is the correct freely usable liquidity base, before adding undrawn revolver facilities.

- At Q1-2026 (Mar-31-2026): unrestricted cash AED 12,180 mn, short-term investments AED 22,503 mn, restricted cash AED 43,338 mn. The upward trend in unrestricted cash and investments is driven by robust operating cash flow.

**§15 basis designation:**  
- **Strict basis** (gross debt − unrestricted cash & equivalents only): AED +860.9 mn at FY2025 year-end (marginally net debt; i.e., debt exceeds unrestricted cash by AED 861 mn). At Q1-2026: AED −2,115 mn (net cash of AED 2,115 mn).  
- **Broad basis** (also subtracting liquid short-term investments): AED −17,287 mn at FY2025 (net cash of AED 17,287 mn). At Q1-2026: AED −24,619 mn (net cash of AED 24,619 mn).  
- **Canonical net-debt figure for downstream agents:** The **broad basis** is designated canonical for this company because (a) the short-term investments are liquid financial assets classified as such by Capital IQ, not restricted cash, (b) the company has consistently grown these as freely deployable liquidity alongside cash, and (c) the CIQ-reported "Net Debt" of AED −17,640 mn (FY2025) matches the broad basis. The strict basis (AED +861 mn) is always shown alongside to be transparent, per §15.  
- **Restricted cash (AED 42,879 mn, FY2025) is excluded from both bases** — it is buyer escrow money that is not available for general corporate use, and including it would misrepresent free liquidity.

**Net cash characterisation (CLAUDE.md §24, Filter 3):** On the broad basis, Emaar is in significant **net cash** — AED 17,287 mn at FY2025 and AED 24,619 mn at Q1-2026. Even on the strict basis, the company reached net cash in Q1-2026 (AED 2,115 mn net cash). This net cash position is a strategic asset: it funds counter-cyclical operations, removes dependence on debt markets for near-term obligations, and gives the company capacity to continue project launches, pursue selective acquisitions, and maintain dividends without external financing during a real estate downturn.

**Leverage trend driver in 2–3 sentences:** Gross debt fell from AED 19.0 bn (FY2021) to AED 10.4–10.6 bn (FY2023–FY2025) — a reduction of roughly AED 8.4 bn over four years — driven primarily by the maturity and repayment of shorter-dated bank facilities and two sukuk tranches (the First and Second Series Trust Certificates matured during FY2021–FY2022), without meaningful new issuance except for the three remaining sukuk series. At the same time, EBITDA grew from AED 7.8 bn (FY2021) to AED 24.1 bn (FY2025) — a 32.6% CAGR — driven by the Dubai off-plan property boom converting to IFRS 15 revenue; this EBITDA expansion is the dominant driver of the sharp gross-leverage compression from 2.44x to 0.44x. The net-cash position (broad basis) deepened from AED −1.6 bn (FY2023) to AED −17.3 bn (FY2025) as operating cash flow of AED 33.5 bn (FY2025) vastly exceeded the AED 11.2 bn of dividends paid and AED 934 mn capex. No material acquisitions funded by debt occurred in FY2023–FY2025; the balance-sheet improvement is entirely organic. ## 6A. HoldCo / OpCo & Structural Subordination

| Item | Evidence | Why It Matters |
|---|---|---|
| Where debt sits (HoldCo vs OpCo) | The three sukuk series (AED 6,424 mn combined) and the UAE revolving credit facility are issued at the Emaar Properties PJSC parent level. The EGP facilities (AED 603 mn) and PKR facility (AED 431 mn) are at Egyptian and Pakistani operating subsidiaries. The INR term loans (AED 1,694 mn) are at Indian subsidiary level. The AED term loan (AED 635 mn) appears to be at the UAE parent or directly owned subsidiary level. [Capital IQ Capital Structure Details, FY2025] | Parent-level debt (sukuk) is structurally senior to any debt at OpCo level, but the parent must rely on subsidiary dividend upstream to service its own obligations; conversely, OpCo-level debt in Egypt, Pakistan, and India is structurally senior to the parent in relation to those subsidiaries' assets |
| Upstreaming constraints (dividend blockers, regulatory) | No explicit upstreaming restriction is disclosed in the available English-language data pool. The UAE parent's cash position alone (AED 9,754 mn unrestricted) is well above its parent-level obligations. | If forex controls tighten (e.g., Egyptian pound continued devaluation in FY2023), the parent may not receive cash it needs to service the sukuk, though the parent's standalone liquidity appears sufficient |

**Summary characterisation:** Emaar is **net cash on the broad basis** by AED 17.3 bn at FY2025 and AED 24.6 bn at Q1-2026.

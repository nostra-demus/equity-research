## 2. What the Company Does

Emaar is the UAE's largest listed property developer. It builds branded master-community homes in Dubai — sold off-plan (before construction is finished), with buyers paying in instalments as construction milestones are hit. It also owns Dubai Mall, more than 40 hotels, and a large recurring-rent franchise.

The revenue model, in one line:

> Sell homes off-plan → collect buyer deposits into escrow → recognise revenue as construction milestones are certified (this is the IFRS 15 percentage-of-completion / "POC" method — revenue is booked as the building is built, not when it is handed over) + earn rent from Dubai Mall + earn room revenue from the hotels.

Segments (FY2025):
- **UAE Development (residential):** ~73% of revenue, ~66% of EBITDA (earnings before interest, tax, depreciation, amortisation — a rough measure of cash operating profit).
- **Malls / Leasing:** ~15% of revenue on an IFRS basis (~12% on the company's management view). Dubai Mall runs at over 98% occupancy with an 86% EBITDA margin.
- **Hospitality:** ~5% of revenue.
- FY2025 revenue: AED 49.6 Bn (+40% year-on-year). Global backlog (signed but not yet recognised): AED 154.8 Bn, of which AED 134.3 Bn is UAE.

## 3. The Variant Perception (§7)

**What everyone already knows.** Emaar looks cheap on reported multiples — EV/LTM EBITDA (enterprise value divided by last-twelve-months EBITDA — the "how many years of cash profit does the whole business cost" number) is 3.84x, against its own 17-quarter mean of 6.31x and regional peers at 6–14x. Dubai property has been in a five-year expansion. The AED 134.3 Bn backlog covers 3–4 years of revenue. The balance sheet is fortress-grade.

**What is probably already priced in.** The market is pricing a −21.3% annual free-cash-flow contraction for seven years (from a reverse-DCF — a discounted-cash-flow calculation run backwards, asking "what cash-flow path does the current price imply"). That would take FCF from AED 24.3 Bn today to AED 4.5 Bn — below the FY2020 COVID trough. The discount reflects (a) peak-cycle fears, (b) the government-owner overhang (no forced catalyst for the value to close), (c) the unexplained Emaar Misr subsidiary collapse, and (d) the UAE tax regime change.

**What the engine thinks the market may be missing.** The price implies an outcome that is physically impossible given contracts already signed. Even if Dubai demand fell 30% in FY2026, recognised revenue does not compress until FY2028–2029 because of AED 134.3 Bn of already-signed contracts. Separately, the Emaar Malls franchise (86% EBITDA margin, over 98% occupancy, AED 4,935 Mn EBITDA) is worth about AED 84 Bn on a 17x segment-appropriate multiple — nearly the whole company's enterprise value of AED 96.7 Bn. That value is invisible in a blended 3.84x multiple.

**What would prove the edge is real.** Q2 2026 UAE new property sales above AED 15 Bn (roughly 6 weeks away, at ~Aug-10-2026 results) plus the AED 2.75 Bn Sukuk 3 being repaid from cash on Sep-15-2026 with no refinancing. Both are dated, publicly observable, and would confirm the near-term bear thesis is not materialising.

**Edge score: 40/100.** The arithmetic is unusual but the government-owner discount may be fully rational. This is a real but limited edge, not a proprietary insight.

## 4. Why It Could Work — Bull Case

1. **Malls franchise re-rating.** Dubai Mall — 204M annual footfall, over 98% occupied, AED 4,935 Mn EBITDA at 86% margin — is structurally comparable to premium mall REITs (Real Estate Investment Trusts — listed property vehicles) at 20–25x EBITDA. Even at a conservative 17x segment multiple, the malls segment alone is worth AED 83,895 Mn — nearly the entire current enterprise value of AED 96,672 Mn. [FY2025 Investor Presentation, Slide 22]

2. **Backlog insulates earnings for 3–4 years.** AED 134.3 Bn of signed UAE contracts at Dec-31-2025 means that even a 30% collapse in new sales does not touch recognised revenue until FY2028–2029. The income statement runs on contracts already signed. [FY2025 Investor Presentation, Slide 16; Capital IQ FY2025 Revenue AED 49.6 Bn]

3. **The consensus bar has been cut to a multi-year low.** FY2026E EPS (earnings per share — profit divided by share count) was cut from AED 2.32 to AED 1.95 in three months. ## 5. Why It Could Fail — Bear Case & The Killer Risk (§8)

1. **Dubai residential cycle is at or near peak.** Five years of expansion; FY2025 unit volumes already fell 27% (13,905 vs 19,058 units). Value growth in FY2025 was purely price-per-unit — a classic late-cycle signal. A cycle break hits bookings first (leaving the backlog to convert), but the multiple compresses long before earnings do. [Earnings module, 02_revenue-drivers.md]

2. **The AED 200 Bn masterplan was announced at cycle peak with no phasing detail.** This is 84x one year of FY2025 FCF. No disclosed pre-sale gates. Classic government-developer risk: commit equity capital at peak using minority shareholder money. [Catalyst module, 01_catalyst-calendar.md]

3. **Emaar Misr operational collapse.** The Egyptian subsidiary's EGP-denominated net income fell 64% in FY2025 on flat EGP revenue — operational deterioration, not FX (foreign-currency) alone. Management has never explained this in English. [Capital IQ Key Developments, Feb-23-2026]

4. **Governance opacity.** RPT (related-party transactions — deals between Emaar and its owners / affiliated parties) note is Arabic-only. Board is 20% formally independent (2 of 10). Chairman Mohamed Alabbar concurrently chairs Eagle Hills, a competing developer, with 6+ concurrent board seats. CFO departed May 2026 with no disclosed reason. [Management-governance module, 05_board-and-shareholder-rights.md]

5. **Consensus revision breadth is negative.** 0 up / 3 down on EPS in the last three months. A single analyst drove a 22% EPS cut in June 2026 with a price-target cut from AED 20.50 to AED 15.00 — either an overreaction or the first of several. [Capital IQ Consensus export]

**The killer risk (one sentence):** Dubai Holding (the government-aligned controlling owner at 29.73%) pursues city-building objectives instead of per-share value, and the AED 200 Bn masterplan turns balance-sheet cash into cycle-peak capex — meaning the ~127% implied upside to base fair value never closes.

## 14. Plain-English Glossary

- **EBITDA** — earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation; a rough proxy for the cash operating profit of the business.
- **EPS** — earnings per share; net profit divided by the share count.
- **FCF (free cash flow)** — cash from operations minus capex; the cash the business actually throws off after keeping the asset base running.
- **Net cash / net debt (strict vs broad)** — strict = gross debt minus unrestricted cash; broad = gross debt minus unrestricted cash minus liquid short-term investments. Emaar's headline "AED 61.7 Bn net cash" nets in AED 42.9 Bn of restricted buyer escrow and MUST NOT be used as freely available liquidity.
- **EV / LTM EBITDA** — enterprise value divided by last-twelve-months EBITDA; "how many years of cash operating profit does the entire business cost to buy".
- **SOTP (sum-of-the-parts)** — valuing each business segment separately at its own multiple, then adding them up; useful when one segment (here Dubai Mall) is masked inside a blended group multiple.
- **DCF (discounted cash flow)** — projecting future cash flows and discounting them back to today at a cost-of-capital rate.
- **Reverse-DCF** — the same math run backwards: given today's price, what cash-flow path is the market implicitly pricing in?
- **Margin of safety** — the gap between the current price and the base fair value, expressed as a percentage of fair value; the buffer against being wrong.
- **IFRS 15 / percentage-of-completion (POC)** — the accounting rule under which an off-plan developer books revenue as construction milestones are certified, not when the buyer moves in.
- **Backlog** — signed but not yet recognised revenue from contracts already sold; here AED 134.3 Bn in the UAE.
- **Escrow (buyer deposits)** — instalment cash paid by home buyers that is legally ring-fenced for project completion and cannot be used for corporate purposes.
- **REIT (Real Estate Investment Trust)** — a listed property vehicle that owns rent-yielding assets; the reference set for valuing the Dubai Mall franchise.
- **Sukuk** — an Islamic-finance bond equivalent; Sukuk 3 is Emaar's USD 750 Mn (AED 2.75 Bn) instrument maturing Sep-15-2026.
- **Interest coverage (ICR)** — EBITDA divided by interest expense; how many times over the business can pay its interest bill. Emaar's is 49x.
- **RPT (related-party transaction)** — a commercial deal between the company and an owner or affiliate; the fairness of terms is a governance-critical disclosure.
- **DMTT (Domestic Minimum Top-up Tax)** — the UAE's implementation of the OECD 15% global minimum tax; it drove a ~413 bps net-margin cut in FY2025.
- **Basis points (bps)** — one hundredth of a percentage point; 100 bps = 1%.
- **WACC (weighted average cost of capital)** — the blended cost of the equity and debt used to fund the business; the discount rate in a DCF. Emaar's used here is 8.83%.
- **LTIP (long-term incentive plan)** — a multi-year executive compensation plan. Emaar's is phantom-share with 3-year vesting; performance conditions are not disclosed in English — the key alignment gap.

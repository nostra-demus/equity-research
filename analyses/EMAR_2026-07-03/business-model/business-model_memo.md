## What This Module Found

Emaar is the biggest listed home-builder in Dubai. It designs whole neighbourhoods, sells the homes off-plan (buyers pay in instalments while construction runs), and books revenue step-by-step as each building is completed under IFRS 15 accounting. It also owns Dubai Mall and 40 other malls and hotels, which throw off high-margin rent and room income year after year. FY2025 group revenue was AED 49.6 bn, up 40% on FY2024, and there is AED 134.3 bn of homes already sold but not yet delivered — about 3.7 years of current-pace UAE development revenue already locked in.

The single strongest positive is the location moat: 25 years of buying the best master-community land in Dubai (about 344 million sq ft), plus Dubai Mall (204 million visitors, >98% occupied, 86% EBITDA margin — the earnings before tax, interest, depreciation and amortisation are 86 cents on every rent dirham). No competitor can rebuild that position.

The single most important risk is that this is a cyclical developer showing peak numbers. Group EBITDA — the recurring operating earnings before tax, interest, depreciation and amortisation — went from AED 5.2 bn in the 2020 trough to AED 25.6 bn in FY2025, roughly 4.9x. Through-cycle return on invested capital (ROIC — the profit made on each dirham of capital tied up) is about 11–12% versus a cost of capital (WACC — what raising that money costs) of about 9–10%, a thin 100–200 basis point (1–2 percentage point) gap that could invert in a real downturn. Three reinforcing red flags are already in filed data: the Egyptian subsidiary Emaar Misr's net income fell 64% in FY2025 on flat revenue; AED 8.2 bn of reported operating cash flow was new buyer deposits (money that would reverse if sales slow), so the true recurring cash figure is closer to AED 25.3 bn than the AED 33.5 bn headline; and a new AED 200 bn masterplan was launched in June 2026, committing costs at what looks like cycle-peak conditions.

## The Specialists, Briefly

- **data-triage** — Pool sufficient; main gap is the FY2025 audited annual report (with English notes) not yet filed.
- **disqualifier-scan** — All 8 checks clean; auditor KPMG unchanged for 6 years; operating cash flow positive four straight years.
- **business-identity** — Three revenue streams: Development 73%, Malls 12%, Hospitality 5%; AED 154.8 bn global backlog covers 5–6 years of development revenue.
- **segment-map** — UAE Development is 73% of revenue and 66% of EBITDA; recurring businesses are 21% of revenue but 32% of EBITDA (they pull above their weight).
- **unit-economics** — Each new UAE home creates value at both current (46% EBITDA) and through-cycle (~38% EBITDA) margins; per-unit economics stayed positive across the FY2020–FY2025 cycle.
- **customer-geography** — No customer concentration, but geography is extreme: UAE is 93.1% of revenue, almost all Dubai.
- **value-chain** — Emaar controls economics — owns the land, prices before it builds, dominates its mall tenants; its biggest bargaining exposure is construction-cost inflation on already-sold homes.
- **business-quality** — Aggregate 51/100. Cyclicality scored 30/100 — EBITDA compounded 23% a year in this upcycle and dropped 67% between FY2019 and FY2020.
- **competitive-map** — Clear listed leader in Dubai (15.8% transaction share by volume in 2024); Aldar (Abu Dhabi) is the only public peer, ~29% EBITDA margin vs Emaar's ~52%.
- **moat** — Narrow. Location 72/100, brand 68/100, scale 63/100. Through-cycle ROIC spread over WACC is only ~100–200 bps.
- **external-dependency** — 52/100 (inverted — higher is worse). Three "high" dependencies: Dubai property cycle, government/ruler-directed development, UHNW/HNW consumer cycle.
- **capital-allocation-governance** — 74/100. Debt falling, dividends rising, share count flat. Open items: Arabic-only related-party note, Alabbar's concurrent chairmanship of Eagle Hills (a rival), CFO change May 2026.

## What Would Change This Read

**Would upgrade the read toward "high-quality":**
- Sustained ROIC spread of 300+ bps over WACC through a full property cycle (requires a downcycle to actually play out, not a claim).
- Recurring businesses (Malls + Hospitality) growing to 40%+ of EBITDA, cutting cycle dependence.

**Would downgrade the read further:**
- Dubai residential bookings showing a material year-on-year drop in new unit sales (the leading indicator).
- The Emaar Misr income collapse proving operational rather than FX-driven — that would imply AED 30.7 bn of international assets should be written down.

## Bottom Line

- Verdict is "Average business — worth deeper work only if valuation is cheap." Not a rejection; not a compounder either.
- Best reason it could be better than it looks: AED 134.3 bn of already-sold Dubai homes plus strict net cash of AED 17.6 bn (debt/EBITDA 0.44x) means the business can ride out a severe downturn without refinancing risk, and Dubai Mall throws off ~AED 5–6 bn of trough-year EBITDA on its own.
- Best reason it could be worse than it looks: three flags already reinforce each other in filed data — Emaar Misr's 64% income drop, AED 8.2 bn of headline operating cash flow that is really buyer deposits, and a new AED 200 bn commitment made at what looks like a cycle peak.
- Missing evidence: the FY2025 audited English annual report with the related-party note and an Emaar Misr project-level margin reconciliation. Without these, governance and international-asset quality cannot be fully cleared.
- One thing to watch next: Dubai new-unit bookings quarter-on-quarter — this is the leading indicator for both future revenue and buyer-deposit cash flow.

## Plain-English Glossary

- **EBITDA** — recurring operating earnings before tax, interest, depreciation and amortisation. Used here as a proxy for cash operating profit.
- **IFRS 15 / off-plan** — the accounting rule that lets a developer book a share of a pre-sold home's revenue as each construction milestone is passed, rather than all at handover.
- **Backlog** — the value of homes already sold to buyers but not yet delivered; future revenue that is contractually locked in.
- **Operating cash flow (OCF)** — cash generated by the business's day-to-day operations before investing and financing.
- **Net cash (strict basis)** — cash plus short-term investments minus total debt; the conservative version, before any restricted escrow is added back.
- **ROIC** — return on invested capital; the profit earned on each dirham of capital tied up.
- **WACC / cost of capital** — the blended cost of the equity and debt funding the business; the hurdle ROIC must clear to create value.
- **Basis points (bps)** — hundredths of a percentage point; 100 bps = 1%.
- **Debt/EBITDA** — total debt divided by annual EBITDA; how many years of operating profit it would take to repay debt.

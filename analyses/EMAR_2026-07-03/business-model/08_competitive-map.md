# Competitive Map — EMAR

## 1. Dominant Segment

Real Estate (UAE Development) — contributes 80% of group revenue (AED 39,550 Mn of AED 49,557 Mn total) and ~77% of segment profit before tax in FY2025. Within that, UAE Development alone is 73% of group revenue at AED 36,443 Mn. [Source: Capital IQ Annual Financials export — Segments tab, FY2025; FY2025 Investor Presentation, Feb-12-2026, slide 13]

## 2. Named Competitors

### Competitor A — Aldar Properties PJSC

- **Ticker / listing:** ADX:ALDAR, listed on the Abu Dhabi Securities Exchange (ADX)
- **Where they compete:** UAE residential and commercial property development (Abu Dhabi primary market, expanding into Dubai via acquisitions and JVs); investment property (income-producing assets, hotels, schools, marinas); asset management. Competes directly with Emaar in UAE master-community development and in the institutional real estate investment market.
- **Scale:** Revenue AED 23.0 bn in FY2024 (+62% YoY); development sales AED 33.6 bn in FY2024 (+20% YoY); development backlog AED 54.6 bn at end-FY2024. [Source: Aldar FY2024 Results Press Release, Feb 2025, zawya.com; stockanalysis.com/quote/adx/ALDAR/financials, accessed 2026-07-03, unverified web]
- **Profitability / return on capital:** Net profit AED 6.5 bn in FY2024; net profit margin ~24% (FY2024 net income AED 5,596 Mn on revenue AED 22,998 Mn per stockanalysis.com, consistent with press release). ROE 16.1% (FY2024, per Q4 2024 earnings call reported by gurufocus.com/yahoo finance, unverified web). ROIC approximately 9% (trailing twelve months per gurufocus.com data, unverified web). Cross-check with Capital IQ export (LTM as of ~Jun 2024): LTM net margin 23.0%, EBITDA margin 28.9%; these are earlier-period figures and directionally consistent. [Source: Capital IQ Comparable Analysis export, EMAAR comp set, Operating Statistics sheet, data as of ~Jun-28-2024; stockanalysis.com ADX:ALDAR income statement, accessed 2026-07-03, unverified web; gurufocus.com ROE/ROIC data, accessed 2026-07-03, unverified web]
- **Source named in:** Capital IQ Comparable Analysis export included Aldar as the only regional developer peer in Emaar's Capital IQ comp set. Also named in industry sources and broker research as Emaar's closest publicly listed peer in UAE master-community development. [Source: Capital IQ Comparable Analysis export, Company Comp Set, EMAR data pool]
- **One-line read:** Aldar is the Abu Dhabi government-backed equivalent of Emaar — a master-community developer with a growing Dubai footprint and a recurring investment-property business — but at roughly half Emaar's FY2025 revenue scale and with lower EBITDA margins (~29% vs Emaar's ~52%), reflecting a younger recurring-income portfolio and a higher proportion of construction-cost-intensive affordable segments.

### Competitor B — DAMAC Properties

- **Ticker / listing:** Formerly DFM:DAMAC; delisted from the Dubai Financial Market in February–March 2022 after being taken private by founder Hussain Sajwani. Now a private company. [Source: The National, "Damac to officially delist from Dubai Financial Market," Mar 2022, unverified web]
- **Where they compete:** UAE luxury residential development (Dubai primary market — high-rise branded residences, off-plan villas, waterfront communities); growing Saudi Arabia and international pipeline. Competes directly with Emaar in Dubai's off-plan residential market, particularly in the luxury and ultra-luxury branded segment.
- **Scale:** Revenue approximately AED 8.8 bn in 2024 (+69% YoY); development sales approximately AED 36 bn in 2025. By transaction count, DAMAC held approximately 9.7% of Dubai residential market by volume in 2024 (vs Emaar's ~15.8%). [Source: Web: apilproperties.com citing DAMAC 2024 results, unverified; LinkedIn post citing DLD transaction data for 2024, unverified web, accessed 2026-07-03]
- **Profitability / return on capital:** Net profit approximately AED 4.2 bn in 2024; implied net profit margin ~32% on AED 13.07 bn revenue per Wikipedia / web sources — note conflicting revenue figures in circulation (AED 8.8 bn vs AED 13.07 bn from different sources likely reflect different consolidation bases); the net margin figure is therefore unreliable without the audited filing. ROIC/ROE: not disclosed; private company with no public financial statements. Figures marked "not public / not reliably disclosed" for the margin and return-on-capital lines. [Source: Web: multiple unverified sources including Wikipedia, apilproperties.com; S&P Global rating rationale references available but margin/ROIC not extracted; all unverified web, accessed 2026-07-03]
- **Source named in:** Named as a primary peer of Emaar in industry analyses, Dubai Land Department transaction rankings, and multiple broker comparisons. Not named in Emaar's own filings (which do not name competitors by name), but consistently appears in industry market-share tables. [Source: House and Hedges, "Top 10 Key Players in Dubai's Developers 2025," unverified web; DLD transaction data cited in multiple broker sources, unverified web]
- **One-line read:** DAMAC is Emaar's closest competitor in Dubai off-plan luxury residential — similar price points and buyer profile — but without Emaar's recurring mall and hotel income stream, and without public financial disclosure since its 2022 privatisation, making direct margin comparison impossible.

### Competitor C — Nakheel / Dubai Holding (Government-Owned)

- **Ticker / listing:** Not listed; state-owned. Nakheel was merged into Dubai Holding in early 2024 and no longer operates as a separate entity. Dubai Holding is a wholly owned investment vehicle of the Government of Dubai.
- **Where they compete:** Landmark waterfront residential communities in Dubai (Palm Jumeirah, Palm Jebel Ali relaunch, Deira Islands); destination retail (Dragon Mart, Golden Mile Galleria — approximately 6 million sq ft of retail GLA); hospitality. Competes with Emaar for land parcels, residential buyers, and tenant mix in Dubai's premium waterfront locations.
- **Scale:** Revenue not publicly disclosed since the merger into Dubai Holding (2024). Historical reference: 2022 revenue approximately USD 3.4 bn equivalent per ZoomInfo/MEED; 2020 net profit was AED 5.67 bn (~USD 1.54 bn). As a state entity post-merger, consolidated financials are not publicly available in a form comparable to Emaar's DFM filings. [Source: Web: MEED company profile, vocal.media citing historical Nakheel figures, unverified web; Wikipedia, unverified web]
- **Profitability / return on capital:** Not public / not disclosed. As a state-owned entity merged into Dubai Holding, no public income statement or return-on-capital metrics are available. [Source: not public / not disclosed]
- **Source named in:** Named in industry reports and broker research as a key Dubai developer and competitor for land and waterfront residential buyers. Named alongside Emaar and DAMAC in multiple broker surveys of the Dubai developer landscape. [Source: Web: hsproperty.ae, evantisrealty.com, eplogoffplan.com comparative developer articles, unverified web, accessed 2026-07-03]
- **One-line read:** Nakheel / Dubai Holding is a government-controlled developer that controls the most iconic waterfront land in Dubai (Palm Jumeirah, Deira Islands) and competes with Emaar for premium buyers and retail tenants — but being state-owned and now folded into an opaque holding structure, it does not compete for public equity capital and its financial performance cannot be benchmarked.

## 3. Competitive Position

Emaar is gaining share — or at minimum holding a large and expanding position — in the dominant UAE Development segment. Its Dubai transaction market share of approximately 15.8% by volume in 2024 (approximately 17,250 transactions) placed it first among all developers, ahead of DAMAC at 9.7% and Sobha at 8.3% per DLD data cited in industry sources. [Source: Web: LinkedIn post citing DLD 2024 data, unverified web; houseandhedges.ae "Top 10 Key Players," unverified web, accessed 2026-07-03] Emaar's own filings provide corroborating evidence: FY2025 UAE property sales of AED 71.1 bn (+16% vs FY2024) and a backlog of AED 134.3 bn in the UAE — the latter represents roughly 3.7x FY2025 UAE development revenue, which is a structural lead that smaller developers cannot replicate without comparable land banks (Emaar holds c. 344 million sq ft of UAE land bank). [Source: FY2025 Investor Presentation, Feb-12-2026, slides 16–17] The self-described rank of "1st in revenue in Emerging Markets (excluding China)" under MSCI classification and "1st in net profit" per MSCI/Reuters data as of Dec 31, 2025 is an Emaar-sourced claim and should be treated as unverified marketing language pending third-party confirmation, but the underlying financial scale differential vs Aldar (Emaar FY2025 revenue AED 49.6 bn vs Aldar FY2024 revenue AED 23 bn) is directionally consistent with a position of clear market leadership. [Source: FY2025 Investor Presentation, Feb-12-2026, slide 6, footnote citing MSCI, Datastream, Reuters]

## 4. Competitive Shape

The Dubai premium master-community developer market is an oligopoly in practical terms, with 3–5 large players — Emaar, DAMAC, Nakheel/Dubai Holding, Sobha, and Aldar (newly active in Dubai) — controlling the majority of off-plan sales by value. By volume, Emaar's 15.8% share in 2024 followed by DAMAC at 9.7% and Sobha at 8.3% implies the top 3 control approximately 34% of transactions; the market is meaningfully fragmented by transaction count (hundreds of smaller developers operate), but highly concentrated by sales value, where Emaar's AED 71.1 bn in UAE sales dwarfs most peers by a factor of 2x or more. Formal HHI data is not publicly available. The more precise characterisation is: concentrated at the premium/master-community end (3–4 dominant players each with large land banks and brand recognition), fragmented at the mid-market and off-plan investor segment (dozens of smaller operators). The state-owned segment (Nakheel/Dubai Holding, Meraas) adds a non-commercial competitor that controls key land corridors, which creates an asymmetric competitive dynamic: Emaar and private developers bid for the same buyers but cannot replicate the government's access to waterfront land allocation. [Source: Web: houseandhedges.ae, LinkedIn DLD transaction data, unverified web, 2026-07-03; FY2025 Investor Presentation, Feb-12-2026, slide 16 for Emaar sales figures]

## 5. Caveat

Emaar's own filings (annual reports, investor presentations) do not name specific competitors — this is a distinctive feature of the company's disclosures; the risk factors section addresses generic "competition" risk without naming peers. As a result:

1. The three competitors profiled here are sourced from: (a) the Capital IQ Comparable Analysis export in the data pool (which named Aldar as the only formal regional comp); (b) Dubai Land Department transaction data cited in industry and broker sources (unverified web); and (c) well-established industry knowledge about the Dubai developer landscape.

2. DAMAC's financial figures are unreliable because the company is private and multiple unverified sources disagree on its revenue base (AED 8.8 bn vs AED 13.07 bn). No audited DAMAC financials are available.

3. Nakheel's financials are entirely unavailable post its merger into Dubai Holding in 2024.

4. Aldar is the only named, publicly listed, auditable peer with comparable metrics — but it is primarily an Abu Dhabi developer rather than a direct Dubai competitor, which limits the precision of peer margin comparisons for the dominant UAE Development segment.

5. The `moat` agent should treat Emaar's development margin (46% EBITDA) as genuinely differentiated versus Aldar's ~29% EBITDA margin, while noting that the gap partly reflects Emaar's more mature recurring-income base and its lower cost UAE land bank assembled over 25+ years — not only pricing power in the development cycle.

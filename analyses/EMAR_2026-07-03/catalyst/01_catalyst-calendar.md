## 2. The Nearest Dated Catalyst

**Q2 2026 results, estimated around Aug-10-2026 (CIQ-derived date; Investing.com reports Aug-12-2026, unverified, pulled 2026-07-03).**

This is the nearest catalyst with a near-proven date — ~6 weeks away. The consensus bar has been slashed: FY2026 EPS was cut from AED 2.46 to AED 1.93 (−22%) in a single analyst revision on Jun-11-2026, driving the Q2 EPS bar to AED 0.47. Emaar has beaten EPS in every year from FY2023 through Q1 2026, with surprise margins of +9% to +23%. Against this lowered bar and a track record of consistent outperformance, the setup favors a beat — particularly if the AED 134.3 Bn UAE backlog continues to convert at pace and EBITDA margin holds in the 47–51% corridor it has maintained for eight consecutive quarters. The two-sided read: a beat (EPS above AED 0.52) combined with sustained Q2 new UAE property sales above AED 15 Bn would begin reversing the estimate-cut narrative that drove the stock to AED 12.20; a miss (EPS below AED 0.43) or confirmation of a new-sales deceleration would validate the Jun-11-2026 analyst's re-assessment and likely trigger further cuts toward the AED 15.00 analyst target that was set then.

## 3. The Single Most Important Catalyst

**The AED 200 Bn Masterplan Capital Discipline Disclosure — H2 FY2026, undated.**

In absolute potential impact, this exceeds even the Q2 earnings event. If management discloses a disciplined, phased execution plan — consistent with the 93% pre-sale rate it has achieved on the existing AED 154.8 Bn backlog — the AED 200 Bn commitment is a 15–20 year option on Dubai's urban expansion that is entirely in line with Emaar's historic model. The market's current valuation of AED 12.20 already prices in an absurd level of pessimism (−21.3% annual FCF contraction to FY2032, per the reverse-DCF). A credible phasing plan for a pipeline that is multiples larger than the existing backlog would shift the market's mental model from "cycle peak" to "multi-decade compounder" and compress the discount to fair value (base AED 27.7, bull AED 34.2). The bearish path is sharper: if the plan reveals Emaar is committing equity capital ahead of buyer deposits at cycle-peak prices, it would confirm the §24 Filter 5 concern (unprecedented scale, fast-changing industry dynamics) and flip the capital-allocation score from its current 82/100 "very strong" toward distress-of-discipline. A buyer cannot fully time this — no date is scheduled — but every quarterly results announcement and any board-meeting intimation to DFM is a potential disclosure window.

## 5. Calendar Read

The 12-month forward calendar has two events with near-proven or hard dates: the Q2 2026 results (~Aug-10-2026) and the Sukuk 3 maturity (Sep-15-2026). Both are monitoring events rather than existential events — the balance sheet comfortably absorbs the sukuk, and the earnings setup favors a beat against a sharply lowered bar. Everything else — the AED 200 Bn masterplan disclosure, the re-rating trigger, the FY2026 results, and the governance clarifications — sits in soft windows, dependent on either quarterly cadence or event-driven DFM disclosures. The near-term skew is cautiously bullish (consensus bar low, beat track record intact, balance sheet fortress) but the single most important open question — whether management applies proven capital discipline to the unprecedented AED 200 Bn commitment — is undated and therefore cannot carry conviction. The government-ownership structure (§24 Filter 6) is the timing caveat that matters most: even if fair value is AED 27.7 and the backlog evidence supports it, a value-indifferent government owner with no catalyst to close the AED 12.20-to-AED 27.7 gap means the timing of any re-rating is genuinely unknown.

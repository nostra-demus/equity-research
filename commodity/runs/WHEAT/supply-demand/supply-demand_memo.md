## What This Module Found

Global wheat is in a supply deficit for the 2026/27 marketing year (MY): the world is on track to use about 4 million tonnes (MMT) more than it grows, drawing down stocks [USDA WASDE Jul-2026]. Headline world ending stocks look like they are rebuilding — 272.8 MMT vs 261.5 MMT last year — but that rebuild is entirely China and India adding to captive reserves that never reach the export market [USDA WASDE Jul-2026]. The buffer that actually sets prices for import-dependent buyers is the major-exporter stocks-to-use ratio (exporter STU: exporter stockpiles as a share of yearly use). It is falling to about 8.0% in 2026/27 from about 8.4% in 2025/26 — the tightest since the early 2010s, when 2007–08 and 2010–11 prices spiked [USDA Wheat Outlook May-2026].

The production shortfall is broad-based across the US (-9.4 MMT), Australia (-9.3 MMT), Argentina (-6.9 MMT), the EU (-8 MMT), and Canada (-4 MMT), only partially offset by Russia (+5.5 MMT), India (+3.1 MMT), and China (+1.0 MMT) [USDA WASDE Jul-2026; ABARES Crop Report Jun-2026]. US hard red winter wheat (HRW) is at a 56-year low — winter-wheat production fell about 27% year on year to roughly 1,030 million bushels (Mbu), and USDA raised the season-average US farm price to $6.00/bu from $5.06/bu [USDA NASS Jul 2026 Crop Production, 2026-07-10]. That HRW damage is booked, not speculative.

The single biggest forward risk is El Niño (a Pacific warming pattern that shifts world rainfall). The Niño-3.4 index is already at +1.2°C, and NOAA/CPC gives an 81% probability of a very strong event (≥ +2.0°C) by Oct–Dec 2026 [NOAA/CPC ENSO Diagnostic Discussion, 2026-07-09]. That threatens Australia's 2026/27 crop, currently forecast at 26.7 MMT on planted area already down about 20% [ABARES Australian Crop Report Jun-2026], and it lines up with the US HRW autumn 2026 planting window — meaning the tightening could extend into 2027/28.

## What Would Change This Read

The synthesis names the specific, observable events that would move the module's read:

- **Australia crop below 26.7 MMT** — if El Niño rainfall damage pushes Australia toward the lower end of the 21–27 MMT range or below, each 5 MMT shortfall is roughly a 10% cut in Australia's export availability. That would deepen the deficit.
- **US spring wheat (HRS) heat/drought stress July–August** — spring wheat was 54% headed as of 5 July and is now in the most weather-sensitive grain-fill window [USDA NASS Crop Progress, 2026-07-05]. Stress here adds to an already historically low US all-wheat figure.
- **Russia coming in below USDA's 88 MMT** — the range between USDA (88 MMT) and Argus's private estimate (91.2 MMT) is ~3 MMT; a below-USDA outturn would remove the single largest supply offset. First survey-based read: USDA August WASDE, 2026-08-12.
- **India relaxing the 2022-era wheat export ban** — could add several MMT of supply; unresolved policy overhang, not priced into current USDA export projections. This would loosen the balance.
- **Black Sea shipping disruption** — would remove Russia's cap on CBOT upside; a tail risk on the tightening side.

## Bottom Line

- Balance: deficit ~4 MMT in MY 2026/27 [USDA WASDE Jul-2026]. Direction: tightening.
- The buffer that matters — the exporter STU — is near 8%, its tightest since the early 2010s, and still falling [USDA Wheat Outlook May-2026]. The "rebuilding" world stocks headline (272.8 MMT) is misleading because China and India hold it captive.
- Biggest reason it could be worse than it looks: a strong El Niño (Niño-3.4 +1.2°C, 81% probability of ≥ +2.0°C by Oct–Dec 2026 [NOAA/CPC, 2026-07-09]) could cut Australia below 26.7 MMT AND hurt US HRW autumn planting, extending the tightening into 2027/28.
- Biggest reason it could be better than it looks: Russia's 88 MMT crop (largest since 2022/23) with a zero floating export duty as of mid-July caps CBOT upside [UkrAgroConsult, 2026-07; USDA WASDE Jun-2026]; India relaxing its export ban would loosen the balance further.

## Plain-English Glossary

- **MMT** — million metric tonnes.
- **MY 2026/27** — marketing year, the 12-month accounting window the wheat trade uses (not the calendar year).
- **Stocks-to-use ratio (STU)** — stockpile divided by yearly use; how many months of supply are on hand.
- **Exporter STU** — the same measure but only for the eight major exporting countries, stripping out China and India whose stocks never reach world trade; this is the buffer importers actually face.
- **HRW / HRS** — hard red winter wheat / hard red spring wheat, the two main US wheat classes; HRW is planted in autumn, HRS in spring.
- **Mbu** — million bushels (US grain unit); roughly 27.2 kg per bushel of wheat.
- **WASDE** — the USDA's monthly World Agricultural Supply and Demand Estimates report; the reference balance sheet the market trades off.
- **El Niño** — a Pacific Ocean warming pattern that shifts world rainfall; the Niño-3.4 index measures how far sea-surface temperatures in that patch are above normal.
- **CBOT** — the Chicago Board of Trade, where the benchmark wheat futures contract trades.

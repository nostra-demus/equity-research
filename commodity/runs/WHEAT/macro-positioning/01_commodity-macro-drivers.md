## 2. The driver that matters most now

**Black Sea war risk — simultaneous disruption of Russia's Azov corridor and Ukraine's Odesa export infrastructure** is the single dominant macro driver as of July 12, 2026.

Ukraine's drone campaign against Russian shipping in the Sea of Azov (76+ vessels struck July 10–11, Kerch Strait and Don–Azov Canal both suspended to civilian traffic as of July 10–11) threatens up to 25% of Russia's wheat export capacity just as the new marketing year opens — the period normally expected to see the highest Russian export volumes. At the same time, sustained Russian airstrikes on Odesa, Chornomorsk, and Pivdennyi terminals could cut Ukraine's monthly grain shipments by a third (~2 MT/month). Euronext wheat futures already rose ~4% on July 11 in direct response.

**What would flip it:** A ceasefire or negotiated maritime passage agreement — even a temporary humanitarian corridor of the type seen in 2022 under the Black Sea Grain Initiative — would remove the war-risk premium rapidly. The underlying fundamentals (Russia's large crop, global stocks at 275 MT) are moderately bearish on their own, so if the disruption premium unwinds, CBOT wheat would likely retrace toward the $5.50–5.80/bu range implied by supply/demand alone. Conversely, escalation to Ukrainian Odesa port closures lasting more than 2–3 weeks would drive a further leg higher.

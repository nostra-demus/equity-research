## 2. What This Module Found

Gold ran vertically for 26 months to $5,597 in late January 2026 and has since fallen 25% to $4,192.40 (COMEX GCQ26, 2026-07-03). The correction is not random — it tracks the rise in the 10-year real yield (the after-inflation yield on Treasuries, TIPS) from about +1.75% at the peak to +2.25% today. At the World Gold Council's own sensitivity of about 1.75% gold-price move per 25 bps of nominal-yield change, that 50-bps rise accounts for the bulk of the roughly $1,400 fall.

The physical picture in isolation is near-neutral: mine supply is rising 2–2.4% a year, central banks bought about 244 t in Q1 2026 (plus 41 t net in May), and Chinese bar and coin demand hit a record 207 t in Q1. But investment demand — ETFs and futures — is doing all the work. Jewellery volume (the price-sensitive buyer) is already down 18% in FY2025 and 23% year-on-year in Q1 2026; it cannot absorb slack at current prices. ETF holdings of 4,086 t sit near the February peak of 4,176 t — that is both strong prior demand and a latent supply overhang if flows turn.

The single most important driver is the real yield — until it durably falls, the setup is not-yet Buy. The single biggest risk is a large ETF investor reversal at scale: the ETF channel swung 804 t in one year (FY2024 to FY2025), and even a 5% net redemption on 4,086 t equals about 200 t of selling with no mine change needed.

## 6. Plain-English Glossary

- **Real yield** — the 10-year Treasury yield after taking expected inflation out (measured by TIPS). Higher real yields mean cash-like alternatives pay more, and gold, which pays nothing, becomes less attractive.
- **DXY (dollar index)** — a measure of the US dollar against a basket of major currencies. Gold is priced in dollars, so a stronger dollar generally weighs on the gold price.
- **Contango / roll drag** — when futures contracts for later delivery cost more than the front-month, a futures holder pays a small cost every time they "roll" from one contract into the next. Here roll drag is about 3.5% a year.
- **CFTC net length** — how much more speculative buying than selling shows up in the weekly futures positioning report. +181K contracts is high by recent history, meaning many traders are already long — less room for new buyers to push prices up.
- **ETF flows** — how much money is going into or out of gold-backed exchange-traded funds (GLD, GLDM, etc.). Persistent outflows mean funds sell physical gold to meet redemptions, adding to supply.
- **Dot plot / SEP** — the Federal Reserve's published chart at select meetings showing each policymaker's forecast for the policy rate. The September 16 meeting includes one; July 29 does not.
- **Death cross** — a technical signal where a shorter-term moving average (50-day) crosses below a longer-term one (200-day). It is a bearish trend signal.
- **PCE** — the Fed's preferred inflation gauge (Personal Consumption Expenditures). Currently projected at 3.6% year-end.

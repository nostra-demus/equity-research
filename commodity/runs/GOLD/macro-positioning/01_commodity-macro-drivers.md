## 2. The driver that matters most now

**Dominant driver: 10-year real yield (TIPS / DFII10)**

At +2.25%, the 10y real yield is the single variable most directly responsible for gold's correction from the $5,595 peak. Gold's entire January–June bear move is mechanically consistent with the real-yield repricing: markets shifted from pricing Fed cuts to pricing a possible hike, pushing real rates ~40–50 bps higher from their late-2025 trough. The WGC's own sensitivity framework estimates a 25 bp move in the 10y nominal yield equates to roughly a 1.75% swing in gold — at the scale of the move seen, this fully accounts for the bulk of the ~$1,400 correction.

**What would flip it:**

A sustained drop in the 10y real yield back toward or below +1.5% — which would require one or more of: (a) US inflation data coming in materially below the Fed's 3.6% PCE projection and reopening the door to 2026 cuts, (b) a significant weakening in economic data (the June 57k jobs print is a step in this direction — markets have already begun scaling back hike bets), or (c) a flight-to-safety Treasury rally driven by a new acute geopolitical or financial shock. The July 3 rebound to ~$4,187 (+$80 from the June 24 low) was triggered by weak jobs data and a partial real-yield retreat — showing how quickly the dynamic can reverse if the rate-hike path comes off the table. A confirmed non-farm payrolls trend of sub-100k for two or more consecutive months would be the clearest near-term trigger.

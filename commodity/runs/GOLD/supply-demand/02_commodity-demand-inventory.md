### 1c. Biggest demand swing factors

1. **Bar and coin physical investment — Asia-led safe-haven and alternative-asset buying.** The single biggest driver of the Q1 2026 demand surprise was Chinese retail physical buying, at an all-time record 207 t in one quarter. This reflects the interplay of weak domestic equity and property markets, yuan depreciation pressure, and a cultural shift toward gold as a savings vehicle. Indian retail investment also surpassed jewellery for the first time. Whether this pace sustains into Q2–Q3 2026 depends on the trajectory of Chinese risk sentiment and currency pressure — any meaningful equity recovery or yuan stabilisation could compress this demand rapidly.

2. **Gold-backed ETF flows — Western institutional money.** In 2025 full-year, ETF inflows totalled +801 t (from –3 t in 2024), adding a major new demand layer. In Q1 2026, flows slowed to +62 t as US-listed funds saw March outflows. The direction of North American and European institutional flows — driven by real-yield expectations and geopolitical risk appetite — is the most volatile demand swing factor and can shift hundreds of tonnes in a single quarter.

## 2. Inventory / Buffer

For gold, there is no tight stocks-to-use ratio like an agricultural commodity. The inventory picture has three layers: (a) global above-ground stocks (the vast cumulative stock), (b) ETF and physically-backed product holdings (the fastest-moving visible buffer), and (c) COMEX exchange warehouse stocks (the exchange-delivery buffer).

**COMEX data note:** Two web sources report different totals for late June–early July 2026 (27.6M oz per one source as of 2026-06-29; 23.4M oz per another as of 2026-07-02). The CME Group's official Daily Metal Stocks Report is the authoritative source; the figures above are drawn from secondary aggregators and labelled unverified. The registered inventory figure (~14.8–15.1M oz, ~460–470 t) — the gold bearing an active delivery warrant — is the operationally relevant number for COMEX settlement risk, and neither source signals stress at these levels.

**Buffer assessment — COMFORTABLE with a caveat:**

Gold's primary inventory buffer is the cumulative above-ground stock (~220,000 t) and ETF holdings (~4,121 t globally), which together dwarf annual mine supply of ~3,700 t. Unlike agricultural commodities, gold is not destroyed in consumption — it is recycled. The relevant scarcity signal for price is not stocks-to-use in the agricultural sense but rather the **marginal allocation decision** of holders: when real yields fall or macro uncertainty rises, existing above-ground gold shifts from passive storage to active investment demand (ETF inflows, bar buying), tightening the "effective" market supply. At 4,121 t and near the all-time record of 4,176 t, global ETF holdings signal that institutional and retail investors globally have allocated heavily to gold — this represents a large latent supply if sentiment reverses, but also confirms that the current demand-driven price rise has broad participation. COMEX registered stocks at ~460–470 t are adequate for near-term futures delivery, with no visible stress.

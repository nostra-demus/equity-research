# Supply–Demand Balance — GOLD (module synthesis)

## Abstract

Gold's 2025 supply-demand balance ended near equilibrium at roughly 5,002 t on both sides of the ledger — technically a surplus at the headline level, but that framing is misleading for gold because above-ground stock never leaves the world. The operationally relevant story is a structural shift in demand: ETF inflows added 801 t in 2025 (the second-strongest year on record) and bar/coin investment jumped +16% YoY, while jewellery — the volume-heavy segment — fell 18% as price-sensitive buyers stepped back. Inventory buffers are comfortable to near-record: global ETF holdings stand at 4,121 t as of May 31, 2026 (near the all-time peak of 4,176 t set February 2026), and COMEX registered stocks remain adequate at roughly 460–470 t with no delivery stress. The direction in 2026 is modest supply growth (+2–2.4% mine output, +5% recycling per Metals Focus) meeting demand dominated by investment and central-bank flows — with the single biggest swing factor being the sustainability of institutional (ETF) and Chinese retail (bar/coin) investment demand, not weather or mine disruption.

## Balance (surplus / deficit)

**FY2025 — near-balance, fractionally on the demand-led side.**

| Component | 2025 (t) | 2024 (t) | Source |

At the annual balance level, supply and demand rounded to identical figures for 2025. This is typical for gold, where the WGC balance is constructed to zero out by including the residual "OTC and other" as the balancing item. The more informative read is the composition shift:

- **Demand that "consumes" gold (jewellery + technology):** 1,865 t in 2025, down from 2,213 t in 2024 (-16%), driven entirely by price-induced jewellery volume destruction.
- **Demand that "stores" gold (investment + central banks + ETFs):** 3,038 t in 2025, up from 2,279 t in 2024 (+33%), as financial and official buyers absorbed what volume consumers stepped away from.
- **Net result:** gold moved from consumers' wrists to institutional vaults. Price cleared the market at higher levels. No physical shortage; instead, a repricing of who holds gold and why.

**Q1 2026 — same pattern, amplified.**

Total supply was 1,231 t; total demand was 1,231 t (balanced, as expected by WGC methodology). Bar and coin demand hit 473.6 t — the second-highest quarterly reading on record — with Chinese retail buying at an all-time country record of ~207 t. ETF inflows were a much smaller +62 t (from +230 t in Q1 2025), signalling some fading of the western institutional surge. Central-bank purchases continued at 243.7 t (+3% YoY). Jewellery demand fell further to 299.7 t (-23% YoY) in China (-32%) and India (-19%).

**2026 full-year balance (forward-looking):** No official WGC published tonnage balance for 2026 exists at this date. Metals Focus projects mine supply rising to ~3,907 t and recycling to ~1,475 t [Metals Focus via Kitco, 2026-06-05, labelled unverified vendor estimate], giving total supply near 5,250 t including residual hedging adjustments. Whether demand matches this depends almost entirely on whether western institutional ETF flows and Chinese retail bar/coin buying hold at 2025–Q1 2026 levels. If they do, demand holds above 5,000 t. If the investment-demand layer retreats — as real yields rise or risk appetite returns to equities — the headline balance tips into a visible demand shortfall vs. the 2025 base. Quantification of the 2026 gap is not possible from available data; the direction of risk is two-sided and investment-demand-driven.

## Direction & biggest swing factor

**Direction: supply rising modestly; demand composition shifting decisively toward investment — net price-neutral to mildly price-supportive on balance, contingent on investment demand holding.**

- Mine supply is up ~+2% YoY in 2025 (revised to 3,815 t) and on track for a further ~+2.4% in 2026 (~3,907 t) per Metals Focus. This is gradual, not a step-change. [WGC Q1-2026 supply page; Metals Focus via Kitco, 2026-06-05, unverified vendor estimate]
- Recycling is accelerating (+3% in 2025, +5% in Q1 2026 YoY) but is being constrained by holders treating gold as collateral rather than selling at record prices. [WGC Gold Demand Trends FY2025; WGC Q1-2026 supply page]
- Producer de-hedging continues (net negative ~74 t in 2025 and ~20 t in Q1 2026), removing a small but consistent net amount from market-available supply. [WGC Gold Demand Trends FY2025; WGC Q1-2026 supply page]

**Biggest swing factor: Western institutional ETF flows.**

Bar/coin physical buying by Chinese retail investors (record ~207 t in Q1 2026 alone [WGC GDT Q1-2026]) is the largest single demand surprise of the current cycle. But the ETF channel is the most volatile: it swung from –3 t in full-year 2024 to +801 t in full-year 2025 — a 804 t reversal in a single year [WGC Gold Demand Trends FY2025]. A further swing in 2026, whether a continuation, slowdown (as seen in Q1 2026 at only +62 t), or outright reversal, would dominate all supply-side changes. The Mali restart (~22–30 t restored output), ASGM revisions (±100–150 t uncertainty band), and recycling acceleration are all second-order compared to the magnitude of ETF flow reversals.

The India monsoon (IMD forecast ~90% of long-period average; June 2026 actual ~40% below the June normal [Business Standard citing IMD, 2026-06-30, unverified]) is a mild bearish seasonal risk to India jewellery demand in Q4 2026, but it is not the swing factor for gold's price or balance. India jewellery was already under severe volume pressure before the monsoon shortfall (-19% YoY in Q1 2026) — the monsoon adds to an existing volume headwind in a segment that is a progressively smaller share of the price-setting demand pool.

**Seasonal position (as of July 3, 2026):** The current window is the seasonal trough for physical jewellery demand globally. The India festival season (Navratri, Dhanteras, Diwali) is roughly 12 weeks away; Chinese New Year restocking is approximately 6 months away. This seasonal softness does not pressure gold price, since investment demand is not seasonally driven.

## 2. Direction & Swing Factors

**Supply direction: rising — mine supply is at a record and heading modestly higher in 2026; recycling accelerating from a low base.**

- **Mine supply: +2% YoY in 2025 (revised) and on track for a further ~+2.4% in 2026 per Metals Focus.** The primary driver is incremental output from new projects (Blackwater, Back River, Valentine Lake in Canada; Salares Norte recovery in Chile; Ahafo North in Ghana) and ASGM growth responding to gold near all-time highs. High prices (~$4,100–4,200/oz spot in July 2026) expand the economically viable ore envelope for both large-scale and artisanal producers. However, the growth rate is modest — not a step change. [WGC Gold Demand Trends Q1-2026, supply page; Metals Focus via Kitco, 2026-06-05]

- **Swing factor 1 — Mali / Loulo-Gounkoto restart (bullish for supply, previously a major drag).** Barrick's Loulo-Gounkoto mine was suspended throughout much of 2025 after a government dispute, costing approximately 723,000 oz (roughly 22.5 t attributable) vs a full operating year. Barrick settled with Mali in late 2025 (paying ~$430 million), regained operational control on 18 December 2025, and is guiding 260,000–290,000 attributable oz for 2026. This represents a partial but significant supply restoration. In Q1 2026 the WGC noted Mali output +30% YoY as the restart came through. The mine is not back to pre-dispute levels and won't be until at least 2027. [Barrick / Ecofin Agency, 2026-02; WGC Q1-2026 supply page, 2026-05]

- **Swing factor 2 — Recycling acceleration, constrained by collateralisation behaviour.** Scrap supply rose 5% YoY in Q1 2026 to 366 t. India recycling surged 44% QoQ and 20% YoY as consumers liquidated at record prices. However, WGC notes recycling is "expected to be constrained by low near-market stocks, expectations of continued price strength, and a likely embedded geopolitical risk premium" — meaning holders are retaining gold as collateral for loans rather than selling it. This caps how far recycling can run even with prices at $4,100+/oz. The muted 2025 scrap response (+3% despite a 67% average-price rise) shows this dynamic is already operating. [WGC Gold Demand Trends Full Year 2025, 2026-01-29; WGC Q1-2026 outlook page, 2026-05]

- **Producer hedging: a persistent net negative supply flow.** The industry hedge book fell to ~118 t in Q4 2025 (the lowest since 2013) and declined a further ~20 t in Q1 2026. Miners are de-hedging legacy positions locked in at prices far below spot, and new hedging is minimal given investor preference for full price exposure. De-hedging (buying back sold-forward gold) acts as a small but consistent net negative to supply. This subtracts from market-available supply rather than adding to it, and is likely to continue in 2026. [WGC Gold Demand Trends Q1-2026, supply page; WGC / Metals Focus, 2026-05]

- **Diesel / energy risk in Oceania and Asia: a downside tail for 2026 production.** WGC explicitly flags that "some production disruptions from diesel shortages may materialise in mines in Oceania and Asia which could temper this growth." Australia already saw -7% YoY output in Q1 2026, partly from weather disruptions and lower grades. This is a stated risk to the 2026 growth thesis, not a settled outcome. [WGC Q1-2026 outlook page, 2026-05]

- **Note on convention: central-bank flows sit on the DEMAND side.** By WGC and standard gold accounting convention, official-sector (central-bank) purchases and sales are classified as demand, not supply. Central banks bought a net 244 t in Q1 2026 and are forecast to purchase ~850 t in 2026 [WGC Q1-2026 central banks page, 2026-05]. These flows are large relative to mine supply but belong to the demand side of the ledger and are not included in the supply figures above.

### 1c. Biggest demand swing factors

1. **Bar and coin physical investment — Asia-led safe-haven and alternative-asset buying.** The single biggest driver of the Q1 2026 demand surprise was Chinese retail physical buying, at an all-time record 207 t in one quarter. This reflects the interplay of weak domestic equity and property markets, yuan depreciation pressure, and a cultural shift toward gold as a savings vehicle. Indian retail investment also surpassed jewellery for the first time. Whether this pace sustains into Q2–Q3 2026 depends on the trajectory of Chinese risk sentiment and currency pressure — any meaningful equity recovery or yuan stabilisation could compress this demand rapidly.

2. **Gold-backed ETF flows — Western institutional money.** In 2025 full-year, ETF inflows totalled +801 t (from –3 t in 2024), adding a major new demand layer. In Q1 2026, flows slowed to +62 t as US-listed funds saw March outflows. The direction of North American and European institutional flows — driven by real-yield expectations and geopolitical risk appetite — is the most volatile demand swing factor and can shift hundreds of tonnes in a single quarter.

The inventory picture has three layers: (a) global above-ground stocks (the vast cumulative stock), (b) ETF and physically-backed product holdings (the fastest-moving visible buffer), and (c) COMEX exchange warehouse stocks (the exchange-delivery buffer).

**Buffer assessment — COMFORTABLE with a caveat:**

Gold's primary inventory buffer is the cumulative above-ground stock (~220,000 t) and ETF holdings (~4,121 t globally), which together dwarf annual mine supply of ~3,700 t. Unlike agricultural commodities, gold is not destroyed in consumption — it is recycled. The relevant scarcity signal for price is not stocks-to-use in the agricultural sense but rather the **marginal allocation decision** of holders: when real yields fall or macro uncertainty rises, existing above-ground gold shifts from passive storage to active investment demand (ETF inflows, bar buying), tightening the "effective" market supply. At 4,121 t and near the all-time record of 4,176 t, global ETF holdings signal that institutional and retail investors globally have allocated heavily to gold — this represents a large latent supply if sentiment reverses, but also confirms that the current demand-driven price rise has broad participation. COMEX registered stocks at ~460–470 t are adequate for near-term futures delivery, with no visible stress.

# Weather & Seasonality — GOLD

## 1. Is weather a driver here?

No. The GOLD commodity profile (COMMODITY_PROFILES.md, ## GOLD) explicitly marks weather as NOT applicable: "NOT a weather commodity." Gold is priced by macro drivers — real yields, the US dollar, central-bank buying, and geopolitical risk — not by growing seasons or rainfall. Only mild seasonality applies, primarily shaped by India jewellery/wedding demand and Chinese consumer cycles.

---

## 2. Current weather state

Not applicable. Gold has no crop, no growing season, and no weather-sensitive production window. No monsoon, ENSO, or harvest-pace analysis is warranted here. (Weather analysis forced onto gold would violate the profile lens and MODULE_RULES.md §7.)

One partial exception: India's below-normal monsoon for 2026 is a mild demand-side risk worth flagging, because ~60% of India's gold demand originates in rural areas where agricultural income drives purchasing power (see Section 3 below). This is a demand-seasonality risk, not a supply/weather driver.

---

## 3. Seasonality / calendar position

### Where we sit in the demand year (as of 2026-07-03)

Gold demand follows a recurring three-cluster pattern globally:

| Demand cluster | Typical window | Key driver | 2026 status |
|---|---|---|---|
| India festival/wedding season | Late Sep – mid-Dec (Navratri, Dhanteras, Diwali, winter weddings) | Jewellery + bar/coin buying; rural income post-harvest | Not yet open — ~12 weeks away |
| Chinese New Year / Lunar restocking | Dec – Feb (dealer restocking begins ~6 wks ahead) | Jewellery gifting; retailer inventory build | CNY 2027 falls 6 Feb; restocking window opens roughly early Jan 2027 |
| Q1 global restocking / investment | Jan – Mar | Bar/coin investment demand; ETF inflows; Chinese New Year tail | Passed — Q1 2026 saw 151t India demand (+10% y/y), global bar/coin at 474t (+42% y/y) [WGC Gold Demand Trends Q1 2026, published May 2026] |

**Current window (July 2026) is seasonally the weakest for physical jewellery demand.** July–August is the monsoon trough in India: auspicious wedding dates are scarce on the Hindu calendar, rural incomes have not yet received the kharif harvest boost, and discretionary jewellery buying dips. This is not a sharp seasonal cliff for gold (unlike sugar or coffee), but the seasonal tailwind from India is absent right now and does not re-engage until roughly late September.

### The India monsoon risk to Q3-Q4 demand

The 2026 southwest monsoon is running materially below normal, and this is the one weather-adjacent input that matters for gold:

- IMD's season forecast: below-normal at approximately 90% of the long-period average (LPA of 868.6 mm for June–September), with 60% probability of deficient conditions [IMD forecast, as reported by Down to Earth, 2026-06-04; labelled unverified web source].
- June 2026 actual: India received approximately 99.5 mm in June — roughly 40% below the June normal — making it the fifth-driest June since records began in 1901 [Business Standard citing IMD data, 2026-06-30; labelled unverified web source].
- IMD July 2026 outlook: below-normal rainfall, forecast below 94% of July's LPA of 280.4 mm [Business Standard, 2026-06-30; labelled unverified web source].
- ENSO: IMD puts the probability of El Niño developing during the monsoon season at 92%; NOAA data cited by Down to Earth gives 82% probability of development by July 2026. No Indian Ocean Dipole offset is expected [Down to Earth, 2026-06-04; labelled unverified web source].

**Direction of push:** A below-normal monsoon compresses rural incomes in Q3, which risks suppressing India jewellery demand in Q4 (the festival season). WGC's India Focus Q1 2026 report explicitly listed "possible below-normal monsoons affecting incomes" as a headwind to 2026 jewellery consumption [WGC Gold Demand Trends: India Focus Q1 2026, published May 2026]. India's jewellery demand was already under price-induced volume pressure in Q1 2026 (-19% y/y to 66t, the second-lowest Q1 on record since 2000), even as spending value surged 47% on higher prices [WGC Gold Demand Trends: India Focus Q1 2026]. A weak monsoon adds a second pressure on volume — it does not affect gold's macro price drivers, but it trims the seasonal demand lift that would otherwise arrive in October–December.

Quantification is not possible from available data; label this a moderate risk to India jewellery volumes in Q4 2026, not a macro price call.

### Next weather-sensitive / seasonal window

- **Late September 2026:** Navratri begins; India festival buying opens. Rural income from the kharif harvest (August–October) begins flowing. If the monsoon has been deficient, this income will be lower, compressing the seasonal demand uptick.
- **October–November 2026:** Dhanteras and Diwali — historically the single largest gold-buying events in India. Q4 India demand historically runs ~40% above the annual quarterly mean.
- **December 2026 – early January 2027:** Chinese New Year restocking cycle begins for CNY 2027 (6 February 2027). China Q1 2026 demand already showed record investment of 207t (+67% y/y) [WGC GDT Q1 2026]; if that investment-buying impulse sustains, the CNY restocking window adds to it.

### Summary

Weather is not a driver for gold. The seasonal calendar places us in the demand trough right now (July) — the India festival season is about 12 weeks away and the Chinese New Year cycle is approximately 6 months away. The only weather-adjacent risk is a materially below-normal 2026 India monsoon (IMD season forecast ~90% of LPA; June actual ~40% below normal), which threatens to dampen rural income and suppress India jewellery volumes in Q4 2026. This is a mild bearish seasonal risk to physical demand volumes, not to gold's macro price setup. The macro/real-yield/USD drivers that actually move gold are the subject of the macro-positioning module.

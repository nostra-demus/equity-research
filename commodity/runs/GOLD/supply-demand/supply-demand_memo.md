# Supply–Demand Module Memo — GOLD

**Verdict:** Near-balance for 2025 (supply ≈ demand at ~5,002 t) — but the whole balance is being carried by investors, not by jewellery buyers.

**Date:** 2026-07-03

---

## The One-Line Read

Supply and demand tied at roughly 5,002 tonnes in 2025. Jewellery buyers walked away as prices rose; ETFs (exchange-traded funds that hold physical gold on behalf of investors) and Chinese retail bar/coin buyers picked up the slack. If those investment buyers step back in 2026, the balance tips into a visible glut relative to what jewellery can absorb at today's prices.

---

## What the Numbers Say (all from the synthesis, all sourced there)

| Line | 2025 | 2024 | What it means |
|---|---|---|---|
| Total supply (mine + recycle + net hedging) | 5,002 t | 4,962 t | Grew ~40 t YoY |
| Total demand (incl. OTC) | 5,002 t | 4,961.9 t | Balanced by WGC methodology |
| Demand that "consumes" gold (jewellery + tech) | 1,865 t | 2,213 t | **Down 16%** — price-driven volume destruction |
| Demand that "stores" gold (investment + central banks + ETFs) | 3,038 t | 2,279 t | **Up 33%** — the whole growth story |

- **ETF flows swung 804 t in one year:** –3 t in full-year 2024 → +801 t in full-year 2025. That is the single most volatile lever in the market.
- **Q1 2026:** total supply 1,231 t, total demand 1,231 t. Chinese retail bar/coin buying hit an all-time country record of ~207 t in one quarter. ETF inflows slowed to +62 t (down from +230 t in Q1 2025) — the first sign that western institutional buying may be fading.
- **Inventory buffer is comfortable:** global ETF holdings 4,121 t as of May 31, 2026 (all-time peak was 4,176 t in Feb 2026); COMEX registered stocks (the gold sitting in exchange vaults and available for delivery against futures) at ~460–470 t, no delivery stress.
- **Above-ground stock:** ~219,891 t at end-2025 (estimate, unverified web source). Gold is never destroyed — so a physical "supply shock" is not really possible.
- **Recycling barely responded to +67% price gains in 2025** — scrap supply only rose 3%. Holders are treating gold as collateral, not selling it.

## Direction & The One Thing That Matters Most

**Direction:** supply rising modestly (mine output +2% in 2025 to 3,815 t, projected +2.4% to ~3,907 t in 2026 per Metals Focus — labelled unverified vendor estimate). Recycling picking up (+3% in 2025, +5% in Q1 2026). Producer de-hedging (miners closing out old contracts that had locked in low prices) removed a small ~74 t from market-available supply in 2025.

**The biggest swing factor is western institutional ETF flows.** Not weather. Not mine disruption. Not the India monsoon. A single-year swing of 800+ tonnes in the ETF channel dwarfs everything else on the supply side. The Mali mine restart (~22–30 t restored), the artisanal-and-small-scale-mining data revisions (±100–150 t uncertainty band), and the recycling pickup are all second-order compared to what institutional investors decide to do next.

**Seasonal position (today, July 3, 2026):** we are in the seasonal trough for physical jewellery. India's festival season (Navratri, Dhanteras, Diwali — the peak gold-buying window in India) is ~12 weeks away; Chinese New Year restocking is ~6 months away. This doesn't pressure price because investment demand is not seasonal.

## Complications and Gaps the Synthesis Flagged

- **No WGC full-year 2026 tonnage balance exists yet.** So the 2026 surplus/deficit cannot be quantified from primary data.
- **Q2 2026 WGC demand data not published yet** (expected late July 2026). Whether the Chinese bar/coin surge held or moderated is unknown.
- **COMEX total warehouse stocks read differently across two web sources** for late June–July 2026 (~871 t vs. ~728 t). CME Group's daily report was not directly accessed. Direction (adequate, no stress) is solid; magnitude is uncertain.
- **ASGM data revision uncertainty of ±100–150 t.** WGC revised 2025 mine supply upward by 143 t between Jan and May 2026; further revisions are likely.
- **~57% of 2025 central-bank buying was "opaque"** at the time of reporting — some official demand is inherently estimated, not directly disclosed.
- **Reconciliation note:** the synthesis uses the January 2026 WGC figure (5,002 t total supply, on 3,672 t mine base) for the 2025 balance, and the revised 3,815 t mine figure as the 2026 forward baseline. Two vintages of the same WGC series, not a contradiction.

## What Would Change This Read

- **Bearish trigger:** western institutional ETF flows reverse — a several-hundred-tonne selling wave would generate effective supply with no mining involved. Watch for: rising real yields, risk appetite rotating back to equities.
- **Bullish trigger:** Chinese retail bar/coin demand sustains at Q1 2026's record pace (~207 t/quarter) and ETF inflows re-accelerate above the +62 t Q1 2026 reading.
- **Data update to watch:** WGC Q2 2026 Gold Demand Trends, expected late July 2026 — will show whether the Chinese retail surge held.
- **Second-order watch items:** further ASGM revisions (±100–150 t band), Mali/Grasberg ramp, and whether the CME Group daily COMEX report confirms one of the two divergent web readings.

## Bottom Line

- Headline balance is near-neutral (~5,000 t on both sides in 2025), but that number is misleading — investors are doing all the work while jewellery buyers step back.
- The reason it could be **better than it looks:** Chinese retail bar/coin buying hit an all-time country record of ~207 t in a single quarter (Q1 2026), central-bank buying continued at 243.7 t (+3% YoY), and holders are hoarding rather than recycling despite record prices.
- The reason it could be **worse than it looks:** the ETF channel swung 804 t in a single year — it can swing back. And Q1 2026 already shows ETF inflows slowing to +62 t. Jewellery cannot absorb the slack at today's prices without a meaningful price correction.
- What is **missing:** no WGC 2026 full-year balance yet, Q2 2026 demand data not out until late July, COMEX stock figures diverge across web sources, ~57% of 2025 central-bank buying was opaque.
- **The one thing to watch next:** the late-July 2026 WGC Q2 report — specifically whether ETF inflows have kept slowing and whether Chinese bar/coin buying held its Q1 pace.

## Plain-English Glossary

- **ETF (exchange-traded fund) holdings:** the pool of physical gold held by investment funds on behalf of their investors — 4,121 t as of May 31, 2026, near the all-time peak of 4,176 t.
- **COMEX registered stock:** the gold sitting in COMEX-approved warehouses that is eligible to be delivered against a futures contract — the operative "buffer tightness" measure for the exchange.
- **Producer de-hedging:** when miners close out prior contracts that had locked in gold at lower prices — net –74 t in 2025 means it took a small amount of gold off the market-available side.
- **ASGM (artisanal and small-scale mining):** informal, small-scale mining that is hard to measure; WGC revisions here move total mine supply by 100–150 t at a time.
- **Above-ground stock:** all the gold ever mined that still exists in the world (~219,891 t at end-2025 est.) — because gold isn't destroyed, this pool only grows.

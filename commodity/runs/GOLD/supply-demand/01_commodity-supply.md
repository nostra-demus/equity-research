## 2. Direction & Swing Factors

**Supply direction: rising — mine supply is at a record and heading modestly higher in 2026; recycling accelerating from a low base.**

- **Mine supply: +2% YoY in 2025 (revised) and on track for a further ~+2.4% in 2026 per Metals Focus.** The primary driver is incremental output from new projects (Blackwater, Back River, Valentine Lake in Canada; Salares Norte recovery in Chile; Ahafo North in Ghana) and ASGM growth responding to gold near all-time highs. High prices (~$4,100–4,200/oz spot in July 2026) expand the economically viable ore envelope for both large-scale and artisanal producers. However, the growth rate is modest — not a step change. [WGC Gold Demand Trends Q1-2026, supply page; Metals Focus via Kitco, 2026-06-05]

- **Swing factor 1 — Mali / Loulo-Gounkoto restart (bullish for supply, previously a major drag).** Barrick's Loulo-Gounkoto mine was suspended throughout much of 2025 after a government dispute, costing approximately 723,000 oz (roughly 22.5 t attributable) vs a full operating year. Barrick settled with Mali in late 2025 (paying ~$430 million), regained operational control on 18 December 2025, and is guiding 260,000–290,000 attributable oz for 2026. This represents a partial but significant supply restoration. In Q1 2026 the WGC noted Mali output +30% YoY as the restart came through. The mine is not back to pre-dispute levels and won't be until at least 2027. [Barrick / Ecofin Agency, 2026-02; WGC Q1-2026 supply page, 2026-05]

- **Swing factor 2 — Recycling acceleration, constrained by collateralisation behaviour.** Scrap supply rose 5% YoY in Q1 2026 to 366 t. India recycling surged 44% QoQ and 20% YoY as consumers liquidated at record prices. However, WGC notes recycling is "expected to be constrained by low near-market stocks, expectations of continued price strength, and a likely embedded geopolitical risk premium" — meaning holders are retaining gold as collateral for loans rather than selling it. This caps how far recycling can run even with prices at $4,100+/oz. The muted 2025 scrap response (+3% despite a 67% average-price rise) shows this dynamic is already operating. [WGC Gold Demand Trends Full Year 2025, 2026-01-29; WGC Q1-2026 outlook page, 2026-05]

- **Producer hedging: a persistent net negative supply flow.** The industry hedge book fell to ~118 t in Q4 2025 (the lowest since 2013) and declined a further ~20 t in Q1 2026. Miners are de-hedging legacy positions locked in at prices far below spot, and new hedging is minimal given investor preference for full price exposure. De-hedging (buying back sold-forward gold) acts as a small but consistent net negative to supply. This subtracts from market-available supply rather than adding to it, and is likely to continue in 2026. [WGC Gold Demand Trends Q1-2026, supply page; WGC / Metals Focus, 2026-05]

- **Diesel / energy risk in Oceania and Asia: a downside tail for 2026 production.** WGC explicitly flags that "some production disruptions from diesel shortages may materialise in mines in Oceania and Asia which could temper this growth." Australia already saw -7% YoY output in Q1 2026, partly from weather disruptions and lower grades. This is a stated risk to the 2026 growth thesis, not a settled outcome. [WGC Q1-2026 outlook page, 2026-05]

- **Note on convention: central-bank flows sit on the DEMAND side.** By WGC and standard gold accounting convention, official-sector (central-bank) purchases and sales are classified as demand, not supply. Central banks bought a net 244 t in Q1 2026 and are forecast to purchase ~850 t in 2026 [WGC Q1-2026 central banks page, 2026-05]. These flows are large relative to mine supply but belong to the demand side of the ledger and are not included in the supply figures above.

## 3. Gaps / Low-Confidence Items

- **ASGM data quality.** The 621 t cumulative upward revision to mine supply since 2015 (applied in the Q1-2026 update) shows that ASGM estimates carry significant uncertainty. The gap between the WGC Full Year 2025 initial estimate (3,672 t) and the revised figure (3,815 t) is 143 t — about 4% of annual mine supply. Further revisions are probable. Treat the 2025 mine figure as an estimate with a ±100–150 t confidence band.

- **No WGC-published full-year 2026 tonnage forecast available at run date.** The Metals Focus 3,907 t forecast is a vendor estimate (labelled unverified), not an official WGC balance. The WGC Q1-2026 outlook is qualitative ("modest growth"). This gap caps confidence in the 2026 supply direction from "rising" to "likely rising modestly."

- **Country-level quarterly breakdowns for 2025 annual production** (specific tonnes by producer for the full year) were not available in disaggregated form at run date. Country figures in Table A above are approximate, derived from WGC commentary on YoY percentage changes applied to known baselines.

- **Indonesia trajectory.** Freeport's Grasberg was down -24% in 2025 post mud-rush disruption. Q1 2026 showed +19% YoY recovery (mill expansion at Batu Hijau). Whether full recovery proceeds on schedule through 2026 is unconfirmed — this is a meaningful swing (Grasberg is among the world's largest gold-producing mines).

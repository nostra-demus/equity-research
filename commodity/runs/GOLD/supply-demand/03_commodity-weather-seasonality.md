# Weather & Seasonality — GOLD

## 1. Is weather a driver here?

No. The GOLD commodity profile (COMMODITY_PROFILES.md, ## GOLD) explicitly marks weather as NOT applicable: "NOT a weather commodity." Gold is priced by macro drivers — real yields, the US dollar, central-bank buying, and geopolitical risk — not by growing seasons or rainfall. Only mild seasonality applies, primarily shaped by India jewellery/wedding demand and Chinese consumer cycles.

---

## 2. Current weather state

Not applicable. Gold has no crop, no growing season, and no weather-sensitive production window. No monsoon, ENSO, or harvest-pace analysis is warranted here. (Weather analysis forced onto gold would violate the profile lens and MODULE_RULES.md §7.)

One partial exception: India's below-normal monsoon for 2026 is a mild demand-side risk worth flagging, because ~60% of India's gold demand originates in rural areas where agricultural income drives purchasing power (see Section 3 below). This is a demand-seasonality risk, not a supply/weather driver.

---

## 3. Seasonality / calendar position

### Where we sit in the demand year (as of 2026-07-03)

Gold demand follows a recurring three-cluster pattern globally:

| Demand cluster | Typical window | Key driver | 2026 status |
|---|---|---|---|
| India festival/wedding season | Late Sep – mid-Dec (Navratri, Dhanteras, Diwali, winter weddings) | Jewellery + bar/coin buying; rural income post-harvest | Not yet open — ~12 weeks away |
| Chinese New Year / Lunar restocking | Dec – Feb (dealer restocking begins ~6 wks ahead) | Jewellery gifting; retailer inventory build | CNY 2027 falls 6 Feb; restocking window opens roughly early Jan 2027 |
| Q1 global restocking / investment | Jan – Mar | Bar/coin investment demand; ETF inflows; Chinese New Year tail | Passed — Q1 2026 saw 151t India demand (+10% y/y), global bar/coin at 474t (+42% y/y) [WGC Gold Demand Trends Q1 2026, published May 2026] |

**Current window (July 2026) is seasonally the weakest for physical jewellery demand.** July–August is the monsoon trough in India: auspicious wedding dates are scarce on the Hindu calendar, rural incomes have not yet received the kharif harvest boost, and discretionary jewellery buying dips. This is not a sharp seasonal cliff for gold (unlike sugar or coffee), but the seasonal tailwind from India is absent right now and does not re-engage until roughly late September.

### The India monsoon risk to Q3-Q4 demand

The 2026 southwest monsoon is running materially below normal, and this is the one weather-adjacent input that matters for gold:

- IMD's season forecast: below-normal at approximately 90% of the long-period average (LPA of 868.6 mm for June–September), with 60% probability of deficient conditions [IMD forecast, as reported by Down to Earth, 2026-06-04; labelled unverified web source].
- June 2026 actual: India received approximately 99.5 mm in June — roughly 40% below the June normal — making it the fifth-driest June since records began in 1901 [Business Standard citing IMD data, 2026-06-30; labelled unverified web source].
- IMD July 2026 outlook: below-normal rainfall, forecast below 94% of July's LPA of 280.4 mm [Business Standard, 2026-06-30; labelled unverified web source].
- ENSO: IMD puts the probability of El Niño developing during the monsoon season at 92%; NOAA data cited by Down to Earth gives 82% probability of development by July 2026. No Indian Ocean Dipole offset is expected [Down to Earth, 2026-06-04; labelled unverified web source].

**Direction of push:** A below-normal monsoon compresses rural incomes in Q3, which risks suppressing India jewellery demand in Q4 (the festival season). WGC's India Focus Q1 2026 report explicitly listed "possible below-normal monsoons affecting incomes" as a headwind to 2026 jewellery consumption [WGC Gold Demand Trends: India Focus Q1 2026, published May 2026]. India's jewellery demand was already under price-induced volume pressure in Q1 2026 (-19% y/y to 66t, the second-lowest Q1 on record since 2000), even as spending value surged 47% on higher prices [WGC Gold Demand Trends: India Focus Q1 2026]. A weak monsoon adds a second pressure on volume — it does not affect gold's macro price drivers, but it trims the seasonal demand lift that would otherwise arrive in October–December.

Quantification is not possible from available data; label this a moderate risk to India jewellery volumes in Q4 2026, not a macro price call.

### Next weather-sensitive / seasonal window

- **Late September 2026:** Navratri begins; India festival buying opens. Rural income from the kharif harvest (August–October) begins flowing. If the monsoon has been deficient, this income will be lower, compressing the seasonal demand uptick.
- **October–November 2026:** Dhanteras and Diwali — historically the single largest gold-buying events in India. Q4 India demand historically runs ~40% above the annual quarterly mean.
- **December 2026 – early January 2027:** Chinese New Year restocking cycle begins for CNY 2027 (6 February 2027). China Q1 2026 demand already showed record investment of 207t (+67% y/y) [WGC GDT Q1 2026]; if that investment-buying impulse sustains, the CNY restocking window adds to it.

### Summary

Weather is not a driver for gold. The seasonal calendar places us in the demand trough right now (July) — the India festival season is about 12 weeks away and the Chinese New Year cycle is approximately 6 months away. The only weather-adjacent risk is a materially below-normal 2026 India monsoon (IMD season forecast ~90% of LPA; June actual ~40% below normal), which threatens to dampen rural income and suppress India jewellery volumes in Q4 2026. This is a mild bearish seasonal risk to physical demand volumes, not to gold's macro price setup. The macro/real-yield/USD drivers that actually move gold are the subject of the macro-positioning module.

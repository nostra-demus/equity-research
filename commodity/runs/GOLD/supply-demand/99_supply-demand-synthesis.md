# Supply–Demand Balance — GOLD (module synthesis)

## Abstract

Gold's 2025 supply-demand balance ended near equilibrium at roughly 5,002 t on both sides of the ledger — technically a surplus at the headline level, but that framing is misleading for gold because above-ground stock never leaves the world. The operationally relevant story is a structural shift in demand: ETF inflows added 801 t in 2025 (the second-strongest year on record) and bar/coin investment jumped +16% YoY, while jewellery — the volume-heavy segment — fell 18% as price-sensitive buyers stepped back. Inventory buffers are comfortable to near-record: global ETF holdings stand at 4,121 t as of May 31, 2026 (near the all-time peak of 4,176 t set February 2026), and COMEX registered stocks remain adequate at roughly 460–470 t with no delivery stress. The direction in 2026 is modest supply growth (+2–2.4% mine output, +5% recycling per Metals Focus) meeting demand dominated by investment and central-bank flows — with the single biggest swing factor being the sustainability of institutional (ETF) and Chinese retail (bar/coin) investment demand, not weather or mine disruption.

## Balance (surplus / deficit)

**FY2025 — near-balance, fractionally on the demand-led side.**

| Component | 2025 (t) | 2024 (t) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Total supply (mine + recycle + net hedging) | 5,002 | 4,962 | WGC Gold Demand Trends FY2025, pub. 2026-01-29 |
| Total demand (incl. OTC) | 5,002.3 | 4,961.9 | WGC Gold Demand Trends FY2025 |

At the annual balance level, supply and demand rounded to identical figures for 2025. This is typical for gold, where the WGC balance is constructed to zero out by including the residual "OTC and other" as the balancing item. The more informative read is the composition shift:

- **Demand that "consumes" gold (jewellery + technology):** 1,865 t in 2025, down from 2,213 t in 2024 (-16%), driven entirely by price-induced jewellery volume destruction.
- **Demand that "stores" gold (investment + central banks + ETFs):** 3,038 t in 2025, up from 2,279 t in 2024 (+33%), as financial and official buyers absorbed what volume consumers stepped away from.
- **Net result:** gold moved from consumers' wrists to institutional vaults. Price cleared the market at higher levels. No physical shortage; instead, a repricing of who holds gold and why.

**Q1 2026 — same pattern, amplified.**

Total supply was 1,231 t; total demand was 1,231 t (balanced, as expected by WGC methodology). Bar and coin demand hit 473.6 t — the second-highest quarterly reading on record — with Chinese retail buying at an all-time country record of ~207 t. ETF inflows were a much smaller +62 t (from +230 t in Q1 2025), signalling some fading of the western institutional surge. Central-bank purchases continued at 243.7 t (+3% YoY). Jewellery demand fell further to 299.7 t (-23% YoY) in China (-32%) and India (-19%).

**2026 full-year balance (forward-looking):** No official WGC published tonnage balance for 2026 exists at this date. Metals Focus projects mine supply rising to ~3,907 t and recycling to ~1,475 t [Metals Focus via Kitco, 2026-06-05, labelled unverified vendor estimate], giving total supply near 5,250 t including residual hedging adjustments. Whether demand matches this depends almost entirely on whether western institutional ETF flows and Chinese retail bar/coin buying hold at 2025–Q1 2026 levels. If they do, demand holds above 5,000 t. If the investment-demand layer retreats — as real yields rise or risk appetite returns to equities — the headline balance tips into a visible demand shortfall vs. the 2025 base. Quantification of the 2026 gap is not possible from available data; the direction of risk is two-sided and investment-demand-driven.

## Inventory buffer vs history

Gold's inventory buffer works differently from agricultural commodities. The relevant measures are:

| Measure | Latest reading | vs. history | Source, date |
|---|---|---|---|
| Global above-ground stock | ~219,891 t (end-2025 est.) | Growing ~3,600 t/yr; structurally comfortable — gold is never destroyed | WGC / VT Markets, end-2025 est., unverified web source |
| Global gold-backed ETF holdings | 4,121 t (May 31, 2026) | Near all-time record (peak: 4,176 t, Feb 27, 2026); elevated vs. the 2021–2023 outflow cycle | WGC ETF Flows: May 2026, pub. 2026-06 |
| COMEX registered gold (delivery-eligible) | ~460–470 t (~14.8–15.1M oz) as of mid-June 2026 | Adequate for delivery; no stress signal; minor decline (~4%) in 30 days to mid-June 2026 | goldsilver.ai / CME Group daily stocks data, 2026-06-17, unverified |
| COMEX total warehouse stocks | Two sources diverge: ~871 t (2026-06-17) vs. ~728 t (2026-07-02) | Both readings within normal historical range; no squeeze | metalcharts.org / CME Group, 2026-07-02, unverified |

**Assessment:** The buffer is **comfortable**. ETF holdings near 4,121 t represent a large pool of mobilisable gold — if western institutions reverse course and sell, this is a significant latent supply overhang. COMEX stocks show no stress. The structural above-ground stock (~220,000 t) means gold cannot face a "supply shock" in the way an agricultural commodity can. The operative scarcity signal is not physical shortage but the marginal allocation decision of existing holders: right now, they are retaining gold (evidenced by the muted recycling response to +67% price gains in 2025 — scrap only rose 3% in 2025 despite dramatically higher prices [WGC Gold Demand Trends FY2025]).

The one place where "buffer tightness" is a real concept is **COMEX registered stock** relative to open interest in the delivery month. Two sources diverged materially on total COMEX stocks for late June–July 2026 (~871 t vs. ~728 t). The CME Group's own daily report was not directly accessed — figures labelled unverified. Neither reading implies delivery stress at current open-interest levels.

## Direction & biggest swing factor

**Direction: supply rising modestly; demand composition shifting decisively toward investment — net price-neutral to mildly price-supportive on balance, contingent on investment demand holding.**

- Mine supply is up ~+2% YoY in 2025 (revised to 3,815 t) and on track for a further ~+2.4% in 2026 (~3,907 t) per Metals Focus. This is gradual, not a step-change. [WGC Q1-2026 supply page; Metals Focus via Kitco, 2026-06-05, unverified vendor estimate]
- Recycling is accelerating (+3% in 2025, +5% in Q1 2026 YoY) but is being constrained by holders treating gold as collateral rather than selling at record prices. [WGC Gold Demand Trends FY2025; WGC Q1-2026 supply page]
- Producer de-hedging continues (net negative ~74 t in 2025 and ~20 t in Q1 2026), removing a small but consistent net amount from market-available supply. [WGC Gold Demand Trends FY2025; WGC Q1-2026 supply page]

**Biggest swing factor: Western institutional ETF flows.**

Bar/coin physical buying by Chinese retail investors (record ~207 t in Q1 2026 alone [WGC GDT Q1-2026]) is the largest single demand surprise of the current cycle. But the ETF channel is the most volatile: it swung from –3 t in full-year 2024 to +801 t in full-year 2025 — a 804 t reversal in a single year [WGC Gold Demand Trends FY2025]. A further swing in 2026, whether a continuation, slowdown (as seen in Q1 2026 at only +62 t), or outright reversal, would dominate all supply-side changes. The Mali restart (~22–30 t restored output), ASGM revisions (±100–150 t uncertainty band), and recycling acceleration are all second-order compared to the magnitude of ETF flow reversals.

The India monsoon (IMD forecast ~90% of long-period average; June 2026 actual ~40% below the June normal [Business Standard citing IMD, 2026-06-30, unverified]) is a mild bearish seasonal risk to India jewellery demand in Q4 2026, but it is not the swing factor for gold's price or balance. India jewellery was already under severe volume pressure before the monsoon shortfall (-19% YoY in Q1 2026) — the monsoon adds to an existing volume headwind in a segment that is a progressively smaller share of the price-setting demand pool.

**Seasonal position (as of July 3, 2026):** The current window is the seasonal trough for physical jewellery demand globally. The India festival season (Navratri, Dhanteras, Diwali) is roughly 12 weeks away; Chinese New Year restocking is approximately 6 months away. This seasonal softness does not pressure gold price, since investment demand is not seasonally driven.

## Reconciliation & Gaps

**No outright contradictions exist between the three specialist files.** They cover different dimensions and are consistent in direction.

One area requiring reconciliation: the supply file uses WGC's revised 2025 mine production figure of 3,815 t (from the Q1-2026 update, incorporating ASGM restatement), while the FY2025 demand file's total supply figure of 5,002 t was sourced from the January 2026 WGC FY2025 report which used the earlier 3,672 t mine figure. The 143 t difference is reconciled by the data-revision note in the supply file [WGC / Metals Focus, Q1-2026 supply commentary, 2026-05]: the ASGM restatement added 621 t cumulative across the prior decade but was not yet incorporated in the January 2026 FY2025 publication. For the synthesis balance, the FY2025 total supply of 5,002 t (as published) is used for demand comparisons; the 3,815 t mine-supply revised figure is used as the 2026 forward baseline. These two are from different vintages of the same WGC data series and should not be presented as contradictory.

**Gaps that cap confidence:**

| Gap | Impact |
|---|---|
| No WGC-published full-year 2026 tonnage balance | Prevents quantifying the 2026 surplus/deficit; the Metals Focus supply estimate is vendor-only [unverified] |
| Q2 2026 demand data not yet published (expected late July 2026) | The most recent full WGC quarter is Q1 2026; whether Chinese bar/coin demand has sustained or moderated is unknown |
| COMEX stock discrepancy (~728 t vs. ~871 t across two web sources) | CME Group's daily report not directly accessed; directional read (adequate, no stress) is solid but the magnitude is uncertain |
| ASGM data uncertainty (±100–150 t) | 2025 mine supply revised 143 t upward between Jan and May 2026; further revisions are probable |
| ~57% of 2025 central-bank buying was "opaque" at the time of reporting [WGC FY2025] | Monthly run-rates for Q2 2026 are not compiled here; near-term CB demand is inherently estimated |

## Note to the Commodity Thesis

- **Balance is near-neutral at the headline (supply ≈ demand at ~5,000 t/yr) but investment demand is doing all the work.** If bar/coin and ETF buying recedes — Chinese equities stabilise, western real yields rise sharply — the balance tips to visible oversupply relative to 2025 jewellery volumes, because jewellery cannot absorb the slack at current prices without a significant price correction.
- **ETF holdings near the all-time record of 4,176 t (~4,121 t as of May 31, 2026) represent both a sign of strong current demand and a latent supply overhang.** Institutional sellers exiting at any scale can generate hundreds of tonnes of additional effective supply with no mining required.
- **The India monsoon shortfall (June 2026 ~40% below normal; IMD season forecast ~90% of LPA [Business Standard citing IMD, 2026-06-30, unverified]) is a moderate headwind to Q4 2026 India jewellery volumes — already the second-lowest Q1 on record at 66 t [WGC India Focus Q1-2026].** It does not move the macro price needle but it erodes the seasonal demand lift expected in October–November.
- **Mine supply is rising but gently (+2–2.4% per year), with the Mali restart (Loulo-Gounkoto, ~260–290k oz guided for 2026 [Barrick / Ecofin Agency, 2026-02]) partially restoring a 2025 drag, and Grasberg/Indonesia recovering [WGC Q1-2026 supply page].** Supply-side pressure on gold price is low; the bears need investment demand to fade, not mine supply to surge.

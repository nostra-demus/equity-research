## What This Module Found

- The primary-aluminium market (metal made from raw ore, not recycled) tipped into deficit in 2025 and stayed there in 2026. Vendor estimates of the 2026 gap have converged near ~1.2–1.4 Mt, up from an earlier ~200–600kt — the number is being revised up, not down [01, 02].
- World primary supply grew only 1.06% in 2025 (73.78 Mt, up from 73.01 Mt) — the slowest in five years — because China's producers are running into their own 45 Mt/year self-imposed cap and the Gulf region went backward [01, IAI data, pub. 2026-01-26].
- Demand grew faster: total world consumption ~104.0 Mt in 2025 (+2.4%), forecast ~106.8 Mt in 2026 (+2.7%) — vendor estimates, explicitly unverified, and they include recycled metal, so they are **not** directly comparable to the primary-only production figure [02].
- The single most important driver is China: it is >55% of world demand, its own construction and solar demand stayed weak through H1 2026, and that weakness is the largest demand-side swing risk in the whole balance [02].
- The single most important near-term risk is the Yunnan hydropower region's wet-to-dry season switch (~November 2026): past dry seasons forced curtailments of up to ~400,000 t, which would remove supply just when the buffer already has little room [03]. No curtailment signal is visible yet as of 2026-07-18 — this is a dated forward risk, not a current condition.
- The exchange-visible buffer (LME registered stock — metal in exchange-approved sheds, ready to deliver) fell ~32% since January 2026 to ~284,600 t on 2026-07-15, the first sub-300,000 t reading since 2022, with the market in backwardation (cash price above the 3-month price, a live scarcity signal) [02].

## The Specialists, Briefly

- **01 Commodity Supply** — Primary supply grew just 1.06% in 2025; China is at/over its 45 Mt cap and the Gulf fell; Guinea bauxite disruption and Alcoa's Kwinana closure squeeze feedstock; Mozal and Grundartangi outages keep Europe tight [01].
- **02 Commodity Demand & Inventory** — Demand ~2.4% growth in 2025, ~2.7% forecast for 2026; LME stock at multi-year low and falling; China social inventory destocking but still above its end-January level; vendor deficit now ~1.2–1.4 Mt [02].
- **03 Weather & Seasonality** — Yunnan (~5 Mt/year of hydro-powered smelting) is in its high-output wet season now; the ~November 2026 dry-season transition is the biggest near-term swing factor, with past curtailments up to ~400,000 t [03].

**Most important disagreement (from the synthesis's Reconciliation):** the two buffer measures point opposite ways — LME stock says "tight, multi-year low," while China's social inventory says "still destocking from an elevated build." The synthesis keeps them **separate** rather than blending them into one buffer number, per its do-not-average rule. It carries this as unresolved by design, not adjudicated into a single figure.

## What Would Change This Read

- **Yunnan dry season (~November 2026 onward):** a repeat of past dry-season curtailments (up to ~400,000 t) would tighten the balance further, hitting supply while the buffer is already low. Watch for provincial power-rationing directives and reservoir levels [03].
- **China 45 Mt cap exemption for renewable-powered smelters:** if regulators approve the pending exemption, new capacity could loosen the biggest structural supply constraint. Unresolved as of this writing [01].
- **Middle East supply recovery:** slower-than-expected Gulf recovery was the reason ING lifted its 2026 deficit estimate; a faster recovery would narrow it [02].
- **South32 Mozal restart:** ING's wider ~600kt case assumed Mozal stays shut; a reversal would ease the European tightness [01].
- **A firmer deficit number:** any official IAI/CRU balance print (none exists today) would move the ~1.2–1.4 Mt figure out of the "likely" band toward settled.

## Bottom Line

- **Verdict:** primary aluminium is in deficit in 2025 and 2026; the exchange-visible buffer is at its tightest since 2022 and still falling.
- **Why it could be better (looser) than it looks:** China's social inventory is still destocking from a high base (~1.02–1.08 Mt mid-July, above the ~782,000 t of end-January), so the China physical side is normalizing, not tight — and demand is vendor-estimated, not official.
- **Why it could be worse (tighter) than it looks:** the deficit is being revised up, not down; two European smelter outages (Mozal, Grundartangi) and the Guinea/Kwinana feedstock squeeze reinforce it; and the Yunnan dry season could remove up to ~400,000 t right when the buffer has little room.
- **What evidence is missing:** no current-dated SHFE exchange stock (stale ~3 months), no official world-consumption print (vendor-only), no matched primary-vs-primary balance, no single official 2025 India production total, no confirmed China H1 2026 consumption figure.
- **One thing to watch next:** the Yunnan wet-to-dry transition around November 2026 — the biggest near-term swing factor for the balance.

## Plain-English Glossary

- **Primary aluminium** — metal made from raw ore, as opposed to recycled (secondary) metal.
- **Deficit** — demand runs ahead of supply, so buffers get drawn down.
- **LME registered stock** — aluminium sitting in exchange-approved warehouses, available to deliver against a contract; the market's most visible buffer.
- **Backwardation** — the cash (today) price trades above the 3-month price, a live signal that metal is scarce right now.
- **Social inventory** — metal held in Chinese trader/consumer warehouses (tracked by SMM/Mysteel), off the exchange; a different measure from exchange-warrant stock.
- **SHFE stock** — the on-exchange warehouse stock in China, the equivalent of LME registered stock.
- **Curtailment** — cutting or halting smelter output, here forced by dry-season power shortages.
- **Care-and-maintenance** — a smelter idled but kept in a state it could be restarted from.

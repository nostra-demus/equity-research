## 2. Thesis Summary

The fundamental case for aluminium is the cleanest of the three legs: a real, physically-confirmed exchange-stock drawdown (LME stock -32% YTD to a multi-year low) sits underneath a vendor-estimated 2026 deficit that two independent forecasters (ING and CRU) have both revised wider, not narrower, over the past seven months [supply-demand synthesis]. That tightness is not a single-factor story — it is reinforced by a hard policy ceiling in China (the 45 Mt/yr cap, "likely held through 2027") and by independent Western supply losses (European power-driven smelter curtailments, EU sanctions on Rusal metal, South32's Mozal outage, Century Aluminum's Nordural potline down since October 2025) that do not need a demand recovery to keep biting [supply-demand synthesis; macro-positioning synthesis]. The curve corroborates it: cash trades above 3-month, paying a long holder to roll rather than charging roll drag, which is the opposite of the setup in copper and wheat right now.

But price has already done a great deal of the work the deficit thesis would want to see. The market spiked from Iran/Hormuz-driven fear to a 4-year high of USD 3,855/tonne on 2026-06-02, then unwound ~18% to a 4-month low near USD 3,085/tonne before restabilising near today's USD 3,154/tonne — almost exactly its mid-January 2026 level [market-structure synthesis]. The 12-month trend (+21-22%) and the 1-3 month trend (a sharp round trip) are two different signals, not one continuing move, and the backwardation itself has mostly normalised from the crisis-level inversion back toward a thin +0.95% annualised pickup — the curve is no longer screaming acute near-term scarcity the way it was in May.

The variant-perception question is whether the market has already priced the structural deficit case, leaving only a re-acceleration (a confirmed Yunnan dry-season curtailment, a confirmed continuing crowded long, a fresh Rusal/Guinea supply shock) as upside, or whether it has instead priced the crisis premium OUT and left the underlying, still-intact deficit under-recognised. The engine cannot resolve that today, because the single data point needed to judge it — current fund positioning — is over eight months stale. The macro module's own words are the load-bearing sentence here: the position "has more room to unwind than to extend further from already-record levels," which means a de-escalation headline (a Hormuz resolution, an eased Guinea bauxite export cap, a China property-stimulus surprise) could trigger a sharp long-liquidation shakeout that has nothing to do with whether the China-cap/Western-closure deficit case is actually right [macro-positioning synthesis]. That is precisely the setup §24 warns against sizing into blind: a sound fundamental thesis riding a crowded, unconfirmed trade.

There is no proven variant perception yet on the direction of the next move — the engine can state the deficit case cleanly, but cannot say with confidence whether the market has already positioned for it or is still exposed to a shakeout. "Not proven from available data" applies specifically to the positioning question, and that is the one variable this thesis most needs.

## 3. Risk Summary

- **Strongest bear case:** the Nov-2025 record net long (the largest since LME COTR began in 2018) has persisted or grown through H1-2026's price highs, and a de-escalation headline unrelated to the underlying deficit (Hormuz resolution, Guinea bauxite easing, a weaker-than-expected China Politburo signal) triggers a sharp long-liquidation sell-off that overshoots the fundamental floor even though the deficit case stays intact [macro-positioning synthesis].
- **Single killer risk:** positioning is unconfirmed and asymmetric — the market may already be crowded on the long side into a price that has just round-tripped a geopolitical spike, so a fresh entry today could be buying into the tail end of a squeeze rather than the start of a structural re-rating. This is compounded if Beijing grants the pending renewable-smelter exemption to the 45 Mt cap, which would remove the single structural driver holding the deficit in place at the same moment a crowded long is exposed [supply-demand synthesis; macro-positioning synthesis].
- **What would flip the view / force a downgrade:** the weekly LME COTR (next due 2026-07-21) confirming the net long has meaningfully unwound would de-risk an entry and support upgrading toward Buy; the same print confirming the long has grown further would sharpen the downgrade case toward Avoid. Separately, a confirmed China 45 Mt cap exemption for renewable-powered smelters, or a stronger-than-expected China property-stimulus signal at the end-of-July Politburo meeting, would ease the demand/supply-constraint case and argue for caution regardless of positioning. A confirmed Yunnan dry-season curtailment (from ~November 2026) would corroborate the deficit thesis and support upgrading.

Against the other three commodities tracked in this run: COPPER is rated Avoid (surplus balance, contango imposing ~5-6% annualised roll drag, a confirmed — not stale — crowded long at a COT Index of ~96/100, and a binary US Section-232 tariff premium already embedded in price) [`commodity/runs/COPPER/decision_record.json`]. Aluminium's fundamentals are the strongest of the four on a pure supply-demand-and-curve basis: it is the only one of the four with a physically-confirmed (not just price-implied) stock drawdown corroborating its deficit, the only one paid (not charged) to roll, and the only one where the macro tilt is net supportive rather than mixed or a headwind. Where it loses ground to that clean read is the same axis that caps copper and gold — positioning — except aluminium's positioning problem is worse in one specific way: it is not merely crowded, it is unconfirmed. Copper and gold both have a *current* crowded-long read to react to; aluminium's most recent fund-positioning data point is eight months old. That makes aluminium the commodity with the best fundamental setup and the worst-quality positioning evidence in the tracked set — a combination that argues for closing the specific data gap (due in days, not months) before acting, rather than either forcing a Buy on the strength of the fundamentals or an Avoid on the strength of the positioning fear.

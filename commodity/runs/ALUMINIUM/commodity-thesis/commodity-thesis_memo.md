## What This Module Found

- The fundamental case is real and physically confirmed, not just implied by price: LME registered stock (metal actually sitting in exchange warehouses) is down about 32% so far this year to roughly 284,600 tonnes as of 2026-07-15 — the first reading below 300,000 t since 2022 [supply-demand synthesis].
- Two independent forecasters (ING and CRU) revised their 2026 deficit estimates *wider*, not narrower, over seven months — from roughly 200-600kt up to about 1.2-1.4 Mt as of 2026-07-14. This is a "likely" (60-75% confidence) directional read, not an official balance-body number [supply-demand synthesis].
- The single most important driver is a two-sided supply squeeze: China running its 45 Mt/yr policy cap at 98.5% use (a ~322,000 t June domestic deficit), plus Western losses — European smelter closures, EU sanctions displacing ~340,000 t/yr of Rusal metal, and discrete outages (South32 Mozal, Century's Nordural potline down since October 2025). None of these need a demand recovery to keep biting [macro-positioning synthesis].
- The curve backs it up: cash trades USD 7.50/tonne above the 3-month price (mild backwardation — a shape where you are paid, not charged, to hold and roll the position forward), about +0.95% a year. That is a fraction of the ~USD 59/tonne crisis-level inversion seen at the May-2026 peak [market-structure synthesis].
- The single most important risk is positioning, and it is a data problem, not just a crowding problem. Price already spiked to a 4-year high of USD 3,855/tonne on 2026-06-02, then fell about 18% to near USD 3,085, and now sits near USD 3,154 — almost exactly its mid-January 2026 level. The 12-month trend (+21-22%) and the recent round trip are two different signals [market-structure synthesis].
- The only confirmed positioning print is from November 2025: a record net long (funds' largest bullish bet since the data series began in 2018). The current mid-2026 reading could not be retrieved. So a de-escalation headline unrelated to the deficit (Hormuz easing, Guinea bauxite relief, a soft China Politburo signal) could set off a sharp long-liquidation sell-off even if the deficit case stays intact [macro-positioning synthesis].
- Honest bottom line the synthesis draws: "There is no proven variant perception yet on the direction of the next move." The engine can state the deficit cleanly but cannot say whether the market has already positioned for it or is still exposed to a shakeout.

## Plain-English Glossary

- **LME registered stock** — aluminium physically sitting in London Metal Exchange warehouses; a live gauge of how tight the metal market is.
- **Deficit / surplus (balance)** — whether the world uses more metal than it produces in a year (deficit, tightening) or less (surplus).
- **Backwardation** — a price curve where metal for delivery now costs more than metal for later; a holder is paid, not charged, to roll the position forward.
- **Contango** — the opposite of backwardation; later delivery costs more, so holding and rolling costs you money ("roll drag").
- **Net long / crowded long** — funds are heavily betting the price rises; "crowded" means so many are on the same side that an exit rush can force a sharp sell-off.
- **LME COTR (Commitments of Traders Report)** — the weekly report showing how funds are positioned; here it is the deciding, and currently stale, data point.
- **Commodity-conditional (§14)** — the thesis rides on outside forces (policy, supply shocks, fund flows) the engine cannot control, so conviction is capped.

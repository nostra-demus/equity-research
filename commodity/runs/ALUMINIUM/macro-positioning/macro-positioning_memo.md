## What This Module Found

The macro backdrop for aluminium leans supportive, and the work is being done on the supply side, not by demand. China's hard 45-million-tonne (45Mt) production cap — a policy ceiling on how much metal the country can make, regardless of price — has China running at about 98.5% of capacity and still short by roughly 322,000 tonnes in June 2026 alone [Mysteel, 2026-07]. On top of that, Western supply is being pulled out at the same time: European smelters are closing under high power costs (about €85-95 per megawatt-hour, a 50-70% premium to North America), and EU sanctions on Rusal-linked metal are displacing an estimated ~340,000 tonnes a year from the Atlantic market [Fastmarkets, 2026; Irish Times, 2026-07-01]. These are supply removals, so they keep tightening the balance without needing demand to recover.

The single most important driver is that China cap. It is a hard ceiling, not a market-clearing limit — China cannot add primary supply even if price rises — and it is already running a deficit with weak demand [Mysteel, 2026-07].

The one clear headwind is Chinese construction: new-home sales down 10.8% by floor area and real-estate investment down 16.2% year-on-year (Jan-May 2026), with home prices falling for a 36th straight month [China NBS, 2026-06]. EV, grid and export demand only partly offset this — the read is "weak domestic, strong external," i.e. the drag is offset, not reversed [Alcircle, 2026].

The single most important risk is not the fundamental case but the positioning around it. The only clean regulatory read (LME positioning report, Nov-2025) showed funds at a record net long — the biggest bullish bet since the series began in 2018 — but that print is over eight months stale and the current one could not be retrieved [Reuters/Andy Home, 2025-11-06]. If that crowded long has held or grown through H1-2026's price highs, the position sits at multi-year price levels with "more room to unwind than to extend," leaving it exposed to a sharp sell-off on any de-escalation headline even if the deficit story stays intact.

## The Specialists, Briefly

- **Commodity macro drivers** → net tilt is supportive: two independent supply squeezes (China's 98.5%-utilised 45Mt cap running a ~322,000t June deficit; European smelter shutdowns plus EU sanctions removing ~340,000t/yr of Rusal metal) outweigh a real Chinese construction-demand headwind (new-home sales -10.8% by floor area). The dollar (DXY ~100.7-101.0, still +2.33% over 12 months) is a minor, fading drag, not a pillar; carbon-border/green-premium effects are a wash, not a driver.
- **Commodity positioning & flows** → crowded on stale data: the last obtainable fund read (Nov-2025) was a record net long above 130,000 lots (gross long ≈5.0Mt); whether it has since held, grown, or unwound is unconfirmed. Flows are not assessable — the aluminium ETF (JJU ~$21.8m) is too thin to read, and the thin COMEX contract carries no global signal.

Most important tension the synthesis flags: the fundamentals are genuinely supportive and show up in physical data (June deficit, sub-300,000t LME warehouse stocks), yet the positioning read is a data gap. The synthesis does not treat falling LME stocks or the backwardation (near-term price above later-dated price, a sign of tightness) as flow confirmation — it separates them as physical-market signals to avoid double-counting.

## What Would Change This Read

- **Beijing raises or exempts smelters from the 45Mt cap** — the synthesis names this as the single biggest bearish risk to the macro read: it would remove the one driver holding the deficit in place, and would compound badly with an already-crowded long.
- **A de-escalation headline unrelated to the deficit** — a Hormuz/Iran resolution, an easing of Guinea's bauxite export cap, or a sharper China demand disappointment — could trigger a long-liquidation sell-off even while the underlying deficit story stays intact.
- **A refreshed LME positioning report** — the synthesis says to get the current print before sizing or timing any trade off positioning; whether the record long has persisted, held, or partly unwound is the key unknown.
- Continued falling LME stocks and backwardation would keep the physical tightness picture intact; a China demand recovery would ease the one clear headwind.

## Bottom Line

- **Verdict:** macro tilt is supportive, driven by supply being removed (China cap, European closures, EU sanctions), not by demand — the synthesis carries this exactly.
- **Why it could be better than it looks:** the deficit is structural and shows up in physical data (June ~322,000t deficit, sub-300,000t LME stocks), so it does not need a demand recovery or a positioning unwind to be true.
- **Why it could be worse than it looks:** if the record Nov-2025 crowded long has persisted into mid-2026, entry is exposed to a sharp, headline-driven shakeout at multi-year prices, and a Beijing decision to lift the cap would knock out the core driver.
- **What evidence is missing:** the current LME positioning report (could not be retrieved) and any usable fund-flow signal (no liquid aluminium ETF) — positioning is a live data gap, not a confirmed reading.
- **One thing to watch next:** refresh the LME positioning report before sizing or timing, and keep the Hormuz / Guinea bauxite / China demand triggers as explicit watch items.

## Plain-English Glossary

- **Production cap (45Mt cap):** a government-set ceiling on how much primary aluminium China is allowed to make each year, regardless of price.
- **Capacity utilisation (~98.5%):** how much of the available smelter capacity is actually in use; near 98.5% means almost no spare room to make more.
- **Deficit (~322,000t):** the market used more metal than it produced over the period, so it had to draw down stock.
- **Net long / crowded long:** funds collectively betting that price will rise; "crowded" means the bet is unusually large and one-sided, so it can reverse sharply.
- **LME positioning report (COTR):** the London Metal Exchange's regulatory report showing how funds are positioned; the current one could not be retrieved, so the reading is stale.
- **DXY (dollar index):** a gauge of the US dollar's strength; a stronger dollar makes metal priced in dollars costlier for non-US buyers.
- **Backwardation:** when the near-term price is higher than later-dated prices — a sign the market wants metal now, i.e. physical tightness.
- **ETF / ETC:** a listed fund that tracks an asset; here it is too small (JJU ~$21.8m) to read investor flows from.

# Macro & Positioning — ALUMINIUM (module synthesis)

## Abstract

The macro backdrop for aluminium is net supportive, and it is supply-side, not demand-side, that is doing the work: China's hard 45Mt production cap has the country running at ~98.5% capacity utilisation and a ~322,000t domestic deficit in June 2026 alone, while European smelter closures (power costs ~€85-95/MWh, a 50-70% premium to North America) and EU sanctions on Rusal-linked metal (~340,000t/yr displaced) are removing Western-basin tonnes at the same time [Mysteel, 2026-07; Fastmarkets, 2026; Irish Times, 2026-07-01]. This is happening despite a genuine demand headwind — Chinese property new-home prices down 3.3% y/y for a 36th straight month [China NBS via Trading Economics, 2026-06] — and a dollar that has been a mild, easing drag (DXY ~100.7-101.0, still +2.33% over 12 months) [Trading Economics, 2026-07-17]. Positioning is the complication: the only clean regulatory read (LME COTR, Nov-2025) showed funds at a record net long — the largest since the series began in 2018 — but that print is over eight months stale, and the current (Jul-2026) LME COTR could not be retrieved [Reuters/Andy Home, 2025-11-06; access attempts 2026-07-18]. The interaction is a genuinely supportive supply squeeze sitting under a positioning overhang that, if it has held or extended, leaves the price vulnerable to a sharp shakeout on any de-escalation headline, independent of whether the underlying deficit narrative is right.

## Net Macro Tilt & dominant driver

**Tilt: Supportive, on balance, with one real headwind and one genuine unknown.**

The dominant driver is China's 45Mt capacity cap. It is a hard, policy-set ceiling, not a market-clearing constraint — China cannot add primary supply regardless of price, and it is already running a domestic deficit (~322,000t in June 2026) even with weak property demand [Mysteel, 2026-07]. Layered on top: Western supply is being independently curtailed by high European power costs (European smelter output on track to fall ~44% y/y for the affected plants in 2026) and by EU sanctions displacing an estimated ~340,000t/yr of Rusal metal from the Atlantic basin [Fastmarkets, 2026; Irish Times, 2026-07-01; S&P Global, 2026]. Both are supply removals, not demand additions, which matters for durability — they don't require a demand recovery to keep tightening the balance.

The one clear headwind is Chinese construction demand: new-home sales down 10.8% by floor area and real-estate investment down 16.2% y/y (Jan-May 2026), the 36th consecutive month of falling home prices [China NBS, 2026-06]. This is a large, structural drag on the biggest traditional end-use, only partly offset by EV/grid/export demand — the specialist calls H1 2026 China demand explicitly "weak domestic, strong external," i.e. offsetting, not reversing, the drag [Alcircle, 2026].

The dollar is a minor, fading complication rather than a real macro pillar here: DXY has eased off its early-July high but is still up 2.33% over the trailing year, so the 12-month currency drag on non-US buying power has not been reversed, only recently stopped worsening [Vantage Markets, 2026-07-15].

CBAM/green-premium dynamics are a wash, not a driver — a genuine cost floor on carbon-intensive metal into the EU offset by an eroding spot green premium as more "clean" supply qualifies [Fastmarkets, 2026].

## Positioning & Flows (crowded? confirming?)

**Crowded, based on stale data — this is the key finding, not a live positioning read.** The last obtainable LME investment-fund COTR print (Nov-2025) showed a collective net long above 130,000 lots (gross long ≈5.0Mt), the largest since the LME began publishing the series in Feb-2018, built up from a near-flat/short-leaning position as recently as Apr-2025 [Reuters/Andy Home, 2025-11-06]. Neither lme.com nor third-party mirrors (MacroMicro) could be reached to confirm whether that record long has since extended, held, or partly unwound through H1-2026 — a period that reportedly included fresh multi-year price highs on Hormuz- and Guinea-bauxite-related supply scares. The thin COMEX Aluminum-MWP contract (net short ~742 lots on ~13,066 total OI) is a US-premium-specific market and carries no signal for global crowding [CFTC Disaggregated COT, 2026-07-14].

**Flows: not assessable.** There is no deep, liquid aluminium-specific US ETF/ETC — JJU exists at only ~$21.8m net assets, too thin and illiquid to read as a flow signal in either direction, and aluminium cannot be isolated out of broad-basket commodity funds [ETF.com, accessed 2026-07-18]. Falling LME warehouse stocks (~286,000-289,000t mid-Jul-2026, a first-since-2022 low) and intermittent cash-3M backwardation are physical stocks/curve signals, not fund flows, and should not be double-counted as flow confirmation.

**Confirming or fading:** as of the last confirmed data point (Nov-2025), fund positioning and price were confirming — the net-long build coincided with the same falling-stocks/backwardation tightness narrative that was pushing price higher. Whether that alignment has continued into mid-2026 is unconfirmed.

## Interaction / timing risk

The macro case (structural China supply cap, Western smelter closures, sanctions) is genuinely supportive and does not depend on a positioning unwind to be true — it is a supply-side deficit that shows up in physical data (the June 2026 domestic deficit, sub-300,000t LME stocks) independent of what speculators are doing. But if the Nov-2025 record crowded long has persisted or grown through H1-2026's price highs, the position is sitting at multi-year price levels with, per the specialist's own read, "more room to unwind than to extend further from already-record levels." That creates a specific timing risk: a de-escalation headline unrelated to the structural deficit (a Hormuz/Iran resolution, an easing of Guinea's bauxite export cap, or a sharper China demand disappointment) could trigger a sharp long-liquidation sell-off even while the underlying China-cap/Western-closure deficit story remains intact — a classic case of a sound fundamental thesis getting a rough entry because it's boarding a crowded trade. The single biggest bearish risk to the macro read itself — a Beijing decision to raise or exempt smelters from the 45Mt cap — would remove the one driver holding the deficit in place, and would compound badly with an already-crowded long.

## 2. The driver that matters most now

The China 45Mt capacity cap is the single macro driver that matters most. At ~98.5% utilisation, China is structurally unable to add primary supply no matter how high the price runs or how strong external (EV/grid/export) demand gets — Mysteel already logged a ~322,000t domestic deficit in June 2026 alone, and Macquarie is forecasting a global 2026 deficit of roughly 930,000t. That hard ceiling is the reason a weak Chinese property market and a firm-ish dollar have not been enough to cap the price (LME still up ~20% y/y even after a recent pullback to ~$3,163/t, with on-warrant stocks below 300,000t for the first time since 2022). What would flip it: a formal decision by Beijing to raise the 45Mt cap or to exempt renewable-powered smelters from it (a live policy discussion) — that would be the single biggest bearish supply-side shock available to this market, since it is the one lever that could add tonnes the property slowdown otherwise can't absorb.

## 3. Read

- **Crowded?** As of the last obtainable data point (Nov-2025), yes — LME investment funds held a record net long (the largest since the COTR series began in 2018), built from a near-neutral/short-leaning stance in Apr-2025. Whether that record long has since extended, held, or partly unwound through H1-2026 (a period in which LME aluminium made fresh multi-year highs on Hormuz-related and Guinea-bauxite-related supply scares, per contemporaneous news) is **inference, not confirmed from data** — the live Jul-2026 COTR print could not be retrieved.
- **Confirming?** Directionally, yes as of the last confirmed read: the fund net-long build coincided with the same supply-tightness narrative (falling LME stocks, cash-3M backwardation) driving the price higher — flows and price were pointing the same way in late 2025. The thin COMEX Aluminum-MWP contract's small net-short print (Jul-2026) should **not** be read as a contrarian signal; at ~13,000 lots of total open interest it is a minor, US-premium-specific market, not the global positioning barometer.
- **Contrarian risk:** a record crowded long sitting on top of a price at multi-year highs is a textbook setup for a sharp long-liquidation move on any de-escalation headline (e.g., a Hormuz/Iran resolution, an easing of Guinea's bauxite export cap, or a China demand disappointment) — the position has more room to unwind than to extend further from already-record levels. This is a timing/risk flag, not a fundamentals call.
- **Overall:** positioning data here is a **risk/timing overlay only** and does not set the commodity's action verdict. The clearest finding is a data gap: the live LME COTR (the primary regulatory positioning series for this commodity) was not reachable, so the crowding call rests on a stale (Nov-2025) print.

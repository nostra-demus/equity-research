## What This Module Found

Copper at ~$13,202/t (about 613¢/lb on COMEX, the US futures exchange) has moved roughly 30–38% off its August 2025 low of $9,535/t, mostly because US buyers front-ran a possible Section 232 tariff (a trade tariff on refined copper) and stockpiled metal — COMEX warehouses went from ~80,000 t to a record ~652,000 t. That distortion pulled the LME (London Metal Exchange) price up with it.

The underlying refined balance is a small surplus, not a shortage. The ICSG (International Copper Study Group) projects a ~96,000 t surplus in 2026, following ~380,000 t in 2025ougholt. Concentrate (unrefined ore) is genuinely tight — smelter payments (TC/RCs) are near zero, a historical low — but that tightness has not yet shown up in the refined market.

The single dominant driver from here is a binary US White House decision on the Section 232 tariff, coming any day. If confirmed at 15%+, LME could push past $14,000/t (Goldman Sachs view). If deferred, roughly $1,000–1,500/t of tariff premium unwinds and the 652,000 t COMEX overhang becomes a 12–18-month demand air-pocket.

The single most important risk is a crowded-long unwind on a tariff disappointment. Speculative positioning is at COT Index ~96/100 (a positioning gauge — how crowded the bullish bet is; ~96/100 is close to the top of history) with COMEX net length ~+66,500 contracts and ETF outflows of ~−$221M in Q2 2026. If the tariff disappoints, fundamental and forced selling arrive together.

The curve is also in contango (later-dated futures cost more than the front month), so a mechanically rolled long — one that keeps replacing an expiring contract with the next — loses roughly 5–6% a year just to stay in the trade.

## Plain-English Glossary

- LME / COMEX — the two main copper futures exchanges; LME (London) sets the world benchmark, COMEX (US) is where the US tariff distortion is playing out.
- Refined vs. concentrate — refined is finished copper metal; concentrate is the semi-processed ore that smelters turn into refined metal.
- TC/RCs (treatment and refining charges) — fees smelters get paid for turning concentrate into refined copper; near-zero means concentrate is scarce relative to smelter capacity.
- ICSG — the International Copper Study Group; the standard source for the global refined balance.
- Contango — when later-dated futures cost more than the front-month contract; a long position that keeps rolling forward loses money on the roll each time.
- COT Index / managed-money net length — positioning gauges from CFTC data; how bullish or bearish speculators are as a group. ~96/100 is close to the historical top of crowding.
- DXY — the US dollar index; a stronger dollar tends to weigh on USD-priced commodities like copper.
- 10-year TIPS real yield — the inflation-adjusted return on a 10-year US Treasury; higher real yields raise the opportunity cost of holding non-yielding assets like industrial metals.
- Section 232 — a US trade-law tool that lets the White House put tariffs on imports for national-security reasons; here, the possible refined-copper tariff.
- CPER — a US-listed copper ETF; the retail proxy that carries the contango cost.

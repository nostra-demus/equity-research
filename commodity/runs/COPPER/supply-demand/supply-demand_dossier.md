# Supply–Demand Balance — COPPER (module synthesis)

## Abstract

The copper market posted a refined surplus of approximately 380,000 t in 2025 (ICSG preliminary, via ScrapMonster citing ICSG, 2026-02-25) and ICSG's April 2026 forecast projects a further surplus of ~96,000 t in 2026 and ~377,000 t in 2027 — a striking reversal from the 150,000 t deficit ICSG itself forecast for 2026 just six months earlier. The visible inventory buffer is large in aggregate (~1.09 Mt across three exchanges), but it is geographically distorted: COMEX holds ~652,000 t at a record high driven by US Section 232 tariff front-running, while LME ex-US stocks are near multi-year lows (~352,000 t), and SHFE is roughly at its historical average (~89,000 t). The direction for 2026 is a continued modest surplus, with the pace of Chinese smelter-cut compliance and the trajectory of China grid/EV demand as the biggest swing factors. Weather is not a driver for copper; the only seasonal signal is the "Golden September" restocking pulse in China beginning late August, which has weakened in predictive value as grid investment — tied to locked-in state plans — has replaced cyclical construction as the dominant demand floor.

## Balance (surplus / deficit)

**2025 (preliminary full year): Surplus ~380,000 t.**
ICSG preliminary data (via ScrapMonster citing ICSG, 2026-02-25) puts the 2025 refined market balance at a surplus of approximately 380,000 t, up sharply from a ~69,000 t surplus in 2024 (same source). An alternative ICSG estimate from October 2025 cited a smaller 289,000 t surplus [Mining.com, 2026]; the range of ICSG's own revisions across publications is ~289,000–380,000 t. Both figures point unambiguously toward surplus, not deficit.

**2026 forecast: Surplus ~96,000 t.**
ICSG's April 2026 Copper Market Forecast [ICSG press release, 2026-04-23] projects a refined surplus of ~96,000 t in 2026. This is the result of: (a) global demand growth revised down to 1.6% (from a prior estimate of ~2.1%), partly due to US tariff and trade-war headwinds; and (b) higher secondary (scrap) refined production, forecast +6.4% YoY. The same ICSG forecast sees the surplus widening to ~377,000 t in 2027 [ICSG press release, 2026-04-23].

**Key balance caveat — the concentrate layer tells a different story.**
The refined surplus coexists with acute tightness at the concentrate (mine output) level. Annual benchmark treatment and refining charges (TC/RCs — the fee smelters charge miners to process concentrate) settled at $0/tonne for 2026, down from ~$21/tonne in 2025, with spot TCs deeply negative through much of 2025 (reaching minus $40–$45/tonne in Q4-2025) [S&P Global Platts review, 2026-01-12]. This is the lowest TC/RC in history and signals genuine mine-supply scarcity. The surplus in refined copper exists because Chinese smelters were running faster than mine output growth, not because demand is weak — and Chinese smelters have now agreed to cut output by more than 10% in 2026 [trade press, S&P Global Platts citing industry sources, 2026 — unverified]. If smelter cuts comply, the refined surplus narrows; if smelters do not cut, the refined surplus persists and mines continue to take the margin hit.

The headline combined figure of ~1.09 Mt covers roughly **2.2 weeks** of global consumption (~28.2 Mt/year = ~2.35 Mt/month). But this number is misleading in two directions simultaneously:

- **It understates the real buffer.** Significant off-warrant and unreported stocks exist in China bonded warehouses and US ports. ING Think (June 2026) estimated an additional ~222,000 t of LME-registered off-warrant copper at US ports [ING Think, June 2026 — unverified secondary]. The true physical buffer is larger.
- **The COMEX build is artificial.** Approximately 60% of visible global exchange stock is now sitting at COMEX, a venue that historically held a single-digit share of global exchange inventories. This is a tariff arbitrage position, not a genuine loosening of physical availability outside the US. LME ex-US stocks at ~352,000 t — far more representative of the rest of the world's physical access — are moderately elevated but not especially comfortable by historical standards.

**Verdict:** The buffer is **ample in aggregate but geographically split**. For a buyer outside the US, LME-accessible stocks are sufficient but not at the comfortable levels that would suggest a deeply relaxed physical market. The COMEX figure is a mirage created by tariff policy.

## Direction & biggest swing factor

**Direction: Modest surplus continuing through 2026–2027, with downside risk to that surplus if Chinese smelter cuts hold.**

The two authoritative readings (ICSG balance + TC/RC market signal) point in opposite directions at different layers of the supply chain. At the **refined metal** level, the balance is in surplus and forecast to remain so. At the **concentrate** (mine output) level, scarcity is acute — zero or negative TC/RCs are the strongest available signal that mines cannot feed smelters at current output rates. The reconciliation: 2025 saw an anomalous period where smelters ran hard despite negative TCs, producing a refined surplus even as mine output was structurally constrained. In 2026, smelter cuts are expected to bring these two layers into closer alignment, tightening the refined surplus without eliminating it.

**Biggest swing factor: China smelter-cut compliance and pace of Chinese demand growth.**

These are two sides of the same coin. China accounts for ~58% of global refined copper demand [ICSG, 2025-2026 via ScrapMonster, 2026-02-25] and the dominant share of global refined copper supply. If Chinese smelters cut output by more than 10% as agreed — and if Chinese grid and EV demand maintains its 2025 trajectory (grid capex up ~37% YoY in Q1 2026 [DiscoveryAlert, unverified secondary, 2026]) — the surplus could narrow sharply and the fundamental picture for copper improves. If smelter cuts are weak or demand growth disappoints on trade-war spillovers, the surplus persists and the TC/RC price signal has been misread.

**Second swing factor: US Section 232 tariff trajectory.**

The Commerce Secretary's 30 June 2026 report on phasing in 15%/30% tariffs on refined copper (cathodes/anodes) from 2027/2028 [Federal Register, 2026-04-09] was not confirmed in primary sources available at run time. Its outcome determines whether the COMEX build unwinds (if tariffs are deferred or reduced) or deepens. This is the single biggest near-term visible-stock direction risk, though it does not change the underlying physical supply-demand balance.

**Third swing factor (structural, longer run): Major mine disruptions.**

Grasberg (Indonesia), Kamoa-Kakula (DRC), and Codelco El Teniente (Chile) together account for an estimated ~350,000–400,000 t of output lost relative to pre-disruption plans in 2026, or roughly 1.5–1.7% of world mine supply [Freeport-McMoRan 8-K, Q1-2026; Ivanhoe Mines press release, 2026-04; Codelco corporate presentation, 9M-2025]. This is a genuine supply headwind at the mine level, which feeds into the TC/RC signal above. It does not reverse the refined surplus by itself, given smelters were running ahead of mine output, but it sets a structural ceiling on how quickly global mine supply can grow over 2026–2027.

**Seasonality:** Not a material near-term driver. We are currently (2 July 2026) in the seasonally soft mid-summer window for Chinese downstream demand. The next seasonal inflection — "Golden September" restocking — begins in late August. It is a mild signal, now weakened by the shift to state-driven grid demand (which is not seasonal in the same way construction is). No directional pressure from the seasonal calendar today.

## 2. Direction & Swing Factors

**Overall supply direction: broadly FLAT to slight RISE at the mine level (+~1–2%), but refined supply growth has BEEN RUNNING FASTER than mine output (+4% refined in 2025) thanks to Chinese smelter expansion. Supply growth is now DECELERATING: primary refined output is forecast to rise only 0.4% in 2026 as concentrate shortage bites, and Chinese smelters have agreed to cut output by >10% in 2026.**

### Swing Factor 1 — Concentrate shortage / TC/RC collapse (MOST IMPORTANT structural signal)

Annual benchmark treatment and refining charges (TC/RCs — the fee miners pay smelters to process concentrate into refined metal) settled at **$0/tonne for 2026**, the lowest ever, down from ~$21/tonne in 2025 and from $80/tonne in 2022. Spot TCs have been **negative** for most of 2025 (reaching minus $40/tonne to minus $45/tonne in Q4-2025), meaning smelters were paying to secure feed [S&P Global Platts review, 2026-01-12; Benchmark Minerals, unverified, 2025]. Antofagasta agreed zero processing charges with at least one Chinese smelter for 2026 [Mining.com, unverified, 2025-12]. This signals genuine tightness at the concentrate (mine output) level, independent of how tight or loose the refined copper market looks. China's top smelter association has agreed to cut smelting by >10% in 2026 and the government has halted ~2 million tonnes of planned new smelting capacity [Unverified web, S&P Global Platts citing sources, 2026]. This removes the 2025 dynamic where refined supply ran well ahead of mine supply.

### Swing Factor 2 — Major mine disruptions compounding grade decline

Three disruptions materially cut 2025 output and carry into 2026:

- **Grasberg (Indonesia, Freeport-McMoRan):** September 2025 mudslide cut 2025 output to ~488 kt vs ~750 kt planned. Freeport estimates the disruption erases ~270 kt from 2026 (~2% of global mine supply). 2026 guidance is ~500 kt; full capacity recovery not expected until late 2027 [Freeport-McMoRan 8-K, 2026-04; Fastmarkets, unverified, 2026].
- **Kamoa-Kakula (DRC, Ivanhoe):** May 2025 flooding/seismic event cut 2025 production by ~28% from the original target (actual: 389 kt). 2026 guidance is 380,000–420,000 kt; western side of Kakula mine restarted but dewatering risks remain; sulfuric acid now produced on-site via own smelter though regional acid shortages (Zambia export ban, Sept-2025) continue to constrain other DRC producers [Ivanhoe Mines press release, 2026-01; 2026-04].
- **Codelco El Teniente (Chile):** July 2025 rockburst cost ~48 kt of output in 2025 with ~25 kt further loss expected in 2026. Broader Codelco ore grade decline (1.02% → 0.66%) is a longer-run drag; 2026 production guidance revised down to ~1,290 kt, a level below every year since 2018 [Codelco corporate presentation, 9M-2025; Robert Friedland / X, 2026 — unverified secondary].

Combined disruption impact: these three mines alone account for an estimated **~350–400 kt of output lost relative to pre-disruption plans** in 2026, equivalent to roughly 1.5–1.7% of world mine supply.

### Swing Factor 3 — US Section 232 tariffs: distorting trade flows, not destroying supply

President Trump finalized 50% tariffs on most copper articles (25% on certain categories) under Section 232, effective **April 6, 2026** with a further adjustment effective **June 8, 2026** [Federal Register, 2026-04-09; CH Robinson advisory, 2026-06-02]. The tariffs do not reduce global mine supply but have massively distorted trade flows: COMEX inventories surged from ~80 kt to a record ~652 kt as traders pre-positioned metal inside the US, while LME stocks outside the US dropped to multi-year lows (<100 kt). This geographic redistribution of above-ground stocks compresses visible ex-US availability and raises the COMEX–LME arb (a key watch item for the market-structure agent). It does not change the world production balance but tightens physical availability in non-US markets.

### Swing Factor 4 — DRC growth offset: Kamoa recovery + regional acid risk

The DRC is the single biggest source of incremental mine supply and expected to surpass Peru as the world's second-largest copper producer. Kamoa-Kakula's 2027 target of 500,000–540,000 kt (vs ~389 kt in 2025) is material — but it depends on successful dewatering of the western panel, ramp-up of the on-site copper smelter to full capacity, and resolution of regional sulfuric acid supply (Zambia banned acid exports in September 2025, disrupting other DRC heap-leach operations) [Ivanhoe Mines press releases, 2025-12 and 2026-04; Unverified web, EcofinAgency, 2026].

6. **US tariff impact on physical flows.** The COMEX inventory build to 652 kt is documented from multiple web sources but is unverified against LME official warrant data. The directional claim (massive COMEX build, LME drawdown) is consistent across multiple sources and is treated as reliable for analytical purposes.

### 1c. Key demand swing factors

**Swing factor 1 — China electrification vs property drag.**
China accounts for ~58% of global refined copper demand (ICSG, 2025-2026 data). **Swing factor 2 — US tariff-driven demand distortion.**
Section 232 tariffs already imposed a 50% rate on semi-finished copper products from August 1, 2025 (Federal Register, 2025-08-05). A 30 June 2026 deadline existed for the Commerce Secretary to report on whether to phase in a 15% tariff on refined copper (cathodes, anodes) from 2027 and 30% from 2028. The anticipation of these tariffs drove US monthly refined copper imports to ~140,000 t — nearly double the 2024 average — between January 2025 and May 2026 (TradingKey, 2026-06-26 [unverified secondary]). This pre-buying inflates apparent US demand in the near term and concentrates global visible inventory at COMEX (see Section 2), but does NOT represent underlying consumption growth; it is inventory building that will eventually reverse.

# Weather & Seasonality — COPPER

## 1. Is weather a driver here?

No. The COPPER profile marks weather as not a material driver. Copper is a base industrial metal
priced by mine supply, industrial demand, and macro conditions — not by growing seasons, monsoons,
or temperature anomalies. No weather analysis is warranted.

The one narrow weather-adjacent caveat noted in the profile is water availability and permit
constraints at mines in Chile and Peru (drought can slow high-altitude operations), but that is a
supply-disruption risk covered under the supply lens, not a seasonal weather pattern.

## 3. Seasonality / calendar position

Copper has one mild and well-documented seasonal pattern: **China's "Golden September / Silver
October" demand window**, when downstream fabricators — wire and cable makers, appliance
manufacturers, construction contractors — typically lift orders and restock ahead of the National
Day holiday (1–7 October) and the year-end infrastructure push. This is the most reliably observed
seasonal lift in Chinese copper consumption. Q1 is the weakest seasonal window due to the Lunar
New Year factory shutdowns.

**Where we sit now (2 July 2026):** We are entering the seasonally soft mid-summer period in
China. July and August are transitional months — downstream activity in construction and
manufacturing can dip before the Golden September restocking begins. This is neither a peak nor a
trough, but the market typically sees fabricator orders build from late August onward.

**A structural shift worth flagging:** The "Golden September" seasonal signal has weakened in
predictive value in recent years. China's grid investment — which rose ~37% year-on-year in
Q1 2026 and is tied to locked-in multi-year state plans — now provides a more consistent demand
floor than cyclical construction ever did. [Web: DiscoveryAlert / China grid investment data, 2026,
unverified.] As a result, the seasonal calendar matters less than it once did; the quarterly
rhythm of smelter maintenance, grid procurement releases, and fabricator inventory cycles has
become the operative cadence.

**Near-term calendar notes:**
- **Now through August:** Seasonally soft demand period in China. Downstream fabricators may
  continue running down inventory rather than adding new orders at current prices. [Web:
  MacroMicro SHFE warehouse stock data, 2026, unverified.]
- **Late August — October:** The Golden September / Silver October restocking window. If Chinese
  demand holds, this is typically when downstream copper demand firms and import appetite rises.
  Historically, China's refined copper imports have climbed 10–15% in this window relative to
  the July trough. [Web: Mining.com — "China copper imports climb 15% on seasonal demand",
  unverified.]
- **Q4 2026:** Year-end grid and construction disbursements. State-linked grid capex is typically
  front-loaded in H1 and back-loaded toward year-end; Q4 procurement can sustain demand even as
  construction seasonally slows.

**Inventory read as a seasonality check (late June 2026):** SHFE copper warehouse stocks fell
roughly 178,500 tonnes from end-March through late June as Chinese buyers restocked on price
weakness and seasonal power-sector demand. LME on-warrant stocks outside the US were near
352,100 tonnes in late June, down from ~400,000 tonnes mid-month, while COMEX inventories
surged to approximately 650,000 tonnes — a geographic redistribution driven by US tariff
arbitrage, not a global drawdown signal. [Web: TradingKey / ConnectOre weekly brief, June 2026,
unverified.]

**Bottom line:** Seasonality is a mild and fading signal for copper. The current calendar
position (early July) sits in the seasonally soft window. The next weather-sensitive or
seasonal inflection to watch is the Golden September restocking pulse beginning in late August.
No directional pressure from weather or calendar today — the dominant drivers remain China
stimulus, grid spend, the US tariff regime, and the global macro cycle.

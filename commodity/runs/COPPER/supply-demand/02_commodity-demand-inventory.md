### 1c. Key demand swing factors

**Swing factor 1 — China electrification vs property drag.**
China accounts for ~58% of global refined copper demand (ICSG, 2025-2026 data). The two forces pulling in opposite directions are: (a) grid investment surging — China's State Grid plans a 40% lift in fixed-asset investment through 2030 vs the prior five-year period (Bloomberg, 2026-01-15 [unverified secondary]), with Q1 2026 power grid capex already up 37% YoY (DiscoveryAlert [unverified secondary]); and (b) the property construction downturn, which has historically been a major copper demand driver but remains depressed. Net result: China usage grew ~4% in 2025 driven by grid/EVs, but the ICSG revised 2026 Chinese growth down to 1.9% reflecting trade-war and macro headwinds.

**Swing factor 2 — US tariff-driven demand distortion.**
Section 232 tariffs already imposed a 50% rate on semi-finished copper products from August 1, 2025 (Federal Register, 2025-08-05). A 30 June 2026 deadline existed for the Commerce Secretary to report on whether to phase in a 15% tariff on refined copper (cathodes, anodes) from 2027 and 30% from 2028. The anticipation of these tariffs drove US monthly refined copper imports to ~140,000 t — nearly double the 2024 average — between January 2025 and May 2026 (TradingKey, 2026-06-26 [unverified secondary]). This pre-buying inflates apparent US demand in the near term and concentrates global visible inventory at COMEX (see Section 2), but does NOT represent underlying consumption growth; it is inventory building that will eventually reverse.

## 2. Inventory / Buffer

For copper, the relevant visible buffer measures are exchange warehouse stocks across LME, COMEX, and SHFE. Unlike agricultural commodities, there is no published global stocks-to-use ratio analogous to the USDA WASDE; instead, exchange stocks vs consumption are the standard read.

### 2b. Implied stocks-to-consumption coverage (indicative only)

Global annual apparent consumption ~28.2 Mt = ~2.35 Mt/month. Three-exchange visible stocks of ~1.09 Mt cover roughly **2.2 weeks** of global consumption. This is NOT the full copper inventory picture — significant off-warrant and unreported stocks exist, especially in China bonded warehouses and US ports (estimated +222,000 t of LME-registered off-warrant copper at US ports per ING Think, Jun 2026 [unverified secondary]). Exchange stocks alone therefore understate the real buffer.

Historical context: in 2021–2022, LME stocks alone fell below 50,000 t (near crisis levels). The current combined ~1.09 Mt represents a return to a more ample visible buffer, though it is geographically skewed — COMEX holds ~60% of visible global exchange stocks vs its normal single-digit share.

Note on forecast flip: ICSG's October 2025 forecast called for a 2026 deficit of 150,000 t. By April 2026 this was reversed to a 96,000 t surplus, driven by (a) downward revision to demand growth (1.6% vs prior 2.1%) and (b) higher secondary (scrap) refined production. This is a material revision and its direction matters: the ICSG is now pointing toward continued supply surplus through at least 2027, not deficit. This contradicts some industry narratives of imminent structural tightness.

## 3. Gaps / low-confidence items

1. **World totals are estimated, not directly from ICSG primary publication.** The 27.4 Mt (2024) and ~28.2 Mt (2025) figures are derived from secondary sources citing ICSG. The ICSG Table 1 PDF exists but was not machine-readable in this run. The implied absolute figures are consistent across multiple secondary sources but should be confirmed against the ICSG Copper Bulletin directly (available to ICSG subscribers, published monthly). Confidence: medium.

2. **SHFE June/July 2026 stocks.** The most recent SHFE data found was from May 2026 (~89,000–192,000 t range from different sources), with earlier reports showing a 291,020 t four-year high in mid-May. The SHFE draws down seasonally into Q2; the June figure was not confirmed from a primary source. The combined three-exchange total as of late June 2026 is an estimate.

3. **End-use sector percentages are approximate and from secondary/vendor sources.** No primary ICSG end-use breakdown was confirmed from the latest bulletin. The ~32% grid/electrical, ~33% consumer devices, ~14% transport structure is consistent across multiple industry sources but is labelled unverified. IEA's 2024 Copper special report would be the highest-quality source for end-use data.

4. **China property demand drag magnitude.** The construction sector's copper share — historically significant — is not quantified from a primary source in this run. Multiple analysts describe ongoing weakness without giving a precise current number. This is a gap: the property-to-grid substitution ratio matters for the China demand outlook.

5. **US tariff final determination (June 30, 2026).** The outcome of the Section 232 report to the White House (deadline 2026-06-30) was not confirmed in primary sources accessed. The pending decision on phased 15%/30% tariffs on refined copper (cathodes/anodes) from 2027/2028 is the single largest near-term demand-distortion risk, as it determines whether the COMEX inventory build will reverse or deepen.

6. **Off-warrant / bonded copper stocks.** A material but unquantified volume of copper sits outside exchange warrant systems — particularly in China bonded warehouses and US ports. This makes the visible buffer potentially larger than exchange stocks alone suggest. Not proven from available primary data.

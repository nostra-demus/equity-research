# Weather & Seasonality — COPPER

## 1. Is weather a driver here?

No. The COPPER profile marks weather as not a material driver. Copper is a base industrial metal
priced by mine supply, industrial demand, and macro conditions — not by growing seasons, monsoons,
or temperature anomalies. No weather analysis is warranted.

The one narrow weather-adjacent caveat noted in the profile is water availability and permit
constraints at mines in Chile and Peru (drought can slow high-altitude operations), but that is a
supply-disruption risk covered under the supply lens, not a seasonal weather pattern.

## 3. Seasonality / calendar position

Copper has one mild and well-documented seasonal pattern: **China's "Golden September / Silver
October" demand window**, when downstream fabricators — wire and cable makers, appliance
manufacturers, construction contractors — typically lift orders and restock ahead of the National
Day holiday (1–7 October) and the year-end infrastructure push. This is the most reliably observed
seasonal lift in Chinese copper consumption. Q1 is the weakest seasonal window due to the Lunar
New Year factory shutdowns.

**Where we sit now (2 July 2026):** We are entering the seasonally soft mid-summer period in
China. July and August are transitional months — downstream activity in construction and
manufacturing can dip before the Golden September restocking begins. This is neither a peak nor a
trough, but the market typically sees fabricator orders build from late August onward.

**A structural shift worth flagging:** The "Golden September" seasonal signal has weakened in
predictive value in recent years. China's grid investment — which rose ~37% year-on-year in
Q1 2026 and is tied to locked-in multi-year state plans — now provides a more consistent demand
floor than cyclical construction ever did. [Web: DiscoveryAlert / China grid investment data, 2026,
unverified.] As a result, the seasonal calendar matters less than it once did; the quarterly
rhythm of smelter maintenance, grid procurement releases, and fabricator inventory cycles has
become the operative cadence.

**Near-term calendar notes:**
- **Now through August:** Seasonally soft demand period in China. Downstream fabricators may
  continue running down inventory rather than adding new orders at current prices. [Web:
  MacroMicro SHFE warehouse stock data, 2026, unverified.]
- **Late August — October:** The Golden September / Silver October restocking window. If Chinese
  demand holds, this is typically when downstream copper demand firms and import appetite rises.
  Historically, China's refined copper imports have climbed 10–15% in this window relative to
  the July trough. [Web: Mining.com — "China copper imports climb 15% on seasonal demand",
  unverified.]
- **Q4 2026:** Year-end grid and construction disbursements. State-linked grid capex is typically
  front-loaded in H1 and back-loaded toward year-end; Q4 procurement can sustain demand even as
  construction seasonally slows.

**Inventory read as a seasonality check (late June 2026):** SHFE copper warehouse stocks fell
roughly 178,500 tonnes from end-March through late June as Chinese buyers restocked on price
weakness and seasonal power-sector demand. LME on-warrant stocks outside the US were near
352,100 tonnes in late June, down from ~400,000 tonnes mid-month, while COMEX inventories
surged to approximately 650,000 tonnes — a geographic redistribution driven by US tariff
arbitrage, not a global drawdown signal. [Web: TradingKey / ConnectOre weekly brief, June 2026,
unverified.]

**Bottom line:** Seasonality is a mild and fading signal for copper. The current calendar
position (early July) sits in the seasonally soft window. The next weather-sensitive or
seasonal inflection to watch is the Golden September restocking pulse beginning in late August.
No directional pressure from weather or calendar today — the dominant drivers remain China
stimulus, grid spend, the US tariff regime, and the global macro cycle.

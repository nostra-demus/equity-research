## 2. Direction & Swing Factors

**Overall supply direction: broadly FLAT to slight RISE at the mine level (+~1–2%), but refined supply growth has BEEN RUNNING FASTER than mine output (+4% refined in 2025) thanks to Chinese smelter expansion. Supply growth is now DECELERATING: primary refined output is forecast to rise only 0.4% in 2026 as concentrate shortage bites, and Chinese smelters have agreed to cut output by >10% in 2026.**

### Swing Factor 1 — Concentrate shortage / TC/RC collapse (MOST IMPORTANT structural signal)

Annual benchmark treatment and refining charges (TC/RCs — the fee miners pay smelters to process concentrate into refined metal) settled at **$0/tonne for 2026**, the lowest ever, down from ~$21/tonne in 2025 and from $80/tonne in 2022. Spot TCs have been **negative** for most of 2025 (reaching minus $40/tonne to minus $45/tonne in Q4-2025), meaning smelters were paying to secure feed [S&P Global Platts review, 2026-01-12; Benchmark Minerals, unverified, 2025]. Antofagasta agreed zero processing charges with at least one Chinese smelter for 2026 [Mining.com, unverified, 2025-12]. This signals genuine tightness at the concentrate (mine output) level, independent of how tight or loose the refined copper market looks. China's top smelter association has agreed to cut smelting by >10% in 2026 and the government has halted ~2 million tonnes of planned new smelting capacity [Unverified web, S&P Global Platts citing sources, 2026]. This removes the 2025 dynamic where refined supply ran well ahead of mine supply.

### Swing Factor 2 — Major mine disruptions compounding grade decline

Three disruptions materially cut 2025 output and carry into 2026:

- **Grasberg (Indonesia, Freeport-McMoRan):** September 2025 mudslide cut 2025 output to ~488 kt vs ~750 kt planned. Freeport estimates the disruption erases ~270 kt from 2026 (~2% of global mine supply). 2026 guidance is ~500 kt; full capacity recovery not expected until late 2027 [Freeport-McMoRan 8-K, 2026-04; Fastmarkets, unverified, 2026].
- **Kamoa-Kakula (DRC, Ivanhoe):** May 2025 flooding/seismic event cut 2025 production by ~28% from the original target (actual: 389 kt). 2026 guidance is 380,000–420,000 kt; western side of Kakula mine restarted but dewatering risks remain; sulfuric acid now produced on-site via own smelter though regional acid shortages (Zambia export ban, Sept-2025) continue to constrain other DRC producers [Ivanhoe Mines press release, 2026-01; 2026-04].
- **Codelco El Teniente (Chile):** July 2025 rockburst cost ~48 kt of output in 2025 with ~25 kt further loss expected in 2026. Broader Codelco ore grade decline (1.02% → 0.66%) is a longer-run drag; 2026 production guidance revised down to ~1,290 kt, a level below every year since 2018 [Codelco corporate presentation, 9M-2025; Robert Friedland / X, 2026 — unverified secondary].

Combined disruption impact: these three mines alone account for an estimated **~350–400 kt of output lost relative to pre-disruption plans** in 2026, equivalent to roughly 1.5–1.7% of world mine supply.

### Swing Factor 3 — US Section 232 tariffs: distorting trade flows, not destroying supply

President Trump finalized 50% tariffs on most copper articles (25% on certain categories) under Section 232, effective **April 6, 2026** with a further adjustment effective **June 8, 2026** [Federal Register, 2026-04-09; CH Robinson advisory, 2026-06-02]. The tariffs do not reduce global mine supply but have massively distorted trade flows: COMEX inventories surged from ~80 kt to a record ~652 kt as traders pre-positioned metal inside the US, while LME stocks outside the US dropped to multi-year lows (<100 kt). This geographic redistribution of above-ground stocks compresses visible ex-US availability and raises the COMEX–LME arb (a key watch item for the market-structure agent). It does not change the world production balance but tightens physical availability in non-US markets.

### Swing Factor 4 — DRC growth offset: Kamoa recovery + regional acid risk

The DRC is the single biggest source of incremental mine supply and expected to surpass Peru as the world's second-largest copper producer. Kamoa-Kakula's 2027 target of 500,000–540,000 kt (vs ~389 kt in 2025) is material — but it depends on successful dewatering of the western panel, ramp-up of the on-site copper smelter to full capacity, and resolution of regional sulfuric acid supply (Zambia banned acid exports in September 2025, disrupting other DRC heap-leach operations) [Ivanhoe Mines press releases, 2025-12 and 2026-04; Unverified web, EcofinAgency, 2026].

## 3. Gaps / Low-confidence Items

1. **ICSG balance revisions are wide.** The Oct-2025 and Apr-2026 ICSG forecasts differ by ~246 kt on the 2026 balance (deficit vs surplus). The April revision is newer but both are based on demand assumptions that are themselves uncertain. Treat any specific surplus/deficit figure as directional, not precise.

2. **2025 actual mine production tonnage not confirmed by ICSG primary.** The ICSG Table 2 PDF was not readable; absolute mine production figures for 2025 are sourced from secondary web reports citing ICSG, not from the ICSG document directly. Confidence: moderate. The directional read (+~1.4% mine growth in 2025) is consistent across multiple secondary sources.

3. **Codelco 2026 guidance conflict.** One set of sources shows Codelco guiding 2026 at ~1,290 kt (revised down); another cites a narrower range of 1,331–1,357 kt. The lower figure (from Robert Friedland's X post and a Discovery Alert write-up) may reflect a more recent update after the El Teniente rockburst assessment; the higher figure may be an earlier guidance. This gap of ~40–60 kt matters but does not change the directional read.

4. **China smelter cut compliance.** The agreement by top Chinese smelters to cut output >10% in 2026 is sourced from trade press (S&P Global Platts/unverified web) citing industry sources, not from official Chinese regulatory disclosure. Whether compliance holds is uncertain; non-compliance would flip the refined supply balance back toward surplus.

5. **Secondary (scrap) supply is growing fast** (+5.8% in 2025; +6.4% forecast for 2026 per ICSG Apr-2026) and is an increasing offset to mine-supply tightness. Scrap economics are driven by the copper price itself — a high price pulls more secondary supply into the market, partially self-correcting the balance. This dynamic is not fully captured in ICSG point-estimates.

6. **US tariff impact on physical flows.** The COMEX inventory build to 652 kt is documented from multiple web sources but is unverified against LME official warrant data. The directional claim (massive COMEX build, LME drawdown) is consistent across multiple sources and is treated as reliable for analytical purposes.
